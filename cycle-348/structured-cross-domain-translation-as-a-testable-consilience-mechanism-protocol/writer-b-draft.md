# Structured Cross-Domain Translation as a Testable Consilience Mechanism: Protocol Design, Truth-Preservation Conditions, and Cross-Domain Precedents from Machine Learning and Mathematics

## Abstract

Interdisciplinary analogy-making is usually treated as an ad hoc rhetorical act rather than a reproducible research procedure, so claims of "deep structural similarity" between fields are difficult to evaluate, fund, or falsify. This paper formalizes a conjecture—that systematically translating a single formal object into multiple domain lexicons and forcing a unified meta-principle synthesis produces genuine consilience—into a testable protocol. We revise and extend the structured cross-domain translation (SCDT) design of [12] with two additions. First, we ground the protocol in cross-domain precedents: machine-learning systems that control what crosses a domain boundary through disentanglement, cycle consistency, intermediate domains, and statistics-based adaptation [1]–[4], [6], [7], [9], [11], and mathematical works that execute genuinely truth-preserving cross-domain translations of integrals into $L$-values, of knot invariants into combinatorial cocycles, and of unitaries into $*$-homomorphisms [5], [8], [10]. Second, we derive the protocol's evaluation structure with full arithmetic: for $n_t = 10$ objects and $n_d = 4$ lexicons the method generates $60$ pairwise analogy hypotheses and $180$ evaluation points, with a $20.0$ h expert budget under stated assumptions; under an assumed per-link spuriousness prior $p_0 = 0.05$ the unmitigated false-link probability is $P_{\text{any}} \approx 0.265$, falling to $\approx 0.059$ with validation sensitivity $s = 0.8$; and the projected novelty-comparison effect is $z \approx 2.728$ under assumed rates $p_0 = 0.05$, $p_1 = 0.15$. No empirical results are reported; all forward-looking numbers are labeled projections.

## 1. Introduction

When a researcher notices that the same formal pattern appears in, say, information theory and cognitive science, the discovery is usually narrated as luck or genius. The cost of leaving analogy-making unstructured is concrete: interdisciplinary research proposals are evaluated without any procedure for distinguishing structural isomorphism from surface metaphor, spurious cross-domain claims consume reviewer and funder attention, and genuinely convergent insights are produced unreproducibly, so they cannot be accumulated, audited, or taught. A working procedure that converts analogy-making into a measurable research-ideation method would change three decisions: how ideation studies are designed (a fixed protocol with pre-registered thresholds instead of open-ended prompting), how interdisciplinary claims are vetted (a computed structural-overlap score plus adversarial validation instead of rhetorical plausibility), and how such programs are budgeted (an explicit expert-hour cost per hypothesis generated).

The conjecture formalized here—developed in the prior protocol paper [12]—is that translating one formal object (a theorem, model, or framework) into several domain lexicons—physics, computer science, cognitive science, and information theory—and then *forcing* a unified "meta-principle" synthesis produces genuine consilience: convergent insights that no single-domain analysis yields. The conjecture has three components that must be separated before they can be tested:

1. **Translation**: a single formal object $O$ is rendered into $n_d$ distinct domain lexicons, each rendering using the native primitives of the target domain.
2. **Comparison**: the $n_d$ renderings are compared pairwise, generating candidate structural analogies.
3. **Synthesis**: a forced unification step produces a single meta-principle claimed to hold across the domains, treated as a prediction about connections no single-domain analysis would have produced.

The central scientific question is not whether such analogies *feel* illuminating but whether the procedure (a) generates hypotheses novel relative to single-domain baselines, (b) generates analogies that experts judge valid, and (c) produces syntheses whose claimed cross-domain connections survive later scrutiny. Each is measurable in principle; none has been measured under a controlled protocol.

Two bodies of work sharpen this question, and both are treated substantively in Section 2. The machine-learning literature on cross-domain transfer shows that naive transfer across domains fails and that every successful system introduces an explicit boundary-control mechanism: one-shot translation methods fail when naively applied and require a two-step generative process [1]; recommendation systems exploit graph structure to transfer across up to $13$ domains [2]; cycle-consistent cooperative networks enforce consistency between unpaired domains [3]; AdaFlow adapts across domains by swapping normalization statistics, about $36$ times faster than fine-tuning [4]; shared discriminators [6], learned priors [7], content–style disentanglement [9], and decomposition into geometry and appearance sub-mappings through an intermediate domain [11] each control a different leakage path. The mathematical literature supplies existence proofs that cross-domain translation can be *truth-preserving*: systematic translation of elliptic-integral moments into critical $L$-values of modular forms yields a dimension formula and explicit identities such as $\int_0^1 K(m)^2\,\mathrm{d}m = \frac{7\zeta(3)}{2}$ [5]; a Vassiliev invariant $v_3$ is translated, via Gauss diagram formulas, into a combinatorial one-cocycle on a moduli space of knots, proving a conjecture of Thomas Fiedler [8]; and functional calculus gives a bijective correspondence between unitary elements of a $C^*$-algebra and unital $*$-homomorphisms from $\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})$, which underwrites a complete classification result [10]. Our truth-preservation condition (Section 3.3) is designed to distinguish the [5], [8], [10]-type translations from the surface similarities the machine-learning literature warns against.

This paper is deliberately a design-and-analysis paper, not an empirical one. We define the protocol formally (Section 3), derive its combinatorial and statistical structure with full arithmetic (Section 4), and specify the computational experiment that would confirm or falsify the conjecture (Sections 5–7). We report no measurements; every forward-looking number is a projection with stated assumptions.

Contributions:

- A four-phase SCDT protocol (translate, compare, synthesize, validate) specified formally over relational structures and domain lexicons, revising the design of [12] (Section 3.2).
- A truth-preservation condition for cross-lexicon analogies, with a graded structural-overlap score and a proved proposition characterizing when synthesis is conservative, calibrated against the truth-preserving mathematical precedents [5], [8], [10] (Sections 3.3, 4.2).
- A derived combinatorial cost structure: $N_{\text{hyp}} = 60$ hypotheses, $E_{\text{total}} = 180$ evaluation points, and a $20.0$ h expert budget for the stated configuration, with sensitivity to the lexicon count and a pre-filter variant reducing the budget to $11.0$ h (Sections 4.1, 4.5).
- Derived risk and power projections under stated assumptions: $P_{\text{any}} \approx 0.265$ unmitigated and $\approx 0.059$ with sensitivity $s = 0.8$, and a projected standardized effect $z \approx 2.728$ at assumed rates $p_0 = 0.05$, $p_1 = 0.15$, falling to $z \approx 1.640$ at $p_1 = 0.08$ (Sections 4.3, 4.4).
- A falsifiable evaluation blueprint with pre-registered thresholds and explicit falsification conditions (Sections 3.4, 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The relevant literature falls into three clusters: boundary-control mechanisms in cross-domain machine learning, truth-preserving cross-domain translation in mathematics, and the prior SCDT protocol itself.

**Boundary control in cross-domain machine learning.** A consistent finding across the applied literature is that naive reuse across domains fails, and that success requires an explicit mechanism deciding what carries over. Benaim et al. pose the one-shot setting—given a single image $x$ from domain $A$ and images from domain $B$, generate the analog of $x$ in $B$—argue this task underlies the ability of cognitive agents to act in the world, and report empirical evidence that existing unsupervised domain-translation methods fail on it; their method instead follows a two-step process built on a perceptual-blending framework in which an agent generates a virtual sample analogous to an observed one and updates its internal model by comparison [1]. For our protocol this is the minimal-data warning: when a formal object is rendered into an unfamiliar lexicon, the protocol cannot assume the lexicon already "knows" the object, so each rendering must be validated against the object's own relations rather than against lexicon-internal statistics. In multi-domain recommendation, Mercier et al. note that treating domains as separate input sources fails to capture the interplay between domains, and propose MAGRec, which uses graph neural networks to model intra- and inter-domain sequential relations with an Interacting History representation; on Amazon review data they form partitions from two up to thirteen domains and report better performance than state-of-the-art methods across these settings [2]. The relevance is architectural: a single model carrying many domain renderings simultaneously is feasible, which supports the plausibility of one LLM agent holding $n_d$ lexicon renderings of one object. In unsupervised cross-domain translation, Xie et al. represent each domain's distribution by a generative cooperative network—an energy-based model plus a latent variable model—and train the two networks by alternating MCMC teaching for maximum likelihood and cycle consistency, generalizing the framework to unpaired image-sequence translation [3]. Cycle consistency is the closest machine-learning analog of our truth-preservation check: a mapping is trusted only if translating there and back reproduces the source, which is the intuition behind our lexicon-perturbation robustness test (Section 3.3). AdaFlow attacks the adaptation-cost problem: because adapting a density estimator to a new domain by fine-tuning requires iterative optimization unsuited to resource-limited devices, Ohkubo et al. replace activation normalization layers with AdaBN so that adaptation reduces to adjusting statistics; they report AdaFlow adapts to a new dataset about $36$ times faster than fine-tuning-based adaptation with slight accuracy decrease, and demonstrate unpaired photo-to-painting translation between $400$ photos and $400$ Van Gogh-style paintings [4]. The lesson for us is that cheap, statistics-level reconfiguration between domains is possible when the domain-specific content is factored into a small, swappable component—exactly the role our lexicon $(V_i, \rho_i)$ plays relative to the shared object $O$. In unsupervised image-to-image translation, a single shared discriminator between the two GANs improves training stability, and evaluation by Fréchet Inception Distance and Kernel Inception Distance shows the method at par with attention-based mechanisms even without masks or attention [6]; the methodological point we import is that a *shared* judge across domains is a workable design, anticipating our condition-blind expert rubric. For adversarial autoencoders, the choice of latent prior has a profound effect on expressiveness when the generative network has limited capacity, so a learned prior—produced by a code generator that transforms a manually specified simple prior into a better-fitting form—is trained jointly with the autoencoder, and the resulting model is shown to perform cross-domain translation [7]. This supports our design choice that the lexicon itself should be extracted from domain-native sources and adapted per object, rather than fixed a priori. Multi-mapping translation unifies two previously separate perspectives: existing methods handle either multi-domain or multi-modal translation but not both, so DMIT disentangles images into content and style representations, where content is excluded from variation among domains and styles, and thereby covers both one-to-many regimes, with reported gains in quality and diversity on season transfer and semantic image synthesis [9]. The content/style split is precisely the distinction our protocol must enforce—domain-invariant structure versus domain-specific surface—when it strips connotations before comparison. Finally, CariGANs decompose unpaired photo-to-caricature translation into CariGeoGAN for geometry and CariStyGAN for appearance; their ablation shows that training the appearance mapping directly from photo domain $X$ to caricature domain $Y$ lets textures corrupt face structure, whereas routing through an intermediate domain $Y'$ that bridges geometric differences allows the GAN to focus on appearance translation alone [11]. This is direct evidence that a hard decomposition of what transfers (structure) from what is re-rendered (surface) is load-bearing, not decorative—the same decomposition our admissibility requirement imposes on renderings.

**Truth-preserving cross-domain translation in mathematics.** Three works show what a successful, verifiable cross-domain translation looks like at full rigor, and they calibrate the upper end of our truth-preservation scale. Wan and Zudilin systematically translate certain integrals involving moments of the elliptic integral into $L$-values of modular forms on $\Gamma_1(4)$, $\Gamma(4)$ and $\Gamma_1(8)$, and then use relations among critical $L$-values of cuspidal newforms to obtain a dimension formula for the space spanned by these integrals; their numerical examples include the identities $\int_0^1 K(m)^2\,\mathrm{d}m = \frac{7\zeta(3)}{2}$ and $\int_0^1 K(m)K(1-m)\,\mathrm{d}m = \frac{\pi^3}{8}$, obtained by translating between the integral space $\mathcal{I}_k(\Gamma)$ and the $L$-value space [5]. This is a worked instance of exactly the structure our protocol posits: a dictionary (their theta-series and modular lambda machinery of Section 2), a preserved relation set (linear dependence over the number field $K$), and a synthesis (the dimension formula) that no single-side analysis had produced—the paper notes that mathematics offers little progress toward lower bounds for this space because it reduces to difficult irrationality questions, so the translation supplies the achievable upper bound. In knot theory, the goal is to prove a conjecture of Thomas Fiedler that a formula based on the Vassiliev invariant $v_3$ defines a one-cocycle in a moduli space of knots in the solid torus; the translation proceeds through Gauss diagrams—combinatorial encodings of knot diagrams with signed crossing arrows—and Gauss diagram formulas, so that the analytic invariant becomes a combinatorial one-cocycle whose evaluation on canonical loops yields knot invariants, with the contribution correspondence checked locally under all eight types of Reidemeister move III [8]. The local check under Reidemeister moves is a truth-preservation audit in our sense: each allowed transformation of the source representation must preserve the cocycle relation, which is the finite-relation analog of our requirement that every $r \in R_O$ have a well-typed, relation-preserving image. In operator algebra, there is a bijective correspondence between the set $\mathcal{U}(A)$ of unitary elements of a unital $C^*$-algebra $A$ and the set of unital $*$-homomorphisms from $\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})$ to $A$, defined through functional calculus by $\varphi_u(f) := f(u)$; building on a conjecture that the Cuntz semigroup classifies unitary elements of unital AF-algebras, the complete existence proof is given by lifting weakly semiprojective $\mathrm{Cu}$-morphisms $\alpha : \mathrm{Lsc}(\mathbb{T}, \overline{\mathbb{N}}) \to \mathrm{Cu}(A)$ to $*$-homomorphisms $\phi : \mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T}) \to A$ with $\mathrm{Cu}(\phi) = \alpha$, first in the finite-dimensional case and then via weak semiprojectivity, with an obstruction to uniqueness exhibited in the $\mathrm{AH}_1$ case by two unitaries agreeing at the $\mathrm{Cu}$ level yet failing approximate unitary equivalence [10]. This supplies our sharpest cautionary case: even a bijective correspondence at the level of one invariant ($\mathrm{Cu}$) does not guarantee full equivalence—classification can hold for existence and fail for uniqueness—so a crosswalk with high structural-overlap score may still fail to preserve distinctions that matter; our score's $R^{-}_{ij}$ term exists to record exactly such one-directional failures.

**The prior protocol.** The SCDT protocol paper [12] specifies the four-phase design, the relational-structure formalism, the truth-preservation proposition, and the derived quantities $N_{\text{hyp}} = 60$, $E_{\text{total}} = 180$, the $20.0$ h expert budget, $P_{\text{any}} \approx 0.265$, and the projected $z \approx 2.728$, and states the falsification conditions for the conjecture. This paper retains that structure and adds what [12] lacks: a substantive grounding of the protocol's design choices in the boundary-control mechanisms of [1]–[4], [6], [7], [9], [11], and a calibration of the truth-preservation condition against the mathematical precedents [5], [8], [10], together with a corrected sensitivity analysis of the power projection (Section 4.4).

**Gap.** None of [1]–[4], [6], [7], [9], [11] addresses scientific ideation: all are engineering works in which the target domain is fixed and labeled data or a benchmark exists. The mathematical works [5], [8], [10] achieve truth-preserving translation but case by case, with the dictionaries constructed by hand by experts. The protocol paper [12] supplies the ideation-side design but not the precedent grounding. The present paper supplies the bridge: a protocol whose boundary controls are modeled on the mechanisms the machine-learning works validate, and whose truth-preservation target is exemplified by the mathematical works.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Definitions

Let $O$ be a formal object (theorem, model, or framework), represented as a relational structure:

$$O = (E_O, R_O)$$

where $E_O$ is a set of entities (variables, states, operators) and $R_O$ is a set of relations among them (equalities, orderings, dynamics, type constraints). A **domain lexicon** $L_i$ ($i = 1, \dots, n_d$) is a pair $(V_i, \rho_i)$ where $V_i$ is a vocabulary of domain primitives and $\rho_i$ is a typing map assigning each primitive a role signature. A **rendering** of $O$ in $L_i$ is a map:

$$\tau_i : E_O \to V_i \cup \{\bot\}$$

where $\bot$ denotes "no faithful rendering exists in this lexicon." A rendering is **admissible** if every relation in $R_O$ among rendered entities has a well-typed counterpart in $L_i$. A **crosswalk** is a pair of renderings $(\tau_i, \tau_j)$ together with a claimed correspondence $\phi_{ij} : \tau_i(E_O) \to \tau_j(E_O)$. A **meta-principle** is a statement $M$ such that every accepted rendering entails $M$ under the lexicon's own semantics.

### 3.2 The SCDT protocol

The protocol has four phases, as specified in [12]:

1. **Phase T (Translate)**: produce renderings $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_{n_d}$ of the same object $O$, one per lexicon, each required to be admissible or to explicitly report $\bot$. Lexicons are extracted from domain-native sources and adapted per object, following the learned-prior principle of [7] and the per-domain statistics adaptation of [4].
2. **Phase C (Compare)**: for each pair $(i, j)$, extract the candidate analogy: a bijection $\phi_{ij}$ between rendered entity sets, together with the set of relations preserved under $\phi_{ij}$. Following the decomposition evidence of [11], each rendering separates structural content from domain surface before comparison, in the sense of the content/style disentanglement of [9].
3. **Phase S (Synthesize)**: force a single meta-principle $M$ stated in lexicon-neutral terms, required to specialize to each rendering. Phase S maintains a beam of $B$ candidate meta-principles rather than one.
4. **Phase V (Validate)**: score novelty against single-domain baselines, submit analogies for expert rating under a single shared rubric blind to condition (the shared-judge design of [6]), and register syntheses as predictions. A cycle-consistency check in the spirit of [3] re-translates each accepted crosswalk back into $L_i$ and requires recovery of the original relation set.

### 3.3 Truth-preservation condition

The central formal question is when the crosswalk $\phi_{ij}$ constitutes real isomorphism rather than surface similarity.

**Definition (Truth-preserving crosswalk).** The crosswalk $\phi_{ij} : \tau_i(E_O) \to \tau_j(E_O)$ is truth-preserving for $O$ if there exists a bijection $\phi_{ij}$ such that for every relation $r \in R_O$ and all tuples of rendered entities, $r$ holds on the $L_i$ side if and only if its $\phi_{ij}$-image holds on the $L_j$ side.

Full truth-preservation is an isomorphism; partial preservation is graded. We quantify it with a structural-overlap score:

$$s(\phi_{ij}) = \frac{|R^{+}_{ij}|}{|R^{+}_{ij}| + |R^{-}_{ij}| + |R^{0}_{ij}|}$$

where $R^{+}_{ij}$ is the set of relations preserved by $\phi_{ij}$, $R^{-}_{ij}$ the set preserved in one direction only, and $R^{0}_{ij}$ the set of relations with no image under $\phi_{ij}$. A crosswalk with $s(\phi_{ij}) = 1$ is a true isomorphism; $s(\phi_{ij}) = 0$ means no relation survives, i.e., pure surface similarity.

The three mathematical precedents calibrate this scale. The translation of [5] preserves linear dependence among integrals exactly, so on the relation set "linear relation over $K$" it attains $s = 1$ and its synthesis (the dimension formula) is conservative. The translation of [8] preserves the cocycle relation only after verifying the contribution correspondence under each of the eight local types of Reidemeister move III—an explicit enumeration of the preserved relation set, which is what our $R^{+}_{ij}$ census formalizes. The correspondence of [10] is bijective at the level of the $\mathrm{Cu}$ invariant, yet two unitaries in $\mathcal{C}([0,1]) \otimes M_{2^{\infty}}$ agree at the $\mathrm{Cu}$ level and still fail approximate unitary equivalence: a relation set that omits the distinguishing relations yields a high $s$ while the crosswalk fails on uniqueness. Hence a high score is necessary but not sufficient, and the relation enumeration itself must be audited (Section 6).

**Proposition (sufficient condition for conservative synthesis).** If conditions (i)–(iii) hold for all pairs $(i,j)$ in a set $\mathcal{P}$ of crosswalks—(i) $\phi_{ij}$ is a bijection; (ii) every relation $r \in R_O$ with arity $a(r)$ satisfies $a(\phi_{ij}(r)) = a(r)$; (iii) every inference in $S_O$ valid under $L_i$'s semantics is valid under $L_j$'s semantics after applying $\phi_{ij}$—and the meta-principle $M$ is derivable from $S_O$ alone, then $M$ is entailed by every rendering $T_j$ with $j$ appearing in some crosswalk in $\mathcal{P}$.

*Derivation.* By (iii), validity of inferences is preserved under each $\phi_{ij}$; by (i)–(ii), the relational structure is identical in both images, so any derivation of $M$ from $S_O$ transfers step-by-step. Since $M$ is derivable from $S_O$ alone, and each $T_j$ preserves the derivation, each $T_j$ entails $M$. $\square$

The proposition shows when the synthesis step is *truth-preserving*: it adds no content beyond what the shared structure carries. Conversely, a meta-principle $M'$ that is *not* derivable from $S_O$ but is compatible with all renderings is a genuine conjecture generated by the crosswalk structure—this is where novelty lives, and it is exactly where spuriousness risk also lives.

**Spuriousness criterion.** A candidate analogy is classified spurious if $s(\phi_{ij}) < \theta$ for a pre-registered threshold $\theta$, or if the preserved relations are all of arity 1 (unary predicates carry no relational structure). Two robustness checks complete the criterion, each modeled on a machine-learning precedent. First, a cycle-consistency check in the spirit of [3]: re-translating the $L_j$ rendering back into $L_i$ must recover the original relation set up to the pre-registered tolerance. Second, a lexicon-perturbation check: re-rendering with a perturbed lexicon (synonym substitution within $V_i$) must leave $s(\phi_{ij})$ unchanged to within tolerance; sensitivity to lexical perturbation indicates that the analogy rides on surface wording rather than structure, the failure mode that one-shot methods exhibit when naively applied to unseen domains [1].

### 3.4 Evaluation design (pre-registered, not yet run)

The computational experiment compares two conditions on a fixed benchmark of $n_t$ formal objects, as specified in [12]:

- **Baseline condition**: LLM agents asked, per object, for "interdisciplinary insights," with no protocol.
- **SCDT condition**: the same agents run through Phases T–S with $n_d$ fixed lexicons.

Outcome measures: (a) novelty of cross-domain hypotheses, judged against a pre-registered corpus of known connections; (b) expert-rated validity of analogies on a defined rubric, blind to condition; (c) predictive success of Phase-S syntheses, scored by whether registered connections are later confirmed. No data for (a)–(c) exist yet; Section 5 reports only quantities computed in Section 4 and clearly labeled projections.
