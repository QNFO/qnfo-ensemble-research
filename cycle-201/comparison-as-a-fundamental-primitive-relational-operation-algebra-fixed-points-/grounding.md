## Research idea

Comparison as the Fundamental Ontological Operation Underlying Physical Law. The author conjectures that comparison—correlation, relation, ratio—is the single primitive operation from which all physical structure, measurement, and even spacetime emerge, and that consciousness differs only in being able to toggle this operation on and off. A paper could formalize this: define a minimal relational algebra in which states are nothing but cross-comparison outcomes, then derive whether familiar structures (metric geometry, quantum correlations, thermodynamic quantities) emerge as fixed points of iterated comparison. Simulations could test whether tensor-network or relational-observer models reproduce spacetime emergence from comparison graphs alone, and whether 'suspending' comparison (removing relational updates) yields a well-defined, information-free limit. The conjecture matters because it would unify measurement theory, entanglement-based spacetime programs, and relational quantum mechanics under one primitive, while making a falsifiable claim: no physical law should be expressible without comparative structure.

The Bloch sphere—the canonical representation of a qubit’s state—is a smooth, continuous manifold with no discrete boundaries, allowing infinitesimal thermal drifts to accumulate linearly into significant errors.   § 1 Introduction: What does it mean for a physical quantity to be "real"? The question has divided physicists and philosophers for centuries. The realist position holds that quantities appearing in successful theories refer to entities that exist independently of measurement. The instrumentalist position holds that theories are computational devices for predicting measurement outcomes, and questions about "reality" are ill-posed. The operationalist position, associated with Bridgman [1], holds that the meaning of a quantity is exhausted by the operations used to measure it.
