# Factoring Hardness as a Bayesian Prior: A Decision-Theoretic Model of Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration Timing

## Abstract

The urgency of post-quantum cryptography (PQC) migration is usually justified by the threat of quantum factoring, yet that threat rests on an unproven complexity-theoretic conjecture: that integer factoring is not in BPP, the class of problems solvable in bounded-error probabilistic polynomial time. This paper treats that conjecture as an explicit Bayesian prior rather than a settled fact, and embeds it in a decision-theoretic model of migration timing for RSA-2048. We model a two-hypothesis mixture: under hypothesis $H_{\mathrm{BPP}}$ a cryptographically relevant breakthrough arrives with annual hazard $h_{\mathrm{BPP}} = 0.10$; under $H_{\mathrm{HARD}}$ the hazard is $h_{\mathrm{HARD}} = 0.01$; the prior weight is $\pi = \Pr(H_{\mathrm{BPP}})$. With migration cost normalized to $C_m = 1$, breach loss $L$, and discount rate $r = 0.05$, we derive closed-form expected discounted losses for any migration date $T$ and a closed-form loss threshold $L^*(\pi)$ above which immediate migration is optimal. At the baseline $\pi = 0.2$ the mixed hazard is $\bar{h} = 0.028$ and $L^* \approx 2.7857$: any asset whose compromise costs more than $2.79$ migration-cost units justifies migrating immediately, independent of timing optimizations. Sensitivity analysis shows the immediate-migration region is robust: for $L = 10$ even $\pi = 0$ triggers immediate migration, while for $L = 2$ the prior must exceed $\pi^* \approx 0.4444$. The framework converts an unproven conjecture into an auditable sensitivity parameter.

## 1. Introduction

The deployment of post-quantum cryptography is one of the largest planned cryptographic transitions in history, and its urgency is almost always argued from the anticipated capability of quantum computers to factor integers in polynomial time via Shor's algorithm. But the security relevance of that argument depends on a logical structure that is rarely made explicit: quantum factoring threatens RSA-2048 only if classical polynomial-time factoring is impossible, i.e., only if FACTORING $\notin$ BPP, where BPP is the class of decision problems solvable in bounded-error probabilistic polynomial time. If a classical algorithm ever factored RSA-2048 efficiently, quantum computers would be irrelevant to the RSA threat; if factoring is hard classically, the quantum threat is real but its arrival time is governed by engineering timelines. The conjecture FACTORING $\notin$ BPP is unproven. Migration policy therefore conditions on a conjecture, and the rational response to conditioning on an unproven proposition is to treat one's confidence in it as a prior and propagate it through the decision.

This paper does exactly that. We construct a minimal decision-theoretic model in which the probability mass assigned to "factoring falls in BPP" (or, more precisely, to a regime in which cryptographically relevant breakthroughs arrive fast) is a first-class parameter $\pi$, and we compute optimal migration schedules for RSA-2048 assets as a function of $\pi$, the breach loss $L$, the migration cost $C_m$, and the discount rate $r$. The model is deliberately simple — two hypotheses, constant hazards, exponential discounting — because its purpose is not forecasting but exposing the sensitivity structure: which conclusions about migration timing survive, and which collapse, when the epistemic status of the hardness conjecture is made explicit.

Our contributions are:

1. A closed-form Bayesian decision model for PQC migration timing under a two-hypothesis mixture over factoring hardness (Section 3).
2. Full arithmetic derivations of the mixed hazard, the expected discounted loss for candidate migration dates $T \in \{0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30\}$, and the critical loss threshold $L^*(\pi)$ (Section 4).
3. A sensitivity analysis over the prior $\pi$ showing that for high-value assets the "migrate now" recommendation is robust even at $\pi = 0$, while for low-value assets the decision flips at a computable prior threshold $\pi^*$ (Sections 4 and 5).
4. A discussion of how much of the claimed quantum-advantage narrative for cryptography depends on the single unproven premise FACTORING $\notin$ BPP, and of the program of classifying known exponential speedups to stress-test claims such as "at least 80% of exponential speedups reduce to abelian hidden subgroup structure" (Sections 6 and 7).

Throughout, every number is either an input declared in Section 3 or computed with shown arithmetic in Section 4. No empirical data on expert surveys or engineering timelines are invented; where such data would refine the inputs, we say so explicitly.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We organize the related work into three threads: the PQC migration problem itself, Bayesian methods for priors and expert judgement, and analogues of migration and displacement modeling in adjacent fields.

**PQC migration management.** The most directly relevant work is the PMMP framework of [2], which addresses the problem that organizations must plan migrations to quantum-resilient cryptographic measures and states that, to the authors' knowledge, no generalized approach existed that manages such a complex migration while explicitly integrating into organizations' steering mechanisms and control systems; PMMP presents a risk-based process for this purpose. Our model is complementary to [2] in a specific sense: PMMP supplies the organizational process through which a migration decision is executed, while we supply the decision-theoretic core that determines *when* the migration should occur, conditional on an explicit prior over the hardness conjecture. A risk-based process needs a quantified risk; our Sections 3–5 provide one candidate quantification whose inputs are auditable.

**Hardness under restrictions.** Work on computational hardness under severe restrictions offers a cautionary analogy. The Tetris analysis of [1] proves that Tetris clearing and survival remain NP-hard even when the piece set is restricted to a single tetromino type, for every tetromino type except the O piece. The structural lesson we import is twofold: first, hardness claims can be established rigorously for restricted problem variants, so "hardness" is a property that can be pinned down rather than asserted; second, and conversely, the entry of [1] concerns NP-hardness of a puzzle, a *worst-case* statement, whereas cryptographic security needs *average-case* hardness against natural distributions — a gap that is exactly why the RSA-2048 threat ultimately rests on the unproven average-case conjecture FACTORING $\notin$ BPP rather than on any worst-case theorem.

**Bayesian priors, mixtures, and expert judgement.** Our two-hypothesis prior over factoring hardness is a spike-and-slab-style mixture: a point mass on "breakthrough regime" and a diffuse mass on "hard regime." The Bayesian group lasso literature of [5] revisits the Bayesian group lasso using spike and slab priors for group variable selection, demonstrates the connection of the model with penalized regression, and points out the role of the posterior median for thresholding, showing the posterior median estimator has the oracle property under orthogonal designs. We borrow the structural idea — a two-component prior in which one component is a point mass — and apply it to a hypothesis rather than a coefficient. On the question of where the prior $\pi$ should come from, the Posterior Belief Assessment framework of [7] addresses precisely the situation in which a Bayesian analysis is too complex to assert a precise correspondence between expert probabilistic judgements and the chosen prior and likelihood, and proposes a finite procedure for attributing meaning to the results of such an analysis; this is the natural methodological home for eliciting $\pi$ from complexity-theory experts, though we do not perform such an elicitation here. On the choice of prior form itself, the objective Bayesian analysis of bilateral data in [8] derives Jeffreys' prior and Bernardo's reference prior for three parameters of interest (the risk difference, the risk ratio, and the odds ratio) under Dallal's model and the saturated model; the analogy is that when little is known, an objective or reference prior over the hardness hypothesis is a defensible default, and our sensitivity analysis over $\pi \in [0,1]$ is the practical substitute for committing to one.
