# Is There a $p$-Adic $\pi$? Haar-Measure Circle Constants over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, an Adelic Product Formula, and a Test of the Conjecture $\pi_p=(p-1)/p$ (Revised Version 2.2.0)

## Abstract

The real constant $\pi \approx 3.14159$ is defined as the ratio of a Euclidean circle's circumference to its diameter, and an active adelic-physics program postulates a place-wise analogue $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ at every prime $p$. No geometric computation had tested whether $p$-adic circle geometry supports any such value. We compute, in the Haar-measure framework on $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ with the sup-norm, the measure of the $p$-adic sphere $S_r = \{x : \|x\| = r\}$ and the associated scale-invariant ratios. The derivation yields $\mu(B_r) = r^2$, $C_p(r) = r^2(1-p^{-2})$, and the circle constants $\kappa_p = 1 - p^{-2}$ and $\kappa'_p = \tfrac{1}{2}(1-p^{-2})$, independent of the radius $r$. The finite product over $p \le 13$ is $P_{13} = 15482880/25050025 \approx 0.618078$, approximating the Euler product $\prod_p \kappa_p = 6/\pi^2 \approx 0.607927$ with a gap of $0.010151$; the all-place combination $\pi \prod_p \kappa_p = 6/\pi \approx 1.909859 \ne 1$. The exact difference from the conjectured value is $\kappa_p - (p-1)/p = (p-1)/p^2 > 0$ for every prime. These results refute the conjecture $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ within the Haar-measure formulation, but not in full generality: an anisotropic ultrametric norm yields $\kappa_p = (1-p^{-2})/c$, so the constant is convention-dependent and no definition of $p$-adic circumference is intrinsically canonical. The computation supplies adelic modelers with a concrete consistency benchmark and a sharpened falsifiability condition.

## 1. Introduction

Participants in the adelic-physics program face a concrete, unresolved decision: whether a theory formulated over all Ostrowski completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ may replace the archimedean circle constant $\pi$ by a family of place-wise constants $\{\pi_p\}$, and in particular by the specific values $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ advocated in the harmonic-paradigm audit [8]. If that identification held, adelic regularizations and place-by-place reconstructions of physical geometry could proceed with rational constants at every finite place, and the archimedean $\pi$ would be demoted to one projection among many. If it fails, every adelic model that inserts $(p-1)/p$ as a circle constant inherits an unverified — and, in at least one natural geometric realization, false — input. Today no member of that program has a computed geometric benchmark: the conjecture $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ is asserted from completion-theoretic considerations [8], [10], not derived from any $p$-adic geometry, and the published computation [9] that first tested it overclaimed its own result, announcing that it "disproves the strong adelic-$\pi$ conjecture" when in fact it only refutes one chosen geometric realization. A correct verdict matters because it changes what an adelic modeler must do next: abandon the conjecture outright, or specify which definition of $p$-adic circumference would make it true.

This paper answers the question inside a precisely stated framework. We fix the sup-norm on $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$, normalize Haar measure by $\mu(B_1) = 1$, define the $p$-adic circle of radius $r$ as the sphere $S_r = \{x : \|x\| = r\}$, and define its circumference as the Haar measure $C_p(r) = \mu(S_r)$. Within this framework the ratio $C_p(r)/D_p(r)$ against the ultrametric diameter $D_p(r) = r$ is well defined, scale invariant, and computable in closed form. We then compare it term by term with the conjectured $(p-1)/p$, combine the place-wise constants into finite and infinite Euler products, and test the sensitivity of the answer to the geometric conventions. Section 2 surveys the number-theoretic, physical, and programmatic context. Section 3 states the model. Section 4 carries out every derivation with the arithmetic displayed. Section 5 collects the results. Section 6 discusses scope, limitations, and falsifiability, including the two corrections this revision makes to the published version [9]. Section 7 translates the findings into practice for adelic modelers.

**Contributions.**

2. The identification of the scale-invariant circle constants $\kappa_p = 1 - p^{-2}$ and $\kappa'_p = \tfrac{1}{2}(1-p^{-2})$, with exact rational and decimal values for $p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13$ (Section 4, Step 4).
3. The exact evaluation of the finite product $P_{13} = 15482880/25050025 \approx 0.618078$ with every multiplication shown, the infinite product $\prod_p \kappa_p = 6/\pi^2 \approx 0.607927$, and the all-place combination $6/\pi \approx 1.909859 \ne 1$ (Section 4, Steps 5–7).
4. The exact term-by-term comparison with the conjecture $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$, giving $\kappa_p - (p-1)/p = (p-1)/p^2$ and $\kappa_p/((p-1)/p) = 1 + 1/p$ (Section 4, Step 8).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The question of a place-wise circle constant sits at the intersection of $p$-adic arithmetic, $p$-adic mathematical physics, and the adelic-physics program. Each of the three bodies of work supplies a specific ingredient.

**Special values and $p$-adic $L$-functions.** The $p$-adic Beilinson conjecture of [1] formulates a $p$-adic analogue of Borel's theorem, relating regulators on higher $K$-groups of number fields to special values of zeta-functions through syntomic regulators and $p$-adic $L$-functions; its Section 2 records the archimedean class-number-formula identity $w\sqrt{|D_k|}\,\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_k(s) = 2^{r_1}(2\pi)^{r_2}\,|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)|\,R$, in which the archimedean $\pi$ enters through the factor $(2\pi)^{r_2}$. This is the paradigm case of a classical constant appearing inside a special-value formula whose $p$-adic analogue is conjectural but actively pursued. In the same direction, [7] constructs $p$-adic multiple $L$-functions in several variables as generalizations of the Kubota–Leopoldt $p$-adic $L$-functions, proves their $p$-adic analyticity and continuity (its Theorems 1.21 and 1.24), and evaluates their special values at non-positive integers in closed form (its Theorem 2.1). These works show that transferring archimedean special-value arithmetic to the $p$-adic places is a live and computable subject — the same transfer that a place-wise $\pi_p$ would require. On the motivic side, [3] introduces a family of $p$-adic Stark regulators for $p$-stabilized Artin representations, formulates an Iwasawa–Greenberg main conjecture, and proves an interpolation formula for a generator of the characteristic module $\mathrm{char}_\Lambda X_\infty(\rho, \rho^+)$ (its Theorem A); interpolation across places is precisely the structural mechanism by which a hypothetical $\pi_p$ family would have to cohere, so [3] supplies the analytic template against which any adelic circle-constant proposal should be checked.

**Place-dependent geometry and physics.** In $p$-adic statistical mechanics, [2] reduces the description of $p$-adic Gibbs measures for a three-state Potts model with competing interactions on a Cayley tree to a recursive equation and proves that a phase transition occurs if and only if $p = 3$, for any nonzero interaction strength; the authors conclude that the transition depends only on the place $p$, behavior they explicitly contrast with the real case. This is a worked instance of geometric quantities being intrinsically place-dependent, which is the phenomenon our $\kappa_p = 1 - p^{-2}$ exhibits. In $p$-adic quantum field theory, [5] constructs two concrete $p$-adic geometric objects — a $p$-adic square root and a $p$-adic light cone — and proves that the convergence radius of the square-root Taylor series is nonzero for $p > 2$, so that the region admitting the square root is a genuine $p$-adic counterpart of the future light cone, built from cylinders of radius $N_p(t) = p^k$. The companion paper [6] develops the underlying machinery: the $p$-adic norm $|x| = p^{-k_0(x)}$ read off the lowest digit of the $p$-adic expansion, algebraic extensions of arbitrary dimension, and the canonical correspondence between $p$-adic and real numbers. Together [5] and [6] demonstrate that $p$-adic geometry can be built place by place from primitives, but neither defines a $p$-adic arc length or circle constant; the Haar-measure route taken here fills that specific gap. In combinatorial geometry, [4] introduces $p$-adic equiangular lines in $\mathbb{Q}_p^d$ and proves the $p$-adic van Lint–Seidel relative bound $|n|^2 \le |d|\max\{|n|, \gamma^2\}$, the $p$-adic analogue of the real bound $n(1-d\gamma^2) \le d(1-\gamma^2)$; the resulting extremal constants are again place-specific, confirming that $p$-adic analogues of classical geometric constants exist and are computable, while illustrating that they need not equal their archimedean counterparts.

**The adelic-physics program and the conjecture under test.** The harmonic-paradigm audit [8] lists as its third finding the claim that "$\pi$ IS adelic — $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ at each prime $p$", resting on Ostrowski's theorem and the adelic product formula $\prod_v |q|_v = 1$ displayed in its Section 1.3. The taxonomy paper [10] catalogues measure-theoretic artifacts of physics' exclusive operation at the archimedean place — sixteen documented completion failures across five categories — and proposes an adelic restructuring of quantum mechanics, cosmology, and physical law with an eight-claim falsifiability matrix; its Section 1.3 states the same product formula over the adele ring $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p' \mathbb{Q}_p$. The program-level framing is given by [11], which asks whether the adelic completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ constrains the values of fundamental physical constants, and — importantly for method — reports an honest negative result (its Chapter 7, item D2): candidate matches between zeta-zero gaps and particle mass ratios were statistically falsified after Bonferroni correction and a permutation null model, demonstrating that mathematical beauty does not imply physical truth. That standard of self-testing is the one this paper applies to the conjecture $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$. Finally, the published predecessor of the present computation [9] reports the Haar-measure results $\kappa_p = 1 - p^{-2}$, $P_{13} = 15482880/25050025 \approx 0.618078$, and $\pi\prod_p \kappa_p = 6/\pi \approx 1.909859$; its Discussion, however, states that the strong conjecture "fails" without scoping the failure to the chosen circumference definition. The present revision keeps every correct number of [9], corrects its two defective claims (documented in Section 6), and adds the convention-sensitivity analysis that the scoping requires.

## 3. Methods

**Arena and norm.** Fix a prime $p$. On $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ we use the sup-norm

$$\|x\| = \max\bigl(\|x_1\|_p,\ \|x_2\|_p\bigr), \qquad x = (x_1, x_2) \in \mathbb{Q}_p^2,$$

where $\|\cdot\|_p$ is the $p$-adic absolute value, characterized on $\mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}$ by $\|p^r n/m\|_p = p^{-r}$ for $p \nmid nm$ [2], [6]. The values taken by $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb{Q}_p^2 \setminus \{0\}$ are exactly $r \in p^{\mathbb{Z}}$, $r > 0$.

**Measure.** Let $\mu$ be the Haar measure on the additive group $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$, normalized by $\mu(B_1) = 1$ where $B_1 = \{x : \|x\| \le 1\}$. Haar measure is the unique translation-invariant Borel measure on $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ up to scaling; under dilation $x \mapsto ax$ it transforms by the factor $\|a\|_p^2$, because $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ is a two-dimensional module over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

**Circle.** For $r \in p^{\mathbb{Z}}$, $r > 0$, define the closed ball and sphere

$$B_r = \{x : \|x\| \le r\}, \qquad S_r = \{x : \|x\| = r\} = B_r \setminus B_{r/p}.$$

$S_r$ is the $p$-adic analogue of a Euclidean circle: the set of points at exact distance $r$ from the origin.
