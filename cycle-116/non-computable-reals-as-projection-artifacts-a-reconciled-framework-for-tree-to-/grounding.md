## Research idea

Non-computable reals as artifacts of tree-to-manifold projection. The author conjectures that the continuum is not primitive but constructed: natural numbers, rationals, and computable reals arise from a single distinction operator via enclosure operations, and the full real line emerges only through a 'Monna-map' projection of a discrete, non-Archimedean Bruhat–Tits tree onto a smooth Archimedean manifold. The key claim: non-computable reals (ℝ ∖ ℝcomp) are not discovered but created by this lossy projection — they are artifacts with no discrete counterpart. This matters because it would re-derive the computable/constructive hierarchy from pure distinction logic (Spencer-Brown style) and explain the measure-theoretic dominance of non-computables as a projection effect. A formal analysis could: (1) define the projection precisely and prove which reals it adds; (2) show the image's measure/dimension properties (e.g., that artifacts form a full-measure set); (3) simulate finite-depth tree approximations and compare their 'shadows' to decimal expansions, testing whether computable reals correspond exactly to finite or limit-definable tree paths.
