# Singularity as Epistemic Artifact: Cyclical Cosmology Without Actual Infinities

## Abstract

Cosmological singularities — points where density, curvature and temperature become infinite — mark the breakdown of predictive physics at the Big Bang and inside black holes, and they block any quantitative continuation of collapse or of the early universe. Whether these infinities are features of reality or artifacts of the mathematical representation is an open question. This paper tests the conjecture that actual infinities in gravitational physics are artifacts of adopting a linear, unbounded number line, and that in a cyclic framework a "singularity" is a transition between epochs rather than a true infinity. We combine a formal audit of the hypotheses of the singularity theorems with explicit toy models. In a closed dust universe with density parameter $\Omega_0$, recollapse occurs at a finite scale factor $a_{\max}/a_0 = \Omega_0/(\Omega_0-1)$; for $\Omega_0 = 1.2$ this gives $a_{\max} = 6\,a_0$ and a finite peak density $\rho_{\max} = 216\,\rho_0$. A re-parametrization of density by an angle, $\rho/\rho_c = \tan(\theta/2)$, sends the finite state $\theta = \pi$ to $\rho \to \infty$, demonstrating that the divergence lives in the chart, not the state. A discrete clock spanning the Hubble radius at Planck spacing contains $N \approx 8.50 \times 10^{60}$ states, making wraparound transitions finite by construction. The results reframe singularity theorems as representation-sensitive and define a concrete simulation and observation program.

## 1. Introduction

Cosmologists and gravitational physicists face a concrete, unsolved problem: the classical theories they use predict that certain physical quantities become infinite. At the Big Bang, "physical quantities like density, pressure, temperature, spacetime curvature etc, were infinite and thus theoretically ill-defined" [1]. Inside classical black-hole collapse the same happens, and the Penrose–Hawking theorems of the 1960s converted singularities from unrealistic limiting cases into "central, unavoidable features of gravitational collapse" [10]. The practical cost is severe: no equation of state, no initial condition and no prediction can be formulated at or beyond an infinite quantity, so the origin of the universe and the final state of collapse are currently outside quantitative physics altogether. Infinities are, in this precise sense, "an end point for physics: their appearance means that we can not associate a finite number to the system under study" [3].

What would change if the conjecture of this paper holds? If the divergences signal the inadequacy of a linear, unbounded number line rather than a physical endpoint, then singularity theorems should be readable as statements about representations, cyclic models should be reformulable with finite, constructively defined quantities, and the observational program comparing cyclic against singular cosmology acquires a well-defined target: finite curvature maxima with Penrose-style conformal boundaries, rather than true infinities. Model builders would gain a design principle — choose parametrizations in which every observable is bounded — and observationalists would gain discriminators between "transition" and "termination" readings of the same data.

This paper pursues that conjecture with formal analysis and toy models, and it is explicit that the toy models are kinematic constructions, not solutions of Einstein's equations. The contributions are:

- A representation result: an explicit re-parametrization $\rho(\theta) = \rho_c \tan(\theta/2)$ in which a finite cyclic state $\theta = \pi$ maps to an infinite linear density, with numerical evaluation at $\theta = \pi - 10^{-3}$ giving $\rho/\rho_c \approx 2000$ (Section 4.3).
- A finite-recollapse result for a closed dust universe: $a_{\max}/a_0 = \Omega_0/(\Omega_0-1)$, evaluated at $\Omega_0 = 1.2$ to give $a_{\max} = 6\,a_0$ and $\rho_{\max} = 216\,\rho_0$, and at $\Omega_0 = 1.0005$ to give $a_{\max} \approx 2001\,a_0$ and $\rho_{\max} \approx 8.012 \times 10^{9}\,\rho_0$ (Section 4.2).
- An audit of which hypotheses of the singularity theorems presuppose an unbounded linear parameter, showing that geodesic incompleteness is defined through an affine parameter ranging over $\mathbb{R}$ and is therefore representation-sensitive (Sections 3.4 and 4.4).
- A discrete clock model with state count $N = c/(H_0\,l_P) \approx 8.50 \times 10^{60}$ across the Hubble radius, in which epoch transitions are wraparound events rather than divergences (Section 4.5).
- A consistency check using horizon evolution: the pure de Sitter event horizon $D_e = c/H_0 = 1.373 \times 10^{26}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 14.5$ Gly is exactly the stationary solution of the horizon-recession equation $\frac{dH_e}{dt} = H_e H - 1$ of [7] (Section 4.6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The starting point is the status of the Big Bang singularity in the standard model. [1] reviews cyclic cosmology and records that in the standard paradigm the universe was born from a spacetime singularity where density, pressure, temperature and curvature were infinite and theoretically ill-defined, and that open geometries must be excluded because they correspond to infinite proper three-volume at the singularity. The same review establishes the structural obstacle this paper must address: within General Relativity, a non-singular cyclic model requires "a non-conventional fluid with negative effective energy density, in order to cancel the matter component and lead to a non-singular bounce," and such fluids "usually leads to quantum instabilities and makes the theory ill-defined" [1]. Their conclusion is that the more promising route is extended Einstein gravity, for example higher-order curvature terms, while noting that the whole discussion assumes homogeneity, which may not hold according to Sloan Digital Sky Survey analysis [1]. For this paper, [1] supplies both the motivation (the singularity is ill-defined, not merely surprising) and the constraint (any cyclic construction must either violate an energy condition or modify gravity, and must answer for inhomogeneity).

The philosophical target — that infinities measure ignorance rather than reality — is argued directly in [3]. That work identifies a minimal length as the main feature of quantum gravity, analogous to the maximal velocity $c$ of special relativity and the minimal action $\hbar$ of quantum mechanics, and argues that a finite region of phase space contains only finitely many distinguishable states [3]. Its conclusion states the position this paper formalizes: every infinity and its overcoming is the turning point for a new paradigm, and "what is truly infinite may just be the abyss of our ignorance" [3]. [3] also frames the Zeno problem as still open, noting that Zeno was confused by the assumption that every infinite series diverges — a caution this paper takes seriously by checking convergence explicitly in its own constructions.

On the quantum side, [2] constructs a Friedmann model in which the Wheeler–DeWitt solution corresponds exactly to classical evolution: using de Broglie–Bohm and modified de Broglie–Bohm trajectory representations, they show that exact quantum-classical correspondence is equivalent to the vanishing of the quantum potential, and that for a total energy density varying as $1/a^{n}$ this happens for $n = 2$ [2]. The resulting coasting evolution is a concrete example of a cosmology in which the "well-known problems in quantum cosmology disappear" [2]. This matters here because a coasting law $\rho \propto a^{-2}$ is exactly the case where the gravitational dynamics is scale-free, and scale-free dynamics is the natural habitat of cyclic (angle-valued) rather than linear (unbounded) variables.

Alternative quantizations support the same direction. [4] proposes a quantization of homogeneous cosmological models combining the Dirac constrained-system method, Fock space construction, second quantization and a Diagonalization Ansatz (Bogoliubov transformation plus Heisenberg equation), arguing that canonical, loop and path-integral quantizations apply quantum mechanics too straightforwardly and that the Wheeler–DeWitt functional differential equation "does not allow to solve the generic problem in principle" [4]. Their resulting quantum cosmology is well defined in a Fock space and has a temperature that is an explicit function of redshift $z$ [4]. This demonstrates that the formal apparatus of cosmology is not wedded to any single representation of its state space — the precondition for the re-parametrization arguments of Section 4.3.

Observationally, the relevant quantities are horizons and curvature, both finite and measurable. [7] defines the particle horizon as the boundary of the region already observed by an observer and the event horizon as the boundary of what will ever be observable, and derives, for a concordance universe with cosmological constant and dust, the remarkably simple recession equations $\frac{dH_p}{dt} = H_p(z)H(z) + 1$ and $\frac{dH_e}{dt} = H_e(z)H(z) - 1$, noting that these results are independent of the current values of the involved constants [7]. Section 4.6 uses these equations as a consistency check. Spatial curvature itself is tightly coupled to structure formation: [8] develops a model with non-zero spatial curvature, a running vacuum energy density and warm dark matter, showing that curvature modifies the evolution of matter perturbations and the shape of the matter power spectrum, and derives observational constraints on the parameters $\nu$, $b$ and $\Omega_k^0$ [8]. Any cyclic model with non-negligible curvature therefore inherits quantitative LSS constraints, not merely qualitative ones.

The observational pathway for testing cyclic signatures runs through facilities such as the Square Kilometre Array. [6] presents the Phase 1 SKA surveys and forecasts that the SKA1 Medium-Deep Band 2 weak-lensing survey will deliver constraints comparable to Stage III optical surveys such as DES, with radio–optical cross-correlations free of wavelength-dependent systematics retaining almost all statistical power [6]. [6] also reports a predicted kinematic radio dipole amplitude $A = 0.0046$ directed along the CMB dipole, with the large-scale-structure contribution $A = 0.0031 \pm 0.0016$ from their mock simulations [6]. These forecasts define the precision floor against which any cyclic-versus-singular discriminator must be measured.

An alternative cosmological phenomenology, in which redshift arises from a global gravitational effect, is due to [5]: for homogeneous matter distribution the cosmological gravitational redshift is proportional to the square of distance, $z_{grav} \sim r^2$, an idea traced through de Sitter (1916, 1917), Eddington (1923), Tolman (1929) and Bondi (1947), with Hubble himself calling the discovered distance–redshift relation the "De Sitter effect" [5]. This is cited here for a specific methodological point: the interpretation of the same redshift data is representation- and mechanism-dependent, which is exactly the epistemic situation this paper claims for singularities.

Finally, the conjecture of this paper sits within a family of informational and non-Archimedean frameworks. [9] models the universe as a computational system in which energy is "an abstraction used to quantify the magnitude of change within a system, rather than a fundamental constituent of the world," with discrete energy levels as eigenvalues of an informational structure [9]. [10] diagnoses the singularity problem in exactly the terms used here: singularities indicate "a loss of predictive power for physical laws, not merely a coordinate artifact," and "strongly suggest that the smooth continuum approximation is fundamentally incomplete" [10]. [11] argues for a "gappy" ontic bedrock at the Planck scale using spectral dimensions and $p$-adic distance metrics, on which smooth spacetime appears only at macro-scale resolution [11]. [12] supplies the mathematical motif this paper exploits: the cross-ratio is invariant under any projective transformation $T \in \operatorname{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F})$, so that "the universe does not speak in English, nor in base-10 integers, nor in differential equations. It speaks in pure, scale-relative, invariant proportions" [12]. The invariance theorem of [12] is the precise statement that physical content should be read from quantities that survive a change of representation — the standard by which Section 4.3 judges whether an infinity is real or chart-bound.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Scope and honesty of the construction

The paper's claims are of two kinds. Formal claims concern definitions and representations and are proven by explicit construction. Physical claims concern the universe and are limited to toy models that are kinematically defined and are not asserted to be solutions of Einstein's equations. Where a number is a projection rather than a derivation, it is labeled as such with its assumptions.
