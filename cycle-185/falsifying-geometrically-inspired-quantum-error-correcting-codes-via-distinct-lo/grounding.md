## Research idea

Falsifying geometric QEC via distinct logical error scaling. The note's original contribution is a falsifiability criterion: a quantum error-correcting scheme derived from non-standard (e.g., p-adic/Bruhat–Tits geometric) foundations is only a theory if it yields a Hamiltonian and a distinct, testable scaling law — specifically logical error rate pL ∝ pphys^(d/2), differing from surface-code scaling — measurable by comparing logical error rates against surface codes at matched physical qubit counts and physical error rates. A paper could formalize this: given any geometrically motivated code, derive whether its stabilizer structure implies a modified threshold exponent, simulate the resulting Lindblad dynamics, and show whether the predicted pL(pphys, d) curve is distinguishable from surface codes. This converts an ambiguous null-result problem into a sharp discriminating experiment between geometric and standard topological codes.
