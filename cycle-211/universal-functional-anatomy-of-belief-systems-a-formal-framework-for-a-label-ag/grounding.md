## Research idea

Belief systems as cultural technologies realizing five universal functions. Claim: beneath doctrinal diversity, all religious, philosophical, and secular traditions instantiate a finite set of universal functions—profound salience, reciprocal conduct codification, orienting narrative, inner transformation techniques, and community formation—operating independently of metaphysical content, so 'religion' is a structural feature of human cognition rather than a category of belief. This matters because it would ground a label-agnostic comparative science of meaning-making and reframe pathologies (dogmatism as narrative rigidity, cults as communal corruption) in functional terms. Testable: formalize the five functions as measurable dimensions, code diverse traditions (Buddhism, Stoicism, humanism, Marxism) on them, and use computational agent-based models or cognitive simulations to show whether the functions emerge from basic cognitive/social mechanisms (agency detection, mortality salience, cooperation needs) and whether dysfunction predicts observed pathologies.
