## Research idea

Physical Irrelevance of Uncountability: Computable Reals Suffice for Continuum Physics. Claim: uncountability of the real line has no physical consequences; any physically meaningful continuum can be modeled on the computable (or constructible) reals, which are countable. The note argues that non-computable reals like Chaitin's Ω are 'physically irrelevant' because they cannot be accessed, and that continuum properties (depth vs. breadth) may not map onto physical reality. A paper could formalize this by (1) proving that standard physical theories (e.g., classical mechanics, QM) restricted to computable reals reproduce all empirically verifiable predictions, or (2) constructing simulation models where dense-but-countable computable continua are empirically indistinguishable from uncountable ones, showing no experiment can detect the cardinality difference. This would test whether cardinality distinctions like the Continuum Hypothesis are empirically vacuous, connecting mathematical foundations to physical observability.
