## Research idea

Parity-Protected Qubits at 4 Kelvin for Scalable Quantum Computing. The author conjectures that quantum computing scalability is thermodynamically bottlenecked at millikelvin temperatures, and that 'parity-protected' qubits operating at 4 K—exploiting the ~20,000x cooling-power increase over dilution refrigerators—could enable scalable architectures. A paper could test this by quantitatively modeling error budgets at 4 K: thermal quasiparticle populations relative to superconducting gaps (e.g., niobium's 2Δ ≈ 700 GHz vs. kBT ≈ 83 GHz), thermally activated two-level-system decoherence in Nb2O5, and achievable two-qubit gate fidelities, then deriving whether any parity-protection scheme can suppress these error channels below fault-tolerance thresholds. Monte Carlo simulation of quasiparticle poisoning rates and comparison against demonstrated 1.5 K silicon spin qubit data would determine if the 4 K operating point is physically viable or fundamentally excluded by material constraints like aluminum's 1.2 K critical temperature.
