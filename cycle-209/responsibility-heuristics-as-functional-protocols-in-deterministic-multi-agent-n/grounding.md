## Research idea

Responsibility Heuristics as Functional Protocols in Deterministic Multi-Agent Networks. The author claims that if agents are deterministic systems, the 'illusion of free will' and the responsibility heuristic are load-bearing protocols whose functional utility can be analyzed mathematically: societies run better when agents treat choice as real, enabling game-theoretic optimization and stability at scale. This matters because it reframes retribution versus corrective feedback as competing intervention policies in a deterministic multi-agent system, predicting that feedback-based policies reduce recidivism and maximize collective payoff. A testable path: formalize agents with internal 'responsibility' heuristics in a deterministic multi-agent simulation, compare retributive versus corrective-feedback governance policies across network sizes, and measure stability, cooperation, and recidivism-like metrics. Complement with an analytical treatment (mean-field or evolutionary game theory) showing conditions under which retaining the choice-illusion protocol outperforms its removal, and derive scaling laws for systemic stability analogous to emergent fluid dynamics from molecular dynamics.
