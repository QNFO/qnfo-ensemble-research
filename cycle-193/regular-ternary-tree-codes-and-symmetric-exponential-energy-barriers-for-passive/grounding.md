## Research idea

Regular Ternary Tree Codes Give Symmetric Exponential Error Barriers. The claim: strict Bruhat–Tits (p=2) tree decoding protects only one logical state because the root's odd branching forces tie-breaking asymmetry; replacing it with a regular ternary tree (every node has 3 children, no ties for odd p) yields a minimum leaf-flip barrier B(d)=2^d for BOTH logical states, with zero logical errors at depth ≥7 for p_err ≤ 0.40. Why it matters: it suggests ultrametric (tree) geometry, not Euclidean lattice geometry, can be a foundation for quantum error correction, with the barrier equal to the cophenetic distance between opposite logical states—a discrete height function. How to test: (1) formally prove the 2^d barrier via induction on the ultrametric inequality and triadic rigidity; (2) extend classical majority-vote simulations to full quantum settings—superposition, entanglement, non-Clifford gates—and compare logical error rates and thresholds against the surface code at matched depth and physical error rates; (3) check whether the root-asymmetry deviation preserves all ultrametric properties, and derive the gate threshold and depth-scaling exponents predicted by the program's falsifiability list.
