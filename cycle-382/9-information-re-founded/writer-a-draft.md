# Re‑Founding Information: An Autaxic Ontological Framework with Quantitative Illustrations

## Abstract
The absence of a unified, ontologically grounded theory of information hampers interdisciplinary research, obscures the design of data‑centric technologies, and inflates costs in fields ranging from quantum physics to artificial intelligence. Existing syntactic accounts such as Shannon’s theory and semantic approaches remain fragmented, while proposals that treat information as a fundamental “stuff” lack clear derivations. This paper integrates the autaxic generative engine—spontaneous symmetry breaking, relational processing, and conservation of distinguishability—into a coherent ontology of information. We complement the conceptual development with two quantitative illustrations derived solely from published sources: (1) a combinatorial estimate of the configuration space of a spin‑glass system limited to $10^{3}$ spins, yielding $2^{1000}\approx1.07\times10^{301}$ distinct states; (2) an average conference acceptance rate computed from reported 10 %–20 % ranges, giving $15\%$ as a representative figure. Both calculations are shown step‑by‑step using only cited inputs. The results demonstrate how autaxic distinctions generate vast informational capacities and how empirical publication statistics reflect informational bottlenecks. By grounding information in objective autaxic processes while acknowledging emergent relational significance, the framework offers a parsimonious bridge between syntax and semantics, and provides a basis for future formalisation efforts.  

## 1. Introduction
Scientists, engineers, and policy makers across physics, biology, computer science, and philosophy confront a persistent obstacle: the lack of a single, ontologically deep definition of “information.” In practice this fragmentation forces interdisciplinary teams to translate between Shannon‑style channel capacities, semantic‑information metrics, and speculative “it‑from‑bit” narratives, incurring hidden coordination costs, duplicated effort, and misaligned design criteria. For example, quantum‑information experiments must reconcile statistical‑mechanical entropy with algorithmic compressibility, while AI developers must map semantic relevance onto syntactic token counts. If a unified theory were available, designers of quantum communication protocols could directly infer semantic payload limits from underlying physical distinctions, and AI pipelines could allocate resources based on a principled measure of relational significance rather than ad‑hoc heuristics.  

This paper addresses the “so what?” by presenting an autaxic ontology of information that (i) grounds informational units in objective distinctions generated by spontaneous symmetry breaking, (ii) explains how relational processing endows these distinctions with functional significance, and (iii) connects the ontology to existing quantitative observations in physics and computer‑science practice.

## 2. Background and Related Work
The literature provides diverse perspectives on information that motivate the autaxic synthesis.

* **[1]** observes that “science itself… can be understood differently” and that computer science “has become a phenomenon and fashionable” shaped by socioeconomic capabilities. This underscores the need for a unifying conceptual substrate that transcends disciplinary fashions.  

* **[2]** demonstrates that “algorithms from computer science have ‘broken the size barrier’ for some problems in statistical physics” and explicitly states that “with the genetic algorithm, we can study up to of order $10^{3}$ spins.” The spin‑glass model therefore supplies a concrete bound on the size of a physical system amenable to computational analysis.  

* **[3]** introduces a practical pipeline for visualising research papers by extracting multi‑word terms from titles, ranking them by frequency, and embedding them in a geographic‑map metaphor. The method exemplifies how relational processing can be formalised through term similarity matrices, providing a concrete instance of mapping semantic distinctions onto a structured space.  

* **[4]** presents the Lean Computer Science Library (CSLib) and its “operational semantics” abstraction, bundling reduction systems and labelled transition systems (LTSs) into a unified API. This illustrates how formal computational structures can serve as reusable abstractions for representing informational transformations.  

* **[5]** surveys the historical impact of mathematical logic on computer science, noting that “computability has had the strongest impact on the younger discipline of computer science.” The emphasis on computability aligns with the autaxic view that informational units arise from generative, algorithmic processes.  

* **[6]** defines “undone computer science” as the set of research questions that are “neglected, ignored, or left unfunded,” highlighting a meta‑informational gap in the discipline that the autaxic framework aims to address by providing a principled hierarchy of informational relevance.  

* **[7]** reports that “acceptance rates at selective computer science conferences range between 10 % and 20 %; for instance, … ICSE … 13 %.” These empirical statistics quantify the bottleneck in information dissemination within the community and serve as a concrete datum for our quantitative illustration.  

* **[8]** reviews spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) in both condensed‑matter and quantum‑field contexts, emphasizing that “the breakdown of the original symmetry is always a discontinuous event at the phase transition point.” SSB is the primary autaxic dynamic that generates the primordial distinctions on which our ontology is built.  

Collectively, these works provide methodological tools (term extraction, operational semantics), empirical bounds (spin‑glass size, conference acceptance rates), and conceptual motivations (computability, undone science) that inform the autaxic reconstruction of information.

## 3. Methods
Our approach proceeds in three stages:

1. **Ontological Construction** – We define four hierarchical levels of information (Section 2) based on autaxic dynamics: (i) *Primordial Distinctions* generated by SSB, (ii) *Stabilised Process‑Patterns* formed through feedback and resonance, (iii) *Relational Information* emerging via relational processing, and (iv) *Functional/Semantic Information* arising in complex adaptive systems.  

2. **Quantitative Illustration I (Spin‑Glass Configurations)** – Using the bound $N_{\text{spins}}=10^{3}$ from [2], we treat each spin as a binary variable ($\pm1$). The total number of distinct configurations is $2^{N_{\text{spins}}}$. We compute the decimal magnitude of this number to illustrate the informational capacity generated by a modest physical system.  

3. **Quantitative Illustration II (Conference Acceptance Rate)** – From the reported range $[10\%,20\%]$ in [7] and the specific value $13\%$ for ICSE, we compute a simple arithmetic mean of the lower and upper bounds to obtain a representative acceptance rate $\bar{r}$. This figure quantifies the informational throughput of a scholarly communication channel.  

All calculations are performed analytically; no empirical measurements or simulations are conducted. Assumptions are explicitly stated (binary spins, uniform acceptance distribution) and labelled as such.

## 4. Analysis
### 4.1 Spin‑Glass Configuration Count
**Input** (source [2]): “we can study up to of order $10^{3}$ spins.”  
**Assumption**: Each spin can be in one of two states, $+1$ or $-1$.  

The number of possible configurations $C$ is:
$$
C = 2^{N_{\text{spins}}}.
$$
Substituting $N_{\text{spins}} = 10^{3}$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C &= 2^{1000} \\
  &= \exp\!\bigl(1000\ln 2\bigr) \\
  &\approx \exp\!\bigl(1000 \times 0.69314718056\bigr) \\
  &\approx \exp\!\bigl(693.14718056\bigr) \\
  &\approx 1.07 \times 10^{301}.
\end{aligned}
\]
**Step‑by‑step arithmetic**:  
1. $\ln 2 \approx 0.69314718056$ (standard constant).  
2. Multiply by $1000$: $0.69314718056 \times 1000 = 693.14718056$.  
3. Exponentiate: $e^{693.14718056} \approx 1.07 \times 10^{301}$ (using $e^{x}=10^{x/\ln 10}$ with $\ln 10\approx2.302585093$).  

Thus the configuration space contains roughly $1.07\times10^{301}$ distinct states, each representing a unique primitive informational pattern.

### 4.2 Average Conference Acceptance Rate
**Inputs** (source [7]):  
- Reported range: “acceptance rates … range between 10 % and 20 %.”  
- Specific example: “ICSE … 13 %.”  

We compute the arithmetic mean $\bar{r}$ of the lower and upper bounds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar{r} &= \frac{10\% + 20\%}{2} \\
        &= \frac{0.10 + 0.20}{2} \\
        &= \frac{0.30}{2} \\
        &= 0.15 \; (=15\%).
\end{aligned}
\]
The result $15\%$ serves as a representative acceptance probability for a typical selective conference, quantifying the fraction of submitted informational artefacts that survive peer filtering.

## 5. Results
- **Result R1** (Section 4.1): The spin‑glass system with $10^{3}$ binary spins possesses $C \approx 1.07\times10^{301}$ distinct configurations, illustrating the exponential informational capacity generated by a modest number of autaxic distinctions.  
- **Result R2** (Section 4.2): The average acceptance rate across the reported 10 %–20 % range is $\bar{r}=15\%$, providing a quantitative measure of the informational bottleneck in scholarly communication channels.  

Both results are derived directly from cited inputs without any additional empirical data.

## 6. Discussion
### 6.1 Limitations
| Assumption | Sensitivity |
|------------|-------------|
| Binary spin model (each spin $\pm1$) | High: multi‑state spins would increase $C$ dramatically. |
| Uniform acceptance distribution across conferences | Moderate: real acceptance rates vary by venue; the mean masks this variance. |
| Neglect of quantum superposition in spin‑glass counting | High: quantum effects could reduce distinguishable configurations. |
| Use of arithmetic mean for acceptance rates | Low: the mean is a simple summary; alternative weighting schemes would shift $\bar{r}$ modestly. |

### 6.2 Failure Modes and Falsifiability
The autaxic framework predicts that informational capacity scales exponentially with the number of primordial distinctions. If a physical system with $N$ binary degrees of freedom were shown to possess fewer than $2^{N}$ distinguishable states due to a fundamental principle that caps distinguishability, the exponential scaling claim would be falsified. Conversely, observing a system that exceeds the $2^{N}$ bound would contradict the conservation of distinguishability meta‑logic.

In the scholarly‑communication illustration, the framework treats acceptance rate as a proxy for informational throughput. If systematic audits revealed that acceptance decisions are independent of informational content (e.g., purely random), the relational‑significance mapping would be undermined.

### 6.3 Open Questions
- How does the autaxic hierarchy accommodate continuous (non‑binary) distinctions?  
- Can the conservation of distinguishability be formalised within a measure‑theoretic framework?  
- What are the dynamics of relational significance in adaptive biological networks beyond the abstract relational processing described here?  

## 7. Practical Implications and Engineering Pathways
**(a) Intended Users and Decision Impact**  
The quantitative illustrations are directly relevant to theoretical physicists modelling many‑body systems, and to computer‑science scholars designing conference submission pipelines. Physicists can use the exponential configuration bound to justify the need for approximate methods (e.g., renormalisation) when tackling large spin systems. Conference organisers can employ the acceptance‑rate estimate to calibrate submission caps and reviewer workloads.  

**(b) Technology Readiness**  
The work is purely analytical and has not undergone peer review; it therefore resides at NASA Technology Readiness Level 2 (TRL 2).  

**(c) Minimal Next Experiment**  
A modest computational experiment that enumerates all $2^{N}$ configurations for $N=20$ spins (feasible on a standard workstation) would verify the combinatorial scaling. Observation of a deviation from $2^{N}$ would falsify the primitive‑distinction counting assumption.  

**(d) Engineering Constraints**  
- **Computational resources**: Exhaustive enumeration scales exponentially; practical limits arise at $N\approx30$ on typical hardware.  
- **Data availability**: Accurate acceptance‑rate statistics require comprehensive conference datasets, which may be proprietary.  

**(e) Projected Cost/Performance Bounds** (Projection)  
Assuming a workstation capable of $10^{9}$ operations per second, enumerating $2^{20}\approx1.05\times10^{6}$ configurations would require roughly $1$ second, while $2^{30}\approx1.07\times10^{9}$ would take about $1$ hour. The cost of such a workstation is on the order of $10^{3}$ USD.  

**(f) Risks and Misuse**  
If the exponential capacity estimate is misapplied to justify unbounded data collection without regard for physical constraints, it could encourage wasteful allocation of computational resources. Proper contextualisation within the autaxic hierarchy mitigates this risk.

## 8. Conclusion
By anchoring information in the autaxic generative engine—spontaneous symmetry breaking, relational processing, and conservation of distinguishability—we have constructed a unified ontology that bridges syntactic, relational, and semantic dimensions. The quantitative illustrations derived from published sources demonstrate that even modest physical systems generate astronomically large informational spaces, and that scholarly communication channels impose measurable informational bottlenecks. The framework thus offers a parsimonious, testable foundation for future formalisation of information across disciplines, while clearly delineating its assumptions and limits.
