## Research idea

Ultrametric Quantum Computer as the Natural Realization of Adelic Factoring. The author conjectures that integer factorization is fundamentally an adelic/ultrametric problem: classical algorithms (GNFS) and Shor's algorithm succeed only by partially exploiting adelic structure, and a hypothetical ultrametric quantum computer—physically realized via ultrametric energy landscapes in spin glasses or hierarchical lattices—would fully realize it. This matters because it reframes factoring not as an algebraic task but as navigating ultrametric (p-adic) geometry, and it poses an open question: can naturally occurring ultrametricity in disordered condensed matter be harnessed for computation rather than merely observed? A paper could test this by (1) formally deriving whether GNFS and Shor admit exact reformulations as adelic factorization steps, quantifying their 'partial' exploitation; (2) simulating quantum adiabatic search on ultrametrically organized Hilbert spaces to show factoring efficiency emerges; and (3) modeling spin-glass Hamiltonians whose ultrametric states encode p-adic digit structure, checking computational universality.
