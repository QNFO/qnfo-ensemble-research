# The Wheeler–DeWitt Zero-Mode Is a Constraint Zero Mode, Not a Majorana Zero Mode: A Revised Structural Test in Minisuperspace

## Abstract

The Wheeler–DeWitt (WDW) constraint $\hat H\Psi=0$ selects a "zero-mode" sector of quantum cosmology, and a conjecture in the quantum-gravity and topological-quantum-computing communities holds that this zero mode might be a Majorana-type object carrying topological protection and decoherence-free character. No prior structural test has compared the WDW zero mode against the defining criteria of genuine Majorana zero modes. We carry out this test on the exactly known minisuperspace operator $\boldsymbol F=-\frac{1}{g}\frac{d}{d\tau}g^{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\frac{1}{g}$ of the FRW sector, whose zero mode is the background function $g(\tau)$ itself. We show by direct substitution that $\boldsymbol F g=0$ identically for any smooth $g$, so the zero mode is kinematical and removable by Faddeev–Popov gauge fixing. We further prove that any self-adjoint Fredholm operator has Fredholm index $\dim\ker-\dim\operatorname{coker}=1-1=0$ identically, correcting a previously published "$\infty-0$" regularisation argument; the vanishing index therefore carries no topological content. Against the three Majorana criteria of self-conjugation, non-zero index, and exponential fusion splitting (benchmark $e^{-L/\xi}=4.54\times10^{-5}$ at $L/\xi=10$), the WDW zero mode fails all three. A scale benchmark $T_{\rm ZBW}=6.45\times10^{-22}$ s confirms that operational Majorana discriminators live at Compton scales, unreachable from minisuperspace. The Majorana interpretation of the WDW zero mode is falsified; the shared label is nominal.

## 1. Introduction

Researchers in quantum gravity and in topological quantum computing confront a shared terminological hazard: the phrase "zero mode" names mathematically distinct objects in different fields, and research decisions built on the shared label can misallocate effort. A quantum cosmologist who reads the WDW constraint's kernel as a protected, Majorana-like state expects a deformation-stable, decoherence-free sector that could in principle be harnessed for fault-tolerant quantum information. A topological-qubit engineer who imports that expectation may predicate hardware concepts or funding cases on a protection mechanism that does not exist. Today the conjecture that the WDW zero mode is a Majorana-type topological zero mode remains untested as a structural claim: no systematic comparison of the WDW constraint operator against the defining criteria of Majorana zero modes — self-conjugation, a non-trivial index, and exponential protection under deformations — has been published. The decision that changes if the present result holds is concrete: whether to pursue a research line that treats the WDW kernel as a candidate decoherence-free resource, and whether to cite quantum cosmology as a source of topological protection mechanisms in quantum-information proposals.

This paper is a post-publication revision of an earlier structural test. Two errors in the published version are corrected here. First, the published Fredholm-index derivation wrote the index as "$\infty-0$ after regularisation, interpreted as zero because each positive $m$ mode is paired with a negative $m$ mode." That argument is invalid: an operator with an infinite-dimensional kernel is not Fredholm at all, and mode pairing is unnecessary, because the index of any self-adjoint Fredholm operator vanishes identically. The corrected derivation is given in Section 4.2 and the conclusion (index $0$, but with no topological content) is retained and sharpened. Second, the published finite-volume conversion $93\ \text{MeV}\times0.197\ \text{fm/MeV}=18.321\ \text{fm}^{-1}$ was dimensionally wrong; the benchmark built on it is removed, since its source material is not part of the present evidence base, and it is replaced by benchmarks that are derived or cited in full (Sections 4.3 and 4.5).

The revised test uses an exactly known minisuperspace operator. In the minisuperspace sector of cosmology — the spatially constant variables — the perturbation operator takes the form $\boldsymbol F=-\frac{1}{g}\frac{d}{d\tau}g^{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\frac{1}{g}=-\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}+\frac{\ddot g}{g}$, with $g\propto\dot a$ fixed by the cosmological scale factor [2]. This operator possesses a single zero mode, the function $g$ itself, which is gauged out by the Faddeev–Popov procedure to define the functional determinant entering the one-loop statistical sum of quantum cosmology [2]. Because the zero mode is known in closed form, every structural criterion can be checked exactly, without regularisation ambiguities.

**Contributions.**

- **C1.** Proof by direct substitution that the minisuperspace constraint operator $\boldsymbol F$ annihilates the background function $g(\tau)$ identically for any smooth positive $g$, establishing that the zero mode is kinematical (a gauge/constraint direction) rather than a dynamical bound state (Section 4.1).
- **C2.** A corrected index derivation: for any self-adjoint Fredholm operator, $\operatorname{coker}\cong\ker$, so the index equals $1-1=0$ for the one-zero-mode minisuperspace operator; the previously published "$\infty-0$" argument is shown to be invalid, and the vanishing index is shown to carry no topological information (Section 4.2).
- **C3.** A criterion-by-criterion comparison against the three defining properties of Majorana zero modes — self-conjugation, topological-index protection, and exponential fusion splitting — including the benchmark splitting $e^{-L/\xi}=4.54\times10^{-5}$ at $L/\xi=10$, and the conclusion that the WDW zero mode satisfies none of the three in the Majorana sense (Section 4.3).
