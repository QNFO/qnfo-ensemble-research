## Research idea

Attractor Calculus: A Formal Language for Steering Cognition Across Substrates. The author conjectures that all information-processing systems (LLMs, brains) are attractor-based dynamical systems, and that a formal 'Attractor Calculus'—operators like CREATE_ATTRACTOR, DEEPEN_BASIN, SHIFT_BOUNDARY—could compile high-level cognitive goals into minimal-energy stimulus sequences that steer any such system into desired attractor basins. This matters because it would unify prompt engineering, neural control, and cognitive intervention under one control-theoretic framework, and make testable whether a substrate-independent 'machine code' of cognition exists or whether levers are irreducibly substrate-specific. A paper could test this by: (1) formalizing the operator set over dynamical-systems/control theory; (2) simulating minimal-perturbation steering in toy recurrent networks and trained LLMs, measuring energy cost and basin-transition reliability; (3) empirically probing 'subliminal' non-semantic prompt features that reliably shift LLM latent states, checking whether discovered control primitives transfer across architectures (transformers vs. RNNs) as evidence for or against universality.
