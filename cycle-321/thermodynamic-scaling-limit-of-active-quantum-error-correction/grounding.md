## Research idea

Thermodynamic scaling limit of active quantum error correction. Claim: active quantum error correction has a hard scalability limit because control wiring and correction cycles generate localized heat that, beyond a critical qubit density, exceeds Kapitza thermal conductance and triggers runaway decoherence—a thermal phase transition. This matters because it would impose a fundamental thermodynamic ceiling on gate-based QEC architectures independent of algorithmic improvements, motivating passive topological substrates (e.g., fractional Chern insulators) instead. Test: derive the critical density from a modified Caldeira-Leggett model coupling control-pulse dissipation to substrate thermal response, using P_local = N_q·ν_cycle·E_pulse + I²R and ΔT_local = P_local/(A·H_K(T)); then simulate heat diffusion in dense qubit arrays (1,000+ qubits) with micro-Kelvin calorimetry parameters to locate the transition and compare against projected hardware roadmaps.
