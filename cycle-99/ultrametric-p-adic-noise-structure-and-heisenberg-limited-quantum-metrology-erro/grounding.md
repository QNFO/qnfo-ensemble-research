## Research idea

Ultrametric p-Adic Noise Structure Enables Fault-Tolerance-Free Heisenberg-Limited Quantum Metrology. Conjecture: if decoherence on a qubit register is modeled as a p-adic (ultrametric) hierarchical process, the strong triangle inequality forces errors to cancel across nested clusters, allowing quantum metrology to reach Heisenberg-limited sensitivity without fault-tolerant error-correction overhead. This matters because it reframes near-term quantum advantage as sensing rather than computation, and yields a falsifiable prediction: precision scaling under ultrametric noise differs qualitatively from Archimedean (depolarizing) noise models. Test: (1) formalize noise as a p-adic-valued stochastic process on an ultrametric lattice and derive error-propagation and precision bounds directly from the ultrametric inequality; (2) simulate quantum Fisher information for entangled probe states under ultrametric versus depolarizing noise, checking whether hierarchical cancellation produces a provable scaling advantage; (3) compare predicted scaling against small-scale photonic or superconducting sensor data. If the bound fails, the paradigm claim collapses; if it holds, it grounds the slogan 'error resilience by geometry, not overhead' in theorem.
