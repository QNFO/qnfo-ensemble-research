## Research idea

Adele-like formal framework for mapping epistemic validity boundaries. The author conjectures that ignorance can be given precise mathematical structure by analogy with the adele ring: each locally validated model is a 'local completion' of some underlying phenomenon, and the unknown is the missing global object that coherently embeds all local truths. The claim: a 'science of epistemic boundaries' is possible, where (1) every model carries an explicit validity boundary, (2) incompatibilities between well-validated models (e.g., QM vs. GR) mark structured, fertile edges of ignorance, and (3) the robust known is exactly what is invariant under changes of representation (radix, metric, observer). A paper could formalize this: define models as local charts with validation domains, construct an adele-like product over representations, and prove or simulate that invariant cores under representation change predict where extrapolation fails—testing the framework on the Poisson-to-Gaussian max-entropy bridge as a worked example of honest ignorance encoding.
