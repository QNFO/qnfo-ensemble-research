## Research idea

Ultrametric Nested Semantic Graphs as Language-Neutral Meaning Representation. Claim: sentences across the full morphological spectrum (isolating, agglutinative, polysynthetic) map to a single language-neutral nested semantic graph whose node distances form an ultrametric (strong triangle inequality via lowest-common-ancestor heights). Why it matters: this would unify semantic parsing, cross-lingual search, and polysynthetic compatibility under one topology, replacing bag-of-words with subgraph matching. Testable: (1) formally define the graph-alignment lattice and prove the proposed d(G1,G2) satisfies the ultrametric inequality, or exhibit counterexamples; (2) empirically parse parallel corpora (English, Turkish, Mohawk/Inuktitut) into graphs and measure whether translations converge to isomorphic or low-distance graphs; (3) simulate subgraph-matching retrieval and compare precision/recall against keyword baselines, especially on polysynthetic word-sentences.
