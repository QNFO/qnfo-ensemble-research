# Does a CPT-Symmetric $t=0$ Boundary Force the Majorana Condition? A Derivation of the Cross-Sheet Conjugation Bound and Its Remnant

## Abstract

CPT-symmetric cosmologies condition the initial state of the universe on a symmetry at the boundary $t=0$, and a natural conjecture is that this boundary condition is strong enough to impose the Majorana reality condition $\psi = \psi^c$ on fermion fields, rather than merely relating fields on opposite sheets of a two-sheeted spacetime. We formalize the $t=0$ CPT boundary as a constraint on the spinor bundle and test whether CPT invariance at the boundary implies $\psi = \psi^c$. We show that it does not: gauge invariance of the most general local boundary condition forces a cross-sheet (or antipodal-point) charge-conjugation relation $\psi_+(0,\mathbf{x}) = \eta\, B\, \psi_-^c(0,\mathbf{x})$ with an undetermined phase $|\eta|=1$, which pairs charge $q$ with charge $-q$ and never sets $q=0$. A pointwise Majorana condition, by contrast, requires $q=0$ for every field it constrains; since 14 of the 15 Weyl fields in each Standard Model generation carry nonzero electric charge, a boundary-forced Majorana condition would neutralize $14/15 \approx 93.3\%$ of the fermionic degrees of freedom per generation and is excluded by the observed charged spectrum. The surviving remnant — a boundary-imposed Majorana condition on neutral fields only — reduces to the standard Majorana-neutrino hypothesis, for which neutrinoless double-beta decay searches with sensitivities below $50\,\mathrm{meV}$ remain the decisive test. The boundary geometry constrains pairing across sheets; it does not impose reality on a sheet.

## 1. Introduction

Whether the neutrino is its own antiparticle is an open question that experimental collaborations are spending tonne-scale budgets to answer. The Majorana collaboration is constructing the Majorana Demonstrator, fielding approximately $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of germanium detectors with mixed enrichment levels, with the technical goal of demonstrating a background of 4 counts per tonne-year exposure in a $4\,\mathrm{keV}$ region of interest around the $2039\,\mathrm{keV}$ $Q$-value of neutrinoless double-beta decay [1]. The white-paper objective of the program is an effective Majorana-neutrino mass sensitivity below $50\,\mathrm{meV}$ in order to characterize the Majorana nature of the neutrino, the Majorana mass spectrum, and the absolute mass scale [2]. Neutrinoless double-beta decay is possible only if neutrinos are Majorana particles — fermions that are their own antiparticles — and its observation would explicitly violate lepton-number conservation [6]. If a cosmological boundary condition could be shown to *force* the Majorana condition, the answer to this experimental question would be fixed by principle rather than by measurement: neutrinoless double-beta decay would be mandatory, lepton-number violation would be a theorem, and the prioritization of isotopes, exposures, and backgrounds in a program costing tonne-scale quantities of enriched germanium would change accordingly. Conversely, if the boundary condition is too weak to force the Majorana condition, two-sheeted CPT cosmologies remain viable with Dirac neutrinos, and the experimental program retains its full motivation.

The question also matters to builders of CPT-symmetric cosmological models. In such models the universe at $t=0$ is conditioned on a CPT symmetry, typically with a second, time-reversed sheet on which particles and antiparticles are exchanged. Whether the boundary geometry does anything more than relate the two sheets — in particular, whether it can impose a *reality condition* on spinors living on a single sheet — is a representational claim: boundary geometry forcing $\psi = \psi^c$ would be a mechanism independent of the usual representation-theoretic requirements for Majorana spinors. Boundary conditions in physics are ordinarily imposed rather than derived; the no-slip condition at a solid-liquid interface, for example, is an assumption that cannot be derived from first principles and could in principle be violated, and its apparent breakdown near structured surfaces shows that boundary conditions can carry less — or different — content than intuition assigns them [3]. The present paper asks the inverse question for a cosmological boundary: not whether an assumed condition holds, but whether a symmetry principle at the boundary *derives* a field condition that representation theory alone does not require.

We answer this question by formalizing the $t=0$ CPT boundary as a constraint on the spinor bundle and deriving the most general local, gauge-invariant boundary condition compatible with CPT invariance of the boundary state. The principal result is negative and sharp. CPT invariance at the boundary forces a conjugation relation between fields at *paired points* — on opposite sheets in a two-sheeted cosmology, or at antipodal spatial points on a single sheet — with a free phase; it does not force a pointwise reality condition. A pointwise Majorana condition requires every field it constrains to carry zero $U(1)$ charge, because charge conjugation flips the charge; we count the resulting conflict with the observed spectrum explicitly. The strong form of the conjecture is therefore false, and its weak remnant is observationally indistinguishable from the standard Majorana-neutrino hypothesis.

The contributions of this paper are:

- A derivation that CPT invariance of a two-sheeted $t=0$ boundary fixes the spinor boundary condition only up to a cross-sheet charge-conjugation relation $\psi_+(0,\mathbf{x}) = \eta\, B\, \psi_-^c(0,\mathbf{x})$ with $|\eta|=1$ undetermined, and that this relation preserves $U(1)$ gauge invariance by pairing opposite charges across sheets (Section 4.1).
- A proof that the pointwise Majorana condition $\psi = \psi^c$ implies $q=0$ for any $U(1)$ charge $q$, with an explicit count showing that 14 of 15 Weyl fields per generation, hence 42 fields over three generations, would be rendered gauge-inconsistent by a boundary-forced Majorana condition (Section 4.2).
- A demonstration that in a one-sheeted CPT-symmetric cosmology the boundary condition is a reflection-conjugation condition $\psi(0,\mathbf{x}) = \eta\, B\, \psi^c(0,-\mathbf{x})$, and that the additional steps needed to reach $\psi = \psi^c$ — dropping the spatial reflection and fixing the phase — are not implied by CPT invariance (Section 4.3).
- Numerical cross-checks of the observable bridge between the abstract constraint $\psi = \psi^c$ and measurement: the Compton-scale quantities $\omega_C \approx 1.55 \times 10^{21}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $\lambda_C/2 \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}$, the $^{76}\mathrm{Ge}$ isotope count $N_{76} \approx 2.726 \times 10^{26}$ nuclei in the Demonstrator's enriched inventory, and an illustrative projected half-life sensitivity $T_{1/2} > 2.05 \times 10^{27}\,\mathrm{yr}$ for a zero-event exposure (Sections 4.4 and 4.5).
- An assessment of the observational distinguishability of the remnant against standard two-sheeted CPT cosmologies, and of the falsification conditions for the negative result (Sections 5 and 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The experimental context of the Majorana question is set by the germanium-based neutrinoless double-beta decay program. The Majorana collaboration pursues research and development aimed at a tonne-scale $^{76}\mathrm{Ge}$ neutrinoless double-beta decay experiment, with the Demonstrator as its current focus: approximately $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of germanium detectors with mixed enrichment levels, of which 20–30 kg enriched to 86% in $^{76}\mathrm{Ge}$, and a technical background goal of 4 counts per tonne-year in the $4\,\mathrm{keV}$ region of interest around the $2039\,\mathrm{keV}$ $Q$-value [1]. The detectors are cylindrical semiconducting HPGe crystals of roughly $500\,\mathrm{g}$, operated with a reverse bias of a few kilovolts so that ionizing radiation generates drift-charge current pulses readable with standard nuclear-physics electronics [1]. The stated physics objective is an effective Majorana-neutrino mass sensitivity below $50\,\mathrm{meV}$, sufficient to characterize the Majorana nature of the neutrino and the absolute mass scale implied by oscillation results [2]. These numbers anchor the practical stakes of the present paper: a boundary-forced Majorana condition would convert this measurement program into a confirmation exercise, while our negative result preserves its discriminating role.

The physics target itself is defined in [6]: neutrinoless double-beta decay can take place in some even-even nuclei only if neutrinos are massive and are their own antiparticles, and the process would explicitly violate lepton-number conservation; observation of $0\nu\beta\beta$ decay would unambiguously establish the Majorana nature of Standard Model neutrinos. The Demonstrator described there is constructed in class-100 clean rooms at the 4850-foot level of the Sanford Underground Research Facility, plans 40 kg of germanium detectors with 30 kg enriched to 87% in $^{76}\mathrm{Ge}$, and uses layered high-density polyethylene shielding (two layers borated), active scintillating-acrylic veto panels read out by photomultiplier tubes, and a radon enclosure purged with boil-off nitrogen [6]. We note a documentation variance relevant to exposure arithmetic: [1] states 86% enrichment for the enriched fraction while [6] states 87%; we adopt 86% in Section 4.5 and record the discrepancy in Section 6. The shield and commissioning status — a prototype module with ten natural HPGe detectors, with the first 20-kg production module entering commissioning in early 2015 [6] — illustrates the scale at which the lepton-number question is actually probed.

On the methodological question of what a boundary condition can and cannot carry, the microfluidics literature provides a instructive precedent. The no-slip boundary condition at a solid-liquid interface is central to fluid mechanics but is an assumption that cannot be derived from first principles and could in theory be violated [3]. The condition was debated throughout the 19th century by figures including Navier, Stokes, Maxwell, and Prandtl [3], and controlled experiments at micron scales have demonstrated apparent violations whose magnitude depends on liquid-solid interaction strength, liquid density, wall density, pressure, wall-fluid commensurability, and molecular roughness [3]. Two lessons transfer to the cosmological setting. First, a boundary condition is a physical assumption with empirical content, not a formal triviality; whether CPT invariance at $t=0$ *derives* a spinor reality condition is therefore a substantive question. Second, boundary conditions can exhibit structure (slip lengths, dependence on surface preparation) beyond the idealized form assumed in textbook treatments — a caution against assuming that a symmetry at the boundary must express itself in the strongest conceivable field condition.
