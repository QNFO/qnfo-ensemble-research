## Research idea

Ultrametric Passive Error Correction on p-ary Trees Beats Surface Codes. The author conjectures that encoding logical qubits on a regular odd-p Bruhat–Tits tree with majority-vote decoding yields an exponentially hard error barrier B(d)=⌈p/2⌉^d, giving logical error rate p_L(d) ≤ C·p_err^⌈p/2⌉^d — passive error correction with zero logical errors at physical error rates as high as p_err=0.40, potentially surpassing surface codes, especially under correlated noise. This matters because it would offer a fundamentally different, geometry-based route to fault tolerance without active syndrome measurement. Testable via: (1) density-matrix simulations with depolarising noise and Clifford+T gates verifying LER(d=3, p_err=0.40) < 10^-3 and depth scaling p_err^d for d=6–8; (2) head-to-head comparison with rotated surface codes at equal qubit count; (3) machine-checked proof of the barrier bound in Lean 4; (4) q-ary extension using alkaline-earth atoms with q>2 nuclear spin states; (5) neutral-atom hardware demonstration on a 40-atom d=3 tree.
