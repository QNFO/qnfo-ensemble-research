## Research idea

Ultrametric Trees as Conditional, Not Universal, Attractors. Claim: the ultrametric (strong triangle) structure is not a universal attractor of nested systems but a conditional one, realized only when three requirements hold simultaneously: recursive branching, post-split isolation (no lateral transfer or cross-branch dynamics), and operation-count (cophenetic) distance. The author conjectures five systematic 'resistance' mechanisms—geometric, dynamical, lateral-transfer, entropic, architectural—that explain why most real systems fail to be tree-like, and that the failure modes are as structured as the tree itself. This reframes ultrametricity from a law of nature into a fragile limit. Testable: derive perturbation bounds showing how much lateral transfer, noise, or non-cophenetic metrics distort the strong triangle condition before it fails; simulate generative models (branching with tunable cross-branch interaction, HGT rates, thermal noise) and measure the distribution of triangle shapes to locate phase boundaries between ultrametric, equilateral, and intermediate regimes; classify empirical taxonomies by which resistance mechanism dominates their deviation from isosceles triangles.
