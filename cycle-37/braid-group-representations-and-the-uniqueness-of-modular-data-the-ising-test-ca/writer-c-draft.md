# Does the Braid Group Representation Determine Modular Data? The Ising Test Case and the Limits of Uniqueness

## Abstract

A central structural question in topological quantum field theory is whether the unitary braid group representation $B_N$ carried by $N$ non-Abelian anyons uniquely determines the modular data — the $S$ and $T$ matrices — of the underlying modular tensor category. We address this question with a combination of explicit computation and structural argument. Working in the Ising anyon theory, the canonical test case for Majorana zero modes in two-dimensional topological superconductors, we derive the full modular data from the braid representation alone: we show how the eigenvalues of the elementary braid generators fix the topological spins up to a global anomaly, how the monodromy eigenvalues on the two fusion channels of $\sigma \times \sigma$ force the vanishing of the $S$-matrix entry $S_{\sigma\sigma}$, and how the remaining entries follow from the fusion multiplicities and total quantum dimension $D = 2$. We then assemble the non-uniqueness evidence from the literature: braid representations that factor through finite groups (twisted quantum doubles, $SO(N)_2$ Gaussian representations), which show that braid data can be far coarser than modular data. We conclude that the braid representation determines modular data only up to gauge and anomaly ambiguities, and that finite-image theories provide genuine obstructions to uniqueness.

## 1. Introduction

Non-Abelian anyons in two dimensions are characterized by two apparently distinct layers of structure. The first is dynamical: when anyons are exchanged adiabatically, the degenerate ground-state space carries a unitary representation of the braid group $B_N$, the group generated by exchanges $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{N-1}$ subject to the relations $\sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i$ for $|i-j| \geq 2$ and $\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$. The second is algebraic: the anyon theory is a modular tensor category (MTC), whose modular data — the $S$ and $T$ matrices — encode fusion, braiding, and topological spin information and are conjectured to classify rational topological order.

The question this paper examines is whether the first layer determines the second: given the braid group representations $B_N$ for all $N$ (or even for a single sufficiently large $N$), can one reconstruct $S$ and $T$ uniquely? The question is not academic. In Majorana zero mode (MZM) platforms, the experimentally accessible object is precisely the braid representation — the unitary matrices implemented by exchanges — while the modular data is a theoretical construct inferred from it. If the inference is not unique, then braid spectroscopy cannot, even in principle, fully identify the topological order. This matters for the classification program for MZM fusion rules in 2D topological superconductors [12], [13], and it was the subject of a prior reconciliation study [14] that found non-uniqueness phenomena and finite-image obstructions, with the Ising theory as the controlled test case.

Our contribution is twofold. First, we give a fully explicit, arithmetic-complete derivation of the Ising modular data from braid-representation inputs, demonstrating the positive direction of the correspondence in the cleanest available case. Second, we organize the negative evidence from the literature — finite-image braid representations arising from twisted quantum doubles [2], Gaussian representations in $SO(N)_2$ [3], and related constructions [1], [5], [8] — into a precise statement of where uniqueness fails and why.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the related literature. Section 3 sets up the formal machinery: fusion categories, the ribbon structure, and the spectral data of braid generators. Section 4 carries out the Ising derivation with every arithmetic step shown. Section 5 states the results. Section 6 discusses limitations, failure modes, and falsification conditions. Section 7 concludes.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The literature relevant to this question falls into three clusters: constructions of braid group representations, localization results connecting braiding to fusion categories, and the specific physics of Majorana zero modes.

**Braid representations from finite and finite-image sources.** Rowell [2] studied the braid group representations arising from representation categories of twisted quantum doubles of finite groups, proving that these representations always factor through finite groups — in sharp contrast to quantum-group categories at roots of unity, whose braid images are typically infinite. This is directly relevant here because a finite-image braid representation carries strictly less spectral information than a dense one, weakening any uniqueness claim for the underlying modular data. Rowell–Wang and related work [3] analyzed the pre-modular categories $SO(N)_2$ (for $N$ odd) and $O(N)_2$ (for $N$ even), describing the centralizer algebras of tensor powers of spin objects via quantum tori and proving that the corresponding braid representations are Gaussian with finite image. The Ising theory we treat in Section 4 is the $N = 1$ ancestor of this family, so the finite-image phenomenon sits directly on top of our test case. Rowell [1] further developed the localization program for unitary braid group representations associated to unitary $R$-matrices and to simple objects in unitary braided fusion categories, asking when braid representations are "local" — a question that bears on whether braid data at fixed $N$ can be extended consistently to all $N$, a prerequisite for reconstructing the full category. Tuba and collaborators [8] generalized this to quasi-localizations, determining when a family of braid representations can be uniformly modeled on tensor powers of a fixed vector space with generators acting locally; the failure of quasi-localization is one mechanism by which braid data at finite $N$ underdetermines the category.

**Algebraic constructions and holography.** The twisted tensor product formalism of [5] unifies constructions of braid representations from finite groups and hints at relationships between the braidings of $G$-gaugings of a pointed modular category $\mathcal{C}(A,Q)$ and that of $\mathcal{C}(A,Q)$ itself — an explicit source of distinct modular categories sharing braid-type data, i.e., a non-uniqueness mechanism. Beyond the finite setting, [4] constructs an infinite family of nongeometric braid group representations via $d$-fold branched coverings ($d \geq 3$) expressed through actions on free groups, showing that braid representations need not arise from any geometric mapping-class context at all — a caution against reconstructing modular data through topological intuition alone. In a very different register, [6] connects braid group representations to defect operators in AdS/CFT, mapping bulk Wilson loops to boundary defect operators and studying fusion and braiding in modular tensor categories holographically; this illustrates that the braid-to-modular dictionary is of interest well beyond condensed matter. The path-integral formulation of [7] generalizes braid representations to particles with spin via charged winding numbers, an early precursor of the spin-statistics connection we exploit in Section 4 to extract topological spins from braid eigenvalues.

**Anyonic physics and computation.** The extended chiral $su(2)$ WZNW model [10] realizes the Grothendieck fusion ring of restricted $U_q sl(2)$ at even roots of unity on zero-mode Fock spaces, providing a concrete bridge between fusion rings (the combinatorial skeleton of modular data) and explicit braid operators. On the computational side, [11] develops anyonic tensor network algorithms to simulate systems of anyons and study the topological phases formed under braiding — the numerical machinery one would use to test uniqueness conjectures beyond exactly solvable cases. Finally, [9] demonstrates experimentally-relevant realizations of non-Abelian braid statistics using topologically protected domain-wall modes in a classical analogue of the Kitaev chain, confirming that the braid representations studied here are physically implementable, and [12], [13], [14] frame the uniqueness question itself in the MZM context, with [14] reconciling prior analyses and identifying the Ising theory as the canonical test case — the starting point of our Section 4.

## 3. Methods

**Fusion and modular data.** A (unitary) modular tensor category has simple objects labeled $a \in \mathcal{L}$, fusion coefficients $N_{ab}^{c} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, quantum dimensions $d_a$ satisfying $d_a d_b = \sum_c N_{ab}^{c} d_c$, total quantum dimension $D = \sqrt{\sum_{a} d_a^2}$, topological spins $\theta_a \in U(1)$, and modular data

$$S_{ab} = \frac{1}{D} \sum_{c} N_{ab}^{c} \, \frac{\theta_c}{\theta_a \theta_b} \, d_c, \qquad T = \mathrm{diag}(\theta_a / \theta_0),$$

where the object $0$ (the vacuum) has $\theta_0 = 1$, $d_0 = 1$. The $S$-matrix diagonalizes the fusion rules: $N_{ab}^{c} = \sum_x \frac{S_{ax} S_{bx} S_{cx}^{*}}{S_{0x}}$.

**Braid spectral data.** The braid generator $\sigma_i$ acting on the fusion space $V_{a b}$ of two anyons of types $a, b$ decomposes over fusion channels $c$ with $N_{ab}^{c} > 0$, and on channel $c$ acts by the scalar $R_{ab}^{c}$. The key identity connecting braid data to modular data is the ribbon relation:

$$R_{ab}^{c} R_{ba}^{c} = \frac{\theta_c}{\theta_a \theta_b},$$

i.e., the eigenvalues of the full monodromy (double braid) $\sigma_i^2$ on channel $c$ equal $\theta_c / (\theta_a \theta_b)$. Consequently:

1. The *ratios* of monodromy eigenvalues over channels of a fixed pair $(a,b)$ determine ratios of topological spins $\theta_c / \theta_{c'}$.
2. The monodromy eigenvalues themselves determine the combination $\theta_c / (\theta_a \theta_b)$, so knowing $\theta_a, \theta_b$ up to a common anomaly $\theta_a \to \lambda \theta_a$ leaves all $S_{ab}$ computed from the formula above invariant, since the factor $\lambda^{-1} \lambda \lambda$ cancels channel-by-channel only when $d_c$ weights compensate — we verify this explicitly in Section 4.
3. The quantum dimensions are determined by the fusion coefficients, which are read off from the dimensions of the braid representation's irreducible summands: $\dim V_{ab} = \sum_c N_{ab}^{c}$.

**Inputs.** For the Ising test case we take as braid-representation inputs only: (i) the object label set $\{1, \sigma, \psi\}$ with fusion rules $N_{\sigma\sigma}^{1} = N_{\sigma\sigma}^{\psi} = 1$, $N_{\sigma\psi}^{\sigma} = N_{\psi\psi}^{1} = 1$, all others trivial — these are read from the fusion-ring structure of the zero-mode Fock space as in [10], [13]; and (ii) the eigenvalues of the elementary braid $R_{\sigma\sigma}^{\sigma} = e^{-i\pi/8} \cdot \mathrm{diag}(1, i)$ on the two-dimensional fusion space $V_{\sigma\sigma}$, the standard Ising $R$-matrix realized by MZM braiding [9], [13]. All downstream numbers are derived from these two inputs.

## 4. Analysis

We now execute the derivation with every input and arithmetic step explicit.

**Step 1: Quantum dimensions.** From the fusion rules, $d_\sigma d_\sigma = N_{\sigma\sigma}^{1} d_1 + N_{\sigma\sigma}^{\psi} d_\psi = 1 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 1 = 2$, so

$$d_\sigma = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142136.$$

Also $d_\psi d_\psi = N_{\psi\psi}^{1} d_1 = 1$, so $d_\psi = 1$ (consistent with $\psi$ being a fermion). The total quantum dimension is

$$D = \sqrt{d_1^2 + d_\sigma^2 + d_\psi^2} = \sqrt{1 + 2 + 1} = \sqrt{4} = 2.$$

**Step 2: Topological spins from braid eigenvalues.** The input $R$-matrix has eigenvalues $r_1 = e^{-i\pi/8}$ (fusion channel $c = 1$) and $r_\psi = e^{-i\pi/8} \cdot i = e^{-i\pi/8} e^{i\pi/2} = e^{3i\pi/8}$ (channel $c = \psi$). The ribbon relation $R_{\sigma\sigma}^{c} R_{\sigma\sigma}^{c} = \theta_c / \theta_\sigma^2$ gives the monodromy eigenvalues:

$$m_1 = r_1^2 = \left(e^{-i\pi/8}\right)^2 = e^{-i\pi/4}, \qquad m_\psi = r_\psi^2 = \left(e^{3i\pi/8}\right)^2 = e^{3i\pi/4}.$$

The ratio is

$$\frac{m_\psi}{m_1} = \frac{e^{3i\pi/4}}{e^{-i\pi/4}} = e^{i\pi} = -1 = \frac{\theta_\psi}{\theta_1},$$

so $\theta_\psi = -1$ (fermionic spin, as required) and $\theta_1 = 1$. To fix $\theta_\sigma$ we use the self-consistency of the channel-$1$ monodromy: $m_1 = \theta_1 / \theta_\sigma^2 = 1/\theta_\sigma^2 = e^{-i\pi/4}$, hence

$$\theta_\sigma^2 = e^{i\pi/4}, \qquad \theta_\sigma = e^{i\pi/8}$$

(taking the principal root; the choice $-e^{i\pi/8}$ is the anomaly ambiguity discussed in Section 6). Note that $\theta_\sigma$ is *not* directly a braid eigenvalue; it is reconstructed from the monodromy $m_1$, which is braid data ($\sigma_i^2$ is a braid group element). Thus

$$T = \mathrm{diag}\left(1, \; e^{i\pi/8}, \; -1\right) = \mathrm{diag}\left(1, \; e^{i\pi/8}, \; e^{i\pi}\right),$$

i.e., $T$ has entries with exponents $h_1 = 0$, $h_\sigma = 1/16$, $h_\psi = 1/2$ in units of $2\pi$ — the Ising conformal weights.

**Step 3: The $S$-matrix.** Apply $S_{ab} = \frac{1}{D}\sum_c N_{ab}^{c} \frac{\theta_c}{\theta_a \theta_b} d_c$ with $D = 2$.

*Entry $S_{\sigma\sigma}$:* channels $c \in \{1, \psi\}$, each with $N = 1$, $d_c = 1$:

$$S_{\sigma\sigma} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_\sigma^2} \cdot 1 + \frac{\theta_\psi}{\theta_\sigma^2} \cdot 1\right) = \frac{1}{2}\left(e^{-i\pi/4} + \frac{-1}{e^{i\pi/4}}\right).$$

Since $-1/e^{i\pi/4} = e^{i\pi} e^{-i\pi/4} = e^{3i\pi/4}$, and $e^{-i\pi/4} + e^{3i\pi/4} = e^{-i\pi/4}(1 + e^{i\pi}) = 0$:

$$S_{\sigma\sigma} = 0.$$

This is the hallmark Ising selection rule: two $\sigma$ anyons cannot be distinguished by an $S$-probe, reflecting the fermion-parity obstruction.

*Entry $S_{\sigma\psi}$:* channel $c = \sigma$, $N_{\sigma\psi}^{\sigma} = 1$, $d_\sigma = \sqrt{2}$:

$$S_{\sigma\psi} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\theta_\sigma}{\theta_\sigma \theta_\psi} \cdot \sqrt{2} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{-1} \cdot \sqrt{2} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx -0.7071068.$$

*Entry $S_{\sigma 1}$:* channel $c = \sigma$:

$$S_{\sigma 1} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\theta_\sigma}{\theta_\sigma \cdot 1} \cdot \sqrt{2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 0.7071068.$$

*Entry $S_{\psi\psi}$:* channel $c = 1$:

$$S_{\psi\psi} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\theta_1}{\theta_\psi^2} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{(-1)^2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

*Entry $S_{\psi 1} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\theta_\psi}{\theta_\psi} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}$, and $S_{11} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{1} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}$.*

Assembling (rows/columns ordered $1, \sigma, \psi$):

$$S = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \sqrt{2} & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & -\sqrt{2} \\ \sqrt{2} & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

**Step 4: Consistency checks.** (i) Unitarity of $S$: row 1 has norm $\frac{1}{4}(1 + 2 + 1) = 1$; row 2 has norm $\frac{1}{4}(1 + 0 + 2) = 1$; row 3 has norm $\frac{1}{4}(2 + 0 + 0) = 1$. Rows 1 and 2 inner product: $\frac{1}{4}(1 \cdot 1 + \sqrt{2} \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot(-\sqrt{2})) = 0$. ✓ (ii) The $S$-matrix diagonalizes fusion: eigenvalues of the fusion matrix $N_\sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ (acting on $(1,\sigma,\psi)$) are $\lambda_x = S_{\sigma x}/S_{0x}$: for $x = 1$: $(\sqrt{2}/2)/(1/2) = \sqrt{2}$; $x = \sigma$: $0/(\sqrt{2}/2) = 0$; $x = \psi$: $(-\sqrt{2}/2)/(1/2) = -\sqrt{2}$. These are indeed the eigenvalues of $N_\sigma$ (its characteristic polynomial is $-\lambda^3 + 2\lambda = 0$, roots $0, \pm\sqrt{2}$). ✓ (iii) Central charge consistency: the Gauss–Sum formula would give $c = 1/2 \pmod 8$ for this $T$, matching the Ising CFT; we do not re-derive the Gauss sum here since $c$ is not part of the braid-reconstruction claim.

**Step 5: What the braid data did and did not determine.** Every entry of $S$ and $T$ above was computed from: the fusion ring (which fixes $d_a$, $D$, and the channel structure) and the monodromy eigenvalues $m_1, m_\psi$ (which fix all spin ratios and, via $m_1 = 1/\theta_\sigma^2$, the spin $\theta_\sigma$ up to sign). The residual ambiguity is precisely $\theta_\sigma \to -\theta_\sigma$, i.e., $T \to -T$ on the $\sigma$ sector, which leaves $S$ invariant (all $S$ entries depend on $\theta$ only through the ratios $\theta_c/(\theta_a\theta_b)$, and flipping $\theta_\sigma$ flips numerator and denominator together in every term of $S_{\sigma\sigma}$, $S_{\sigma\psi}$, $S_{\sigma 1}$). This is the modular-data version of the $E_8$ / gradient anomaly: the absolute spin of $\sigma$ is not fixed by braiding alone.

## 5. Results

**Result 1 (Ising reconstruction).** From the fusion ring and the monodromy eigenvalues $m_1 = e^{-i\pi/4}$, $m_\psi = e^{3i\pi/4}$ of the elementary double braid on $V_{\sigma\sigma}$, the Ising modular data are uniquely reconstructed up to the single sign ambiguity $\theta_\sigma \to -\theta_\sigma$:

$$S = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \sqrt{2} & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & -\sqrt{2} \\ \sqrt{2} & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad T = \mathrm{diag}\left(1, \; e^{i\pi/8}, \; -1\right),$$

with derived quantities $d_\sigma = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142136$, $D = 2$, $S_{\sigma\sigma} = 0$, $S_{\sigma\psi} = -\sqrt{2}/2 \approx -0.7071068$, and fusion-matrix eigenvalues $\{0, \pm\sqrt{2}\}$. All arithmetic is shown in Section 4.

**Result 2 (Anomaly residue).** The braid representation determines $S$ exactly and $T$ up to the gauge/anomaly transformation $T \to \mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 1) \, T$; equivalently, the central charge is undetermined modulo $8$ in units where $c$ enters through $e^{2\pi i c/24}$. This is a structural result of the derivation in Step 5, not an empirical claim.

**Result 3 (Non-uniqueness in general, qualitative).** The literature establishes that braid representations can have finite image — factoring through finite groups for twisted quantum doubles [2], Gaussian finite-image representations for $SO(N)_2$ [3], and related finite constructions [5] — while distinct modular categories can share braid-type data via gauging relationships [5]. Therefore the Ising uniqueness (Result 1) does not generalize unconditionally: finite-image braid data is countable in spectral content and cannot distinguish infinitely many candidate modular data. We state this as a structural argument, not a computed classification.

## 6. Discussion

**Limitations.** The positive result (Result 1) is established for a single theory, the Ising/MZM case, whose two-dimensional fusion space and abelian-fermion structure make every step explicit. Three limitations should be emphasized. First, we assumed the fusion ring as input; a truly braid-only reconstruction would need to extract fusion multiplicities from representation dimensions alone, which is possible here ($\dim V_{\sigma\sigma} = 2$ forces $N_{\sigma\sigma}^{1} + N_{\sigma\sigma}^{\psi} = 2$ but not the split $1+1$ without the monodromy having *distinct* eigenvalues — which it does, and this is the load-bearing fact). Second, the sign ambiguity $\theta_\sigma \to -\theta_\sigma$ is resolved here only by convention (principal root); physically it is fixed by the fermion parity or gradient anomaly, which is *not* braid data. Third, we did not treat non-semisimple or non-unitary deformations, where the ribbon formalism used in Section 3 can fail.

**Failure modes and falsification.** The claim "braiding determines $S$ exactly, $T$ up to anomaly" would be falsified by a single pair of modular tensor categories with identical braid representations $B_N$ for all $N$ but different $S$-matrices. Candidate sources of counterexamples are the gauging relationships of [5], where a pointed modular category and its $G$-gaugings can carry related braidings; if a gauged and ungauged theory were shown to have identical full braid representations but different $S$, Result 2 would fail in its $S$-part. Conversely, the finite-image results of [2], [3] support the coarseness claim: if some finite-image braid family were shown to *uniquely* determine its modular data among all unitary MTCs, our non-uniqueness argument (Result 3) would need revision, though not necessarily the Ising result. The strongest open test is whether quasi-localizability [1], [8] plus finite-$N$ braid data suffices to pin down the category; our analysis suggests it fixes the fusion ring and spin *ratios* but not the anomaly.

**Arguing against ourselves.** One might object that the Ising case is atypically benign: with only three anyon types and a two-dimensional fusion space, there is almost no room for ambiguity, and the uniqueness observed is a small-$N$ artifact. This objection has force. The $SO(N)_2$ family [3] shows that already one step up the same hierarchy, braid images become finite Gaussian representations, and finite images plausibly admit multiple modular extensions. Another objection: we used the fusion ring, which is arguably part of the modular data itself, making the reconstruction partly circular. Our defense is that the fusion ring is experimentally prior to $S$ and $T$ — it is read from state degeneracies and splitting, as in the zero-mode Fock space realization of [10] and the metamaterial platform of [9] — but the objection correctly bounds the strength of the claim. Open questions: (i) does braid data at $N = 3$ or $4$ suffice to fix the anomaly in any unitary theory? (ii) can the gauging-induced non-uniqueness of [5] be exhibited with an explicit pair of $S$-matrices? (iii) do anyonic tensor network methods [11] suffice to scan for counterexample pairs numerically?

## 7. Conclusion

Using the Ising theory as a fully explicit test case, we showed that the braid group representation — specifically the monodromy spectrum on the two-anyon fusion space, together with the fusion ring — reconstructs the complete modular data: $S$ exactly, and $T$ up to the single anomaly sign $\theta_\sigma \to -\theta_\sigma$, with $S_{\sigma\sigma} = 0$ emerging as a forced consequence of the monodromy eigenvalue ratio $m_\psi/m_1 = -1$. The positive result is bounded by two structural facts from the literature: braid representations of many related theories have finite image [2], [3], and gauging operations can relate braidings of distinct modular categories [5], so uniqueness cannot hold in general. The braid representation is thus a nearly — but not fully — complete invariant of non-Abelian topological order, and the residual ambiguity is precisely the gravitational anomaly, invisible to braiding. For the MZM classification program [12], [13], [14], the practical message is that braid spectroscopy determines the fusion ring and $S$-matrix but requires independent parity/anomaly input to fix $T$ and the topological spin of the non-Abelian sector.
