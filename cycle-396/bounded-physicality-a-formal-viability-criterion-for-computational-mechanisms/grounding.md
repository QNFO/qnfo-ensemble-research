## Research idea

Bounded Physicality as a Formal Viability Criterion for Computational Mechanisms. The author proposes a 'Principle of Bounded Physicality': any proposed physical or computational mechanism should be judged non-viable if its resource requirements (time, energy, mass, precision, component count) exceed known physical limits of the universe for non-trivial problem instances. The conjecture is that such a check can be formalized as a derivable 'Resource Scaling Law' — a theorem stating how resources scale with problem parameter n — and that a systematic 'Universe Check' (order-of-magnitude comparison against ~10^18 s and ~10^80 atoms) can yield a binary PLAUSIBLE/NON-VIABLE verdict. This matters because it would turn informal feasibility intuitions into a rigorous, reusable filter for proposals in physics, cryptography, and computation. A paper could test it by: (1) formalizing the scaling-law derivation for canonical cases (e.g., brute-force key search, hypercomputation schemes, large-scale simulations); (2) proving the verdict procedure is well-defined and decidable for a class of resource functions; (3) simulating the check on known-viable and known-non-viable mechanisms to measure false verdicts.
