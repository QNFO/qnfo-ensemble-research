## Research idea

Phononic crystal scaffold replicating protein vibrational spectra for room-temperature quantum transport. The claim: protein scaffolds act as programmable phonon sources whose structured vibrational spectral density enables environment-assisted quantum transport (ENAQT), and that an exact isomorphism exists between biological ENAQT and Floquet-engineered transport — so a synthetic 'phononic crystal' engineered to match a protein's phonon spectrum could stabilise quantum transport in a conductor (graphene or monolayer FeSe) without external laser driving or cryogenics. Why it matters: it reframes thermal noise as an engineered signal and yields design rules (modularity, resonance tuning via geometric doping, phononic insulation) for ambient-temperature quantum devices. Testable: derive the mapping between a static phonon spectral density and Floquet drive Hamiltonians; simulate exciton/electron transport on model pigment or lattice networks driven by engineered spectral densities (Lorentzian/structured vs white noise) to find efficiency optima; compute whether lithographic phononic band structures can reproduce photosynthetic spectral densities and whether chirality-induced spin selectivity adds a controllable spin dimension.
