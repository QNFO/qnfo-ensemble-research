## Research idea

Resistance as Geometric Decoherence in Coherent Electron Transport. The note's strongest conjecture is that electrical resistance is not intrinsic material dissipation but a special case of soliton decoherence caused by suboptimal conductor geometry, and that efficiency should be redefined as coherence preservation across scales, governed by geometric quality factors rather than resistivity. This matters because it reframes energy-loss limits as design-optimization boundaries, implying orders-of-magnitude efficiency gains via topological band-structure matching. A paper could test it by: (1) deriving whether charge transport in a lattice can be modeled as topological soliton propagation whose decay rate depends on boundary-condition geometry; (2) simulating electron wave-packet coherence in lattices with varied geometric symmetries to see if 'resistance' correlates with decoherence rate rather than scattering cross-section; (3) comparing predicted geometric quality factors against measured coherence times in candidate materials. The vacuum-coupling extension (zero-point transduction via geometric resonance) is speculative and lacks a mechanism, so it should be treated as a secondary, clearly flagged hypothesis.
