## Research idea

Differential EKG-Style Readout Cancels Common-Mode Noise in Qubit Measurement. Claim: a balanced, Einthoven-inspired differential readout—measuring the same qubit via two resonators or symmetric feedlines and processing the difference signal—can cancel common-mode noise (amplifier drift, thermal fluctuations, stray coupling) in dispersive qubit readout, yielding substantially higher single-shot fidelity than standard single-ended IQ integration. This matters because readout error is a dominant bottleneck for quantum error correction, and common-mode rejection is a proven principle from electrocardiography never systematically applied to quantum measurement. Testable via derivation and simulation: model the two-channel readout chain in QuTiP/custom Python with realistic amplifier noise, drift, and crosstalk; derive the SNR and single-shot fidelity of the difference channel versus single-ended boxcar integration; then extend to redundant multi-resonator channels and quantify logical error rate reduction for a small code (e.g., [[3,1,2]]), producing quantitative trade-off maps between hardware overhead and fidelity gain.
