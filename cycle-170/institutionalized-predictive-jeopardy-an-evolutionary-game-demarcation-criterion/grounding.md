## Research idea

Institutionalized Predictive Jeopardy as Demarcation Criterion for Knowledge Systems. Claim: The demarcation between science and pseudo-knowledge lies not in theory content but in institutionalized willingness to expose claims to prospective falsification and consilience; knowledge-producing communities systematically drift toward unfalsifiable narratives because incentive structures (peer review, tenure, funding) reward post-hoc certainty over predictive risk. A formal analysis could model this as an evolutionary game: agents choose between risky prospective predictions and safe post-hoc fitting, with reputation/payoff structures parameterized to real institutions. Simulations could test whether (a) unfalsifiable strategies dominate under current incentive configurations, (b) specific interventions—pre-registration, adversarial collaborations, mandatory forecasting benchmarks—shift equilibria toward falsifiable claims, and (c) 'antifragile' configurations exist where system-level epistemic reliability increases with each failed prediction. Comparative case analysis (economics DSGE vs. meteorology, evolutionary psychology vs. molecular genetics) could validate the model's predictions about which institutional features correlate with predictive success.
