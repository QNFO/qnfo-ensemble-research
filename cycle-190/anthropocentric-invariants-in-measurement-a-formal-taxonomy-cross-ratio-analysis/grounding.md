## Research idea

Units as anthropocentric invariants: separating cognitive artifacts from true constants. The author claims that conventional measurement units (meter, kilogram, base-10 radix) are 'anthropocentric invariants'—projective-geometry-style cross ratios in which the human observer is secretly the fixed fourth point—whereas only dimensionless constants like the fine-structure constant α are observer-independent. A paper could formalize this: define a taxonomy of measurement conventions as functions of cognitive parameters (radix, body scale, biological medium), prove which quantities are invariant under 'cognitive transformations' (change of radix, alien physiology) in analogy with projective invariance, and show dimensionless ratios are the sole fixed points. A computational test: simulate alien observers with different radices, body scales, and chemistries reconstructing physics, verifying they converge on α while deriving different 'natural' unit systems—quantifying how much anthropocentric bias each convention carries.
