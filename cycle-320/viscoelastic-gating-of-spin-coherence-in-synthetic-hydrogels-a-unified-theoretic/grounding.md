## Research idea

Viscoelastic Gating: Hydrogel Viscosity Extends Warm-Temperature Spin Coherence. The conjecture: lowering or tuning local viscosity in a synthetic hydrogel decelerates spin-rotational dephasing, so Posner-like synthetic clusters embedded in a viscoelastic gel can maintain spin-coherence times (T2*) exceeding 1 millisecond at 310 K, enabling a 'wet' biomimetic spintronic medium for warm-temperature quantum information processing. This matters because it would establish soft-matter engineering—not just rigid solid-state systems like NV centers—as a viable route to room-temperature quantum technology, and would explain biological quantum coherence as an engineered viscoelastic effect. Testability: (1) Derivation—model spin-rotational dephasing as a function of viscosity η and sol-gel transition dynamics, predicting a monotonic T2*(η) relation; the conjecture fails if NMR shows dephasing accelerates as η → 0.001 Pa·s. (2) Simulation—molecular dynamics of spin-bearing clusters in gels with varied water mobility to quantify fluctuating-field dephasing versus structural noise filtering. (3) Experiment—synthetic Posner-like clusters in hydrogels, measuring T2* at 310 K against the 1 ms threshold, with rigid inorganic substrates as controls.
