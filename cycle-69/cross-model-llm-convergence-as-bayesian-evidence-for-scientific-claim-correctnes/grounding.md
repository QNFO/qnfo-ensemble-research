## Research idea

Cross-Model LLM Convergence as Bayesian Evidence of Scientific Claim Correctness. Claim: When two independently trained LLMs (different architectures, data, organizations) converge not only on a conclusion but on the same multi-step argument structure when evaluating a fringe physics claim, that convergence constitutes Bayesian evidence of correctness beyond single-model evaluation — provided shared training-data bias is ruled out. Why it matters: it could yield a scalable, low-cost gate-checking method for novel scientific claims without domain-expert bottlenecks. Testing: (1) formalize a Bayesian model where the likelihood of identical argument structures under a shared-bias null differs from under independent correctness; (2) run a benchmark of fringe claims with known ground truth, including deliberately designed 'trap' claims that induce false consensus, to measure convergence's discriminative power; (3) compare convergence value on retrieval-only questions versus multi-step synthesis questions, where the null hypothesis of shared-corpus retrieval predicts convergence only for the former.
