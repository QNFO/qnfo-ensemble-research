## Research idea

Simulated Stability: Cognition as Entropy-Masking Predictive World-Modeling. The author conjectures that human cognition constructs a 'simulated stability'—a deliberately over-structured internal world-model that masks the underlying determinism, entropy, and absence of cosmic purpose, serving as a psychological buffer against nihilistic panic. This is a testable claim: formalize it via predictive-processing/free-energy models in which agents gain fitness not from veridical representation but from a controlled distortion that suppresses awareness of irreducible uncertainty. A computational study could evolve Bayesian agents in stochastic environments, comparing agents forced toward accurate entropy estimates versus agents permitted self-serving model compression, measuring survival, motivation-like goal persistence, and model collapse. Alternatively, a formal analysis could derive whether optimal inference under existential-uncertainty costs predicts systematic overestimation of personal agency and world order, yielding empirically checkable signatures (optimism bias, illusion of control) as necessary features rather than bugs of cognition.
