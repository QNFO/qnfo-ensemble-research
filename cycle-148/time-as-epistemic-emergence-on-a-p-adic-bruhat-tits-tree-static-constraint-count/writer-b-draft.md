# Time as Epistemic Emergence on a $p$-Adic Bruhat–Tits Tree: A Static Wheeler–DeWitt Constraint and the Observer-Generated Arrow

## Abstract

The problem of time in quantum gravity blocks any straightforward union of general relativity, in which spacetime time is dynamical, with quantum mechanics, in which evolution is governed by an external background parameter; a resolution is needed before a non-perturbative quantum theory of gravity can specify what its observables are. Existing timeless proposals keep a continuum configuration space and leave the emergence of an experienced arrow unquantified. We formulate a conjectural but internally precise alternative: the fundamental ontology is a static, rooted $(p+1)$-regular Bruhat–Tits tree carrying a quantum state $\Psi$ satisfying a Wheeler–DeWitt-type constraint $\hat{H}\Psi=0$, with $\hat{H}=\hat{A}-(p+1)\hat{I}$ built from the tree adjacency operator $\hat{A}$; experienced time is a subsystem-relative ordering of node traversals, not an ontology-level parameter. We derive, with shown arithmetic, the exact combinatorial consequences for $p=2$: the depth-$10$ ball contains $V_{10}=3070$ events, of which only $C_{10}=27651$ ordered pairs out of $\binom{3070}{2}=4{,}710{,}915$ are temporally comparable, a fraction $f_{10}\approx 5.8696\times 10^{-3}$ with asymptotics $f_D\sim D/p^{D}\to 0$; the future cone of a depth-$5$ vertex within depth $10$ holds $62$ events against $5$ ancestors, an asymmetry that grows to $65534$ against $5$ by depth $20$; and a single observer path costs $10$ bits to specify against $3070$ bits for the full depth-$10$ state, a compression factor of $307$. These results make linear narrative ordering an optimal predictive compression of branching structure rather than a fundamental fact, and they specify what any competing model of emergent time on a discrete geometry must reproduce.

## 1. Introduction

Physicists constructing a quantum theory of gravity face a concrete, unresolved decision problem: what is the time variable of the theory? Quantum mechanics assumes a fixed, external background time parameter to govern unitary evolution, while general relativity treats spacetime, including its time coordinate, as a dynamical entity; the QNFO program states this incompatibility as the reason the pursuit of quantum gravity is "fundamentally stalled" [12]. The cost of the stalemate is practical: canonical quantizations of generally covariant systems produce a constraint $\hat{H}\Psi=0$ with no explicit time, and without an account of why experiments nonetheless record sequential outcomes, such theories cannot say which correlations in $\Psi$ correspond to measurements. A derivation of sequential experience from a static ontology would change that decision: model-builders could omit fundamental time from the ontology and treat all temporal structure as a property of subsystems and their records.

The specific gap this paper addresses is quantitative. Proposals that time is emergent — the Page–Wootters mechanism, adapted to the Bruhat–Tits tree in [12], and the conjectural identification of time with tree depth in [14] — assert that an arrow and a linear ordering arise from a static structure, but the literature contains no closed-form accounting of how much temporal order a branching tree actually contains, how scarce that order is among all pairs of events, or how expensive it is for an observer to represent. Without those numbers, the claim that "linear narrative ordering emerges as optimal compression" is a slogan; with them, it is a checkable statement about description lengths and cone sizes on a specific graph.

We work on the Bruhat–Tits tree $\mathcal{T}_{p}$, the regular infinite tree in which each vertex has $p+1$ neighbours, constructed from lattices over a discretely valued field and central in number theory and $p$-adic AdS/CFT [1], [3]. The boundary of such a tree is a $p$-adic projective line [11], and tree geodesics compute $p$-adic conformal correlators with a cross-ratio $u=p^{-d(c_{1},c_{2})}$ built from graph distance [8]; the tree is therefore a geometry on which Archimedean time is already known to be absent, making it the natural substrate for a timeless ontology.

Our conjecture has three parts, matching the program sketched in the source idea. First, the fundamental reality is a static $\mathcal{T}_{p}$ whose quantum state satisfies a Wheeler–DeWitt-type constraint; we show the constraint $\hat{H}\Psi=0$ with $\hat{H}=\hat{A}-(p+1)\hat{I}$ admits the constant solution, so the ontology is genuinely static, and that subsystem "records" — vertex occupation correlations — accumulate monotonically with tree depth, defining a thermodynamic arrow. Second, node-to-node amplitude structure admits a semiclassical limit: the adjacency spectrum is bounded by the spectral radius $2\sqrt{p}$, which plays the role of a maximal propagation speed in the manner of the Lieb–Robinson bound invoked in [12], and the boundary $\partial\mathcal{T}_{p}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})$ supplies the real-line surrogate in a continuum limit, in line with the boundary constructions of [2]. Third, an observer that compresses the tree's branching structure optimally represents it by a single traversed path, because a path costs $\mathcal{O}(d\log p)$ bits while the full depth-$d$ ball costs $\Theta(p^{d})$ bits; linear narrative is then the cheapest predictive code, connecting to evolutionary epistemology.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The Bruhat–Tits tree is a combinatorial object serving as a powerful tool in number theory and arithmetic geometry; in the simplest setting of $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ it is built from lattices in $\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}$ and is a regular infinite tree in which each vertex has $p+1$ neighbours, with deep connections to the representation theory of $\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})$ [1]. That article formalises the tree in the Lean 4 theorem prover on top of mathlib, using the Cartan decomposition $\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)=\bigsqcup_{t\in T^{-}}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)\,t\,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)$ as the entry point, and verifies a result on harmonic cochains [1]. The existence of a machine-checked formalisation matters for this paper's test program: our counting claims in Section 4 are of the kind that can be mechanised in the same environment.

Structurally, a Bruhat–Tits tree is a connected graph without circuits in which every vertex belongs to $m+1$ distinct edges for a fixed $m\geq 2$, with distance $d(x,y)$ the number of edges in the unique path joining $x$ to $y$ [3]. That reference develops the decomposition theory of subgroups of the automorphism group of the tree of $\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(F)$ — Borel, unipotent radical, Iwahori and torus subgroups — precisely because those decompositions organise the tree's symmetries [3]. We use the acyclicity and the unique-path property as the geometric source of causal acyclicity (Claim 4.6), and the $(p+1)$-regularity as the input to all growth counts.

The tree's boundary behaves like a conformal boundary. For a spinor field theory on $\mathcal{T}_{p}$, integrating out the interior yields a boundary theory whose action and partition function resemble a scalar field theory over $p$-adic numbers; the kinetic term of the boundary theory takes the form $\varphi^{*}D^{2\Delta-1}\varphi$, and the factor $p^{\Delta}-p^{1-\Delta}$ appears throughout, with its zero corresponding to the BF bound [2]. The cut-off boundary $E_{R}=\{a: z(a)=p^{R}\}$ becomes the boundary $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ as $R\to+\infty$ [2]. This construction is the template for our semiclassical limit: a bulk tree theory with a boundary theory on a $p$-adic line, so that "recovering real-line time" can be stated as a limit of boundary correlators rather than an extra postulate.

$p$-adic AdS/CFT treats the tree as the analogue of pure AdS, with the asymptotic boundary the $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ line and the radial coordinate $p^{n+1}$ encoding the accuracy to which the horizontal coordinate $x^{(n+1)}$ is defined [4]. In a sequel, the BTZ black hole connection is transplanted to the tree, and Wilson-line expectation values relate to a $p$-adic CFT at finite temperature, with the black-hole background related to the pure tree by a local diffeomorphism implementing a non-trivial Weyl transformation [4]. The accuracy reading of the radial coordinate — depth as resolution — is exactly the reading our observer model needs: depth is information-theoretic resolution, not a flowing parameter.

Geodesic structure on the tree computes conformal data. Geodesic bulk diagrams are the geometric objects computing global conformal blocks, and the duality continues in $p$-adic AdS/CFT with the bulk replaced by the tree; in the four-point contact diagram the conformal cross-ratio is $u=p^{-d(c_{1},c_{2})}$, the graph distance between bulk points [8]. The $p$-adic operator product expansion admits no derivative expansion, no descendants appear, and a local stress-tensor is absent [8]. The absence of a local stress-tensor is significant here: the object that in Archimedean field theory generates time translations has no direct $p$-adic analogue, which is consistent with our treatment of dynamics as emergent rather than fundamental.

Tree quotients and substructures show that the tree supports rich order structures that can serve as records. The set of maximal orders in $\mathbb{M}_{2}(k)$ corresponds to the vertices of the Bruhat–Tits tree for $\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(k)$, and the branch of a suborder is a "thick line" — a maximal subtree whose vertices lie within a fixed depth of a stem path — with computable invariants [6]. An algorithm computes the largest subtree whose vertices correspond to orders containing a given suborder, in terms of generators, and complete tables of invariants are given for orders generated by pairs of quadratic elements [6]. Lemma 3.1 of [6] gives an exact intersection criterion: branches at distance $d$ intersect under radius-$t$ thickening if and only if $t_{1}+t_{2}\geq d$ — a tree-native causality condition of the same shape as a light-cone criterion. Related branch characterisations classify Bass orders by whether their branch is a line [5], showing that "line-like" versus "branching" is a sharp, decidable property of tree subtrees; our arrow construction relies on exactly this dichotomy between the linear past chain and the branching future cone.

Quotients of the tree by arithmetic groups produce finite combinatorial skeletons. For the rational function field $\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)$, the orbit space $\mathsf{PGL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\{p\}})\backslash X$ is computed for every place degree $d$, with explicit edge multiplicities such as $q^{2l-1}+q^{2l-2}$ edges between $X_{n}$ and $X_{d-n-2l}$, and the even-degree case forcing a bipartite double cover [7]. The unit groups of maximal orders in quaternion algebras over $\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)$ act on the tree with applications to arithmetic geometry, and the graph formalism with oriented edges, origins and termini is developed there in detail [9]. These works demonstrate that structured, finite, computable quotients of the tree exist; in our framework such a quotient is a natural model of a subsystem's record algebra.

Field extensions act on the tree by dilation. For a finite extension $F$ of $\mathbb{Q}_{2}$ and a quadratic extension $E/F$, the injection of trees $\mathcal{T}_{F}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{E}$ enjoys a global dilation factor of $\mathrm{e}_{E/F}$ under the graph metric, and the Galois action on $E/F$ induces one on $\mathcal{T}_{E}$ [10]. Stabilizers of quadratic points are computed there as a step toward parametrising irreducible admissible representations of $\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)$ [10]. The dilation factor shows that "one tick" of depth is scale-relative — a depth step in $\mathcal{T}_{E}$ maps to $\mathrm{e}_{E/F}$ steps in $\mathcal{T}_{F}$ — which we use in Section 6 to argue that the tick size of emergent time is not absolute.

Geodesic flows on the tree connect to continued-fraction dynamics. The geometric Farey map is the time-$1$ map of the geodesic flow on the tree of $\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K)$, extending Artin's relation between continued fractions and the geodesic flow on the modular surface, following Broise-Alamichel and Paulin's work on trees [11]. The boundary $\partial T_{q}$ is identified with the projective line $\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)$ via geodesic rays, and the tree is $(q+1)$-regular with vertices the homothety classes of lattices [11]. A geodesic flow with a "time-$1$ map" exists on the tree as a dynamical system on its boundary; our claim is that this flow time is a derived, observer-level object, not the ontology's parameter.

Closest to our program are the three QNFO works. The first maps the timeless adjacency operator of the tree to a dynamic time-evolution operator using the Page–Wootters mechanism within a rigged Hilbert space, taking $\hat{A}$ as the effective Hamiltonian, proving time "an epistemic, coarse-grained parameter," deriving a light-cone structure from Lieb–Robinson bounds, and deriving the dimensionless speed-of-light bound from the tree's spectral radius [12]. The second benchmarks $p$-adic embeddings of hierarchical data against Euclidean and hyperbolic alternatives using distortion and rank-correlation metrics, and demonstrates via a quantum walk that ballistic transport, quadratically faster than classical diffusion, yields linear $\mathcal{O}(D)$ scaling for traversing a tree latent space on a 1D proxy [13]. The third, the direct predecessor of this paper, formalises time as tree depth $d\in\mathbb{N}$ conjecturally identified with the $p$-adic valuation $v_{p}(x)$, derives $V_{10}=3070$, $C_{10}=27651$ out of $\binom{3070}{2}=4{,}710{,}915$, $f_{10}\approx 5.8696\times 10^{-3}$, mean depth $\bar{d}\approx 9.0068$, and future-cone counts $C(5)=62$ ($p=2$) and $363$ ($p=3$), and states its own limitations: the model is a kinematic skeleton with no dynamics, the identification $t\leftrightarrow v_{p}(x)$ is a conjecture, the prime $p$ is a free parameter, and there are no spatial degrees of freedom [14]. This paper extends [14] in the two directions it flags as missing: we add a constraint dynamics ($\hat{H}\Psi=0$) that makes the ontology static in a quantum-mechanical sense, and we add the observer-level compression argument that turns "linear ordering" from a kinematic possibility into an optimality claim with computed description lengths.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Ontology and notation

Let $\mathcal{T}_{p}=(X^{0},X^{1})$ be the rooted $(p+1)$-regular Bruhat–Tits tree: a non-empty connected graph without circuits in which every vertex belongs to $p+1$ edges [3], with a distinguished root $r$ of depth $d(r)=0$ and distance $d(x,y)$ the edge-count of the unique path [3]. The root has $p+1$ children; every other vertex has one parent and $p$ children. The boundary $\partial\mathcal{T}_{p}$ is the set of equivalence classes of infinite geodesic rays, identified with $\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})$ [11], and the radial coordinate of a vertex encodes the accuracy to which a boundary point is resolved [4].

The ontology is the pair $(\mathcal{T}_{p},\Psi)$. The tree is static: nothing in the ontology evolves. $\Psi$ is a normalisable vector in the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(X^{0})$ of square-summable amplitudes on vertices, satisfying the constraint of Section 3.3. An event, for an observer, is a vertex; a record is a correlation among amplitudes on a set of vertices accessible to one subsystem. Time is not an ontology-level parameter; it is the ordering a subsystem imposes on the vertices it traverses. This is the epistemic reading of the Page–Wootters mechanism as applied to the tree in [12], sharpened by the depth-based ordering conjectured in [14].

### 3.2 The adjacency operator and the constraint

Let $\hat{A}$ be the adjacency operator of $\mathcal{T}_{p}$, $(\hat{A}\psi)(v)=\sum_{w\sim v}\psi(w)$, where $w\sim v$ means $w$ is a neighbour of $v$. On the $(p+1)$-regular tree, $\hat{A}$ is bounded and self-adjoint on $\ell^{2}(X^{0})$. We adopt as a standard spectral input (Assumption A1, used only in Claim 4.10) that the spectrum of $\hat{A}$ is $[-2\sqrt{p},\,2\sqrt{p}]$, so the spectral radius is $\rho(\hat{A})=2\sqrt{p}$; this is the quantity identified with the dimensionless speed bound in [12].
