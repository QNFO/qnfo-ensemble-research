# Projective Braid Group Representations from a Long-Range Kitaev Chain: A Constructive Derivation

## Abstract

We construct an explicit projective representation of the $N$-strand braid group $B_N$ from the microscopic Hilbert space of a Kitaev chain extended with algebraically decaying long-range hopping and pairing. The construction proceeds in three steps. First, we identify the topological phase of the long-range Kitaev chain (power-law exponent $\alpha$) and show that unpaired Majorana zero modes localize at domain walls, with localization length controlled by $\alpha$. Second, we write the braid unitary $U_{ij}=\exp(\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_i\gamma_j)$ acting on the zero-mode Majorana operators $\gamma_i$ and verify, by explicit $2\times 2$ matrix multiplication in a fixed-parity sector, that the braid relation $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ holds exactly while the far commutator relation is lifted: $U_{ij}^4=-\mathbb{1}$ rather than $+\mathbb{1}$. This defines a genuine projective (2-cocycle-twisted) representation with cocycle $\omega(\sigma,\sigma)=-1$, matching the structure familiar from Abelian and non-Abelian Chern-Simons theory and from projective ribbon permutation statistics. Third, we give quantitative estimates of the physical requirements: zero-mode splitting $\delta E\sim e^{-L/\xi}$, adiabatic exchange times, and the dimension $2^{n-1}$ of the representation on $2n$ Majoranas. We argue that the long-range hopping is not essential to the projectivity but controls localization and gap, and we discuss falsification criteria and open questions.

## 1. Introduction

Topological phases of matter support exotic quantum statistics: when quasiparticles are exchanged adiabatically, the wave function transforms by a unitary matrix, and for non-Abelian anyons these matrices form a representation of the braid group $B_N$. A recurring theme across exactly soluble field theories is that such representations are generically *projective*: the unitaries satisfy the braid group relations only up to phases, because the phase ambiguity is the fingerprint of a nontrivial central extension or 2-cocycle. Canonical Chern-Simons theory on a Riemann surface provides the canonical example: its wave functions carry projective representations whose cocycles depend on the (possibly rational) Chern-Simons level $k$ [1, 2], and the symplectic approach to the Wess-Zumino-Witten model yields projective representations of the loop group by the same mechanism [4].

The purpose of this paper is to derive such a projective braid representation not from a topological field theory but from a *microscopic lattice model*: the Kitaev chain, generalized to include long-range (power-law decaying) hopping and pairing. The motivation is threefold.

First, microscopic derivations anchor the abstract theory. Chern-Simons derivations [1, 2] are continuum and topological; a lattice derivation shows precisely which microscopic operators generate the braid action and where the projective phase comes from (it comes from the Clifford algebra $\{\gamma_i,\gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$, i.e., from fermionic anticommutation, not from any gauge field).

Second, long-range hopping changes the locality structure. In the short-range Kitaev chain the Majorana zero modes are exponentially localized with a model-dependent length; with hopping $t_\ell\sim t_0/\ell^\alpha$, the localization and the splitting scale differently, and for $\alpha\le 1$ zero modes can fail to be localized at all. We quantify this and show that the *algebraic* structure of the braid representation is independent of $\alpha$, while its *physical* realizability is not.

Third, there is a live question in the literature about what braid representations do and do not determine. Recent analyses reconcile the view that braid group representations of non-Abelian anyons need not uniquely fix the modular data ($S$ and $T$ matrices) of the underlying topological order, with the Ising anyon theory serving as the canonical test case [12, 13]. A microscopic lattice construction of the braid representation, decoupled from any modular tensor category, is a useful control experiment for such uniqueness questions: here the braid representation exists by construction, and one can ask independently whether any modular data is implied (we argue it is not, without further input).

Our main results are: (i) an explicit verification that the Majorana braid matrices satisfy the braid relation exactly and the relation $\sigma_i^4=1$ only projectively, with $\sigma_i^4=-\mathbb{1}$ in the fixed-parity sector — a full arithmetic derivation at the $2\times 2$ level; (ii) explicit numerical estimates of zero-mode splitting, gap, and adiabatic exchange times for representative parameters; and (iii) a roadmap for completing the derivation within nine months, including the long-range regime $\alpha\le 2$ where the standard short-range arguments require modification.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Chern-Simons braid representations.** The canonical route to anyon statistics is canonical quantization of Chern-Simons theory coupled to non-dynamical matter. In [1], the quantization is generalized to rational Chern-Simons coefficient $k$, arbitrary genus, and arbitrary total charge, and the question is posed of which braid group representations on a Riemann surface the wave functions carry. In [2], an explicit solution of the Schrödinger equation for Chern-Simons theory coupled to charged particles is given for rational $k$ and zero total charge, and the wave functions are shown to carry a *projective* representation of the group of large gauge transformations. These works establish the template our lattice construction mirrors: a quantization ambiguity (rational $k$ there, fermion parity here) produces a projective rather than honest representation. Our derivation reproduces the projective structure with the source of the cocycle made completely explicit — it is the Clifford algebra of Majorana operators.

**Symplectic and loop-group perspectives.** The symplectic approach to the Wess-Zumino-Witten model [4] obtains the quantum theory as a projective representation of the loop group, with Gauss constraints imposed at a finite set of points. This is conceptually parallel to our construction: the Majorana zero modes are precisely "Gauss constraints at points" (defects), and the projectivity arises from quantizing a symplectic space whose prequantum line bundle has nonzero curvature. The Kashaev quantization of universal Teichmüller space similarly yields projective representations of the Ptolemy-Thompson group and associated dilogarithmic central extensions [5]; the structural lesson — that quantization of a classical symmetry group naturally produces central extensions — is the same one we instantiate for $B_N$.

**Projective statistics beyond braiding.** Wilczek's proposal, analyzed by Nayak-Wilczek and critiqued in [3], suggests non-Abelian *projective* representations of the permutation group as a statistics valid in any dimension; [3] clarifies the relation between such projective permutation statistics and true non-Abelian braid statistics. In a related direction, [8] analyzes the 3D model of Teo and Kane, in which defect lines carry Majorana zero modes whose exchange and twist implement a "ghostly" remnant of 2D Ising-anyon braiding; the governing group $T_{2n}$ (projective ribbon permutation group) is computed there. Our work is the 1D-chain analogue: the Kitaev chain's Majorana zero modes, moved around a network, implement braid-like unitaries whose projectivity is the same remnant phenomenon. The key difference is that in 1D the exchange must be effected by an external braiding protocol (e.g., T-junction networks or measurement-based protocols), which we discuss in Section 6.

**Algebraic machinery.** The Drinfeld associator machinery [6] associates to any representation of a certain Hopf algebra a braid group representation over a Laurent-series field, and studies the dependence on the associator for GT-rigid representations. This is the categorical counterpart of our question: which braid representations arise from a given algebraic input, and how rigid are they? The cluster-geometric construction of [7] endows moduli spaces of $G$-local systems on decorated surfaces with a cluster Poisson structure equivariant under the mapping class group, providing a geometric arena in which braid-type actions arise from quantization of cluster varieties — a possible future home for a geometric quantization of our lattice zero-mode manifold. Braid group actions on representation categories also appear in [11], where an action of the braid group of a simple Lie algebra on imaginary roots of a quantum affine algebra is used to determine cyclicity of tensor products and zeros of $R$-matrices; and in [9], where brane quantization connects Lagrangian branes on the $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ character variety to representations of the spherical double affine Hecke algebra of $C^\vee C_1$, with an affine braid group action on the category. Both illustrate that braid actions on zero-mode/defect Hilbert spaces are a robust algebraic phenomenon, not an artifact of any one field theory.

**Modular data and uniqueness.** Two recent analyses [12, 13] address whether the braid group representation carried by $N$ non-Abelian anyons uniquely determines the modular data of the underlying topological order, with the Ising/Majorana fusion rules as the central test case; the emerging picture is one of non-uniqueness: the same braid representation can be compatible with distinct modular data. Our microscopic construction is directly relevant: since the Majorana braid representation we derive in Section 4 is fixed entirely by the Clifford algebra, any "modular data" attributed to it must come from extra structure (a full anyon theory, a chiral central charge, a spin-TQFT extension), not from the braid representation alone. Finally, [14] studies ultrametric relaxation dynamics in topological quantum memory, which bears on the stability question of any memory built from our zero modes: the hierarchical (ultrametric) structure of relaxation barriers is a plausible model for error processes in a Majorana-based qubit whose protection is exponentially weakened by long-range hopping.

We note that reference [10] (a QCD collider-physics report) is part of the provided bibliography but is topologically unrelated to this work and is not cited substantively.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The long-range Kitaev chain

The standard Kitaev chain on $L$ sites is
$$H_0=-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\bigl[\mu\, a_j^\dagger a_j + t\,(a_j^\dagger a_{j+1}+\text{h.c.}) + \Delta\,(a_j a_{j+1}+\text{h.c.})\bigr],$$
with $t,\Delta$ real and $\Delta=|t|$ in the ideal BDI point. We generalize to long-range hopping and pairing with algebraic decay:
$$H_\alpha=-\sum_{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-j}\Bigl[\frac{t}{\ell^{\alpha}}(a_j^\dagger a_{j+\ell}+\text{h.c.})+\frac{\Delta}{\ell^{\alpha}}(a_j a_{j+\ell}+\text{h.c.})\Bigr]-\mu\sum_j a_j^\dagger a_j,$$
with power-law exponent $\alpha>0$. The model is in symmetry class BDI (or D with a phase on $\Delta$); in the topological regime $|\mu|<2\sum_\ell \ell^{-\alpha}t$ (the generalization of $|\mu|<2t$; note $\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-\alpha}=\zeta(\alpha)$ for $\alpha>1$), the open chain hosts unpaired Majorana zero modes $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ at its ends, with
$$\gamma_1=\sum_{j} v_\alpha(j)\,(a_j+a_j^\dagger),\qquad \gamma_2=\sum_j (-1)^j v_\alpha(j)\,(a_j+ a_j^\dagger),$$
where $v_\alpha$ is a normalized Majorana wave function whose decay is controlled by $\alpha$. For $\alpha>1$ the decay is exponential with a length $\xi(\alpha)$ that diverges as $\alpha\to 1^+$; for $\alpha\le 1$ the zero modes delocalize and the topological protection is lost. We treat $\xi(\alpha)$ as a parameter to be bounded, not assumed.

### 3.2 From zero modes to a braid action

Given $2n$ well-separated Majorana zero modes $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{2n}$ with $\{\gamma_i,\gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$, an adiabatic exchange of modes $i$ and $j$ implements, on the zero-mode Hilbert space, the unitary
$$U_{ij}=\exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_i\gamma_j\Bigr),\qquad U_{ij}\gamma_i U_{ij}^\dagger=\gamma_j,\quad U_{ij}\gamma_j U_{ij}^\dagger=-\gamma_i,$$
which we verify directly in Section 4. The generators $\sigma_i:=U_{i,i+1}$, $i=1,\dots,2n-1$, are the candidate braid generators. The Hilbert space of $2n$ Majoranas is $2^{2n}$-dimensional but splits into two parity sectors of dimension $2^{2n-1}$; fixing total parity (superselection) gives an irreducible representation of dimension $2^{2n-1}$... we correct: with $2n$ Majoranas the fixed-parity sector has dimension $2^{n-1}$ (the $2^{2n}$-dimensional Fock space factors as $2^n$ complex fermions; fixed total parity halves it: $2^{n-1}$... precisely, $2^n$ states total, $2^{n-1}$ per parity sector). We use this in Section 4.

### 3.3 Exchange protocol in a chain

In a strictly 1D chain, "exchange" is not a native motion; we assume the standard network implementation: the chain is patterned into a T-junction or tri-junction network in which Majoranas are moved by tuning local chemical potentials, or exchanges are implemented by parity measurements. The braid unitary is the same either way; only the adiabaticity condition changes. We quantify this in Section 4.4.

### 3.4 Scope and deliverables

Within the nine-month program: (months 1–3) rigorous bounds on $\xi(\alpha)$ and the gap $\Delta_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in(1,\infty)$, including the marginal regime $\alpha\in(1,2]$; (months 3–6) explicit finite-size diagonalizations at the free-fermion level (the model is quadratic, so exact) verifying the braid unitaries and their projective phases; (months 6–9) analysis of the 2-cocycle, its dependence on parity sector and protocol, and the relation to modular-data uniqueness questions [12, 13].

### 4.1 The braid unitary and its action on Majoranas

**Input (standard Clifford algebra of Majorana zero modes; source: definition of Majorana operators, $\gamma_i^\dagger=\gamma_i$, $\{\gamma_i,\gamma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}$).** Let $A=\gamma_i\gamma_j$ with $i\neq j$. Then $A^2=\gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_i\gamma_j=-\gamma_i^2\gamma_j^2=-1$ (one anticommutation), so $A$ has eigenvalues $\pm i$, and
$$U_{ij}=e^{\frac{\pi}{4}A}=\cos\frac{\pi}{4}\,\mathbb{1}+\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\,A=\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(\mathbb{1}+\gamma_i\gamma_j).$$
*Step 1:* $U_{ij}\gamma_i U_{ij}^\dagger$. Using $A\gamma_i=\gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_i=-\gamma_j$ and $\gamma_i A=\gamma_i\gamma_i\gamma_j=\gamma_j$:
$$U\gamma_i U^\dagger=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbb{1}+A)\gamma_i(\mathbb{1}-A)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\gamma_i + A\gamma_i - \gamma_i A - A\gamma_i A\bigr).$$
Compute each term: $A\gamma_i=-\gamma_j$; $\gamma_i A=\gamma_j$; $A\gamma_i A=(-\gamma_j)(-\gamma_j)=\gamma_j^2=1$. Sum: $\tfrac12(\gamma_i-\gamma_j-\gamma_j-1)\cdot$ — the scalar $1$ cannot appear; recheck: $A\gamma_i A = (\gamma_i\gamma_j)\gamma_i(\gamma_i\gamma_j)$. Directly: $A\gamma_i A = (-\gamma_j)(\gamma_i\gamma_j) = -\gamma_j\gamma_i\gamma_j = \gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_j = \gamma_i$. Correcting: $A\gamma_i A=\gamma_i$. Then $U\gamma_i U^\dagger=\tfrac12(\gamma_i-\gamma_j-\gamma_j+\gamma_i)=\gamma_i-\gamma_j$. This is wrong — the error is that $U^\dagger=e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}A}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\mathbb{1}-A)$ is correct, but the conjugation formula for exponentials is cleaner: $e^{\theta A}B e^{-\theta A}=e^{\theta\,\mathrm{ad}_A}B$ with $\mathrm{ad}_A(B)=[A,B]$. Since $A$ is anti-Hermitian with $A^2=-1$, $[A,\gamma_i]=A\gamma_i-\gamma_i A=-\gamma_j-\gamma_j=-2\gamma_j$, and $[A,\gamma_j]=\gamma_j\gamma_j-(-\gamma_j\gamma_j)\cdot$ — compute: $A\gamma_j=\gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_j=\gamma_i$; $\gamma_j A=\gamma_j\gamma_i\gamma_j=-\gamma_i\gamma_j\gamma_j=-\gamma_i$; so $[A,\gamma_j]=\gamma_i-(-\gamma_i)=2\gamma_i$. Then $\mathrm{ad}_A^2(\gamma_i)=[A,-2\gamma_j]=-4\gamma_i$, so
$$U\gamma_i U^\dagger=\gamma_i\cos\frac{\pi}{2}+\gamma_j\cdot\frac{-2}{2}\sin\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{?}$$
Explicitly: $e^{\theta\,\mathrm{ad}_A}\gamma_i=\gamma_i\cos 2\theta + \frac{[A,\gamma_i]}{2}\sin 2\theta\cdot$ — standard rotation: $=\gamma_i\cos 2\theta - \gamma_j\sin 2\theta$. At $\theta=\pi/4$: $U\gamma_i U^\dagger = \gamma_i\cos\frac{\pi}{2}-\gamma_j\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=-\gamma_j$. Hmm, sign: with $[A,\gamma_i]=-2\gamma_j$, the series gives $\gamma_i + \theta(-2\gamma_j)+\frac{\theta^2}{2}(4\gamma_i)+\cdots=\gamma_i\cos 2\theta - \gamma_j\sin 2\theta$, so at $\theta=\pi/4$: $-\gamma_j$. Similarly $U\gamma_j U^\dagger = \gamma_j\cos\frac{\pi}{2}+\gamma_i\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=\gamma_i$. So the exchange is $i\leftrightarrow j$ up to signs: $\gamma_i\mapsto -\gamma_j$, $\gamma_j\mapsto \gamma_i$. (The overall sign convention depends on orientation; either sign is a valid braid generator convention.) ✓.

### 4.2 Explicit $2\times 2$ computation: braid relation and projective lift

**Input:** three Majoranas $\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3$; fix the parity sector spanned by $\{|0\rangle,\gamma_3|0\rangle\}$, where $|0\rangle$ is the vacuum of the complex fermion $f=(\gamma_1+i\gamma_2)/2$. In this basis, $\gamma_1\gamma_2 = 2f^\dagger f - 1 = i\sigma_3$ (with $\sigma_3|0\rangle=-|0\rangle$, $\sigma_3\gamma_3|0\rangle=+\gamma_3|0\rangle$), and, choosing phases consistently, $\gamma_2\gamma_3=i\sigma_1$.

**Step 1 — the generators.** With $\theta=\pi/4$, $\cos\theta=\sin\theta=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\approx 0.70711$:
$$U_{12}=e^{\frac{\pi}{4}(i\sigma_3)}=\begin{pmatrix} e^{i\pi/4} & 0\\ 0 & e^{-i\pi/4}\end{pmatrix},\qquad U_{23}=e^{\frac{\pi}{4}(i\sigma_1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1 & i\\ i & 1\end{pmatrix}.$$

**Step 2 — fourth power (projectivity).** $U_{12}^4=e^{i\pi\sigma_3}=\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\pi},e^{-i\pi})=-\mathbb{1}$. So the braid group relation $\sigma_1^4=1$ is satisfied only up to the phase $-1$: the representation is projective with 2-cocycle $\omega(\sigma_1,\sigma_1,\sigma_1,\sigma_1)=-1$. Equivalently, $U_{12}^2=i\sigma_3=i\gamma_1\gamma_2$: the square of an exchange is the fermion parity operator, and four exchanges differ from the identity by the fermion parity $(-1)^F$ — the well-known "fermion parity anomaly" of Majorana braids, here derived rather than assumed.

**Step 3 — the braid relation, exact.** Compute $P=U_{12}U_{23}U_{12}$. First $U_{12}U_{23}$: using $e^{i\theta\sigma_3}e^{i\theta\sigma_1}=\cos^2\theta\,\mathbb{1}+i\sin\theta\cos\theta(\sigma_3+\sigma_1)-\sin^2\theta\,\sigma_3\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_3\sigma_1=i\sigma_2$, with $\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta=\frac12$, $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\frac12$:
$$U_{12}U_{23}=\tfrac12\mathbb{1}+\tfrac{i}{2}(\sigma_3+\sigma_1)-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_2.$$
Multiply by $U_{12}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\mathbb{1}+i\sigma_3)$:
$$P=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\Bigl[\tfrac12\mathbb{1}+\tfrac{i}{2}(\sigma_3+\sigma_1-\sigma_2)\Bigr](\mathbb{1}+i\sigma_3).$$
Expand: the first factor times $\mathbb{1}$ gives $\tfrac12\mathbb{1}+\tfrac{i}{2}(\sigma_3+\sigma_1-\sigma_2)$. The first factor times $i\sigma_3$: $i\sigma_3\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_3$; $i\sigma_3\cdot\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_3=-\tfrac12$; $i\sigma_3\cdot\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_1=-\tfrac12\sigma_3\sigma_1=-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_2$; $i\sigma_3\cdot(-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_2)=+\tfrac12\sigma_3\sigma_2=+\tfrac12(-i\sigma_1)=-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_1$. Summing all terms: scalar $\tfrac12-\tfrac12=0$; $\sigma_1$: $\tfrac{i}{2}-\tfrac{i}{2}=0$; $\sigma_2$: $-\tfrac{i}{2}-\tfrac{i}{2}=-i$; $\sigma_3$: $\tfrac{i}{2}+\tfrac{i}{2}=i$. So
$$P=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\,i(\sigma_3-\sigma_2).$$
By the $\sigma_3\leftrightarrow\sigma_1$ symmetry of the computation (exchanging the roles of $U_{12}$ and $U_{23}$ and repeating the identical algebra), $U_{23}U_{12}U_{23}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}i(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)$.
