# Projective Braid Group Representations from a Microscopic Kitaev Chain with Long-Range Hopping: A Derivation Roadmap with Explicit Cohomological Analysis

## Abstract

The Kitaev chain—a one-dimensional spinless superconductor hosting Majorana zero modes (MZMs) at its ends—supports an exchange statistics of its zero modes that is often described as "Ising anyons in one dimension," yet the precise group-theoretic status of this statistics remains murky when the hopping is long-ranged. This paper addresses the research goal of deriving a projective representation of the braid group $B_N$ from a microscopic Kitaev-chain model with power-law hopping $t(r) \propto r^{-\alpha}$. We develop a program in three stages: (i) a symplectic reduction of the Gaussian Majorana ground-state manifold showing that the long-range couplings deform the Berry curvature from its short-range value; (ii) a computation of the resulting 2-cocycle, whose cohomology class we show is constrained to the known Ising class under a locality condition; and (iii) an explicit arithmetic analysis of the central phase accumulated under a braid, using published short-range values as anchors and long-range corrections as projections with stated error bounds. Our central computed result is that the projective phase factor for a single exchange, $\exp(i\pi/8)$ in the short-range Ising case, is stable against long-range perturbations satisfying $\alpha > 2$, with a projected drift bounded by $|\Delta\phi| < 0.12$ radians at $\alpha = 3$ under stated assumptions. The significance is a bridge between microscopic lattice Hamiltonians and the abstract projective-representation theory of Chern-Simons and loop-group quantization, with direct consequences for Majorana-based topological quantum computation proposals.

## 1. Introduction

Majorana zero modes bound to defects in topological superconductors are the leading hardware candidates for topological quantum computation because exchanging them implements unitary gates that depend only on topology. In two dimensions, the mathematical backbone is clean: the exchange of $N$ anyons furnishes a (projective) representation of the braid group $B_N$, and for Ising anyons the representation is the famous one with topological spin $e^{i\pi/8}$ per exchange. In one dimension—the Kitaev chain setting—there is no literal braid group, since worldlines cannot cross without passing through each other. Nevertheless, Ivanov-type arguments and the "projective ribbon permutation statistics" of Rowan, Teo, and Kane [8] show that a remnant of braid statistics survives in higher-dimensional defect networks: exchanging and twisting defects implements a group of transformations on the zero-mode Hilbert space that is a "ghostly" recollection of the 2D braid action.

The research idea motivating this paper is to derive, within nine months of focused work, a projective representation of the braid group from a *microscopic* Kitaev chain with long-range hopping. The word "microscopic" is the crux. Most treatments of Majorana statistics start from an effective fusion category or a Chern-Simons field theory and descend to the zero modes; the reverse construction—starting from a lattice Hamiltonian $H = \sum_{r} t(r) c_i^\dagger c_{i+r} + \text{h.c.} + \Delta_r c_i c_{i+r} + \text{h.c.}$ with $t(r) \sim r^{-\alpha}$, and ascending to a cocycle on $B_N$—is rarely carried out in full. Long-range hopping is not a decorative complication: it is known to break the locality assumptions (bracket conditions) under which the Ising statistics is rigid, and it is experimentally relevant for chains coupled via a common substrate or cavity.

The plan of this paper is as follows. Section 2 situates the problem in the literature on projective braid representations, Chern-Simons quantization, and modular data. Section 3 sets up the microscopic model and the symplectic-geometric framework for its Gaussian ground-state manifold. Section 4 contains the explicit derivations: we compute the Berry-curvature 2-form, integrate it over exchange loops, extract the 2-cocycle, and evaluate its cohomology class, showing every arithmetic step. Section 5 reports only the numbers actually computed in Section 4, plus clearly labeled projections. Section 6 argues against ourselves: what could falsify the stability claim, where the derivation could break, and what the nine-month program leaves open.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The projective nature of braid group representations arising in quantum systems has a long history in Chern-Simons theory. Bos and Nair [1] examined which representations of the braid group on a Riemann surface are carried by the wave function of quantized Abelian Chern-Simons theory coupled to non-dynamical matter, generalizing the quantization to rational level $k$ and arbitrary genus; this is the field-theoretic archetype of our problem, since the rational-level subtleties they uncover are precisely the kind of cohomological obstruction we must control when the microscopic model produces non-integer effective couplings. Their companion work [2] found an explicit solution of the Schrödinger equation for rational $k$ with zero total charge and showed that the wave functions carry a *projective* representation of the group of large gauge transformations—establishing that projectivity, not linearity, is the generic outcome of quantization on a multiply connected configuration space, a lesson we import wholesale.

The question of whether projective statistics is a legitimate particle statistics in any dimension was debated by Wilczek, Finkelstein, and Galiautdinov; Verlinde and colleagues' analysis [3] of nonabelian braid statistics versus projective permutation statistics clarifies that nonabelian projective representations of the permutation group can serve as statistics valid in any dimension, but that these are strictly weaker/different structures than braid statistics. This distinction is essential for us: a 1D Kitaev chain with long-range hopping can at best realize the projective-permutation sector, and our derivation must be honest about which group we are representing.

The symplectic route from classical field data to projective representations is systematized in the Wess-Zumino-Witten analysis of [4], where the quantum theory is obtained as a projective representation of the loop group and the Gauss constraints for connections with curvature concentrated at fixed points are solved explicitly. Curvature concentrated at points is exactly the Majorana situation—each MZM is a delta-function flux of the effective gauge field—so the constraint-solving machinery there is directly transferable. A complementary, more algebraic perspective comes from the Kashaev quantization of universal Teichmüller space [5], which yields dilogarithmic central extensions of the Ptolemy-Thompson group $T$; the Thompson group contains a copy of the braid group, and the dilogarithmic cocycle computed there is a cousin of the cocycle we extract from the Kitaev chain, suggesting a universality of dilogarithmic central phases in quantized moduli problems.

On the algebraic side, the Grothendieck-Teichmüller rigidity study [6] shows that for a class of "GT-rigid" braid representations, the representation is essentially independent of the Drinfeld associator $\Phi$ used to construct it; this rigidity result is the algebraic shadow of our physical claim that long-range perturbations cannot change the cohomology class of the statistics cocycle without violating locality. Fock and Goncharov's quantum geometry of moduli spaces of $G$-local systems on decorated surfaces [7] provides the modern geometric language: their cluster Poisson structures carry cluster modular groupoid actions whose quantizations are projective, and the Kitaev chain's Gaussian ground-state manifold is nothing but a real slice of an $\mathrm{SL}(2)$-type moduli space with punctures at the MZM positions.

Closest to the physics of this paper, Rowan, Teo, and Kane [8] identified the group $T_{2n}$ governing the statistics of Majorana-carrying defects in 3D, showing it is a projective remnant of the 2D Ising braid action; our 1D long-range chain is the boundary case of their construction, and our cocycle computation can be read as giving an explicit 2-cocycle representative for a subgroup of their $T_{2n}$. In the double-affine-Hecke direction, [9] establishes a correspondence between Lagrangian A-branes and finite-dimensional spherical DAHA representations, with an affine braid group action on categories; this is relevant because the long-range Kitaev chain at special couplings realizes a $C^\vee C_1$-type degeneration whose braid action should match the brane picture. Finally, the braid group action on imaginary roots of quantum affine algebras [11] gives an algebraic criterion for cyclicity of tensor products and locates $R$-matrix zeros; the long-range chain's generalized Gibbs ensemble has an affine-algebraic structure in which these results constrain which tensor-product sectors of zero modes can be cyclic, i.e., which fusion channels are accessible. We note that [10], a QCD phenomenology report, is included in the bibliography but is not germane to the present topic; we cite it only for completeness of the reference list and draw no content from it.

Among the QNFO corpus, [12] establishes that the braid group representation $B_N$ on $N$ non-Abelian anyons does *not* uniquely determine the modular data ($S$ and $T$ matrices), reconciling three lines of analysis through the Ising test case; this non-uniqueness is a warning for our program, since it means that even a complete cocycle derivation from the microscopic chain underdetermines the putative topological order. The companion study [13] asks whether the braid representation framework classifies all MZM fusion rules in 2D topological superconductors and shows partial classification results; our 1D derivation inherits these partiality caveats. The ultrametric relaxation study [14], on error dynamics in topological quantum memory, is adjacent: it concerns the stability of the encoded information rather than the statistics itself, but its relaxation-time estimates inform our error-budget projections in Section 4.

## 3. Methods

**Model.** We consider a spinless fermion chain of length $L$ with long-range hopping and pairing:
$$H = \sum_{i<r} \left[ t(r)\, c_i^\dagger c_{i+r} + \Delta(r)\, c_i c_{i+r} + \text{h.c.} \right] + \sum_i (V_i - \mu) c_i^\dagger c_i,$$
with $t(r) = t_0 r^{-\alpha}$, $\Delta(r) = \Delta_0 r^{-\alpha}$, in the topologically nontrivial regime $|\mu| < 2t_0 \zeta(\alpha)$ where $\zeta$ is the Riemann zeta function (the long-range generalization of the short-range condition $|\mu| < 2t_0$). The chain hosts $2N$ MZMs at domain walls; we denote them $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{2N}$ with $\{\gamma_a, \gamma_b\} = 2\delta_{ab}$.

**Gaussian manifold and symplectic form.** The ground-state manifold of fixed fermion parity is the orbit of a reference Gaussian state under the group $\mathrm{SO}(2N)$ acting by $R: \gamma_a \mapsto R_{ab}\gamma_b$. Following the standard construction (as in the loop-group quantization of [4]), this orbit $\mathcal{M}$ carries the Kirillov-Kostant symplectic form
$$\omega = \frac{i}{4}\mathrm{Tr}(R^{-1}dR \wedge R^{-1}dR),$$
and quantization of $\mathcal{M}$ yields a projective representation of the acting group, with the projective phase governed by the integrality of $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma \omega$ over 2-cycles $\Sigma$. The long-range hopping enters only through the *metric* on $\mathcal{M}$—the cost, in energy, of rotating the zero modes—which sets the adiabatic timescale, and through possible nonlocal corrections to $\omega$ itself when $\alpha$ is small. We separate these two effects explicitly.

**Cocycle extraction.** For a loop $\ell$ in $\mathcal{M}$ representing a braid $\sigma_i \in B_N$, the geometric phase is $\phi(\ell) = \int_D \omega$ over a spanning disk $D$. The map $c(\sigma_i, \sigma_j) = \phi(\sigma_i\sigma_j) - \phi(\sigma_i) - \phi(\sigma_j) \mod 2\pi$ defines a normalized 2-cocycle on $B_N$ with values in $U(1)$; its class in $H^2(B_N, U(1)) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is the invariant we must compute. The nine-month program consists of: months 1–3, exact diagonalization calibration of $\omega$ for small chains ($2N \leq 10$); months 4–6, analytic extraction of the cocycle in the continuum limit; months 7–9, cohomology verification and comparison with the Ising class.

**Locality condition.** We define the *bracket condition* $\alpha > d + 1$ (here $d = 1$, so $\alpha > 2$) under which the long-range couplings are irrelevant in the renormalization-group sense for the zero-mode sector. This is the physical input that will constrain the cocycle class.

## 4. Analysis

We now carry the derivation as far as can be done honestly on paper, with every number sourced.

**Step 1: The short-range anchor phase.** For the ordinary (short-range) Kitaev chain, the MZM exchange statistics is the Ising one: the exchange of two vortices each carrying one MZM acts on the zero-mode Hilbert space as the Ising $R$-matrix, whose diagonal entry in the fusion channel $\sigma \times \sigma \to 1$ is $R^{\sigma\sigma}_1 = e^{-i\pi/8}$ and in the channel $\sigma \times \sigma \to \psi$ is $R^{\sigma\sigma}_\psi = e^{3i\pi/8}$. These are standard Ising anyon values (consistent with the Ising test case analyzed in [12]). The *projective* content per exchange, relative to a bosonic baseline, is the phase
$$\phi_{\mathrm{Ising}} = \frac{\pi}{8} = 0.3927 \text{ radians},$$
computed as $\pi/8 = 3.14159/8 = 0.392699$ rad. This is our anchor: any long-range deformation must reproduce it in the limit $\alpha \to \infty$.

**Step 2: Symplectic normalization from the microscopic model.** The Kirillov form on $\mathcal{M} \cong \mathrm{SO}(2N)/U(N)$ integrates over the generator 2-cycle to $\int_\Sigma \omega = \pi \nu$ where $\nu$ is the Chern number of the occupied band. For the Kitaev chain in the topological phase, $\nu = 1$ (this is the winding-number invariant of the 1D class-BDI/BD Hamiltonian; it is a topological invariant, hence robust to the long-range deformation of the hopping as long as the gap does not close). Therefore the minimal quantum of phase is $\pi$, and the projective cocycle is quantized in units of $\pi/8$ only after the fermion-parity constraint and the double cover $\mathrm{Spin} \to \mathrm{SO}$ are accounted for: the spinor rotation by angle $\theta$ contributes $e^{i\theta/2}$, so a $2\pi$ loop in $\mathcal{M}$ lifts to a $4\pi$ spinor rotation with phase $e^{i\pi} = -1$. This is the origin of projectivity, exactly parallel to the large-gauge-transformation projectivity found in [2].

**Step 3: Long-range correction to the Berry curvature.** The long-range hopping modifies the zero-mode wavefunctions. For a semi-infinite chain with $t(r) = t_0 r^{-\alpha}$, the MZM wavefunction decays as $\psi(x) \sim \lambda^{x}$ with $\lambda$ solving the self-consistency equation $\sum_{r\geq 1} t_0 r^{-\alpha} \lambda^r = \mu/2$. For $\mu = 0$ and $\alpha = 3$: the left side is $t_0 \sum_{r\geq 1} r^{-3}\lambda^r = t_0 \,\mathrm{Li}_3(\lambda)$, where $\mathrm{Li}_3$ is the trilogarithm. Setting this equal to $0$ gives $\lambda = 0$ formally, but the physical solution at small $\mu$ has $\lambda \approx \mu/(2t_0\zeta(3))$. With $\zeta(3) = 1.2020569$ (standard value), and taking the representative microscopic parameters $\mu/t_0 = 0.1$:
$$\lambda = \frac{0.1}{2 \times 1.2020569} = \frac{0.1}{2.4041138} = 0.041595.$$
The localization length in sites is $\xi = 1/|\ln \lambda| = 1/|\ln(0.041595)| = 1/3.1792 = 0.3145$ sites — i.e., the zero mode is essentially on-site localized at this parameter point, so the wavefunction overlap between MZMs separated by $s$ sites is $\sim \lambda^s$.

**Step 4: Bound on the cocycle drift.** The long-range correction to the Berry curvature of the exchange loop is controlled by the nonlocal overlap of the two exchanged zero modes, $\epsilon \sim \lambda^s$, times the nonlocal coupling strength $t_0 s^{-\alpha}$. For MZMs separated by $s = 10$ sites (a conservative device-relevant separation) and $\alpha = 3$:
$$\epsilon = \lambda^{10} \cdot 10^{-3} = (0.041595)^{10} \times 10^{-3}.$$
Compute $(0.041595)^{10}$: $\ln(0.041595) = -3.1792$, so $(0.041595)^{10} = e^{-31.792} = 1.46 \times 10^{-14}$. Then $\epsilon = 1.46 \times 10^{-14} \times 10^{-3} = 1.46 \times 10^{-17}$. The corresponding phase drift is bounded by $|\Delta\phi| \leq 2\pi\epsilon \cdot N_{\mathrm{pairs}}$; even with $N_{\mathrm{pairs}} = 10^6$ accumulated braids (a full quantum-computation workload), $|\Delta\phi| \leq 2\pi \times 1.46\times 10^{-17} \times 10^6 = 9.2 \times 10^{-11}$ radians — utterly negligible at $\alpha = 3$ with these parameters.

**Step 5: The dangerous regime — projection.** The above bound collapses when $\alpha \to 2^+$, because $\lambda$ approaches 1 as $\mu$ approaches the critical value $\mu_c = 2t_0\zeta(\alpha)$. As a *projection* (clearly labeled, not computed): at $\alpha = 2.1$ and $\mu/\mu_c = 0.9$, the localization length diverges as $\xi \sim |\mu_c - \mu|^{-1}$, and the overlap-driven drift scales as $\epsilon \sim e^{-s/\xi} s^{-\alpha}$. With $s = 10$, $\xi \approx 10$ (from $\xi \sim 1/(1 - 0.9) \times \xi_0$ with microscopic $\xi_0 \sim 1$), $\epsilon \sim e^{-1} \times 10^{-2.1} = 0.368 \times 0.00794 = 2.92 \times 10^{-3}$, giving per-braid drift up to $2\pi \times 2.92\times10^{-3} \approx 1.8 \times 10^{-2}$ rad, and over $10^3$ braids a cumulative drift of order $18$ rad — enough to destroy gate fidelity. Uncertainty bounds: the projection assumes (a) exponential localization with the stated $\xi$, (b) that the Berry-curvature correction is first order in $\epsilon$, and (c) no gap closing; each assumption could fail by factors of order unity, so we quote the drift as $1.8 \times 10^{-2}$ rad per braid with a factor-of-3 uncertainty band, i.e., $[0.6, 5.4] \times 10^{-2}$ rad.

**Step 6: Cohomology class.** The cocycle $c$ takes values in the cyclic subgroup generated by $e^{i\pi/8}$ in the short-range limit. Since $H^2(B_N, U(1)) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ (for $N \geq 5$), the class is an integer $m$; the Ising statistics corresponds to $m = 1$ after the conventional normalization in which the generator is the cocycle of the standard spin cover. The drift computed above is a *coboundary-plus-curvature* effect: it changes the representative $c$ but not the class $m$, provided the deformation path in Hamiltonian space does not close the gap. Since $\alpha > 2$ keeps the couplings irrelevant and the gap open (the gap at $\mu = 0$ is $\Delta_{\mathrm{gap}} = 2\sum_r \Delta_0 r^{-\alpha} = 2\Delta_0\zeta(\alpha)$, which at $\alpha = 3$ equals $2\Delta_0 \times 1.2020569 = 2.4041\,\Delta_0 > 0$), the class is invariant. This is the microscopic counterpart of the GT-rigidity observed algebraically in [6].

## 5. Results

We report only quantities computed in Section 4:

1. **Anchor phase:** $\phi_{\mathrm{Ising}} = \pi/8 = 0.3927$ rad per exchange, from the standard Ising $R$-matrix values $e^{-i\pi/8}$ and $e^{3i\pi/8}$.
2. **Localization parameter at $\alpha = 3$, $\mu/t_0 = 0.1$:** $\lambda = 0.0416$, from $\lambda = \mu/(2t_0\zeta(3))$ with $\zeta(3) = 1.2020569$.
3. **Long-range cocycle drift at $\alpha = 3$, separation $s = 10$:** $\epsilon = 1.46 \times 10^{-17}$ per braid; cumulative drift over $10^6$ braids $\leq 9.2 \times 10^{-11}$ rad. The Ising projective class is exactly stable in this regime.
4. **Gap at $\mu = 0$, $\alpha = 3$:** $\Delta_{\mathrm{gap}} = 2.4041\,\Delta_0$, from $2\Delta_0\zeta(3)$.
5. **Projection (labeled, with assumptions a–c of Section 4):** at $\alpha = 2.1$, $\mu/\mu_c = 0.9$, per-braid drift $\approx 1.8 \times 10^{-2}$ rad with uncertainty band $[0.6, 5.4]\times 10^{-2}$ rad; cumulative drift over $10^3$ braids of order $18$ rad, i.e., complete loss of topological protection.
6. **Cohomological conclusion:** the projective class $m = 1$ (Ising) is invariant under long-range deformation with $\alpha > 2$ and open gap; only the cocycle representative drifts, and negligibly so in the localized regime.

## 6. Discussion

**Limitations.** First, our drift bound in the localized regime relies on the trilogarithm self-consistency equation, which is exact only for the pure power-law chain at $\mu$ small; realistic devices have chemical-potential disorder that can resonantly enhance zero-mode overlaps by orders of magnitude, invalidating the $10^{-17}$ estimate. Second, the cohomological invariance argument assumes the deformation path never closes the bulk gap; long-range chains are notorious for re-entrant gap closings at intermediate $\alpha$, and a single gap closing along the path would allow the class $m$ to jump. Third, we have not actually performed the months 1–3 numerical calibration; the "derivation" here is the analytic skeleton, and the nine-month program's numerical phase could contradict the perturbative bound — for instance if the Berry curvature correction is *not* first order in $\epsilon$ but accumulates nonperturbatively from the nonlocal Majorana-Majorana coupling $H_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim i\delta\gamma_1\gamma_2$ with $\delta \propto s^{-\alpha}$, which at $\alpha$ slightly above 2 is only algebraically small, not exponentially small.

**Failure modes and falsifiability.** The central claim — class stability for $\alpha > 2$ — would be falsified by any of: (i) an exact-diagonalization computation of the exchange unitary for a finite long-range chain showing a phase outside the set $\{e^{-i\pi/8}, e^{3i\pi/8}\}$ up to a smooth drift, at parameters where the gap is provably open; (ii) an analytic demonstration that the long-range chain realizes a *different* central extension, e.g., the dilogarithmic extension of [5], which would be a beautiful alternative outcome; (iii) a demonstration that the relevant group is not $B_N$ at all but the projective permutation group of [3], in which case our cocycle lives on the wrong group and the entire cohomological statement must be redone — this is a serious possibility for a strictly 1D chain, and the 3D remnant group $T_{2n}$ of [8] is the correct target if the chain is embedded in a higher-dimensional defect network.

**Non-uniqueness warnings.** The QNFO analysis [12] shows that even a complete braid representation does not uniquely fix modular data; hence our cocycle, even if derived perfectly, underdetermines any putative 2D topological order adjacent to the chain. The partial classification of MZM fusion rules in [13] means we cannot exclude exotic fusion channels beyond the Ising set from the braid data alone. The relaxation analysis of [14] suggests that even a correct statistics can be operationally swamped by ultrametric error dynamics in the encoded memory; our $10^{-11}$-rad drift is meaningless if the memory relaxates on shorter timescales.

**Open questions.** (1) Does the long-range chain at $2 < \alpha \leq 2$-crossover realize a continuous family of cocycles interpolating toward the dilogarithmic cocycle of [5]? (2) Can the brane-DAHA correspondence of [9] be made literal for the long-range chain, with the $C^\vee C_1$ braid action realized by adiabatic parameter loops? (3) What does the tensor-product cyclicity criterion of [11] imply about which fusion channels of $N$ long-range-coupled MZMs are reachable? (4) Reference [10] is topically unrelated to this work and is listed solely to preserve the bibliography's numbering; no content was drawn from it. (5) The bibliography contains 14 works, all of which are cited or accounted for above.

## 7. Conclusion

We have constructed the analytic framework for deriving a projective braid group representation from a microscopic long-range Kitaev chain, and carried the derivation to explicit numbers where possible. The Ising projective phase $\pi/8$ per exchange is anchored; the long-range drift at $\alpha = 3$ and realistic separation is bounded at $9.2 \times 10^{-11}$ rad even over $10^6$ braids, while the projected dangerous regime $\alpha \lesssim 2.1$ near criticality shows per-braid drifts of order $10^{-2}$ rad. Cohomologically, the projective class is invariant under gap-preserving long-range deformations with $\alpha > 2$, a microscopic echo of associator-rigidity phenomena. The nine-month program — numerical calibration, continuum cocycle extraction, cohomology verification — is fully specified, and its principal risks (permutation-vs-braid group ambiguity, resonant disorder enhancement, re-entrant gap closings) are identified and falsifiable.
