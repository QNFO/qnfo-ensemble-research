# Time as Bruhat-Tits Tree Depth: Discrete $p$-Adic Temporal Ordering and Tree-Geodesic Causality

## Abstract

We develop a conjectural but internally precise model in which physical time is not a continuous real parameter but a discrete depth coordinate on a Bruhat-Tits tree: an event is a vertex, and its time is the tree depth $d \in \mathbb{N}$, identified conjecturally with the $p$-adic valuation $v_p(x)$. The gap addressed is that no existing framework derives quantization of time, partial temporal order, and acyclic causality from a single non-Archimedean geometric structure with exact, checkable combinatorics. Our approach is purely analytical: all consequences are exact counts on the rooted $(p+1)$-regular tree, derived with fully shown arithmetic and no simulation. Principal results, for the depth-$D$ ball with $p=2$, $D=10$: the ball contains $V_{10}=3070$ events; only $C_{10}=27651$ of the $\binom{3070}{2}=4710915$ pairs are temporally ordered, a fraction $f_{10}=27651/4710915\approx 5.8696\times 10^{-3}$, with the exact asymptotic $f_D\sim 2(p-1)D/((p+1)p^{D})\to 0$; the mean depth is $\bar{d}=27651/3070\approx 9.0068$; the future cone of an event contains $(p^{\Delta+1}-p)/(p-1)$ events within $\Delta$ ticks ($62$ for $p=2$, $\Delta=5$; $363$ for $p=3$); and tree-geodesic causality — $A$ causes $B$ iff $B$ lies on the unique path from $A$ to the root — admits no causal cycles by a strict-decrease argument on depth. In practice, the work gives quantum-gravity researchers a falsifiable, exactly solvable skeleton in which discreteness, partial order, and acyclicity are theorems of the proposed structure rather than assumptions, with continuum recovery stated only as a labeled projection.

## 1. Introduction

Physicists constructing a quantum theory of gravity face a concrete unresolved decision: what is the time variable of the theory? Quantum mechanics assumes a fixed external background time parameter to govern unitary evolution, while general relativity treats spacetime, including its time coordinate, as dynamical; this incompatibility is why, as the QNFO program states, the pursuit of quantum gravity is "fundamentally stalled" [10]. Until the time variable is specified, a non-perturbative quantum theory of gravity cannot even enumerate its observables. The cost of the status quo is structural: every candidate theory must import a time continuum from calculus, and every such import re-creates the tension between a dynamical spacetime and a background clock. A derivation of the time variable from a discrete combinatorial structure would change this decision problem at its root: it would replace the choice of a continuum with the choice of a graph, whose consequences are theorems rather than postulates.

The continuum model of time — a real parameter $t \in \mathbb{R}$ with a total order — is inherited from calculus, not from measurement. We adopt, as an explicitly labeled modeling postulate, that no operational procedure resolves time below the Planck scale, and that the real line is therefore not forced as the temporal substrate; this postulate is not established here. This paper takes seriously an alternative suggested by non-Archimedean geometry: that time is a discrete depth coordinate on a Bruhat-Tits tree, the combinatorial tree on which $p$-adic valuation becomes literal distance.

A Bruhat-Tits tree is an infinite $(p+1)$-regular tree (a connected acyclic graph in which every vertex has exactly $p+1$ neighbors) associated with the $p$-adic field $\mathbb{Q}_p$; in the $p$-adic AdS/CFT literature it serves as the bulk geometry whose isometry is the conformal group of a CFT over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [2]. The core conjecture is:

$$t \;\leftrightarrow\; v_p(x), \qquad d(v) \in \mathbb{N},$$

where $v_p$ is the $p$-adic valuation on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $d(v)$ is the depth of the vertex $v$ representing an event, counted as the number of edges on the unique path from a distinguished root $v_0$. The root is the vertex of minimal depth and is provisionally identified with the Planck-scale origin of a causal history.

Three consequences are developed:

1. **Quantization.** Depth is integer-valued, so time comes in indivisible steps of a fundamental tick $t_0$; all measurable durations are integer multiples of $t_0$.
2. **Partial ordering.** Two vertices neither of which is an ancestor of the other have no intrinsic temporal relation. Temporal order is a partial order, not a total order.
3. **Tree-geodesic causality.** $A$ causes $B$ iff $B$ lies on the unique tree path from $A$ to the root. Because the tree is acyclic and every vertex has exactly one parent, this relation is automatically acyclic and antisymmetric.

The strong triangle inequality of the $p$-adic valuation, $|x+y|_p \leq \max\{|x|_p,|y|_p\}$, whose ultrametric character is reviewed in [3], has as its combinatorial shadow the fact that tree distance between two events is determined by the depth of their most recent common ancestor — a fact with no Archimedean analogue.

We stress the epistemic status of the proposal: it is a conjecture. The contributions are:

- **(i)** A precise formalization of time as tree depth, with definitions of event, temporal order, tree-geodesic causality, and tree distance (Section 3).
- **(ii)** Exact combinatorial consequences derived with full arithmetic: the growth law $N_d=(p+1)p^{d-1}$, the ball size $V_{10}=3070$, the comparability count $C_{10}=27651$ and fraction $f_{10}\approx 5.8696\times 10^{-3}$ with exact asymptotic constant, the mean depth $\bar{d}\approx 9.0068$, future-cone counts $62$ and $363$, and a proof that causal cycles are impossible (Section 4, reported in Section 5).
- **(iii)** A mapping of the Bruhat-Tits-tree literature onto the questions the conjecture must answer, including the boundary-theoretic route by which a $p$-adic continuum emerges from tree bulk degrees of freedom (Section 2), and an explicit statement of who could use the result, at what readiness level, and what the smallest falsifying test is (Section 7).

No experiment is proposed that has been run, and no simulation is reported; the tree is exactly solvable, so closed-form statistics replace Monte Carlo.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We discuss the twelve works of the bibliography in order, each for a specific result its text states, and relate each to the tree-time conjecture.

**[1] On the missing branches of the Bruhat-Tits tree.** For a local field $k$ and the algebra $\mathfrak{A}=\mathbb{M}_2(k)$, this work computes the set $S(\Omega)$ of maximal orders containing a given order $\Omega$; this set is realized as a sub-tree of the Bruhat-Tits tree called the *branch* of the order, and branches have been used to study the selectivity problem and normalizers of Eichler orders [1]. The text also fixes graph conventions — full subgraphs, valency of a vertex — and works with a valuation $\nu:k^*\to\mathbb{Z}$ normalized by $\nu(\pi)=1$, so that one unit of valuation is one unit of tree distance [1]. Two points matter here. First, the normalization $\nu(\pi)=1$ is the precise mathematical precedent for identifying one valuation step with one edge, which is exactly our Definition 3.2. Second, the branch construction shows that sub-trees of the Bruhat-Tits tree selected by algebraic conditions are well-defined and computable; the causal past of an event in our model is precisely such a sub-tree (the set of ancestors), so [1] supplies the precedent that our central objects are mathematically legitimate.

**[2] Bending the Bruhat-Tits Tree II.** In the $p$-adic AdS/CFT correspondence, an $n$-dimensional CFT lives on $\mathbb{Q}_{p^n}$ and the dual bulk is the Bruhat-Tits tree, the analogue of pure AdS space; Witten diagrams on the tree satisfy the expected features of the $p$-adic CFT [2]. The work introduces coordinates on the tree in which the asymptotic boundary is the $\mathbb{Q}_p$ line and the "radial coordinate" $p^{n+1}$ encodes the accuracy to which the boundary coordinate $x^{(n+1)}$ is defined [2]. Its conclusion demonstrates that a BTZ-type black hole connection can be transplanted to a $\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_p)$ connection, that the resulting Wilson-line expectation values relate to a $p$-adic CFT at finite temperature, and that this black-hole background is related to the pure tree by a local diffeomorphism reflecting a non-trivial Weyl transformation [2]. This is the strongest existing precedent that gravitational-type causal infrastructure — horizons, connections, thermal states — lives consistently on tree geometry, which any tree-time model must eventually reproduce. The radial-coordinate reading of $p^{n+1}$ as "accuracy" is also a direct antecedent of our identification of depth with valuation.

**[3] Phase transitions for the $p$-adic Potts model on a Cayley tree.** This paper studies the three-state $p$-adic Potts model with competing interactions on a Cayley tree of order two, reduces the description of $p$-adic Gibbs measures to a recursive equation, and proves that a phase transition occurs if and only if $p=3$ for any nonzero interaction; for $p\neq 3$ the Gibbs measure is unique [3]. The text also reviews the $p$-adic norm $|x|_p=p^{-r}$ and its strong triangle inequality, the defining ultrametric property our distance formula exploits [3]. For us, [3] establishes two things: that ensemble statistics on regular trees is mathematically mature, with exact criteria rather than approximations; and that specific primes can be dynamically distinguished — here $p=3$ — a hint, though for a Potts system and not for temporal ordering, that the free prime of our model may not remain free in a dynamical extension.

**[4] Mumford dendrograms and discrete $p$-adic symmetries.** This work encodes dendrograms — trees arising in hierarchical classification of data — by embedding them into Bruhat-Tits trees of $p$-adic fields, shows that any dendrogram can be viewed as a subtree of the Bruhat-Tits tree for $\mathrm{PGL}_2(K)$ over a field $K$ chosen large enough to capture the maximal number of children, without changing the prime $p$, and proves that the dendrogram is uniquely determined by a suitable $p$-adic encoding, with the $p$-adic distance coinciding with the Baire distance for strings [4]. This is a representational completeness result of direct use: arbitrary hierarchical partial orders of the kind our model assigns to event sets embed faithfully into Bruhat-Tits trees. It also suggests, speculatively, a coarse-graining mechanism in which incomparable branches are merged by re-encoding.

**[5] $p$-adic colligations and 'rational maps' of Bruhat-Tits trees.** This work considers matrices of order $k+N$ over a $p$-adic field up to conjugation by $\mathrm{GL}$ over the $p$-adic integers, defines a product of such conjugacy classes, constructs analogues of characteristic (transfer) functions as maps from Bruhat-Tits trees to Bruhat-Tits buildings, and proves, for the case $\alpha=1$, that the semigroup $[\mathrm{Coll}_\infty(1)]$ is commutative [5]. The tree-to-building transfer maps are the natural language for composing local tree structure into larger geometries; if the single-trunk tree-time model must be upgraded to a building to recover spatial degrees of freedom (Section 6), [5] is the existing mathematical machinery for that upgrade.

**[6] Boundary theory of a spinor field theory on the Bruhat-Tits tree.** For a spinor field theory on the tree $\mathrm{T}_p$, this work calculates the action and partition function of the boundary theory by integrating out the interior of the tree, following the scalar-field method of taking a cut-off boundary $E_R$ and the limit $R\to+\infty$, in which the boundary becomes $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [6]. The summary of findings states that although the bulk kinetic term has spinor form, the boundary kinetic term takes the scalar-like form $\varphi^*D^{2\Delta-1}\varphi$, and that the factor $p^{\Delta}-p^{1-\Delta}$ appears throughout, with its zero point corresponding to the BF bound [6]. This is the closest existing result to our continuum-recovery question: bulk tree degrees of freedom integrate out to a boundary theory on $\mathbb{Q}_p$, i.e., a $p$-adic continuum emerges from the tree in a controlled field-theoretic sense. Our Projection P1 (Section 4.9) adopts exactly this mechanism as a hypothesis for the time coordinate.

**[7] Effective field theories on subspaces of the Bruhat-Tits tree.** For a free massless scalar field on $\mathrm{T}_p$, this work calculates effective actions on two subspaces $\Sigma_{1N}$ and $\Sigma_{2N}$ and finds that their limits, as the subspaces are taken to the boundary ($N\to+\infty$), are the same conformal field theory over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [7]. The text motivates the study of physics over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ by two points: the possibility that spacetime is non-Archimedean at small scales, and the number-field invariance principle [7]. This reinforces [6] from a second direction: different tree subspaces yield the same boundary conformal field theory, suggesting a robustness of the tree-to-$\mathbb{Q}_p$ limit that a tree-time model would want to exploit — the observable content of time may live at the boundary, where the exponential bulk is projected out.

**[8] $p$-adic equiangular lines and the $p$-adic van Lint-Seidel bound.** This work introduces $p$-adic equiangular lines in $p$-adic Hilbert spaces — defined by an inner product satisfying the non-Archimedean analogues of the usual axioms — and derives the first fundamental relation between common angle, dimension, and number of lines: if $\{\tau_j\}_{j=1}^{n}$ is a set of $p$-adic $\gamma$-equiangular lines in $\mathbb{Q}_p^d$, then $|n|^2 \leq |d|\max\{|n|, \gamma^2\}$, the $p$-adic van Lint-Seidel relative bound [8]. This shows that $p$-adic magnitude arithmetic supports packing bounds parallel to the Archimedean ones; in a tree-time model, such bounds would constrain how many mutually distinguishable branch directions can coexist at a given angular scale, a constraint relevant if the model is extended with internal state spaces.

**[9] The predecessor QNFO paper of the same title.** This work states the same core results we re-derive here with full arithmetic: the growth law $N_d=(p+1)p^{d-1}$, the ball $V_{10}=3070$ verified by two independent summations, the comparability count $C_{10}=27651$ out of $\binom{3070}{2}=4710915$ with fraction $f_{10}\approx 5.8696\times 10^{-3}$, and the proof that causal cycles are impossible [9]. Its discussion already flags the two central limitations we retain: the identification $t\leftrightarrow v_p(x)$ is a conjecture, and the model has no spatial degrees of freedom, so the light-cone question is answered only by cone counting [9]. We treat [9] as the prior version of this paper; our additions are the exact closed form and asymptotic constant for $f_D$ (Section 4.5), the refined related-work mapping, and the practical-implications analysis (Section 7).

**[10] Formal derivation of emergent spacetime to the Bruhat-Tits tree.** This work addresses the same crisis of time — quantum mechanics' fixed background time versus general relativity's dynamical spacetime — and derives macroscopic spacetime and the invariant speed of light from the discrete properties of the Bruhat-Tits tree, mapping the tree's timeless adjacency operator to a dynamic time-evolution operator via the Page-Wootters mechanism in a rigged Hilbert space [10]. Its summaries state that time is an epistemic, coarse-grained parameter; that the Archimedean projection yields continuous real coordinates; that a causal light-cone structure emerges from Lieb-Robinson bounds; and that the dimensionless upper bound of the speed of light is the Lieb-Robinson velocity derived from the spectral radius of the tree [10]. This is the closest published program to our conjecture, and it differs from ours on the key ontological point: [10] makes time epistemic and emergent from a static tree, while we conjecture that depth *is* physical time. The two are compatible — our Section 4 combinatorics are the static counting laws on which any epistemic emergence would rest — but the disagreement is real and we do not resolve it.

**[11] Bruhat-Tits tree as a unifying geometric object.** This work benchmarks $p$-adic, hyperbolic, and Euclidean embeddings of hierarchical data, reporting near-perfect fidelity for the $p$-adic embedding across distortion, hierarchical consistency cost, and rank correlation metrics, and demonstrates via simulation that a quantum walk provides ballistic transport, quadratically faster than classical diffusion, with evidence for linear $O(D)$ scaling for traversing a Bruhat-Tits tree latent space [11]. For us, [11] contributes a practical lesson: navigation of tree-structured spaces by quantum walk scales linearly in depth $D$, so if event retrieval or causal-path queries on a tree-time database were ever implemented, the traversal cost of the unique root path — which is exactly $d$ steps for an event at depth $d$ — is the operationally cheap primitive, matching our Definition 3.4 in which causality is a path query.

**[12] Time as epistemic emergence on a $p$-adic Bruhat-Tits tree.** This work formulates a timeless ontology: a static rooted $(p+1)$-regular tree carrying a state satisfying the Wheeler-DeWitt-type constraint $\hat{H}\Psi=0$ with $\hat{H}=\hat{A}-(p+1)\hat{I}$, whose constant solution makes the ontology static, with experienced time a subsystem-relative ordering of node traversals [12]. Its results report the same counting laws we derive — $N_d=(p+1)p^{d-1}$, $V_{10}=3070$, $C_{10}=27651$, $f_{10}\approx 5.8696\times 10^{-3}$ — together with the asymptotic $f_D\sim 2(p-1)D/((p+1)p^{D})\to 0$, a cone asymmetry of $62$ future against $5$ past events at depth $5$ with horizon $10$, a compression factor of $307$ for a depth-$10$ observer path against the full ball, and the spectral radius $\rho(\hat{A})=2\sqrt{2}=2.8284271\ldots$ adopted from a Lieb-Robinson-type argument [12]. We independently re-derive the shared counting laws in Section 4 and confirm the asymptotic constant $2(p-1)/(p+1)$ by direct computation; the compression factor $307 = 3070/10$ reappears in our Section 4.6 as the ratio of ball size to root-path length.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Definitions

**Definition 3.1 (Event).** An *event* is a vertex $v$ of the Bruhat-Tits tree $\mathcal{T}_{p}$, a $(p+1)$-regular tree with a distinguished root $v_0$.

**Definition 3.2 (Time).** The *time* of an event $v$ is its depth $d(v) \in \mathbb{N}$, the number of edges on the unique path from $v_0$ to $v$.
