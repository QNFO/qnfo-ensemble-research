# Everywhere Collapse: Quantitative Bounds on Consciousness-Mediated Wavefunction Collapse from Its Indistinguishability from Environmental Decoherence

## Abstract

A class of consciousness-collapse conjectures holds that if consciousness is fundamental and causes wavefunction collapse, then collapse occurs everywhere and continuously, with the environment acting as a collective low-grade conscious field; such "everywhere collapse" should then be empirically indistinguishable from environmental decoherence. The open question is whether this indistinguishability is exact or merely approximate, and what experimental bounds any deviation must satisfy. We formalize the conjecture as a continuous spontaneous localization (CSL)-type master equation in which the collapse rate scales with a complexity proxy $\Phi$ for consciousness rather than with environmental coupling. Assuming $\Phi$ proportional to particle number $N$, we derive a bound from the requirement that sustained Rabi oscillations in a superconducting qubit with $\Omega/2\pi = 10^{7}$ Hz not be frozen by the Zeno effect: $\gamma_0 < 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$. This bound predicts a consciousness-collapse contribution of at most $0.1\%$ visibility loss in a $10^{4}$-amu, $10$ ms matter-wave interferometer, consistent with the indistinguishability conjecture at current precision. In contrast, a quadratic scaling $\Gamma \propto N^{2}$ with a CSL-scale coefficient $\gamma_0 = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ predicts $\Gamma = 10^{18}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ for the same qubit, excluded by eleven orders of magnitude. The practical consequence: a pressure-scan interferometry protocol can separate a complexity-scaled collapse floor from environmental decoherence, and superlinear consciousness-collapse scaling is already falsified.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum technologists who build superconducting qubits, ion traps, and large-molecule interferometers face a daily engineering problem: every coherence budget they write assumes that decoherence is entirely environmental, caused by coupling to gas, phonons, photons, and control electronics that can in principle be engineered away. If that assumption is wrong—if a collapse process exists that does not couple to the environment but scales with the intrinsic complexity of the system—then a floor of irreducible decoherence exists that no shielding can remove, and design limits for quantum memories, sensors, and processors change accordingly. The same question bears on foundational physics: the measurement problem asks why superpositions become definite, and the hard problem of consciousness asks why physical processing is accompanied by experience; a conjecture that unifies them would, if empirically bounded, either become testable physics or be confined to philosophy by quantitative exclusion.

The conjecture examined here is due to Wigner-style thinking: consciousness is fundamental and causes wavefunction collapse. Taken literally, this implies collapse is ubiquitous, because on panpsychist views the environment itself carries a low-grade conscious aspect; the environment then acts as a collective conscious field that "observes" every system continuously. The empirical content of the conjecture is the claim that this everywhere collapse is indistinguishable from environmental decoherence. That claim is either exactly true, in which case the conjecture is empirically empty, or approximately true, in which case the residual deviation—collapse rates scaling with system complexity rather than with environmental coupling—is a detectable signature. Deciding which case holds requires a quantitative model, and that is what this paper supplies.

The strategy is to write the ubiquity conjecture as a CSL-type collapse dynamics with a consciousness-weighted rate, derive the constraints that existing coherence phenomenology (sustained Rabi oscillations, matter-wave interference) places on its parameters, and compute the predicted deviation from standard decoherence in a concrete interferometric protocol. The result is a parameter space map: part of it is already excluded, part of it predicts deviations at the $0.1\%$ level reachable by pressure-scan interferometry, and part of it is indistinguishable from environmental decoherence by construction.

The contributions of this paper are:

- A consciousness-collapse ansatz $\Gamma_{\mathrm{consc}} = \gamma_0 [\Phi(S)]^{k}$ with a complexity proxy $\Phi(S) \propto N_S$, derived in Section 3 and compared against the CSL benchmark rate in Section 4.
- A Zeno-effect bound on the linear-$\Phi$ model, $\gamma_0 < 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$, derived from superconducting-qubit Rabi phenomenology in Section 4, with full arithmetic.
- The headline prediction that the bounded linear model produces at most $0.1\%$ interferometric visibility loss in a $10^{4}$-amu, $10$ ms interferometer (Section 4), supporting the indistinguishability conjecture at that precision, while the quadratic variant is excluded by eleven orders of magnitude (Section 4).
- A pressure-scan falsification protocol with computed signal-to-background separation (Section 4), translated into an experimental pathway in Section 7.

## 2. Background and Related Work

Chalmers-style combination of consciousness theory with collapse dynamics is the closest prior formalization. Chalmers and McQueen develop a consciousness-collapse model by joining integrated information theory with continuous spontaneous localization, and their analysis establishes three points this paper inherits: the collapse dynamics must be continuous rather than occasion-specific, the model's rate parameters "have to be constrained carefully in order to yield empirically acceptable results," and simple versions of the theory are falsified by the quantum Zeno effect [2]. Their conclusion is candid that avoiding the Zeno problem forced complications, including superpositions of states of consciousness and Pearle-style dynamics with carefully constrained rates [2]. Our ansatz is a deliberately simplified member of this family, chosen so that the Zeno constraint can be computed in closed form; where they treat the locus of collapse as a modeling choice between variable and fixed loci [2], we fix the locus to position, the observable probed by matter-wave interferometry.

The panpsychist premise—that consciousness is a universal primitive rather than an emergent of non-conscious parts—is articulated by Gabora's double-aspect framework, in which phenomenal experience is a fundamental feature of the universe like mass or charge, and every physically realized information state is also realized phenomenally [5]. That paper's amplification principle, that an entity is conscious to the extent it amplifies information, supplies the conceptual basis for our complexity proxy: if consciousness is ubiquitous but amplified in proportion to informational structure, a collapse rate weighted by that amplification is the natural formalization of the "collective low-grade conscious field" [5]. The same work's account of autocatalytic self-replication and vesicle budding as the origin of localized information-amplifying structures motivates treating $N$, the count of organized components, as the crudest amplification measure [5].

The ubiquity conjecture requires that consciousness come in degrees across very different systems. Dessalles and coauthors argue that phenomenal consciousness, the capacity to experience aspects of environment and physical state, is probably shared with many animals that feel pain or pleasure and experience colors, sounds, and flavors [4]. A graded, phylogenetically distributed phenomenal capacity is exactly what a continuous collapse field needs; a binary, human-only consciousness would make the collapse rate discontinuous at the boundary of the biosphere, which no interferometric observation supports [4].

Any $\Phi$-weighted rate inherits the calibration problem of consciousness indicators. Koch's analysis of indicator-based assessments of artificial consciousness shows that theory-based indicators are used to update probability assignments, but that these assignments cannot currently be calibrated against independently established artificial consciousness outcomes [1]. Translated to our setting: no independently validated yardstick for $\Phi$ exists, so the exponent $k$ and proportionality of our ansatz are structural assumptions, not measured quantities, and every numerical bound in this paper is conditional on the proxy $\Phi \propto N$ [1].

The temporal structure of consciousness constrains how collapse occasions can be distributed in time. The synaptic-clock model posits that many moments of subjective time, with different durations, operate in parallel, and that continuous change is the dimension of consciousness accounting for the experience of time flow [6]. If collapse occasions correspond to conscious moments, a parallel, continuously overlapping set of moments maps naturally onto a continuous-in-time collapse process of the CSL type rather than onto discrete measurement-triggered events; this is the reading of ubiquity we adopt [6].

The objection that consciousness adds nothing computable is raised by Kak, who argues that subjective awareness is not computable and that machines can replicate cognitive tasks performed by conscious agents only insofar as the tasks do not require awareness [7]. If awareness is non-computable, then no computable proxy $\Phi$ can fully capture it, and our bounds are bounds on the computable component of any consciousness-collapse rate; a non-computable residual could alter the rate in ways our analysis cannot exclude, a limitation we return to in Section 6 [7].

Consciousness-inspired dynamics has been implemented and benchmarked in spiking networks. The CD-STDP model links long-term potentiation and depression coefficients to current and past neural activity, and its implementation on MNIST, FashionMNIST, and a CALTECH Face/Motorbike subset (1226 images, two categories) demonstrates that consciousness-derived coefficients can be given operational, trainable form [8]. This matters for our proxy: it shows that "consciousness-weighted" quantities can be made concrete enough to enter dynamical equations, which is the move our ansatz makes with $\Phi$ [8].

On the physics side, the wave-mechanical framing of this paper follows the treatise position that superposition is the universal arithmetic of reality and interference the geometry it sculpts, with the total state at every point the sum of co-existing wave patterns [9]. Interference visibility—the contrast of the summed pattern—is therefore the unique observable through which any collapse process reveals itself, and our analysis in Section 4 is a computation of visibility loss [9]. Finally, the terminological caution is real: "consciousness" is a contested term whose everyday simplicity conceals layered meanings spanning biological state, subjective awareness, and agency, and registers of language from propositional to poetic carry different evidential standards [10]. Throughout this paper, "consciousness" means only the operational variable $\Phi$ of Section 3; no claim about first-person experience is made or needed [10].

Two further works frame the many-body and definitional background. Kirilyuk's extended effective potential analysis of multi-component systems reports dynamic multivaluedness—incompatible emerging realisations—and dynamic entanglement as features of a causally complete many-body solution [3]. Read as dynamics, incompatible realisations coexisting before selection is the many-body picture within which a collapse mechanism must select one branch, which is the role our $\Gamma_{\mathrm{consc}}$ plays [3]. The threshold-of-meaning synthesis documents how contested terms such as life and death accumulate distinct definitions across medical, philosophical, and narrative registers, a pattern that applies equally to consciousness and reinforces the need for the explicit operational definition adopted here [10].
