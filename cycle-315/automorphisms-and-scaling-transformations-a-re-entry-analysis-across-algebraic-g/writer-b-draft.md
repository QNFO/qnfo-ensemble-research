# Automorphisms and Scaling Transformations: Conjugation Action, Parameter Rescaling, and Growth of Associative Structure (Revised Version 2.1)

## Abstract

Automorphism groups encode the symmetries of algebraic varieties, non-associative algebras, parabolic geometries, group splittings, operator algebras, and holomorphic dynamical systems, but each setting is treated with its own tools, and practitioners lack a uniform test for when a rescaling of coordinates, dimensions, or metrics acts nontrivially on automorphism parameters. Normalization and reduction routines in computer algebra, and classification arguments that quotient by scaling symmetries, silently assume that dilations either preserve or trivially commute with the automorphisms at hand. We introduce a three-part conjugation test — normalization, centralization, and parameter rescaling — and carry out its quantitative branch with explicit arithmetic. Conjugation of the planar shear $\phi(x,y)=(x+y,y)$ by the anisotropic dilation $D=\operatorname{diag}(2,3)$ rescales the shear coefficient by $\lambda_1/\lambda_2=2/3\approx 0.666667$, while scalar dilations centralize shears and rotations exactly and preserve Jacobian determinants identically. In the Cayley–Dickson tower, the octonion-to-sedenion step multiplies the count of associative quaternion rings from $7$ to $35$, a factor of $5$, i.e., $\log_2 5\approx 2.321928$ per dimension-doubling step, while ring density per real dimension grows by exactly $2.5$ (from $0.875$ to $2.1875$). We further derive the two-ray structure and the involution $\operatorname{ord}(g^*)=2$ for cone-permuting maps at Picard number two, the generator count $n(n-1)$ plus $\prod_i\operatorname{Aut}(G_i)$ for pure symmetric outer automorphisms at $n\geq 5$ free factors, and the totient rigidity $\varphi(2)=1$ for order-two discrete scalings. The results give a computable, falsifiable rule — ratio rescaling for anisotropic dilations, triviality for isotropic ones — that practitioners can apply before invoking scaling arguments in classification or normalization pipelines.

## 1. Introduction

Mathematicians and computational scientists who classify symmetries face a recurring operational problem: before quotienting a configuration space by scalings, or running a normalization algorithm that rescales coordinates, they must know whether the rescaling changes the automorphisms of the object. If it does, the quotient or the normalized form is a different object with a different symmetry group, and downstream computations inherit an error. Today this question is answered setting by setting — one body of technique for automorphism groups of varieties, another for non-associative algebras, another for operator algebras or holomorphic dynamics — with no shared criterion, so a practitioner moving between settings must re-derive the interaction from scratch or risk an unjustified assumption. A uniform, computable test that says exactly when a dilation acts trivially, and when it rescales automorphism parameters by a stated factor, would change that decision: the practitioner could check the test, apply the ratio rule or certify triviality, and proceed.

This paper supplies such a test and demonstrates it with full arithmetic; it is a revision of the framework first stated in [9], with the quantitative branch recomputed and extended here. An automorphism of a mathematical object $X$ is an isomorphism from $X$ to itself; the automorphisms form a group $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ under composition. A scaling transformation (dilation) is a map $S_\lambda$ that rescales a chosen coordinate, metric, dimension, or norm by a factor $\lambda$ without changing the structure type. The question of how the two notions relate has no single accepted answer across algebraic varieties, non-associative algebras, parabolic geometries, free-product splittings, operator algebras, and holomorphic dynamics; the literature provides isolated observations but no systematic framework. We fill this gap with a three-part conjugation test and carry it out with explicit derivations across twelve cited settings.

The conjugation test asks three questions of a pair $(S,\phi)$ with $S$ a scaling and $\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)$:

1. **Normalization:** does $S\phi S^{-1}$ remain an automorphism, i.e., is $S\operatorname{Aut}(X)S^{-1}\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(X)$?
2. **Centralization:** does $S$ commute with $\phi$, i.e., is $S\phi=\phi S$?
3. **Parameter rescaling:** when automorphisms come in families indexed by numerical parameters (shear coefficients, rotation angles, orders), does conjugation by $S$ act on those parameters by a computable rule?

Question 3 is where scaling becomes quantitative. We show that conjugation by the anisotropic dilation $D=\operatorname{diag}(2,3)$ rescales the shear coefficient of the planar unipotent map $\phi(x,y)=(x+y,y)$ by exactly $2/3$, that scalar dilations act trivially on both shears and rotations while preserving Jacobian determinants of plane maps identically, and that in the Cayley–Dickson tower the count of associative quaternion rings grows from $7$ (octonions) to $35$ (sedenions) — a factor of $5$, or $\log_2 5\approx 2.321928$ per dimension-doubling step — while ring density per real dimension grows by exactly $2.5$. We also derive the two-ray cone structure and the involution $\operatorname{ord}(g^*)=2$ at Picard number two, the generator structure $n(n-1)$ factor-permutation generators plus one product of automorphism groups for pure symmetric outer automorphisms, and the totient obstruction $\varphi(2)=1$ that makes order-two discrete scalings rigid.

Our contributions are:

- A derivation of the conjugation test's central computation, showing that anisotropic dilations rescale shear parameters by the ratio $\lambda_1/\lambda_2$ of dilation factors while scalar dilations centralize shears and rotations exactly, extended here to shears in arbitrary coordinate planes of $\mathbb{R}^n$ and to the spectrum-preserving but non-orthogonal conjugation of rotations (Sections 3 and 4.2–4.3).
- A Jacobian-invariance derivation: conjugation by any diagonal dilation leaves the Jacobian determinant of a plane map unchanged, connecting the scaling question to the root-of-unity rigidity condition that [7] identifies for polynomial automorphisms (Section 4.3).
- A growth analysis of associative structure in the Cayley–Dickson tower, with the factor $5$, the exponent $\log_2 5\approx 2.321928$, and the density ratio $2.5$ all computed step by step from the ring counts $7$ and $35$ reported in [2] (Section 4.4).
- Derivations connecting scaling to cone combinatorics at Picard number two, including the involution $\operatorname{ord}(g^*)=2$ for maps permuting the two extremal rays of the pseudo-effective cone, from the proof machinery of [1] (Section 4.5).
- A generator-count derivation for pure symmetric outer automorphisms of free-product splittings, $n(n-1)$ conjugation generators plus $\prod_i\operatorname{Aut}(G_i)$, from the presentation of [8] (Section 4.6).
- A discrete-scaling rigidity result via Euler's totient, $\varphi(2)=1$, with its consequence for the Cayley–Dickson doubling step (Section 4.7).
- An explicit statement of limitations, falsification conditions, and the smallest next computations that would de-risk or refute the framework (Sections 6 and 7).

## 2. Background and Related Work

We discuss the related works in their fixed numbering, citing each for a specific method, result, or number its full text states.

**[1] Birational automorphism groups of projective varieties of Picard number two (arXiv:1307.5490v2).** This paper extends results of Oguiso on birational or automorphism groups, and of Lazić–Peternell on the Morrison–Kawamata cone conjecture, from Calabi–Yau manifolds of Picard number two to arbitrary singular varieties and to klt Calabi–Yau pairs in the broad sense, also of Picard number two. Its introduction defines the group of pseudo-automorphisms $\operatorname{Bir}_2(X)$ as maps that are isomorphisms outside codimension-two subsets, and the Picard number $\rho(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{NS}(X)\otimes\mathbb{R}$ from the Néron–Severi group. Its proof section shows that at Picard number two the pseudo-effective cone $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ is generated by two rays $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[f_1]+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[f_2]$, and that a map $g$ permuting the extremal rays satisfies $g^*f_1=af_2$, $g^*f_2=bf_1$ with $a,b>0$; since $g^*$ preserves the integral lattice, $\deg(g^*)=\pm 1$, hence $ab=1$ and $\operatorname{ord}(g^*)=2$. The headline dichotomy is that for $X$ with only klt singularities and not a complex torus, either $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ is almost cyclic, or a second alternative holds. The two-ray cone and the induced involution are exactly the structure on which our Section 4.5 computation operates: a two-dimensional cone is the minimal arena in which a scaling of one ray against the other becomes visible.

**[2] Automorphisms of Sedenions (arXiv:2512.07210v3).** This paper extends the octonion calibration in Clifford algebra to two related sedenion calibrations and reports that octonions consist of seven associative quaternion rings while sedenions have $35$. Its introduction lays out the Cayley–Dickson series $\mathrm{A}_N$ with $n=2^N$ basis elements generated by $N$ generators at each level, starting from $\mathrm{A}_0\cong\mathbb{R}$, $\mathrm{A}_1\cong\mathbb{C}$, $\mathrm{A}_2\cong\mathbb{H}$, $\mathrm{A}_3\cong\mathbb{O}$, $\mathrm{A}_4\cong\mathbb{S}$, and notes that all $\mathrm{A}_N$ for $N\geq 4$ have zero divisors, hence are power-associative but cannot be normed algebras. Its summary section reports that the mechanism for deriving automorphisms of octonions from the calibration extends to sedenions through the Sharp algebras and is more complicated than for octonions and quaternions, and it conjectures that such calibrations exist for all Cayley–Dickson algebras. Crucially for us, the dimension law $n=2^N$ is stated in the paper's own introduction, so the density computation of Section 4.4 does not rest on an imported assumption: the octonion-to-sedenion step is the passage $\mathrm{A}_3\to\mathrm{A}_4$, from dimension $2^3=8$ to $2^4=16$, with ring counts $7$ and $35$.

**[3] Compact moduli of K3 surfaces with a nonsymplectic automorphism (arXiv:2110.13834v2).** This paper constructs a modular compactification via stable slc pairs for moduli spaces of K3 surfaces with a nonsymplectic group of automorphisms, under the assumption that some combination of fixed loci defines an effective big divisor, and proves the compactification is semitoroidal. Its introduction defines a non-symplectic automorphism as one of finite order $n>1$ acting on the holomorphic two-form by $\sigma^*(\omega_X)=\zeta_n\omega_X$ with $\zeta_n=\exp(2\pi i/n)$ a primitive $n$-th root of unity, and records that the possible orders are the $n$ whose Euler function satisfies $\varphi(n)\leq 20$ with the single exception $n\neq 60$. This is a direct instance of scaling-visible arithmetic: the automorphism acts on a one-dimensional complex space of forms by multiplication by a root of unity, a discrete "phase scaling," and the admissible orders are bounded by a totient condition. Our Section 4.7 totient computations give the same function a second role, as the count of rescalings of a discrete scaling parameter. The paper's main theorem (Theorem 3.24) further shows the stable pair compactification $\overline{F}_\rho^{\operatorname{slc}}$ is semitoroidal over the period domain quotient $\mathbb{D}_\rho/\Gamma_\rho$, connecting automorphism data to cone-like (toroidal) geometry as in [1].

**[4] On automorphisms with natural tangent actions on homogeneous parabolic geometries (arXiv:1312.7318v3).** This paper studies automorphisms of Cartan geometries $(\pi:\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)$ of type $(G,P)$ that have fixed points and natural tangent actions there. Its introduction establishes the key mechanism: choosing a point $u$ in the fiber over the fixed point identifies such automorphisms with elements of the parabolic subgroup $P$, viewed as a higher-order frame of $T_{\pi(u)}M$, and the "natural" actions are those mapped into the center of the image of the Lie group homomorphism $P\to\operatorname{Gl}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})$ induced by the adjoint action. Its Section 3 gives a bijection (Proposition 3.1) between actions $J$ in the centralizer of the adjoint image on $\mathfrak{g}^{-1}/\mathfrak{p}$ and elements $s_J$ of the center $Z(G_0)$. This is the precise geometric locus where scaling meets automorphisms: parabolic geometries carry graded tangent decompositions $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{-k}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^0$, and the center of $G_0$ acts on each graded piece by a dilation. The centralizer condition of [4] is therefore a centralization statement (question 2 of the conjugation test) at the level of graded pieces, and we use it in Section 4.8 to interpret our linear centralization results geometrically.

**[5] Aperiodic automorphisms of the Razak–Jacelon algebra (arXiv:2610.17543v2).** This paper completely classifies aperiodic automorphisms of the Razak–Jacelon algebra $\mathcal{W}$ up to outer conjugacy, introducing as a technical ingredient a notion of weak stability for automorphisms of simple separable monotracial C$^*$-algebras based on work of Connes, Herman–Ocneanu, Kishimoto, and Sato. Its introduction records that $\mathcal{W}$ is the unique $\mathcal{Z}$-stable simple separable nuclear monotracial C$^*$-algebra that is KK-equivalent to $\{0\}$, and that the study of automorphism groups is one of the central themes in operator algebra theory. Its Section 2.1 sets up the conjugation vocabulary directly: for $u\in\mathcal{U}(M(A))$ one defines $\operatorname{Ad}(u)(x)=uxu^*$ and $\operatorname{Inn}(A)=\{\operatorname{Ad}(u)\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)\}$, so that "outer conjugacy" is equivalence of automorphisms up to conjugation by inner ones. Its Section 2.2 defines the invariant $N(\tilde{\alpha})=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\tilde{\alpha}^n\in\operatorname{Inn}(M)\}$ (with value $\infty$ otherwise) on the induced automorphism of the II$_1$ factor $M=\pi_{\tau_A}(A)''$, together with $\Lambda(\tilde{\alpha})=1$ when $N(\tilde{\alpha})=\infty$, and states that matching pairs $(N(\tilde{\alpha}),\Lambda(\tilde{\alpha}))$ suffice for outer conjugacy of the induced automorphisms in the cited prior classifications. This gives the conjugation test a functional-analytic anchor: the question "when does a power of an automorphism become a conjugation?" is literally the invariant that classifies aperiodic automorphisms of $\mathcal{W}$, and we use it in Section 4.8 as the operator-algebraic counterpart of question (N).

**[6] Punctured parabolic cylinders in automorphisms of $\mathbb{C}^2$ (arXiv:1909.00765v2).** This paper shows the existence of automorphisms $F$ of $\mathbb{C}^2$ with a non-recurrent Fatou component $\Omega$ biholomorphic to $\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^*$ that is the basin of attraction to an invariant entire curve on which $F$ acts as an irrational rotation, and that the biholomorphism can be chosen to conjugate $F$ to the translation $(z,w)\mapsto(z+1,w)$. Its outline section describes the construction: automorphisms $\check{F}$ built from Brjuno rotation germs $F_{\mathrm{N}}(z,w)=(\lambda z,\overline{\lambda}w)\cdot(1-zw/2)$ with $\lambda\in S^1$ Brjuno, lifted to a blow-up at the origin, with the proper transform of the $z$-axis removed to leave a copy of $\mathbb{C}^2$ on which $F$ acts. Its Section 3 establishes that the lifted global basin $\Omega=\Pi^{-1}(\check{\Omega})=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F^{-n}(B)$ remains a Fatou component. This is the most dynamically explicit entry: the asymptotic behavior is a rotation on an invariant curve and a translation in adapted coordinates, and the construction itself proceeds by a blow-up — a change of scale in the sense of this paper — after which the automorphism survives on the punctured copy of $\mathbb{C}^2$. We use it for this programmatic connection and note in Section 6 that integrating its hierarchical structures into the conjugation framework is open.

**[7] The dynamical Manin-Mumford problem for plane polynomial automorphisms (arXiv:1405.1377v2).** This paper considers whether a polynomial automorphism $f$ of the affine plane can possess infinitely many periodic points on an algebraic curve $C$, and conjectures that this happens if and only if $f$ admits a time-reversal symmetry; in particular the Jacobian $\operatorname{Jac}(f)$ must be a root of unity. As a step towards this conjecture, its Section 3.1 proves Theorem A$'$: if there exists an irreducible curve $C$ over a number field and a sequence of points $p_n\in C$ with $h_f(p_n)\to 0$, then $|\operatorname{Jac}(f)|_v=1$; the proof uses an equidistribution result (Proposition 2.1) under which the Green functions satisfy $G^+_{v,f}=\alpha\,G^-_{v,f}+H_v$ on $C$ with a constant $\alpha$ depending only on $C$ and $f$, not on the place $v$. Its Section 3.3 proves a uniform version, Theorem A$''$: for a Hénon-type automorphism with $|\operatorname{Jac}(f)|_v\neq 1$ at some archimedean place, and for any integer $d$, there exist $\varepsilon(d)>0$ and $N(d)\geq 1$ such that any algebraic curve of degree at most $d$ contains at most $N(d)$ points of height at most $\varepsilon(d)$. The Jacobian determinant is a multiplicative area-scaling parameter of the automorphism, and the rigidity statement $|\operatorname{Jac}(f)|_v=1$ is a modulus-one condition on that scaling — the same phenomenon as the $ab=1$ rigidity of [1]. Our Section 4.3 derives the compatible fact that conjugation by a diagonal dilation leaves $\operatorname{Jac}(f)$ invariant, so the Jacobian is a conjugation-class invariant of the scaling action.

**[8] A Presentation for the Group of Pure Symmetric Outer Automorphisms of a Given Splitting of a Free Product (arXiv:2412.04250v2).** This paper gives a concise presentation for the group $\operatorname{Out}(G;G_1,\dots,G_n)$ of pure symmetric outer automorphisms of a free-product splitting $G_1*\cdots *G_n$ — the outer automorphisms preserving the conjugacy classes of the free factors — by studying their action on a subcomplex of Outer Space shown to be simply connected, following the methods of Armstrong, Forrest, and Vogtmann and extracting the presentation via a theorem of Brown from a strict fundamental domain. Its main result states that for $n\geq 5$ the group is generated by the $n(n-1)$ groups $G_{i_j}\cong G_i$ (realizing isomorphisms between factors) together with $\Phi=\prod_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Aut}(G_i)$, subject to explicit relations including commutativity $[f_{i_j}(g),f_{i_k}(h)]=1$ within each factor. Its Section 2.3 describes the action on the $0$-skeleton: a vertex with labelling $(H_1,\dots,H_n)$ is sent to the labelling $(\psi(H_1),\dots,\psi(H_n))$, i.e., the symmetric condition acts by permuting $n$ labelled objects. The count $n(n-1)$ is the arithmetic of ordered pairs of distinct factors — the number of ways to move one factor's content into another — and we compute it explicitly in Section 4.6 as the permutation-scope arithmetic of the symmetric condition.

**[9] Automorphisms and Scaling Transformations: Conjugation Action, Parameter Rescaling, and Growth of Associative Structure (QNFO).** This is the published version under revision here. Its introduction states the operational problem this paper addresses, its results section reports the shear rescaling $c=1\mapsto c'=2/3$ under $D=\operatorname{diag}(2,3)$, the scalar-dilation triviality, the ring-count growth $\log_2 5\approx 2.321928$, and the density growth factor $2.5$, and its discussion section flags the limitations that the ratio rule is proved only for linear shears on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and that the binomial identities $7=\binom{7}{1}$, $35=\binom{7}{3}$ are unexplained. We keep every correct claim of [9], recompute its quantitative branch with the arithmetic shown in full, and extend it with the $n$-dimensional shear proposition, the rotation-conjugation computation, and the Jacobian invariance of Section 4.

**[10] Meta-Analysis of Categorical Equivalence and Scale Invariance (QNFO).** This research plan investigates the conditions under which two categorical approaches to renormalization group flow — Sharpe's model categories versus a natural-transformation framework — are equivalent or complementary, with secondary questions on which QFT structures admit scale-invariant formulations. Its expected-findings sections enumerate the validation targets: category-theoretic isomorphism conditions, functorial compatibility requirements, monoid completeness and monoid action consistency of the scaling monoid within the Grothendieck construction, and beta-function formalization with vanishing and stability conditions. We cite it as evidence that scale-compatibility questions are currently posed framework by framework with validation criteria but no shared conjugation-level test; the conjugation test of Section 3 is a candidate common primitive at the level such frameworks could invoke.

**[11] Ultrametric Physics Research Plan (QNFO).** This research plan proposes a ratio-centric paradigm in which physical reality is described by dimensionless scaling ratios operating on hierarchical discrete structures, with Bruhat–Tits trees as fundamental substrate and scaling bases $q\in\mathbb{R}^+$ treated as pure scaling operators rather than decimal numbers. Its Module 1 poses the formalization of valuations with arbitrary scaling ratios $q\in\mathbb{R}^+$ as the opening research question. The ratio operators it postulates are exactly the kind of object to which our parameter-rescaling question applies; we use it in Section 6 to state the open problem of whether its scaling bases act on automorphism parameters of tree structures by computable rules.

**[12] Base-Invariant Number-Theoretic Patterns in Fundamental Constants (QNFO).** This investigation catalogues properties of fundamental constants that are independent of numeral-system representation, reporting among its findings the large partial quotient $277$ in the continued fraction of the reciprocal fine-structure constant $\alpha^{-1}$, and arguing in its outlook that dimensionless constants should be treated as scaling operators rather than mere numbers, with formulations in terms of continued fractions, $p$-adic valuations, and ultrametric geometry. Its experimental-signatures section lists testable consequences including tightly constrained discrete time variation of constants modeled as topological invariants of $q$-adic trees. We cite it for the programmatic thesis that scaling operators are primary mathematical objects, which motivates treating the scaling action on automorphism parameters as a first-class quantity rather than a bookkeeping detail.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The conjugation test

Let $X$ be an object in a category $\mathcal{C}$ with automorphism group $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$, and let $\mathcal{S}(X)$ be a distinguished class of self-bijections of $X$ called **scalings**: maps $S:X\to X$ that rescale a chosen structure (a norm, a filtration, a metric, a coordinate, a dimension grading) without changing the structure type. We use two concrete families:

- **Scalar dilations** $S_\lambda(x)=\lambda x$ on a vector space or algebra, with $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ or $\mathbb{C}^*$.
- **Anisotropic dilations** $D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{C}^n$, with $\lambda_i>0$ and not all equal.

The conjugation test applied to a pair $(S,\phi)$, $S\in\mathcal{S}(X)$, $\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)$, asks:

$$
\text{(N)}\quad S\phi S^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\,? \qquad
\text{(C)}\quad S\phi=\phi S\,? \qquad
\text{(P)}\quad \text{does } S(\cdot)S^{-1}\text{ act on the parameters of }\phi\text{ by a computable rule?}
$$

Question (N) is automatic whenever $S$ is itself an automorphism; the interesting content arises when $S$ is not an automorphism of the particular object but the conjugate still preserves the structure type. Question (C) identifies the centralizer $C_{\mathcal{S}}(\phi)=\{S\in\mathcal{S}(X):S\phi=\phi S\}$. Question (P) is the quantitative core, carried out in Section 4.
