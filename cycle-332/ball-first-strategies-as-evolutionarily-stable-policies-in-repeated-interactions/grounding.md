## Research idea

Ball-First Strategy as Evolutionarily Stable in Repeated Interactions. The note conjectures that 'playing the ball, not the player' — addressing arguments/issues rather than persons — is a strategically superior long-run policy, not merely an ethical one. Formalize this as a game-theoretic claim: model repeated interactions (debates, negotiations, iterated prisoner's dilemma variants) where agents choose between issue-directed moves (cooperative, information-preserving) and person-directed moves (ad hominem, intimidation, costly punishment). Hypothesis: ball-first strategies form an evolutionarily stable strategy or dominate person-first strategies under plausible payoff structures (reputation dynamics, trust accumulation, partner choice, noise). Test via evolutionary simulations varying population structure, memory length, and observability of moves; derive conditions (e.g., reputation transmission, exit options) under which person-directed aggression is strictly dominated. Compare to tit-for-tat and forgiveness variants. This would give a rigorous foundation for the informal claim that clean engagement outperforms personal attacks in the long game.
