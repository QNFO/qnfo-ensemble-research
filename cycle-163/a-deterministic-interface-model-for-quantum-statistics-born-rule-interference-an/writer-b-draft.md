# Quantum Mechanics as the Interface between a Continuous Deterministic World and Discrete Measurement

## Abstract

Physics faces a persistent gap: our best fundamental theories describe nature through continuous deterministic evolution, while every observation yields discrete, probabilistic outcomes. Deterministic reconstructions of quantum mechanics exist, but none explains why the Born rule, interference, and Tsirelson's bound take exactly the form they do. We develop a measure-theoretic interface model: a continuous deterministic state space with a measure-preserving, mixing flow, observed through a finite measurement partition. We derive the Born rule as the invariant measure of a measurement basin, reproduce interference as $p_A(\varphi) = (1+\cos\varphi)/2$ (giving $p_A = 0.75$ at $\varphi = \pi/3$), and rederive the CHSH bounds algebraically: the local bound $S \le 2$ and the quantum value $S = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.82842712$, an excess of $41.42\%$ over the local bound. Within a binary conspiratorial-split model we show that exact reproduction of quantum correlations requires full measurement dependence ($\delta = 1$), while matching correlators to precision $\varepsilon$ requires a measurement-dependence budget $\delta \ge \varepsilon/2$. The model is analytical and unreviewed (TRL 1–2); its immediate use is as a null model for device-independent certification, where the next concrete test is a reanalysis of existing Bell data under an independently bounded measurement-dependence budget.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum technologies now make promises that depend on which interpretation of quantum mechanics is correct. Device-independent quantum key distribution certifies security from the observed magnitude of a Bell violation; quantum-error-correction benchmarks assume that measurement outcomes are Born-rule distributed and free of hidden correlations with setting choices. If a deterministic underlayer with even a small measurement-dependence budget can mimic quantum statistics, certification bounds change, and the change is quantifiable. Today the practitioner facing this problem has no concrete alternative model with which to stress-test certification assumptions: deterministic reconstructions such as the cellular-automaton programme exist, but they do not deliver closed-form predictions for the quantities a certification engineer actually uses — the CHSH value, the marginal click probabilities, and the sequential-measurement statistics.

The cost of the gap is concrete. A certification proof that assumes measurement independence is only as strong as that assumption, and no published alternative model tells the cryptographer how much violation a bounded amount of measurement dependence can fake. A capability that would change the situation is an explicit, computable interface model: one that takes a continuous deterministic ontology as given, adds a discrete measurement act as a mathematical operation, and outputs numbers for the same observables that quantum mechanics outputs. If such a model reproduces the Born rule, interference, and the Tsirelson bound from deterministic premises, then the certification community gains a null hypothesis against which device-independent claims can be tested, and the foundations community gains a target that any complete deterministic reconstruction must hit.

This paper builds that interface model and computes its predictions. The ontology is deliberately minimal: a continuous phase space, a measure-preserving flow, and a measurement act defined as a finite partition. Everything else is derived.

The contributions are:

- A derivation of the Born rule as the invariant measure of a measurement basin under a mixing deterministic flow, with the two-outcome case computed explicitly (Section 4.1).
- A derivation of single- and sequential-measurement interference, $p_A(\varphi) = (1+\cos\varphi)/2$ and the sequential probability $\cos^2(\theta/2)$, with worked numbers (Section 4.2).
- An algebraic derivation of the CHSH local bound $S \le 2$ and the quantum value $S = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.82842712$, an excess of $41.42\%$ (Section 4.3).
- A measurement-dependence budget analysis: exact quantum correlations require $\delta = 1$ in the binary-split construction, and precision $\varepsilon$ requires $\delta \ge \varepsilon/2$ (Section 4.4).
- A falsification criterion for the model expressed as a bound on certifiable CHSH values given an independently bounded $\delta$ (Sections 5 and 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The programme of deriving quantum mechanics from deterministic premises has a long history, and the present model sits inside it while differing from each of its neighbours in a specific, checkable way.

Bell's original analysis of the Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen argument [10] established the constraint that any local hidden-variable theory must satisfy: the paper constructs a local model for the EPR correlations and then proves the inequality that such a model obeys, demonstrating that quantum predictions for the singlet state conflict with local determinism. Our model accepts this result as a boundary condition rather than an obstacle: it is explicitly non-local in Bell's sense only through the measurement act, and the measurement-dependence budget derived in Section 4.4 is the quantitative statement of how much of Bell's assumption the model gives up.

't Hooft's cellular-automaton interpretation [1] proposes that quantum mechanics is the statistical mechanics of an underlying deterministic cellular automaton evolving at the Planck scale; the work argues that wavefunctions behave like probability distributions over automaton states and that the apparent indeterminism of quantum mechanics is an artefact of our ignorance of the initial state. Our model shares the statistical-mechanics reading of the wavefunction but replaces the discrete automaton with a continuous measure-preserving flow, so that discreteness enters only at the measurement interface; this is the central structural difference and the reason our Born-rule derivation (Section 4.1) proceeds through invariant measures rather than counting automaton states.

Hossenfelder's survey of objections to superdeterminism [2] catalogues the criticisms directed at theories that relax statistical independence between hidden variables and measurement settings, and argues that the assumption of freely chosen settings is itself an unproved physical postulate whose cost has never been quantified. We adopt that framing directly: Section 4.4 quantifies the budget, showing that the binary-split construction needs $\delta = 1$ for exact reproduction but only $\delta \ge \varepsilon/2$ for precision $\varepsilon$, which turns an objection into a measurable design parameter.
