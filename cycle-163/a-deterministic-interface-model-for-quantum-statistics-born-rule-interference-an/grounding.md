## Research idea

Continuous deterministic universe with discrete measurement as unique QM bridge. The author claims the universe is continuous, local, and deterministic, while measurements are inherently discrete, and that quantum mechanics is the logically necessary and unique calculus bridging these two domains; quantum paradoxes arise from reifying mathematical models. A paper could test the strongest, most falsifiable version: whether a local deterministic model exploiting only the measurement-independence relaxation quantified by Hall (fewer than 1/15 bit of setting-system correlation) can reproduce not just singlet correlations but the full statistics of modern loophole-free Bell experiments (Hensen, Giustina, Shalm 2015), including randomness-certification and device-independent protocols that assume free choice. Derivation: formalize the minimal correlation budget required per experimental protocol and prove bounds on how much setting dependence any local deterministic model needs. Simulation: build a threshold-detector model (La Cour style) driven by continuous deterministic dynamics, inject bounded setting-system correlations, and compare predicted violation distributions against published data. Uniqueness could be probed by showing any calculus bridging continuous states and discrete outcomes with Born-rule statistics is isomorphic to QM.
