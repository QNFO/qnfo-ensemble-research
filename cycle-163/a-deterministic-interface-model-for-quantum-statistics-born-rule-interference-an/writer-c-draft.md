# The Price of Free Choice: Bounded Measurement Dependence in a Continuous Deterministic Universe with Discrete Measurement

## Abstract

We investigate a falsifiable form of the thesis that the universe is continuous, local, and deterministic, while measurements are inherently discrete, with quantum mechanics acting as the unique calculus bridging the two domains. The strongest testable version asks whether a local deterministic model, allowed only the measurement-independence relaxation quantified by Hall's budget of fewer than $1/15$ bit of correlation between measurement settings and hidden variables, can reproduce not only singlet correlations but the full statistics of modern loophole-free Bell experiments, including device-independent protocols that assume free choice. We formalize the correlation budget, convert it between bits, nats, and total-variation distance, and derive the exact cost of maximal Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) violation within a canonical one-parameter "tilted-prior" model family: achieving $S = 2\sqrt{2}$ requires $I_{\text{tilt}} = 0.127567$ bits of setting-hidden-variable mutual information, a factor of $1.914$ more than Hall's budget. We further show that within the same family, threshold-level violations are far cheaper: a violation margin $\Delta S = 0.1$ costs only $0.001804$ bits. The efficiency gap between naive and optimal measurement dependence is the paper's central quantitative result and defines a concrete research program: either an optimal construction closes the gap, or the free-choice assumptions of device-independent protocols are provably safe against local deterministic models at Hall-scale budgets.

## 1. Introduction

The research program examined here begins from an ontological commitment: the universe is continuous, local, and deterministic; measurement acts are inherently discrete; and quantum mechanics (QM) is claimed to be the logically necessary and unique calculus bridging these two domains, with quantum paradoxes arising from reifying mathematical models as physical entities. This is a strong claim, and most of it is philosophy rather than physics. But one corner of it is quantitatively falsifiable, and that corner is the subject of this paper.

Bell-type experiments are the empirical arena. A local deterministic hidden-variable model must reproduce the observed correlations between spacelike-separated measurement outcomes. The only known escape from Bell's theorem within local determinism is to relax measurement independence — the assumption that the hidden variables $\lambda$ are statistically independent of the measurement settings $x, y$. Hall quantified how little such "measurement dependence" is needed: fewer than $1/15$ bit of mutual information between settings and hidden variables suffices to reproduce the singlet correlations exactly [context as supplied in the source proposal; see Section 4 for the precise input]. This number is remarkable because it is small: $1/15$ bit is $6.7\%$ of a single bit, a seemingly negligible fine-tuning.

The question this paper poses is whether that budget is sufficient for more than the singlet. Modern loophole-free Bell experiments do not merely measure a CHSH value; they feed their statistics into device-independent (DI) protocols — randomness certification, key distribution — whose security proofs assume free choice of settings. A local deterministic model that reproduces the raw correlations but violates free choice at the $1/15$-bit level would undermine the DI conclusions drawn from those experiments. Conversely, if one can prove that reproducing the full statistics of such experiments requires strictly more setting-system correlation than Hall's budget allows, then the continuous-deterministic ontology is empirically constrained in a sharp, quantitative way.

Our contributions are:

1. A unit-consistent formalization of the measurement-dependence budget: we convert Hall's $1/15$ bit into nats and into a total-variation bound on the deformation of the hidden-variable prior, obtaining $\mathrm{TV} \le 0.152003$ via Pinsker's inequality.
2. An exact derivation, within a canonical one-parameter model family, of the mutual-information cost of maximal CHSH violation: $I_{\text{tilt}} = 0.127567$ bits, exceeding the Hall budget by a factor of $1.914$.
3. A cost curve for sub-maximal violations, showing that threshold-level violations are orders of magnitude cheaper than maximal ones, which reframes what is at stake in DI protocols.
4. A discussion of the uniqueness thesis — that any calculus bridging continuous states and discrete outcomes with Born-rule statistics is isomorphic to QM — and of what would falsify each claim.

We are explicit about scope: we do not simulate a full threshold-detector experiment here, and we do not claim the tilt family is optimal. The gap factor $1.914$ is a property of the family, and its closure or non-closure by optimal constructions is the open problem this paper isolates.

## 2. Background and Related Work

Because the bibliography for this preprint is drawn from a broad corpus rather than the Bell-test literature proper, we cite each work for the methodological role it plays in the argument, and we flag the limitation that no primary Bell-experiment or hidden-variable papers appear in the reference list; the Hall $1/15$-bit figure enters as a stated input from the research proposal, not as a citable derivation here.

[1] presents a feature-detection method for visuotactile sensors based on continuous marker patterns, constructing discrete feature models (checkerboard-like corners) from a continuous deformation field via a double-layer circular sampler. This is a direct engineering analogue of our core bridge problem: extracting stable discrete outcomes from continuous underlying dynamics, with the "detector" design playing the role of the measurement apparatus in our framework.

[2] develops a continuous functional framework for treatment effects with correlated spillovers and derives a master equation from three independent economic foundations — heterogeneous agent aggregation, market equilibrium, and cost minimization. Its argumentative strategy, establishing that a bridging calculus is forced by multiple independent foundations rather than chosen ad hoc, is exactly the structure our uniqueness thesis requires for QM: the Born-rule bridge should be derivable, not postulated.

[3] is the closest methodological antecedent. It establishes observational equivalence between continuous-time measure-theoretic deterministic and indeterministic descriptions, including equivalence results between deterministic and indeterministic models at the level of observable statistics. This bears directly on our program: if deterministic and indeterministic descriptions are observationally equivalent in continuous time, then the empirical content of "determinism" must be sought in correlations involving the measurement act — precisely where measurement dependence enters Bell analysis.

[4] measures Google AI Overviews, documenting activation, source quality, claim fidelity, and publisher impact for a system whose users "may not realize the answers they see are AI-generated." We cite it as a cautionary analogy for reification: an inference layer interposed between the underlying world and the observer can be mistaken for the world itself, which is the same error our ontology attributes to quantum paradoxes.

[5] considers a Bianchi I (ellipsoidal) universe and derives a cosmic-shear expression relating the universe's eccentricity to the cosmic microwave background, with only two parameters. It illustrates how a continuous global geometry leaves discrete, statistical imprints in measurements — the pattern our bridge calculus generalizes — and how small parameter budgets can still be observationally constraining.
