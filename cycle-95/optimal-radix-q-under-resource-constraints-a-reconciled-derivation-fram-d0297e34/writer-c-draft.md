# Optimal Radix $q$ under Resource Constraints: A Cost-Balanced Derivation for Ultrametric Hierarchies

## Abstract

Hierarchical (ultrametric) representations organize $N$ items in a $q$-ary tree of depth $n = \lceil \ln N / \ln q \rceil$, and prior ultrametric modeling has largely treated the branching factor (radix) $q$ as a fixed structural parameter inherited from the underlying algebra or hardware. This paper asks the optimization question directly: under explicit resource constraints, what is the optimal $q$? We formalize a two-term per-leaf cost model $f(q) = c_s q + c_t \ln N / \ln q$, where $c_s$ prices storage and interconnection (which grow linearly in $q$) and $c_t$ prices traversal (which shrinks as $q$ grows, since depth falls). We derive the first-order optimality condition $q (\ln q)^2 = (c_t / c_s) \ln N$, prove interior minima exist and are unique for $q > 1$, and evaluate the model numerically for $N = 10^6$ at storage-to-traversal price ratios $r = c_t / c_s \in \{1, 10, 100\}$, obtaining continuous optima $q^\ast \approx 5.15$, $17.1$, and $74.4$ respectively, with integer optima $q = 5$, $17$, and $74$. Against a binary baseline ($q = 2$), the balanced case saves $33.5\%$ of per-leaf cost and the traversal-heavy case saves $67.3\%$. We show the integer-rounding penalty is below $0.1\%$ in all cases, situate the result within the QNFO corpus on radix-to-ultrametric synthesis, and discuss falsification conditions, model limitations, and the discrete-continuous mismatch that motivates integer-aware refinement.

## 1. Introduction

Ultrametric hierarchies — trees in which the distance between any two leaves is determined by the depth of their least common ancestor — are a standard representational substrate whenever data exhibit nested structure. In such a representation, $N$ items are organized in a $q$-ary tree of depth $n = \lceil \ln N / \ln q \rceil$, and the choice of branching factor $q$ determines how storage, interconnection, and traversal resources scale [9]. The QNFO corpus context supplied for this work states the motivating gap explicitly: prior ultrametric modeling has treated the radix as a fixed structural parameter, inherited rather than chosen. This paper is a re-entry of that prior QNFO work on radix-to-ultrametric synthesis, addressing the question posed there: given resource constraints, what is the optimal $q$?

The question is not academic. A radix chosen too small produces deep trees whose traversal costs dominate; a radix chosen too large produces shallow trees whose fan-out storage and interconnection costs dominate. Between these regimes lies an optimum that depends on the relative price of the two resource families. Because the depth term $\ln N / \ln q$ is concave and the fan-out term $q$ is linear, the total cost is guaranteed to have an interior minimum, and that minimum can be located in closed form up to the solution of one transcendental equation.

Our contributions are:

1. A minimal two-term cost model $f(q) = c_s q + c_t \ln N / \ln q$ with explicitly defined price coefficients $c_s$ (per-unit storage/interconnection) and $c_t$ (per-level traversal), stated so that any practitioner can substitute their own prices.
2. A closed-form first-order condition $q (\ln q)^2 = (c_t / c_s) \ln N$, a proof that the minimum is unique and interior for $q > 1$, and a second-order verification.
3. Fully shown numerical evaluations for $N = 10^6$ at three price ratios, including integer optima and rounding penalties.
4. A discussion of failure modes, falsification conditions, and the relationship of this derivation to the surrounding QNFO corpus and to multi-metric synthesis practice in the cited literature.

All quantitative claims in this paper are either derived with shown arithmetic in Section 4 or explicitly labeled as projections with stated assumptions. No empirical measurements are reported; the paper is a derivation, not an experiment.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We discuss the twelve supplied bibliography entries in their given order. Because several entries carry only short summaries, we restrict every statement about a work to what its own supplied text supports.

**[1] Radar-based Re-Entry Predictions with very limited tracking capabilities: the GOCE case study (arXiv:1805.09171v1).** The supplied summary states that the re-entry predictions of GOCE have been deeply investigated in the literature due to the large amount of data, mainly radar and GPS, available until re-entry, and that accurate GPS and attitude measurements are used to compute a precise reference orbit for the three weeks of decay and to extrapolate the ballistic coefficient evolution of the object. We cite this work as a methodological analogy: it exemplifies inference about a decaying system under limited tracking capability, which is structurally similar to our problem of choosing a structural parameter under resource limits. The summary supplied gives no further detail on the prediction machinery itself.

**[2] Synthesis of Reversible Functions Beyond Gate Count and Quantum Cost (arXiv:1004.4609v1).** The summary states that many synthesis approaches for reversible and quantum logic generate circuits with respect to simple metrics, i.e. gate count or quantum cost, while physically realizing reversible and quantum hardware involves additional constraints, and that the paper describes cost metrics beyond gate count and quantum cost. This is the closest methodological precedent for our argument: optimizing a single metric (here, depth alone, or storage alone) is inadequate, and a synthesis procedure should be driven by a bundle of cost metrics. Our two-term model is precisely such a bundle, restricted to two terms for tractability.

**[3] InstructLayout: Instruction-Driven 2D and 3D Layout Synthesis with Semantic Graph Prior (arXiv:2407.07580v3).** The summary states that existing layout synthesis methods implicitly model object joint distributions and express object relations, hindering controllability, and that InstructLayout integrates a semantic graph prior and a layout decoder to improve controllability and fidelity. We cite it as an example of structure synthesis governed by an explicit prior: our radix choice plays the role of a structural prior for ultrametric layouts, chosen to satisfy explicit constraints rather than inherited implicitly.

**[4] Synth-by-Reg (SbR): Contrastive learning for synthesis-based registration of paired images (arXiv:2107.14449v3).** The summary states that nonlinear inter-modality registration is challenging due to the lack of objective functions that are good proxies for alignment, and that the proposed method converts this into an easier intra-modality task via a registration loss for weakly supervised image translation that does not require perfectly aligned training data.
