## Research idea

Manifold Modulation for Latent Space Steganography. Claim: any transmission from a sufficiently complex, stateful source carries two parallel information streams—content (the explicit message) and source information (the sender's internal statistical state)—so fine-tuning a student on a teacher's outputs acts as demodulation that extracts the teacher's hidden state. The author proposes engineering this deliberately: constrain a teacher's internal states to a chosen latent 'covert manifold' while it performs an unrelated carrier task, so the manifold's geometry is steganographically encoded into token statistics, then a student fine-tuned on that data inherits the targeted behavior. Testable: (1) formalize an information-theoretic bound on covert bits per carrier token given carrier-task constraints; (2) simulate teacher-student distillation where the teacher's logits are modulated toward a target manifold, measuring whether students recover the behavior better than from unmodulated data; (3) ablate modulation strength to map encoding capacity versus carrier-task degradation.
