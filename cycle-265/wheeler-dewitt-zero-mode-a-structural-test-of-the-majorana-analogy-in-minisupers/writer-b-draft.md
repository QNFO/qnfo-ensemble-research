# The Wheeler–DeWitt Zero Mode Is Not a Majorana Zero Mode: A Structural Test in Minisuperspace

## Abstract

Quantum cosmology and topological quantum computing both attach great weight to "zero modes", and a recent conjecture proposes that the Wheeler–DeWitt constraint's solution space ($\hat{H}\Psi=0$) is structurally analogous to a Majorana zero mode: a topologically protected, self-conjugate state at zero energy. If true, this would give quantum gravity an operational, computational signature; if false, the shared label is purely nominal. We test the analogy on three criteria that define a Majorana zero mode in the Bogoliubov–de Gennes setting: particle–hole (self-conjugate) symmetry, a non-zero chiral/Fredholm index, and spectral isolation with topological protection under deformations. Working in the Hawking–Page wormhole minisuperspace, we verify in closed form that the constraint admits the Bessel-family solutions $\psi(a,\phi)=J_{\pm i m/2}(ia^{2}/2)e^{im\phi}$ for every integer $m$, that these solutions grow like $e^{a^{2}/2}$ and are non-normalizable in the scale factor, and that the constraint operator—self-adjoint on any Fredholm domain—has index $0$, whereas the chiral Dirac operators of lattice QCD carry index $n_{R}-n_{L}=Q\neq 0$. We further show that $E=0$ is not an isolated spectral point of the constraint, so protection cannot be defined; the contrast with a gapped Jackiw–Rebbi zero mode, whose pair splitting scales as $e^{-L/\xi}$ (numerically $4.54\times 10^{-5}$ at $L/\xi=10$), is explicit. The analogy fails on all three criteria: the shared "zero-mode" label is nominal. We distill the failure into a four-point checklist and propose the calculation that would falsify our conclusion.

## 1. Introduction

Researchers in quantum gravity and in topological quantum computing face a shared and expensive problem: the word "zero mode" is used for objects with completely different mathematical structures, and analogies built on the shared word can steer research programs and resources. A quantum cosmologist who believes the Wheeler–DeWitt constraint's kernel is a Majorana-like protected state expects a robust, deformation-stable object with an operational signature; a topological-qubit engineer who imports that expectation into condensed-matter design space imports a promise of decoherence-free behavior that the gravitational side cannot cash. Today the conjecture stands untested: no work in the minisuperspace literature checks the Wheeler–DeWitt kernel against the defining properties of a Majorana zero mode, and no work extracts an observable from it comparable to the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ observables proposed for Majorana systems [12, 13]. The decision that changes if our result holds is the decision to stop treating the constraint kernel as a candidate protected state and instead to treat zero-mode analogies as hypotheses requiring a criterion-by-criterion test.

The stakes are concrete. In lattice QCD, topological zero-mode wavefunctions are operationally valuable precisely because they are protected by an index theorem and their residues are computable: matching the poles in $1/m^{2}$ of finite-volume correlators extracts the low-energy constants of QCD, including the pion decay constant [1, 3, 7]. In topological superconductors, field-free Majorana zero-energy excitations are sought as qubit-relevant states because topology protects them against local perturbations. In disordered topological chains, the zero-mode wavefunction's decay profile alone fixes the ac transport scaling $\sigma(\omega)\sim\log\omega$ at criticality [6]. In each case the zero mode earns its role through structure: a symmetry, an index, a gap. The question this paper answers is whether the Wheeler–DeWitt kernel has any of this structure.

We answer it by a structural test. We take the best-studied minisuperspace with an exactly solvable constraint—the Hawking–Page wormhole model, whose Wheeler–DeWitt Hamiltonian admits a $U(1,1)$ hidden symmetry and an infinite tower of eigenstates [2]—and we check the constraint's kernel against the three defining criteria of a Majorana zero mode in the Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) setting: (i) particle–hole (self-conjugate) symmetry, $\mathcal{C}\hat{H}\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-\hat{H}$ with $\mathcal{C}^{2}=\pm 1$; (ii) a non-zero Fredholm/chiral index of an off-diagonal (Dirac-type) operator; (iii) spectral isolation of $E=0$ with protection quantified by exponentially small splitting under deformations. Each criterion is evaluated with explicit derivations, and each fails. The failure is itself informative: it converts a verbal analogy into a checklist that any future "zero-mode bridge" between quantum cosmology and topological quantum computing must pass.

The contributions of this paper are:

- We verify, by direct substitution with every arithmetic step shown, that the Hawking–Page constraint admits the Bessel-family solutions $\psi=J_{\pm i m/2}(ia^{2}/2)e^{im\phi}$ and that these solutions grow as $e^{a^{2}/2}$ at large scale factor, so the kernel is not a space of normalizable bound states (Section 4.1).
- We prove that the Wheeler–DeWitt constraint operator, being self-adjoint on any Fredholm domain, has Fredholm index $0$, while the chiral Dirac operators whose zero modes power the lattice-QCD program carry index $n_{R}-n_{L}=Q$; the index mechanism is therefore absent in principle, not merely in this model (Section 4.2).
- We show that $E=0$ is not an isolated spectral point of the constraint, so topological protection is undefined; we quantify what protection would require by computing the normalized Jackiw–Rebbi zero mode (norm $2\xi$ for $m_{0}\xi=1$) and its pair splitting $e^{-L/\xi}=4.54\times 10^{-5}$ at $L/\xi=10$ (Section 4.3).
- We compile the three criteria, plus an operational-signature criterion drawn from the Zitterbewegung $\mathbb{Z}_2$ proposal [12, 13], into a checklist and apply it to six zero-mode systems in the literature, showing that the Wheeler–DeWitt kernel is the only one that fails all criteria (Section 5).
- We state the falsification condition for our result and propose the smallest calculation that would test it: a search for a Dirac-type factorization of a minisuperspace constraint with an antiunitary particle–hole symmetry on a positive-definite kinematical Hilbert space (Section 7).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The literature relevant to this paper falls into three groups: works in which topological zero modes have precise structure and operational payoff, works that solve the Wheeler–DeWitt constraint in minisuperspace, and works that propose operational zero-mode observables.

**Zero modes with structure and payoff.** In lattice QCD, the zero modes of the Dirac operator in gauge fields of non-trivial topology are protected by the index theorem: in a chiral basis $\not{D}=\begin{pmatrix}0 & D_{L}\\ D_{R} & 0\end{pmatrix}$, the index theorem reads $n_{R}-n_{L}=Q$, and generic configurations carry $Q$ right-handed and no left-handed zero modes [5]. The same work shows these modes survive a thermodynamic deformation: zero modes present at $\mu=0$ due to index theorems remain at nonzero chemical potential, with the dyon zero-mode center growing like $\mu^{4}$ and the zero separation like $1/\mu$ [5]. This persistence under deformation is exactly the protection property our test checks for in the Wheeler–DeWitt kernel. The zero modes are also computationally exploitable: correlation functions in a fixed topological sector in the $\epsilon$-regime contain poles $C_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)=\mathrm{Res}_{n}/(mV)^{n}+\ldots$ whose residues $\mathrm{Res}_{n}=\lim_{m\to 0}(mV)^{n}C_{\nu}$ are easier to compute than the full correlators, and matching these residues to chiral perturbation theory determines the low-energy couplings of the $\Delta S=1$ weak Hamiltonian [1]. The method has been carried through quantitatively: classical scattering amplitudes of the zero-mode eigenfunctions measured at finite volumes allow extraction of the infinite-volume pion decay constant [3], and a quenched numerical test on lattices of size $16^{4}$ and $24^{4}$ at $\beta=5.8458$ and $6.0735$ with $Z_{A}=1.710$ and $1.505$ matched three-point correlators of two pseudoscalar densities and a weak operator to NLO $\epsilon$-regime predictions, finding $g_{1}^{-}$ significantly affected by higher-order chiral corrections at the volumes considered [7]. The common thread—index protection plus a computable residue structure—is what the Wheeler–DeWitt kernel lacks, as Sections 4.2 and 4.3 show.
