## Research idea

Transmon Leakage as an Empirical Test of Digital Physics. The conjecture: if 'it from bit' (digital ontology) were fundamental, a transmon would natively confine to two levels with no leakage; instead, leakage rates measure the cost of projecting an infinite-dimensional continuous bosonic Hilbert space onto a discrete qubit. This reframes leakage not as an engineering nuisance but as quantitative evidence that continuous degrees of freedom (phase, charge) are ontologically prior to discrete bits, while fermionic spins naturally match the digital map. Test: derive a scaling law relating leakage probability and error-correction overhead to the truncation dimension and anharmonicity E_J/E_C of the cosine Hamiltonian, then compare predicted overhead against measured surface-code leakage budgets across qubit modalities (transmon vs. fluxonium vs. cat qubits vs. spin qubits). A formal analysis could bound how much entropy the forced 2D projection generates per gate, predicting that bosonic platforms systematically exceed fermionic ones—falsifiable via existing experimental data.
