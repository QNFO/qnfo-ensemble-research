## Research idea

Positional Notation as an Ultrametric Tree of Cyclic Time. The claim: positional notation is not merely a number encoding but a rooted ultrametric tree whose levels encode nested cyclic time (hour within day within week within month), so numerals natively represent time hierarchies that the 'silent' decimal default flattens into an Archimedean line. Mixed-radix historical systems (Babylonian sexagesimal, Mayan 20/18) are cited as evidence that bases once declared observed periodicities. Why it matters: it would give a formal geometry for calendar-structured time-series data, linking p-adic/symbolic ultrametrics (Holly; Böcker–Dress) to temporal hierarchy, and reframe zero as the marked unmarked state (contra Manca's zero-free representations). How to test: (1) prove that mixed-radix cyclic numeral systems induce a symbolic ultrametric whose distance equals depth of least common ancestor in the calendar tree; (2) simulate time-series clustering under this 'time tree' metric versus linear metrics, measuring whether calendar-aligned periodicities are recovered more efficiently; (3) analyze whether logarithmic number compression (Dehaene) corresponds to ultrametric grouping by the notation's own tree.
