# The Pythagorean Semigroup $\{2^a 3^b 5^c\}$ as a Candidate Common Lattice of the Bruhat–Tits Trees $\mathcal{T}_2$, $\mathcal{T}_3$, $\mathcal{T}_5$: Structure, Statistics, and a Critical Assessment

## Abstract

A conjecture circulating in the adelic cross-domain program holds that the product of the Bruhat–Tits trees $\mathcal{T}_2 \times \mathcal{T}_3 \times \mathcal{T}_5$, through its diagonal embedding into the Pythagorean semigroup $P = \{2^a \cdot 3^b \cdot 5^c\}$, underlies Standard Model mass ratios, GKP quantum-error-correction lattice spacings, holographic bulk geometry, and Efimov log-periodic scaling. We subject the statistical core of this conjecture to explicit computation. We exactly enumerate $P \cap [1,1000]$ (86 elements), derive the asymptotic density $dN/d\ln x \approx (\ln x)^2/(2\ln 2 \ln 3 \ln 5)$, and compute the probability that a random physical ratio lands within $\pm 2\%$ of some element of $P$. The match probability crosses 50% already at $x \approx 677$, so above hadronic scales a fit to $P$ is nearly guaranteed and carries little evidential weight; eleven low-scale ratios give a naive joint probability $\approx 1.3 \times 10^{-6}$, inflated to $\approx 1.4 \times 10^{-5}$ by a look-elsewhere correction over prime triples. We further show the Efimov scaling factor $e^{\pi/s_0} \approx 22.69$ differs from $3^3 = 27$ by 5.3% in the log-period, a concrete tension. We propose discriminating tests based on small-exponent structure rather than raw value matching.

## 1. Introduction

The Bruhat–Tits tree $\mathcal{T}_p$ of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is an infinite $(p+1)$-regular graph with no cycles; it is the standard $p$-adic analogue of hyperbolic space and the geometric stage for $p$-adic AdS/CFT [5], [6]. A conjecture in the adelic cross-domain program [9], [11], [12] proposes that the product of the trees for the primes 2, 3, and 5, via the diagonal embedding of $\mathcal{T}_2 \times \mathcal{T}_3 \times \mathcal{T}_5$ into the multiplicative semigroup

$$P = \{2^a \cdot 3^b \cdot 5^c \;:\; a,b,c \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\},$$

is a single geometric structure manifesting in four apparently unrelated places: (i) Standard Model (SM) mass ratios, claimed to match elements of $P$ within about 2%; (ii) lattice spacings of GKP (Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill) quantum-error-correcting codes, indexed by the $p$-adic valuation $\mathrm{ord}_p(k)$; (iii) holographic bulk geometry on $\mathcal{T}_p$ [10]; and (iv) the log-periodic scaling of Efimov trimers.

This paper has two goals. First, we consolidate the geometric side of the conjecture: we make precise what the diagonal embedding is and how it connects to the existing Bruhat–Tits literature. Second, and more importantly, we perform the statistical test proposed in the research program — assessing whether 11 or more mass ratios matching $P$ within $\sim 2\%$ exceeds chance, including a control over random prime triples — with every arithmetic step shown. The outcome is deliberately two-sided: the combinatorial structure of $P$ is genuinely restrictive at small scales, but its density grows so quickly that value-matching above $x \approx 677$ is a near-certainty. Any claim that SM mass ratios "fit" $P$ must therefore be evaluated against the correct null hypothesis, which we construct here. We also compute the Efimov log-period against $\ln 27$ and find a 5.3% discrepancy that the conjecture must address.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The Bruhat–Tits tree of a local field $k$ is the building of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)$; its automorphism group and subgroup structure are classical. We review the literature in four clusters.

**Structure and orders on the tree.** Reference [1] develops the theory of "branches" of orders: for a local field $k$ and the matrix algebra $A = M_2(k)$, the set of maximal orders containing a given suborder forms a subtree of the Bruhat–Tits tree, used for the global selectivity problem and local embedding computations. This matters for us because the diagonal embedding of $\mathcal{T}_2 \times \mathcal{T}_3 \times \mathcal{T}_5$ into $P$ can be read as a statement about which vertices of the product tree are selected by the multiplicative valuation map $(a,b,c) \mapsto 2^a 3^b 5^c$ — a selectivity problem in the spirit of [1]. Reference [8] proves the classical decomposition theorems for subgroups of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{T}_p) \cong \mathrm{PGL}_2(F)$ and shows they extend to any closed transitive subgroup; this supplies the group-theoretic language in which the "diagonal" subgroup of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_2) \times \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_3) \times \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_5)$ must be understood. Reference [7] constructs transfer-function analogues ("rational maps") from Bruhat–Tits trees to Bruhat–Tits buildings via $p$-adic colligations, providing a systems-theoretic handle on maps between trees that parallels the valuation map we study. Reference [3] formalises the Bruhat–Tits tree in the Lean theorem prover and verifies a result on harmonic cochains; machine-checked foundations are a prerequisite if the cross-domain conjecture is ever to be promoted from numerology to theorem.

**$p$-adic holography.** Reference [5] establishes that geodesic bulk diagrams on the Bruhat–Tits tree compute $p$-adic global conformal blocks, with the boundary described by $p$-adic numbers; this is the precise sense in which $\mathcal{T}_p$ is a bulk geometry, and it is the hook on which the holographic leg of the conjecture [10] hangs. Reference [4] integrates out the interior of the tree for a spinor field theory and finds a boundary theory very similar to a scalar field theory over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, reinforcing the bulk–boundary dictionary. Reference [6] computes effective actions on two subspaces of the tree and shows that the boundary limits coincide with the same $p$-adic conformal field theory — directly relevant because the diagonal embedding selects a subspace of the product tree, and [6] suggests a criterion for when such subspaces retain a universal boundary limit. Reference [2] transplants the BTZ black hole onto the Bruhat–Tits tree via a novel $p$-adic "exponential function" and evaluates $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ Wilson lines on the analogue connection; a $p$-adic exponential is exactly the object one would need to relate additive tree distance to the multiplicative semigroup $P$, so [2] is the closest existing construction to the embedding conjecture.

**The cross-domain program.** Reference [9] states the key claim: $P = \{2^a 3^b 5^c\}$ is simultaneously the SM mass spectrum, the GKP code lattice, the Efimov discretuum, and the diagonal embedding of $\mathcal{T}_{2,3,5}$, with a calibration register of 15 entries. Reference [11] is the v5.0 synthesis mapping the SM architecture onto Bruhat–Tits geometry, including the fine-structure constant and RG flow. Reference [10] establishes bosonic quantum error correction as RG fixed-point subspaces on Bruhat–Tits trees and argues the structure is holographic, with tensor networks on $\mathcal{T}_p$ as the holographic code.
