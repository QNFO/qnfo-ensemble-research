# The Gödelian Boundary as a Generative Phase Transition: Optimal Operating Points for Constrained Cognitive Systems

## Abstract

Cognitive systems that reason about themselves—self-critiquing language models, formal reasoners that prove statements about their own beliefs, and adaptive controllers that rewrite their own policies—face a concrete trade-off: increasing self-referential depth raises the rate of novel, useful outputs but also the rate of incoherent ones, and neither pure regime is usable. Existing treatments name the two limits involved, a Gödelian boundary where self-reference generates paradox and a metabolic boundary where energy cost binds, but supply no quantitative account of where the optimal operating point sits between them or what bounds how long a system can dwell there. This paper formalizes the trade-off with a Gödelian proximity $\Gamma$, a Landau-type coherence functional $\Psi(\Gamma)=1-\Gamma^{2}$, a power-law novelty rate $N(\Gamma)=N_{0}\Gamma^{\alpha}$, and a quadratic metabolic cost $P(\Gamma)=P_{0}\Gamma^{2}$, all stated as explicit premises. Analytic derivations yield: the utility-maximizing proximity $\Gamma^{*}=\sqrt{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}$, which for $\alpha=2$ gives $\Gamma^{*}=1/\sqrt{2}\approx0.7071$ and maximum utility $0.25\,N_{0}$; critical-slowing-down and avalanche signatures $\tau=100\,\tau_{0}$ at distance $0.01$ from the boundary and $\langle s\rangle=100$ at branching ratio $m=0.99$; an excursion-duration bound $\tau_{\text{exc}}=24.39\,t_{0}$ for a representative energy budget; a proof that under a power ceiling $\bar{P}=0.3\,P_{0}$ the globally optimal strategy is a single setpoint $\Gamma=\sqrt{0.3}\approx0.5477$ with utility $0.21\,N_{0}$, a $2.56\times$ improvement over permanent conservatism and a correction of a previously published duty-cycle result; and a Landauer-scale floor $E_{\min}=2.967\times10^{-21}\,\text{J}$ per bit of sustained novelty at $T=310\,\text{K}$. The results convert a heuristic tuning problem into a measurement problem: designers can monitor relaxation time and avalanche statistics to steer toward an analytically defined optimum while respecting energy constraints.

## 1. Introduction

Teams building self-improving AI systems, automated theorem provers, and neuro-inspired adaptive controllers face the same operational failure every day. Pushing a self-referential system—one that critiques, rewrites, or proves statements about its own reasoning—toward deeper self-reference raises the rate of genuinely new results, but simultaneously raises the rate of confident nonsense: proofs of false statements, hallucinated citations, or oscillating control signals. Pulling the system back to shallow self-reference yields stability but sterility, re-deriving what is already known. Today this trade-off is managed by hand-tuned thresholds, safety filters, and temperature knobs, with no principled answer to the question a system designer actually asks: **how close to logical inconsistency should I let my system operate, and for how long?** The cost of getting it wrong is paid twice—lost discovery when the system is too conservative, and wasted human-review effort when it is too aggressive. A result that identifies where the optimum lies, what observable signature it leaves, and what bounds the duration of safe excursions would convert this tuning problem into a measurement problem: designers would monitor a small set of observables and steer toward a computed target rather than guess.

This paper argues that the optimum is a phase-transition problem. The claim is that a cognitive system's optimal operating point lies near a critical boundary—a region where self-referential logical complexity approaches inconsistency—and that controlled excursions into this region generate new axioms and ideas, while the thermodynamic cost of sustaining reduced coherence bounds the duration of such excursions. The framing treats creativity as bounded risk-taking between two failure modes: decoherence into hallucination on one side and stagnation on the other. The two-boundary picture—a Gödelian boundary and a metabolic boundary jointly constraining any self-referential system—is adopted from the resonant-equilibrium framework [9], which also characterizes Gödelian failures operationally as a form of leakage into incoherence when a resonant system is forced to process a paradoxical self-referential state.

All quantitative results in this paper are consequences of four explicitly stated premises (Section 3.1); no empirical measurement, simulation, or survey was performed for this work, and every number is derived with shown arithmetic in Section 4.

The contributions are analytical:

- **C1.** Derive the utility-maximizing Gödelian proximity $\Gamma^{*}=\sqrt{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}$ for a quadratic coherence functional and power-law novelty rate, and evaluate it at $\Gamma^{*}=1/\sqrt{2}\approx0.7071$ with utility $0.25\,N_{0}$ (Section 4.1).
- **C2.** Derive the critical-slowing-down signature $\tau=\tau_{0}|\Gamma-\Gamma_{c}|^{-\nu}$ with $\nu=1$, giving $\tau=100\,\tau_{0}$ at distance $0.01$ from the boundary, and the branching-process avalanche law $\langle s\rangle=1/(1-m)$, giving $\langle s\rangle=100$ at $m=0.99$ (Section 4.2).
- **C3.** Derive the metabolic duration bound $\tau_{\text{exc}}=E_{\text{budget}}/[P_{0}(\Gamma_{e}^{2}-\Gamma_{h}^{2})]$, and evaluate it at $24.39\,t_{0}$ for the stated parameters (Section 4.3).
- **C4.** Correct the previously published duty-cycle claim: under a power ceiling $\bar{P}=0.3\,P_{0}$ the globally optimal strategy is a **single setpoint** $\Gamma=\sqrt{0.3}\approx0.5477$ with utility $0.21\,N_{0}$, a $2.56\times$ improvement over permanent conservatism; the duty-cycled schedule ($f=0.51220$, $\bar{U}=0.168\,N_{0}$, $2.05\times$) is retained as a suboptimal benchmark (Section 4.4).
- **C5.** Derive a Landauer-scale floor $E_{\min}=k_{B}T\ln2=2.967\times10^{-21}\,\text{J}$ per bit of sustained novelty at $T=310\,\text{K}$ (Section 4.5).
- **C6.** Provide a sensitivity analysis for a quartic correction to the coherence functional, showing with shown arithmetic that the interior optimum migrates toward larger $\Gamma$ as the quartic coefficient $\beta$ grows, reaches the boundary $\Gamma=1$ at $\beta=1/3$, and disappears beyond it (Section 4.6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The literature that informs the present model falls into several strands. Each is cited here for a specific point its text makes; the functional forms used in Section 3 remain explicit premises of this paper, and Section 2 closes by stating precisely what the cited works do and do not justify.

**Emergent symbol systems.** The survey of symbol emergence in cognitive developmental systems [1] argues that symbols "are not given by a designer but instead they emerge and dynamically evolve through social interaction," and its conclusion distinguishes symbol systems in society from agent-centric, internal representation-based symbol systems, calling for the two aspects to be treated together. This supports treating the degree of self-referential symbol processing as an emergent, gradually adjustable variable of an agent rather than a fixed design constant, which is the role the control variable $\Gamma$ plays in Section 3.

**An engineered exploration dial.** The cognitive-diversity radar target-classification framework [2] is built on the premise that "the sensor parameters to be transmitted should be variable so that they can be altered to acquire the most useful information." Its results show that varying the angular diversity $\Delta\theta$ and using both processing channels outperforms single-perspective classification ($\Delta\theta=0$) and also outperforms a fixed-number multi-perspective scheme (two perspectives at $\Delta\theta=5^{\circ}$ reached $85\%$, while the cognitive framework with a median of $3$ perspectives did better). This is a concrete engineered instance of the central claim shape of this paper: an intermediate, actively chosen degree of exploratory diversity beats both zero diversity and an indiscriminate fixed setting, which is the behavioral signature the utility curve $U(\Gamma)=N_{0}\Gamma^{\alpha}(1-\Gamma^{2})$ is built to capture.
