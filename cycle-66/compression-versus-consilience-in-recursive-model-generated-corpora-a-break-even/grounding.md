## Research idea

Synthetic Data Compression Versus Emergent Consilience in Recursive Model-Generated Corpora. Claim: training on model-generated data is lossy compression of high-dimensional real-world structure, so recursive synthetic-data training should monotonically shrink tail entropy and effective dimensionality, yielding 'shallower' inference. Counter-conjecture from the author's own 800+ LLM-written papers: large synthetic corpora can still yield novel cross-disciplinary consilience that no human-scale research effort would find. Research question: can emergent cross-domain link structure compensate for per-sample compression, or does compression inevitably dominate? Testable program: (1) derive information-theoretic bounds on tail-entropy and effective-dimension decay per generation of model-on-model training; (2) simulate recursive fine-tuning loops, measuring tail-event generalization and a consilience metric (density of unexpected cross-domain associations in embedding or citation-graph space) across generations; (3) compare pure synthetic versus real-anchored hybrid pipelines to identify conditions—diversity enforcement, formal verification, periodic re-anchoring—under which consilience gains offset compression losses, or prove that they cannot.
