# Thoughts as Quantum-Informational Hamiltonians: Coherence and Capacity Constraints on a Two-Phase Pre-Geometric Model of Cognition

## Abstract

A standing puzzle in neuroscience and quantum biology is whether any quantum degree of freedom in a warm, wet brain can remain coherent long enough to play a computational role, and no existing framework connects such receptor-scale coherence to any proposal about how spacetime itself emerges. We formalize the conjecture that cognition is a quantum-informational Hamiltonian state coupling a physical universe to a pre-geometric phase, with the Big Bang modeled as a pre-geometric-to-geometric phase transition that selects a Hamiltonian subspace. We build a two-phase model in which pre-geometric degrees of freedom are informational, the phase transition fixes which "thought" states can manifest, and biological receptors (microtubule-like, Posner-like, magnetoreception-like structures) are treated as candidate channels to the selected subspace. Using a thermal decoherence criterion, we derive that at an assumed physiological temperature of $T = 310$ K the thermal decoherence rate is $\Gamma_{\mathrm{th}} = 4.06 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ ($\tau_{\mathrm{th}} = 2.46 \times 10^{-14}$ s), that a receptor transition of $0.2$ eV supports only $1.19$ coherent cycles, that even a $10$ eV transition supports at most $59.6$ cycles, and that any conjectured coupling to the pre-geometric subspace must carry an energy scale above $k_B T = 26.7$ meV to avoid being driven classical. The framework yields falsifiable coherence-time and channel-capacity targets that distinguish it from standard neuroscience.

## 1. Introduction

Neuroscientists, quantum biologists, and theorists of quantum gravity all face the same unresolved question from different sides: does quantum coherence play any functional role in cognition, and if so, through what physical channel? For the experimentalist, the question costs money and time, because candidate "quantum receptors" such as microtubule-like structures, Posner-like molecules, and magnetoreception-like radical-pair systems are repeatedly proposed and repeatedly challenged on the ground that a thermal bath at body temperature destroys coherence in femtoseconds to picoseconds. For the theorist, the question blocks progress on a deeper issue: if consciousness is informational rather than mechanical, physics currently offers no formalism in which an informational state couples to the substrate from which spacetime emerges. Today the two communities cannot even agree on a shared quantity to measure, because no model specifies what a receptor would have to do — at what coherence time, at what channel capacity, at what coupling strength — for a quantum-informational account of cognition to survive.

This paper supplies that specification for one specific conjecture. The conjecture, stated as a hypothesis and not as an established result, is that cognition is not an ordinary physical process but a quantum-informational (Hamiltonian) state that couples a physical universe to a pre-geometric phase, with the Big Bang interpreted as a pre-geometric-to-geometric phase transition that "froze" which thoughts can manifest. The value of formalizing it is decision-theoretic even if the conjecture is false: the formalization produces numerical thresholds — a decoherence time, a coherent-cycle count, a channel capacity, a minimum coupling energy — that any candidate receptor must meet, and those thresholds can be checked in existing laboratory setups. If the thresholds are met by no plausible receptor, the two-phase conjecture is falsified at the receptor level and quantum biology can proceed without it; if some receptor meets them, a specific experimental program follows.

The pre-geometric side of the model is not built from nothing. A body of work already treats spacetime geometry as emergent from a non-metric informational substrate: pre-geometric gauge theories in which a Higgs-like field spontaneously breaks a spacetime symmetry and recovers Einstein–Cartan gravity in the infrared [5], and a monistic framework in which reality is a pre-geometric informational substrate structured as a Hamiltonian superposition satisfying $H|\Psi\rangle = 0$, with geometry emerging through an exact functor from information states to events [10]. On the quantum-informational side, phase transitions in quantum many-body systems have been shown to carry operational significance for information processing, so that the phase a system lands in changes what computational processes its ground state supports [2], and the information capacity of quantum communication is bounded by natural physical assumptions on the emitting devices [3]. The gap this paper fills is the coupling: none of these works derives what a biological receptor at $310$ K would have to satisfy to act as a channel between a geometric phase and a pre-geometric informational subspace.

We treat the receptor problem with the standard tool that decides whether an interaction is quantum or classical: the comparison of decoherence rates with the frequencies that define the strength of an interaction Hamiltonian. Analysis of quantum feedback shows that an interaction Hamiltonian's dynamics can be interpreted as a completely classical information exchange when the decoherence rates are comparable to the interaction frequencies, and that the separable-versus-entangling character of an interaction is quantified by the decoherence rates associated with information available in the environment [4]. We apply exactly this criterion, with the thermal bath playing the role of the environment.

The contributions are:

- A two-phase formal model (Section 3) in which a pre-geometric informational Hilbert space, a geometric phase, and a transition-selected Hamiltonian subspace are related, drawing on the spontaneous-symmetry-breaking recovery of gravity [5] and the $H|\Psi\rangle = 0$ substrate structure [10].
- A derived thermal decoherence scale for candidate receptors at $T = 310$ K, $\Gamma_{\mathrm{th}} = 4.06 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{th}} = 2.46 \times 10^{-14}$ s, with full arithmetic (Section 4, D1).
- A derived coherent-cycle bound: a receptor transition of energy $E$ supports $N_{\mathrm{cyc}} = E/(2\pi k_B T)$ coherent cycles, giving $1.19$ cycles at $E = 0.2$ eV and $59.6$ cycles at $E = 10$ eV (Section 4, D2 and D5).
- A derived channel-capacity bound of $3.09$ bits per mode for a $0.2$ eV receptor under a Shannon-type thermal-noise assumption, cross-checked against a dimension bound of $\log_2 d$ bits (Section 4, D3).
- A derived minimum coupling energy for the conjectured pre-geometric coupling, $\hbar\Omega \geq k_B T = 26.7$ meV, below which the coupling is classical in the sense of the feedback criterion [4] (Section 4, D4).
- A derived thermodynamic cost of digital translation at error rate $\alpha_r = 10^{-4}$, $R = 0.246$ eV per event using the constant $R = k_B T \ln(1/\alpha_r)$ of the Rosetta framework [11], which is of the same order as a receptor vibrational quantum (Section 4, D6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The informational reconstruction of quantum theory provides the interpretive stance of this paper. The introduction to the volume "Quantum Theory: Informational Foundations and Foils" distinguishes the dynamicist tradition, in which physics describes natural dynamical behaviour without reference to agents, from the pragmatist tradition, in which the laws of physics are characterized by the extent to which we can learn and control physical systems, and documents that the recent foundational wave has shifted from interpreting quantum theory to reconstructing it from physical principles [1]. Our model adopts the pragmatist stance deliberately: "thought" is defined operationally, as a state distinguished by what information-processing it supports, not by a dynamicist story about mental substance. This choice matters because the two-phase conjecture is otherwise vulnerable to category errors in which informational and mechanical descriptions are conflated.

That phase transitions have computational consequences is the key existing result supporting the idea that a phase transition can "freeze" which thoughts manifest. In the transverse-field Ising model with Hamiltonian $H_I(g) = -\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\sigma_i^x \sigma_{i+1}^x + g \sigma_i^z)$, two quantum phases are separated by a critical point at $g = 1$, and the study of differential local convertibility of ground states gives direct operational significance to the phase transition for quantum information processing, with the authors connecting their analysis to adiabatic quantum computation, where a Hamiltonian $(1-s)H_0 + s H_p$ is evolved by varying $s$ [2]. This is precisely the structure our conjecture needs at cosmological scale: a parameter crossing a critical value changes what the ground state can computationally support. We generalize from their spin-chain critical point to a hypothesized pre-geometric-to-geometric transition, and we flag that generalization as the model's central unproven step.

The capacity side of the model rests on bounds from semi-device-independent quantum information. In the prepare-and-measure scenario, a sender encodes classical data into quantum states $\rho_x$ and a receiver decodes with measurements yielding correlations $p(b|x,y) = \mathrm{tr}(\rho_x M_{b|y})$; with no restriction on the states any correlation is simulable, so an assumption such as a fixed Hilbert-space dimension $d$ is needed to limit achievable correlations, and the information assumption is identified as a relaxation of other natural assumptions, corresponding to a limitation on one-shot accessible information [3]. We use this to bound what a receptor of given dimension can carry: the dimension assumption directly yields the $\log_2 d$ bit bound we cross-check in Section 4.

The quantum-versus-classical criterion that decides our central result comes from the analysis of interaction Hamiltonians simulated by quantum feedback. That work shows the decoherence master equation $\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho} = \gamma(\hat{X}\hat{\rho}\hat{X} - \frac{1}{2}\hat{X}^2\hat{\rho} - \frac{1}{2}\hat{\rho}\hat{X}^2)$ for coupling rate $\gamma$, and concludes that the dynamics of an interaction Hamiltonian can be interpreted as a completely classical information exchange when decoherence rates are comparable to the frequencies defining the interaction strength, with the separable-versus-entangling difference expressed quantitatively through those decoherence rates [4]. Our coupling threshold in D4 is this criterion applied with the thermal decoherence rate $\Gamma_{\mathrm{th}}$ in the role of $\gamma$.

The pre-geometric phase is given concrete Hamiltonian form by recent work on emergent gravity. In pre-geometric gauge theories of the Yang–Mills type, neither the spacetime metric nor the tetrads are assumed to exist in the unbroken phase; the field content is a gauge field $A_\mu^{AB}$ of $SO(1,4)$ or $SO(3,2)$ plus a Higgs-like scalar $\phi^A$, and once $\phi^A$ acquires a nonzero vacuum expectation value the symmetry breaking reduces the gauge group to the Lorentz group $SO(1,3)$, recovering Einstein–Cartan theory with a cosmological constant term; the Hamiltonian analysis confirms that canonical general relativity is recovered in the infrared limit of the spontaneously broken phase, with the Lagrangian mapping under SSB to $k_W v^3 m^2 \epsilon_{abcd}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}e_\mu^a e_\nu^b R_{\rho\sigma}^{cd} \pm 48 k_W v^3 m^4 e$ [5]. This supplies the geometric phase of our two-phase model with an explicit mechanism: geometry is the broken phase, and the transition is spontaneous symmetry breaking rather than a mere change of description.

Decoherence itself we model with the non-Hermitian effective-Hamiltonian formalism. Non-Hermitian operators are traditionally employed in the effective description of systems displaying decay or dissipation, a generic non-Hermitian operator has complex eigenvalues whose imaginary part may be associated with decay rates, and the class of pseudo-Hermitian and $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$-symmetric operators with real spectra can describe unitary systems once a suitable inner product is defined [6]. We use the decaying branch: in Section 3 the thermal bath is represented by an anti-Hermitian part $-i\Gamma_{\mathrm{th}}/2$ of an effective Hamiltonian, which is legitimate precisely because the imaginary part of a non-Hermitian eigenvalue encodes a decay rate [6].

The selection of which informational states survive the transition we model as an information bottleneck. The information bottleneck extracts information $T$ with respect to a system $Y$ from a system $X$ by minimizing $I(T:X) - \beta I(T:Y)$, achieving irreversible compression that keeps relevant and discards unessential information; the quantum generalization with a classical-quantum joint state $\rho_{XY} = \sum_x P_X(x)|x\rangle\langle x| \otimes \rho_{Y|x}$ has been given an accelerated algorithm whose convergence is proven and whose numerics show that a quantum bottleneck variable $T$ has an advantage over a classical one even when $X$ and $Y$ are classical [7]. In our model, the phase transition acts as a bottleneck on the pre-geometric plenum: the subspace of "manifestable thoughts" is the compressed representation $T$.

The QNFO corpus supplies the closest existing statements of the conjecture and its limits. The monistic-reality framework posits that reality is fundamentally a pre-geometric informational substrate structured as a Hamiltonian superposition in a universal Hilbert space satisfying $H|\Psi\rangle = 0$, formalizes the emergence of geometry as a functor $F: \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Ont}} \to \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Geo}}$ that preserves causal structure but is generally not injective, and models perceivers as nodes of a correlation graph whose edges carry mutual information $I(X_p;X_q) = \sum_{x_p,x_q} P(x_p,x_q)\log[P(x_p,x_q)/(P(x_p)P(x_q))]$ [10]. Our paper takes that architecture as its hypothesis and does what the framework itself does not: it derives numerical constraints under which a biological perceiver node could couple to the ontologic-information domain at all. The geometric-unity-of-computation work contributes the category-theoretic machinery for self-referential frameworks, constructing a reflexive object $R \cong [R \to R]$ in a category of formal frameworks and applying Lawvere's fixed-point theorem so that a framework can describe its own descriptive apparatus consistently [8]; we use this in Section 3 to argue that the two-phase model's description of thought is not viciously self-referential, since the fixed point exists by construction. The wave-harmonics textbook contributes the reading of the Hamiltonian as a wave-dispersion object: it derives the Schrödinger equation from classical energy conservation applied to a wave-like reality, with $\hbar$ as the universal scaling factor linking wave frequency to discretely exchanged energy [9]; this supports our treatment of a "thought Hamiltonian" as a frequency-structured object whose cycles, not particles, are the countable resource. Finally, the Rosetta framework warns against over-digitalizing the substrate: it proves the functor $F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}$ from symplectic physical dynamics to computational state spaces is non-exact by Gromov's non-squeezing theorem, so symplectic capacity is an invariant with no image in the discrete target, and it defines Rosetta's Constant $R = k_B T \ln(1/\alpha_r)$ as the thermodynamic cost of translation between the two [11]. Our D6 computation uses that constant, and our interpretation of the phase transition as selecting a subspace respects the non-exactness result by keeping the pre-geometric phase continuous rather than discretizing it.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Model overview

We posit two phases and a transition.

**Phase P (pre-geometric).** An informational Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{P}}$ with Hamiltonian constraint $H_{\mathrm{P}}|\Psi\rangle = 0$, following the monistic-reality architecture in which information states exist ontologically independently of any geometric realization [10]. States $|\Psi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{P}}$ are candidate "thoughts" in the operational sense of [1]: they are defined by the information-processing they support, not by a dynamical story.

**Phase G (geometric).** The broken phase in which a metric structure exists. We take the mechanism from pre-geometric gravity: a Higgs-like field acquiring a vacuum expectation value spontaneously breaks the pre-geometric symmetry and recovers Einstein–Cartan gravity in the infrared [5]. The transition $P \to G$ is modeled as this symmetry breaking, and — this is the conjecture under test — it acts as an information bottleneck [7] that selects a Hamiltonian subspace $\mathcal{K} \subset \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{P}}$: only states in $\mathcal{K}$ are "manifestable" in the geometric phase.
