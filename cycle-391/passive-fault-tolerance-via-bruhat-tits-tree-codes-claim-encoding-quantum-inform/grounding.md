## Research idea

Passive Fault Tolerance via Bruhat-Tits Tree Codes. Claim: encoding quantum information on Bruhat-Tits trees (p-adic ultrametric geometry) yields passive fault tolerance — errors are geometrically confined to local branches and cannot accumulate — enabling depolarizing error thresholds of ~75% (versus ~1% for surface codes), operation at 4 K on pulse-tube cryocoolers, and zero syndrome measurements, thereby avoiding the Carnot-limited millikelvin cooling wall of active error correction. Why it matters: if true, it reframes scalability as a thermodynamic/geometric problem rather than an engineering one, and makes falsifiable predictions testable cheaply. How to test: derive the threshold rigorously by mapping the tree code's logical error rate under local depolarizing noise (compare against surface-code thresholds via standard formalism); simulate error propagation on finite Bruhat-Tits trees (Monte Carlo over branch-confined error chains) to verify confinement and threshold scaling; and analyze the heat budget to confirm that passive correction removes the 20,000-fold cooling gap constraint.
