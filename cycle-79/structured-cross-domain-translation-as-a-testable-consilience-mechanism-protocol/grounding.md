## Research idea

Structured Cross-Domain Translation as a Testable Consilience Mechanism. The author conjectures that systematically translating a single formal object (theorem, model, or framework) into multiple domain lexicons—physics, computer science, cognitive science, information theory—and forcing a unified 'meta-principle' synthesis produces genuine consilience: convergent insights that no single-domain analysis yields. This matters because it treats interdisciplinary analogy-making as a reproducible, structured procedure rather than ad hoc metaphor, potentially measurable as a research-ideation method. A computational paper could test it: run LLMs with and without the structured translation protocol on a benchmark of theorems/concepts, then evaluate outputs for (a) novelty of cross-domain hypotheses, (b) expert-rated validity of the analogies, and (c) whether the 'synthesis' step predicts later-confirmed connections. Formal analysis could also ask when such structural analogies are truth-preserving versus spurious, defining conditions under which lexicon crosswalks constitute real isomorphism rather than surface similarity.
