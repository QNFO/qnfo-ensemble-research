## Research idea

Cross-Ratios as Universal Invariants of Coarse-Grained Ultrametric Trees. Claim: any system generated by recursive branching (causal sets, phylogeny, language diversification, concept hierarchies) yields an ultrametric space; when the internal tree scaffold is coarse-grained away, the only surviving relational invariants are boundary cross-ratios, so effective 'laws' in such domains should be expressible as cross-ratio (Möbius/conformal) symmetries. Why it matters: it would explain the ubiquity of conformal and projective invariance across physics, biology, and cognition as a logical consequence of hierarchical differentiation rather than coincidence. Test: (1) prove rigorously that cross-ratios are the complete invariant set of ultrametric spaces under monotone rescaling and identify the boundary automorphism group; (2) simulate branching processes (random causal sets, Yule trees), coarse-grain them, and verify that measurable cross-ratios of boundary points remain stable while distances rescale; (3) check empirical ultrametric datasets (phylogenies, semantic embeddings) for cross-ratio-preserved structure, and compare against Lorentz/conformal invariants in physics as the predicted limiting case.
