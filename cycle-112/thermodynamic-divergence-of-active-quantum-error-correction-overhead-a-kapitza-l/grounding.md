## Research idea

Thermodynamic Divergence of Active QEC Overhead via Kapitza-Limited Cooling. The author conjectures that active quantum error correction enters a self-defeating thermodynamic regime: QEC resource overhead scales quadratically while local thermal dissipation from control wiring scales exponentially, so at a critical qubit count the localized heat load exceeds Kapitza conductance of interconnects, causing decoherence regardless of code distance. This defines a 'thermodynamic feasibility limit' for gate-based architectures, implying scalability requires passive topological substrates (fractional Chern insulators) whose intrinsic energy gaps relax states without active feedback. It matters because it reframes the scalability debate from algorithmic overhead to heat-flow physics. A derivation could extend the Caldeira-Leggett model with explicit Kapitza thermal resistance and RF-line heat sources, proving whether the QEC overhead diverges at a finite threshold; Monte Carlo thermal microenvironment simulations of 1,000+ qubit arrays could map micro-Kelvin spikes and locate the breaking point, while comparative error-rate scaling of uncorrected anyonic substrates versus corrected gate systems would test the proposed crossover.
