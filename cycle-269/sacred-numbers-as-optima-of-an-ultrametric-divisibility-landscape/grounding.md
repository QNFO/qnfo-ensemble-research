## Research idea

Sacred Numbers as Optima of an Ultrametric Divisibility Landscape. Claim: cross-culturally recurring 'sacred' numbers (12, 60, 108, 7, 144) are attractors because they maximize an ultrametric fitness functional of the valuation vector v(n) — e.g., high divisor counts and deep p-adic structure relative to magnitude — making them cognitive optima for a mind that tracks divisibility (non-archimedean) alongside magnitude (archimedean). Why it matters: it would give a formal, base-invariant explanation of numerical symbolism, linking p-adic geometry to cognitive science, rather than cultural accident. Test: (1) define candidate fitness scores from valuation vectors (rad(n), divisor density, smoothness, tree depth in the product of Bruhat–Tits trees); (2) simulate agents with dual magnitude/divisibility salience and check whether 12, 60, 108 emerge as stable attractors; (3) correlate predicted optima against ethnographic databases of culturally salient numbers, controlling for magnitude and utility; (4) test human preference for smooth numbers experimentally. A derivation could show the fitness functional is maximized by highly composite numbers with bounded prime support, predicting the observed set.
