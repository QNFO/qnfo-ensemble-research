## Research idea

Monna Map Projection of p-adic Trees as Origin of Quantum Randomness. The author conjectures that quantum-mechanical phenomena—Born-rule randomness, wave-particle duality, Bell nonlocality, decoherence, and even prime distribution—are a single geometric effect: projecting a hierarchical Bruhat-Tits (p-adic) tree structure onto the Archimedean number line via the Monna map. Planck's 1900 choice of the real metric allegedly discarded Hensel's p-adic alternative, so apparent quantum randomness is claimed to be deterministic tree distance scrambled by the wrong ruler (via Shapiro's lemma). This matters because it would unify quantum foundations with number theory and suggest intrinsically fault-tolerant quantum hardware built on tree topologies. Testable routes: (1) derive log-periodic oscillations in the CMB power spectrum and prime-modulated noise in quantum systems from the Monna map and check against data; (2) simulate measurement/decoherence as tree-to-line projection and compare statistics to Born-rule predictions; (3) formally analyze whether Shapiro's lemma reproduces Bell-inequality violations; (4) model threshold behavior in tree-structured gates to assess fault-tolerance claims.
