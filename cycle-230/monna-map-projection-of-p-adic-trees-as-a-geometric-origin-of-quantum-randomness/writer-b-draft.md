# Tree Distances, Wrong Rulers, and the Born Rule: A Quantitative Assessment of the Monna-Map Conjecture for Quantum Randomness

## Abstract

Certified randomness underpins cryptography, Monte Carlo simulation, and sampling, and quantum random number generators (QRNGs) are its commercial workhorse; yet whether quantum randomness is fundamental or emergent remains unresolved, and no quantitative account exists of what happens to statistics when a hierarchical $p$-adic tree is projected onto the real line by the Monna map. This paper supplies such an account for the conjecture that Born-rule randomness is deterministic tree distance scrambled by an Archimedean ruler. We prove-style derive, for $p=2$, that the Monna map is exactly measure-preserving (a ball of radius $2^{-n}$ of Haar measure $2^{-n}$ maps to a dyadic interval of length $2^{-n}$), that shell distances smear to a uniform distribution on $(0,2^{-n}]$, and that the projected ensemble reproduces the Poissonian pair-correlation benchmark $2s$ with relative deviation $s/2 = 0.78125\%$ at $s = 2^{-6}$. We further derive that any measurement-independent deterministic tree model obeys $|S| \le 2$ for the CHSH combination, a factor $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41421356$ below the quantum value $2\sqrt{2}$, and we compute a toy fault-tolerance suppression factor of $33.58$ for a three-branch, six-level repetition encoding at physical error rate $0.01$. The results convert a sweeping conjecture into a bounded, falsifiable research program at TRL 1–2.

## 1. Introduction

Every cryptographic system, Monte Carlo estimate, and sampling-based computation consumes random numbers, and the demand is met today at scale by quantum random number generators (QRNGs), which exploit the intrinsic probabilistic character of quantum mechanics and have advanced to the point where off-the-shelf commercial devices are available and inexpensive [1]. The review in [1] organizes the field around entropy measures such as the Shannon entropy $H(X)=-\sum_{x\in X}P_{X}(x)\log_{2}P_{X}(x)$ and around the distinction between pseudo-random number generators, which are deterministic algorithms, and true random number generators, whose unpredictability comes from physical events [1]. Two problems face the users of these devices. First, certification: the field pursues device independence precisely because one must trust that the physical source, not an unmodeled determinism, supplies the entropy [1]. Second, foundations: the origin of the randomness that QRNGs harvest is postulated, not explained.

The conjecture examined here addresses both problems at once. It holds that quantum-mechanical randomness, wave-particle duality, Bell nonlocality, decoherence, and even prime distribution are a single geometric effect: the projection of a hierarchical Bruhat-Tits ($p$-adic) tree structure onto the Archimedean number line via the Monna map. On this view the choice of the real metric at the foundation of quantum mechanics discarded the $p$-adic alternative, and apparent quantum randomness is deterministic tree distance read with the wrong ruler. If the conjecture holds, the decision that changes is concrete: entropy certification of QRNGs would become a problem of modeling the underlying deterministic tree process rather than testifying to irreducible physical chance, and hardware built on tree topologies might be intrinsically fault-tolerant. If it fails in a specific way, the failure still locates precisely which statistical property of quantum randomness cannot be geometric, which sharpens device-independent certification assumptions.

The literature already contains the ingredients but not the quantitative bridge. On the tree side, $p$-adic Gibbs measures on the Cayley tree exhibit phase transitions that depend only on the prime $p$ [2].
