# The Price of the Digital Abstraction: Entropy Overheads of Imposing Discrete Error Models on Continuous Quantum Systems

## Abstract

Digital quantum error correction encodes quantum information into finite-dimensional, Pauli-stabilized abstractions, yet many leading physical platforms—including the transmon—are natively continuous bosonic systems whose spectra, though quantized, are not naturally binary. We conjecture that forcing a discrete, number-theoretic error abstraction onto such systems incurs a quantifiable overhead that is a direct consequence of the map-territory mismatch between the digital model and the continuous physical system. We formalize this overhead through explicit entropy accounting. Using the radix-correction framework of the Project Rosetta analysis, which identifies the transmon as a qudit of dimension approximately $d \approx 12$ and assigns a base discretization entropy $S_{\text{Base}} = \ln(d/2) \approx 1.79$ nats, we derive: (i) a qubit embedding overhead of $11.6\%$ per logical qudit; (ii) a truncation entropy of $\ln(4/3) \approx 0.288$ nats per encoded qudit; and (iii) a striking asymmetry under Pauli twirling: on the dark state $|0\rangle$ of an amplitude-damping channel with damping parameter $\gamma = 0.01$, the continuous channel generates exactly zero entropy while its Pauli-twirled abstraction generates $0.0175$ nats per cycle—entropy fabricated purely by the discrete abstraction. We argue these quantities constitute a lower-bound-style accounting of the cost of digital abstraction, and we discuss implications for analog Hamiltonian evolution and natively discrete fermionic platforms.

## 1. Introduction

Quantum error correction (QEC) in its dominant form is a digital discipline: errors are expanded in a discrete Pauli basis, syndromes are binary measurement outcomes, and theorems such as the threshold theorem are proved for discrete error sets. Yet the physical systems on which QEC is most often implemented—superconducting circuits such as the transmon—are natively continuous bosonic systems. Their spectra are quantized by Planck's law, but they are not naturally two-level objects, and their dominant continuous error processes, such as amplitude damping (photon loss), do not live natively in the Pauli group.

This paper takes seriously a conjecture suggested by the QNFO research program: that the digital/discrete paradigm in quantum computing is an engineering convenience rather than a physical universal, and that imposing discrete abstractions on continuous systems generates a measurable overhead—leakage, discretization-induced entropy, and error-correction cost—that is itself a consequence of the mismatch between the digital map and the continuous territory. The related QNFO analysis of the resource burden of active QEC emphasizes that QEC protects information only by continuously consuming physical resources—qubits, measurement bandwidth, classical processing, and energy [12]; we ask a complementary question: how much of this burden is attributable not to noise itself, but to the *discretization step* through which noise is modeled and corrected?

Our approach is deliberately conservative. We do not claim new physics; we perform explicit entropy and dimension accounting of the abstraction step itself, with every arithmetic step shown. The central objects are:

1. The **radix overhead**: the cost of embedding a $d$-dimensional qudit (here the transmon, $d \approx 12$ [10]) into a binary qubit register.
2. The **truncation entropy**: the entropy associated with the unused Hilbert-space volume created by rounding $d$ up to a power of two.
3. The **twirling entropy fabrication**: the entropy generated by replacing a continuous channel (amplitude damping) with its closest discrete Pauli-channel abstraction, on input states where the two disagree maximally.

The headline result is an asymmetry: for amplitude damping with $\gamma = 0.01$, the Pauli abstraction *fabricates* $0.0175$ nats of entropy per cycle on the dark state $|0\rangle$, where the true continuous channel generates exactly zero, while *underestimating* entropy generation on the excited state $|1\rangle$ by a factor of approximately $3.2$. The abstraction is not merely lossy; it is *biased*, and the bias is directional and computable.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Scope and conventions

We work in nats throughout ($1$ nat $= \ln 2 \approx 0.6931$ bits of entropy). We adopt the Rosetta corrected convention [10]: the transmon is a qudit with $d \approx 12$ usable levels; its spectrum is discrete but not binary. We consider two error models:

- **Continuous model $\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ (amplitude damping):** Kraus operators $K_0 = |0\rangle\langle 0| + \sqrt{1-\gamma}\,|1\rangle\langle 1|$ and $K_1 = \sqrt{\gamma}\,|0\rangle\langle 1|$, representing photon loss with damping parameter $\gamma$ per cycle.
- **Discrete model $\mathcal{P}_q$ (Pauli channel):** a channel of the form $\mathcal{P}_q(\rho) = \sum_{\sigma \in \{I,X,Y,Z\}} q_\sigma\, \sigma \rho \sigma^\dagger$, the canonical digital abstraction of noise.

The **Pauli twirl** of $\mathcal{A}_\gamma$ is the unique Pauli channel with the same Pauli transfer matrix eigenvalues $(\lambda_X, \lambda_Y, \lambda_Z) = (\sqrt{1-\gamma}, \sqrt{1-\gamma}, 1-\gamma)$. Twirling is the standard operation by which continuous noise is forced into the discrete Pauli formalism required by stabilizer codes; our object of study is precisely the entropy accounting of this step.

### 3.2 Entropy functional

For a diagonal two-outcome distribution with probabilities $(p, 1-p)$ we use the binary entropy

$$H_1(p) = -p \ln p - (1-p)\ln(1-p).$$

For a thermal bosonic mode with mean occupation $\bar{n}$, the entropy is

$$S_{\text{th}}(\bar{n}) = (\bar{n}+1)\ln(\bar{n}+1) - \bar{n}\ln \bar{n},$$

and the tail probability of the geometric photon-number distribution beyond $n_{\max}$ levels is

$$P(n \geq k) = \left(\frac{\bar{n}}{1+\bar{n}}\right)^{k}.$$

All derivations below use only these three functionals and elementary algebra; every input number is stated with its source.
