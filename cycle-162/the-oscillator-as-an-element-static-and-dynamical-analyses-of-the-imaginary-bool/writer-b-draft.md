# The Oscillator as an Element: An Algebraic Analysis of the Imaginary Boolean Value in the Algebra of Eventually Periodic Truth Sequences

## Abstract

Revision-theoretic treatments of the liar paradox produce an oscillator $\iota = \langle U,P,U,P,\ldots\rangle$ that alternates between two states forever, but they leave $\iota$ a process rather than a value. This paper tests the conjecture that $\iota$ can be promoted to a static element of a genuine Boolean algebra of eventually periodic truth sequences. We define the algebra $B_{\mathrm{EP}}$ of eventually periodic binary sequences under pointwise negation, conjunction, and disjunction, and prove it is a Boolean algebra. The central identity fails: $\iota \neq \neg\iota$ in $B_{\mathrm{EP}}$, since the sequences differ at coordinate $0$. We prove a collapse theorem: any Boolean congruence identifying $\iota$ with $\neg\iota$ is the universal congruence, because in a Boolean algebra $x = \neg x$ forces $x = 0 = 1$; no nontrivial Boolean algebra therefore contains a self-negating element, and $B_{\mathrm{EP}}$ is provably non-isomorphic both to the two-element classical algebra and to Kleene's strong three-valued algebra. What survives is an exact dynamical characterization: $\iota$ is one of exactly $2$ shift-exact period-$2$ sequences, satisfies $\sigma(\iota) = \neg\iota$ for the shift $\sigma$, and its depth-$d$ unfolding truncations converge pointwise, pinning $\iota$ to a cylinder of uniform product measure $2^{-d}$ (for $d = 10$, $9.765625 \times 10^{-4}$). The oscillator is thus a computably approximable element of a Boolean algebra whose self-negation holds dynamically, not algebraically — a discrimination that separates it from static three-valued values and that turns the conjecture's promotion claim into a precise, partially refuted, partially confirmed theorem.

## 1. Introduction

The liar paradox is not merely a curiosity for philosophers of logic; it is a live engineering obstacle wherever a formal system must evaluate a sentence that refers to its own truth. Any truth-evaluation engine — a theorem prover with a reflective truth predicate, a database of self-referential definitions, an implementation of a distinction-based calculus — that admits the sentence $L$ with $L \leftrightarrow \neg L$ either halts with no value, diverges in an evaluation loop, or must be walled off by a syntactic hierarchy (Tarskian levels) that forbids the very reflexivity the application wants. The revision-theoretic response, due to Herzberger, Gupta, and Belnap, is to let the evaluation run: $L$ is revised to $\neg L$, then to $\neg\neg L$, and so on, producing the oscillator $\iota = \langle U,P,U,P,\ldots\rangle$ that alternates between unmarked and marked, false and true, forever. The cost of this response is ontological: the oscillator is a process, not a value, and a system that can only assign values cannot use it. What would change if the result of this paper holds: a practitioner could treat the liar oscillator as a well-defined, finitely approximable object inside an ordinary Boolean algebra, with a theorem saying exactly in what sense it is and is not a self-negating truth value — turning a metaphysical dispute ("is the oscillator a third value?") into a checkable algebraic statement, and telling implementers precisely what their evaluator converges to and at what rate.

The conjecture under test, stated in the source notebook, has three parts: (1) the eventually periodic binary sequences form a Boolean algebra under pointwise operations; (2) in that algebra the distinguished element $\iota$ satisfies $\iota = \neg\iota$ as an algebraic identity, and the resulting structure is not isomorphic to classical two-valued or three-valued $(T,F,N)$ logic; (3) finite-depth unfoldings of the rewriting dynamics converge to $\iota$'s equivalence class, so the value is well-defined and computationally approximable. The conjecture is attractive because it promises the best of both worlds: the static, compositional clarity of a Boolean algebra and the dynamic, paradox-tolerant behavior of revision semantics. The central tension, also stated in the notebook, is that if $\iota$ is only a sequence it adds nothing beyond period-$2$ revision theory, while if it is a value it must live in a static domain whose operations compose coherently.

This paper resolves the tension by proving both halves of it, with opposite verdicts. The algebra exists and is Boolean (Theorem 3.2). But the identity $\iota = \neg\iota$ is false in it (Proposition 4.3), and it cannot be repaired by quotienting: any Boolean congruence that identifies $\iota$ with $\neg\iota$ collapses the entire algebra (Theorem 4.5), a consequence of the elementary fact that a self-negating element of a Boolean algebra forces $0 = 1$. The non-isomorphism claims, however, survive and strengthen: $B_{\mathrm{EP}}$ is not isomorphic to classical logic (it is infinite), and no structure preserving $\wedge, \vee, \neg$ embeds Kleene's strong three-valued algebra into it (Proposition 4.8), because excluded middle holds pointwise in $B_{\mathrm{EP}}$ and fails at the value $N$ in Kleene's tables. The dynamical half of the conjecture is confirmed with a quantitative rate: reading the depth profile of the liar's finite unfoldings as a sequence, one more unfolding acts as the shift $\sigma$, the profile is exactly $\iota$, and the depth-$d$ truncation determines $\iota$ on its first $d$ coordinates, confining it to a cylinder set of uniform product measure $2^{-d}$.
