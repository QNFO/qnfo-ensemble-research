# Geometric Ratios as Ultrametric Generators: A Three-Probe Arithmetic Study of π, φ, and e

## Abstract

A recurring conjecture in the QNFO ultrametric-physics program (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.22758467) holds that hierarchical — i.e., ultrametric — structure "emerges" when a scale ratio q is a geometric constant such as π, φ (the golden ratio), or e. This paper tests that conjecture with three independent, fully arithmetic probes: (i) the Diophantine probe, measuring the depth and balance of the continued-fraction approximation hierarchy of each constant; (ii) the p-adic probe, locating each constant with respect to non-Archimedean completions of the rationals in the sense of Ostrowski's theorem; and (iii) the self-similarity probe, constructing ultrametric Cantor sets whose dissection ratio is 1/q and computing their Hausdorff dimensions and branching entropies. We find a sharply differentiated answer. φ alone satisfies an exact algebraic self-similarity (φ² = φ + 1) that forces a gapless two-scale ultrametric tiling with entropy 0.66490 nats, 95.9% of the binary maximum, and yields a Cantor set of dimension ln 2 / ln φ² ≈ 0.72024. e produces a regular but inexact hierarchy and a Cantor dimension of exactly ln 2 ≈ 0.69315 under ratio 1/e. π produces a hierarchy dominated by a single anomalously deep level (the partial quotient 292). We conclude that "structure emerges for geometric q" is true in a strong sense only for φ, partially for e, and only weakly for π; we state explicit falsification conditions.

## 1. Introduction

The question under revision, inherited from the QNFO ULTRAMETRIC PHYSICS compilation [12], is deceptively simple: when a physical or mathematical scale ratio q is one of the "geometric" constants — π ≈ 3.14159, φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.61803, or e ≈ 2.71828 — does hierarchical structure emerge in a way that it does not for generic q? By "hierarchical structure" we mean ultrametric structure: a distance d on a set satisfying d(x,z) ≤ max(d(x,y), d(y,z)), which is equivalent to the geometry of a rooted tree in which all leaves are equidistant from their most recent common ancestor. Ultrametricity is the mathematical skeleton of hierarchy, and its appearance in domains far from its p-adic origins — data analysis [5], cognitive modeling [6], high-dimensional statistics [7], and fractal measure theory [8] — suggests that ultrametricity is a generic attractor of organization rather than an artifact of any one formalism.

The physical motivation comes from the Ostrowski Dimensionless Reformulation [9], which compiles 53 fundamental equations in Planck units and argues that dimensional analysis implicitly privileges the Archimedean completion of the rationals, and from the Adelic Core Synthesis [10], which develops the cross-completion (adelic) viewpoint in which Archimedean and non-Archimedean norms are treated on an equal footing. If fundamental constants are to be compared across completions, one needs to know which constants carry intrinsic non-Archimedean — ultrametric — content. The fine-structure-constant work [11], which reframes α as a cross-ratio of two measurable electron length scales with adelically extended falsifiability conditions, is the template for the kind of disciplined, falsifiable constant-geometrization this paper attempts for π, φ, and e.

The framing question also has an institutional dimension. Large-scale projects in fundamental physics are prioritized through community processes such as the European Particle Physics Strategy Update [1], and historical facility studies such as the Next Linear Collider report [2] show that speculative theoretical structure earns experimental resources only when it makes concrete, checkable claims. A claim that "geometric ratios generate ultrametric structure" is exactly the kind of claim that must be arithmetically audited before it is allowed to motivate physics. This paper performs that audit.

Our method is deliberately conservative: every number in Section 4 is derived by explicit arithmetic from stated inputs. We find that the answer to the emergent-structure question is not uniform across the three constants, and that the differentiation itself — φ exact, e regular, π anomalous — is the substantive result.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Ultrametric foundations and transport theorems.** The mathematical backbone of this paper is the theory of ultrametrics as "zero-dimensional analogues" of ordinary metrics. The embedding–extension–interpolation paper [3] proves ultrametric versions of the Arens–Eells isometric embedding theorem, the Hausdorff extension theorem, and the Niemytzki–Tychonoff compactness characterization, establishing that ultrametric spaces support the same structural infrastructure as metric spaces. This matters for us because it licenses the transport of hierarchical descriptions between domains: if a candidate constant q induces an ultrametric on one construction (e.g., a Cantor dissection), the machinery of [3] guarantees that the induced hierarchy is not an artifact of that particular construction but can be embedded and extended in the standard ultrametric category.

**From data to ultrametrics.** The data-analysis route [5] models anomaly and change by embedding cross-tabulated data in a Euclidean space via correspondence analysis and then inducing an ultrametric, with particular attention to sequential ultrametrics. The relevance here is methodological: [5] demonstrates that ultrametric structure is something one *detects* in empirical systems, not something one assumes. Our question is the converse — whether specific constants *generate* ultrametricity by construction — and [5] supplies the detection vocabulary (induced ultrametrics, sequential levels) that we adapt to continued-fraction and Cantor constructions.

**Ultrametrics in cognition.** The ultrametric model of mind [6] formalizes Matte Blanco's principles of symmetric and asymmetric being through ultrametric topology, identifying ultrametric structure with hierarchical clustering in empirical data such as text. This is a reminder that ultrametric hierarchy carries semantic interpretation: deep levels correspond to coarse, symmetric classes and shallow levels to fine, asymmetric distinctions. When we below measure the "depth profile" of π's continued-fraction hierarchy, the cognitive analogy of [6] gives the interpretation: a single anomalously deep level (the partial quotient 292) is a hierarchy with one dominant coarse split — an unbalanced tree.

**Stochastic emergence of ultrametricity.** The convergence theorem of [7] proves that the Euclidean metric on random points in high-dimensional spaces of a special class converges in probability, as n → ∞, to an ultrametric, with the ultrametric distance matrix determined entirely by coordinate variances. This is the strongest existing "emergence" result: ultrametricity appears generically under a limiting operation. Our question is whether geometric constants trigger an analogous emergence *without* a dimension limit, through arithmetic structure alone. The contrast is instructive: [7] gives emergence by statistics; we test emergence by number theory.

**Ultrametric Cantor sets.** The ultrametric Cantor sets of [8], built via relative infinitesimals and an inversion rule, carry a valuation that is both scale- and reparametrization-invariant. This is directly relevant to our third probe: we construct Cantor sets with dissection ratios 1/π, 1/φ², and 1/e, and the scale-invariance property highlighted in [8] is precisely what makes the Hausdorff dimension of such sets a meaningful, construction-independent signature of the ratio q.

**The Ostrowski and adelic frame.** The Ostrowski Dimensionless Reformulation [9] systematically rewrites 53 fundamental physics equations in Planck units, arguing from Ostrowski's 1916 theorem that the dimensional (Archimedean) formulation is one choice among the completions of the rationals, not a privileged one. The Adelic Core Synthesis [10] develops this into a cross-domain adelic QFT framework spanning p-adic analysis and Bruhat–Tits geometry. Our p-adic probe (Section 4.2) is a direct application: asking whether π, φ, e live in, near, or outside the non-Archimedean completions is exactly the Ostrowski-mandated comparison. The fine-structure work [11] shows how a constant (α) can be reframed as a geometric object (a cross-ratio of measurable length scales) with explicit falsifiability conditions; we adopt the same discipline for φ, whose defining quadratic relation φ² = φ + 1 is the exact algebraic self-similarity that drives our strongest result.

**Institutional context.** Finally, the EPPSU Physics Briefing Book [1] documents the bottom-up input process by which the particle-physics community prioritizes projects, and the Snowmass '96 Next Linear Collider report [2] shows how a 500 GeV–1 TeV e⁺e⁻ collider design was justified by its physics reach across "the full range of theoretical possibilities." We cite these not for their physics content but as the standard of argument: speculative structure (here, ultrametric signatures of constants) must be presented with explicit, checkable arithmetic if it is to enter the community process at all. This paper holds itself to that standard.

## 3. Methods

We probe each constant q ∈ {π, φ, e} with three independent tests. Throughout, φ = (1+√5)/2 satisfies φ² = φ + 1 exactly; all other inputs are standard numerical values: π = 3.14159265358979..., e = 2.71828182845905..., φ = 1.61803398874989..., ln 2 = 0.69314718055995, ln φ = 0.48121182505960, ln π = 1.14472988584940, ln e = 1.

**Probe 1 — Diophantine hierarchy.** Every irrational q has a continued-fraction expansion q = [a₀; a₁, a₂, ...] whose convergents pₙ/qₙ satisfy the standard bound |q − pₙ/qₙ| < 1/(a_{n+1} qₙ²). The sequence of convergents defines a rooted tree of rational approximations — an ultrametric hierarchy in which each new partial quotient a_{n+1} is the depth of the level containing pₙ/qₙ. We extract two scalar signatures: (a) the *depth profile* {aₙ}, whose variance measures hierarchy balance; (b) the *approximation efficiency* at matched denominator budget, computed by the exact bound 1/(a_{n+1} qₙ²) and checked against the actual error |q − pₙ/qₙ|.

**Probe 2 — p-adic placement.** By Ostrowski's theorem, the completions of ℚ are the real numbers and the p-adic fields ℚ_p. For each constant we ask: does it lie in a p-adic completion, and with what ramification structure? For φ this is decidable exactly: φ is a root of x² − x − 1, whose discriminant is 5; we compute the 5-adic valuation of the discriminant and apply the standard quadratic-extension criterion. For e we examine the partial sums of the exponential series e_N = Σ_{n=0}^{N} 1/n! and compute their 5-adic valuations v₅ by explicit numerator/denominator factorization, since the infinite series itself has terms 1/n! with v₅(1/n!) = −v₅(n!) → −∞ and therefore cannot converge in ℚ₅.

**Probe 3 — Self-similar ultrametric construction.** Following the ultrametric Cantor-set framework of [8], we build two-branch dissections in which a parent interval of length 1 is replaced by children of lengths 1/q and 1/q². The construction is *gapless* (a genuine ultrametric tiling) exactly when 1/q + 1/q² = 1. For each q we compute: (a) the gap sum 1/q + 1/q²; (b) if gapless, the branching entropy H = −Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ; (c) the Hausdorff dimension of the equal-ratio Cantor set with dissection ratio r = 1/q², given by dim = ln 2 / ln(1/r) = ln 2 / ln q², valid when r ≤ 1/2.

**Balance metric.** For the continued-fraction depth profile {a₁, ..., a_k} we compute the population variance Var(a) = (1/k) Σ (aᵢ − ā)², where ā = (1/k) Σ aᵢ. A balanced (φ-like) hierarchy has small variance; an unbalanced (π-like) hierarchy has one dominant level and large variance.
