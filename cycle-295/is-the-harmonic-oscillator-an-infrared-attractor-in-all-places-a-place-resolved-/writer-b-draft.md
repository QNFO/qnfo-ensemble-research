# Is the Harmonic Oscillator an Infrared Attractor in All Places? A Place-Resolved Analysis of the Harmonic Paradigm

## Abstract

The conjecture that the quantum harmonic oscillator is a universal infrared (IR) attractor for bosonic systems has been formulated and tested only over the Archimedean place, that is, with respect to the usual real absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$. Whether the attractor status survives a change of place in the sense of Ostrowski's classification of absolute values has not been resolved. We set up a place-resolved renormalization-group (RG) framework in which a dimensionless distance $\alpha_v$ from a flowing bosonic system to the oscillator fixed point is defined separately for the Archimedean place $v=\infty$ and for the $p$-adic places $v=p$, with a putative $p$-adic fixed point whose spectrum we model on the levels of the Bruhat–Tits tree of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ under an explicitly stated modeling assumption. We derive: the Archimedean ladder $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac{1}{2})$ with constant gaps; a geometric $p$-adic ladder $E^{(p)}_n=E_0\,p^{-n}$ with adjacent-gap ratio exactly $p$ (computed as $2$ for $p=2$ and $3$ for $p=3$); an exponential RG approach $\alpha_v(\ell)=e^{-\gamma_v\ell}$ reaching $\alpha_v(20)\approx 0.135335$ at $\gamma_v=0.1$; and a test of adelic linkage via the product formula, verified exactly for $x=2/3$. Attraction is place-dependent in structure: constant-gap attraction in $\mathbb{R}$ coexists with geometric-gap attraction in $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and the adelic product formula constrains amplitudes, not spectra, so no spectral identity links the two fixed points. The attractor claim is neither simply preserved nor reversed but structurally transformed, and the exact $p$-adic oscillator spectrum is the single calculation that would convert the model-based results into theorems.

## 1. Introduction

The Harmonic Paradigm proposes that the quantum harmonic oscillator (HO) is the universal infrared attractor of quantum theory: generic bosonic systems, when probed at sufficiently long wavelengths, flow toward oscillator-like behavior regardless of microscopic detail. The program's own synthesis paper reports quantitative support in the Archimedean setting: transmon anharmonicity scaling with exponent $\nu = 0.5084 \pm 0.017$ and a Bayes factor of $9.3 \times 10^{18}$, a zero-point-energy no-go theorem consistent with $6$ of $6$ edge cases, and a harmonic ladder connecting transmon circuits at $10^{-6}$ m to quantum gravity at $10^{-35}$ m [11]. The paradigm's retrospective audit, however, reached a sharper diagnosis: the Harmonic Paradigm versions V1.0–V4.0 (2026) proposed the oscillator as the universal IR attractor across an 8-rung ladder — transmon anharmonicity, phonon equal spacing, molecular vibrations, the QED and QCD $\beta$-functions, the GUT scale, the $p$-adic oscillator on the Bruhat–Tits tree, and ultrametric quantum gravity — yet its core mechanisms, the $\beta$-function analysis and the equal-$\ln(\mu)$-spacing order-statistics test, were tested exclusively over $\mathbb{R}$, the Archimedean place, even though the bibliography invoked Vladimirov–Volovich–Zelenov $p$-adic analysis and Ostrowski's theorem [12]. The same audit records that the paradigm's central logistic $\beta$-function ansatz $\beta(\alpha) = B\cdot\alpha(1-\alpha)$ was retracted after direct calculation showed it incompatible with the textbook QED $\beta$-function $\beta(\alpha) = (2/3\pi)\alpha^2$, because the logistic ansatz is linear at leading order while real QED is quadratic [12].

This matters to a real decision. If the oscillator-attractor thesis is a theorem of quantum theory in every place, then theoretical work that extends quantum mechanics to $p$-adic and adelic settings — as in the adelic re-evaluation program [12] — inherits oscillator universality for free, and model-building in non-Archimedean settings can proceed with the oscillator as a guaranteed IR limit. If instead the thesis is an artifact of the Archimedean place, then every non-Archimedean completion must re-derive its own IR fixed point, and resources spent assuming universality there are misallocated. The audit's own framing poses exactly this question: do any of the Harmonic Paradigm's mechanisms survive at non-Archimedean completions, or was the paradigm an Archimedean-only theory with a non-Archimedean bibliography [12]? Answering it changes what the next paper in the program must compute.

The problem is one of place-ambiguity. Every statement of the form "the oscillator attracts" implicitly quantifies over states, observables, and a number field equipped with an absolute value. By Ostrowski's theorem, as recalled in [12], the nontrivial absolute values on $\mathbb{Q}$ are, up to equivalence, the usual real absolute value and the $p$-adic absolute values $|\cdot|_p$, and the adele ring $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod'_p \mathbb{Q}_p$ embeds every nonzero rational diagonally with the product formula $\prod_v |q|_v = 1$ connecting all places [12]. The attractor claim has only been tested under the first of these. The sharp question is therefore: is the oscillator's attractor status (a) preserved, (b) reversed, or (c) structurally transformed under $p$-adic and adelic norms?

We answer with a framework and a diagnosis. We define a place-resolved distance $\alpha_v$ from a flowing bosonic system to the oscillator fixed point in each place $v$, derive the RG flow toward each oscillator, and test whether the adelic product formula imposes a genuine constraint linking the Archimedean and $p$-adic attractors. Our conclusion is option (c): attraction can exist in every place, but with structurally different spectra — constant gaps in $\mathbb{R}$, geometric gaps in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ — and the adelic product formula constrains amplitudes rather than spectra, so the two attractors share a name by structural analogy, not by a spectral identity. This is a negative result for the strongest form of the Harmonic Paradigm, the place-invariant upgrade, and a positive result for a weaker form: the oscillator can be an IR attractor in all places, each in its own spectral dialect.

Throughout, "place" means an equivalence class of absolute values on $\mathbb{Q}$, and "Bruhat–Tits tree" means the locally infinite $(p+1)$-regular tree on which $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ acts. All quantitative claims are derived in Section 4 with every input number stated; Section 5 reports only those computed values or clearly labeled projections. The contributions are:

- A place-resolved RG framework defining the attractor distance $\alpha_v(\ell)=\alpha_v(0)\,e^{-\gamma_v\ell}$ independently at each Ostrowski place, with attraction, repulsion, and structural transformation given precise meanings (Section 3).
- An exact spectral contrast between the two fixed points: constant Archimedean gaps $\hbar\omega$ versus geometric $p$-adic gaps with adjacent ratio exactly $p$, computed for $p=2$ (gaps $1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16$) and $p=3$ (gaps $2/3,2/9,2/27$), together with Bruhat–Tits vertex counts $(p+1)p^{n-1}$ per level and the closed cumulative form $S_p(N)=1+(p+1)\frac{p^N-1}{p-1}$ (Section 4, A2–A3).
- A negative adelic-linkage result: the hypothesis $\prod_v\alpha_v(\ell)=c$ along the flow fails for generic couplings, holding only under the fine-tuned condition $\sum_v\gamma_v=0$, which the product formula does not impose (Section 4, A5).
- A precise localization of where adelic structure does bind: on amplitudes at rational evaluation points, $\prod_v|\Psi(x)|_v=1$, which fixes no eigenvalue (Section 4, A6).
- The resulting verdict on the attractor trichotomy — structural transformation, option (c) — with the falsification conditions stated (Sections 5–6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The harmonic oscillator has been generalized along many algebraic and geometric axes, and each generalization is evidence about which features of the oscillator are robust and which are artifacts of the ambient number structure. We review the relevant literature, stating for each work the specific result it contributes to our argument.

**Oscillators over exotic number systems.** The bicomplex quantum harmonic oscillator of [5] investigates the oscillator problem over bicomplex numbers — pairs of complex numbers forming a commutative ring $\mathbb{T}$ with zero divisors, built from units ${\rm i}_1, {\rm i}_2, {\rm j}$ with ${\rm i}_1^2=-1={\rm i}_2^2$ and ${\rm j}^2=1$ — by starting from the commutator of bicomplex position and momentum operators and adapting the algebraic treatment of the standard oscillator, finding eigenvalues and eigenkets. Its conclusion section shows that the eigenkets and eigenfunctions of the standard harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian can be recovered from the bicomplex ones in three distinct ways, and that the usual Hermite polynomials are recovered from the bicomplex ones [5]. That the algebraic oscillator construction survives even over a ring with zero divisors supports the working hypothesis that "oscillator" names a robust algebraic fixed point, not merely a real-variable solution — but the bicomplex recovery also shows the standard structure re-emerges as a special case, which is exactly the pattern our place-resolved analysis tests at the $p$-adic places. A generalized harmonic oscillator on noncommutative spaces is treated in [4], where the $2D$-dimensional noncommutative phase space is described by variables $U_I$ with commutators $[U_I,U_J]=iM_{IJ}$ restricted by the Jacobi identities, the isotropic oscillator Hamiltonian $H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{I=1}^{2D}U_I^2$ is $O(2D)$-invariant, and the three-dimensional case is classified by dynamical symmetry groups $U(3)$, $U(2)\times U(1)$, and $[U(1)]^3$ with an explicit parametrization of the commutation matrix $M$ for each; the discussion records two physically distinct phases defined by $\kappa=\prod\omega_i$ and shows that for a special parametrization with $h_i=1$ and $\phi_{ij}=\psi_{ij}=0$ there is no $U(3)$ symmetry [4]. The lesson we draw is stated by the paper's own abstract-level result on physical equivalence: systems sharing the same energy spectrum can be physically inequivalent, so a place-resolved attractor claim must specify more than eigenvalues.

**Dynamics, damping, and the inverted sector.** The anti-PT-symmetric treatment of [6] works in the Schrödinger picture with the inverted Hamiltonian $H^{\mathrm{r}}=\frac{1}{2m}p^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}$, which is symmetric but not positive definite, and shows that the common formal replacement $\omega\to\pm i\omega$ used to obtain the inverted oscillator leads to unbounded eigenvectors and involves unclear points in the redefinition; the paper records that the inverted system gives rise to generalized complex eigenvalues because its potential is unbounded from below, solves the problem via quasi-Hermiticity, expresses the system in terms of idealized states related to those of the harmonic oscillator through a scaling operator $\rho$, and notes the eigenstates are found in terms of parabolic cylinder functions $D_\nu(x)$ [6]. This matters because an RG flow away from a stable oscillator fixed point is often modeled by exactly such an analytic continuation; [6] warns that the "inverted" sector is not a harmless mirror of the stable one, a caution we adopt when discussing repulsion in Section 6. On the classical side, the coupled-oscillator technique of [9] models each voltage-controlled oscillator in an array by a piecewise linear admittance function, formulates the synchronized steady-state solutions of the coupled system globally, and reports validation by comparison with circuit-level harmonic-balance simulations of a free-running array of Van der Pol-type oscillators and with measurements on a coupled system of FET-based oscillators at 5 GHz, in which the circuit-level technique fails to converge while the piecewise technique predicts the synchronization ranges under both free-running and injection-locked conditions [9]. Classical coupled-oscillator synchronization is the engineering counterpart of our RG approach: a many-body system relaxing to a common oscillation, and [9] shows the approach-to-synchronization problem is tractable even where naive methods diverge — a template for how a $p$-adic RG flow computation might be organized numerically.

**Spectral and analytic structure.** The characterization of quantum limits and semi-classical measures in [3] treats eigenfunctions of Schrödinger operators $\widehat{P}:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}+\frac{1}{2}Q$ with $Q$ a positive definite quadratic form, constructs sequences of joint eigenfunctions of periodic Hamiltonians $\mathcal{H}_{n}$ that concentrate on minimal invariant tori with periods $T_{n}:=T_{v_{n}\nu_{n}}$, and proves that the structure of the set of semi-classical measures depends strongly on the arithmetic relations between the frequencies of the decoupled oscillators, using a Krein–Milman argument showing the admissible measures are convex combinations of orbit measures $\delta_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega}(z_{0})}$ [3]. This is directly relevant: arithmetic relations among frequencies are precisely what changes when one changes place, so place-dependence of attractor structure is already visible in the Archimedean theory through arithmetic. On the analytic side, [2] shows that harmonic oscillator propagators and fractional Fourier transforms are essentially the same, deduces continuity properties and fixed-time estimates for these operators on modulation spaces (including Wiener amalgam spaces) and Pilipović spaces defined through Hermite functions $h_{\alpha}(x)$ and sequence spaces indexed by an extended $\mathbf{R}_{\flat}$, and applies the link to extend Strichartz estimates for the harmonic oscillator propagator on modulation spaces [2]. This gives a function-analytic handle on oscillator propagation that is independent of spectral-gap structure and could be transplanted to $p$-adic modulation spaces in future work; we return to this in Section 6.

**Attractors and universality claims.** The RG–harmonic isomorphism of [13] argues that the renormalization group and harmonic quantum mechanics share a mathematical isomorphism rooted in the spectral decomposition of a self-adjoint operator under scale transformations: Pillar I identifies Wilson's momentum-shell integration $[\Lambda/b,\Lambda]$ with projecting the oscillator's Hilbert space onto its lowest $N$ levels below the equally spaced spectrum $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + 1/2)$; Pillar II identifies RG fixed points $g^*$ where $\beta_i(g^*) = 0$ with stationary states, the Gaussian fixed point with the oscillator ground state, and relevant operators with creation operators $a^\dagger$; Pillar III writes the Callan–Symanzik equation as a Schrödinger-type evolution equation in which the RG scale $\mu$ plays the role of time [13]. This supplies the conceptual license for our linearized flow $d\omega_v/d\ell=-\gamma_v(\omega_v-\omega_{v,*})$: in the isomorphism's own terms, the flow of a coupling toward a fixed point is the motion of a system toward a stationary state, and the exponential approach is the linearized version of that motion. The synthesis paper [11] consolidates the program's evidence: the transmon anharmonicity exponent $\nu = 0.5084 \pm 0.017$ with Bayes factor $9.3 \times 10^{18}$, the ZPE observability no-go theorem with explicit assumptions $\mathcal{A}_1$–$\mathcal{A}_5$ and $6/6$ edge-case consistency (Casimir effect, Lamb shift, spontaneous emission, cosmological constant, dynamical Casimir effect, Unruh effect — all consistent, with only the cosmological constant requiring gravity), and the harmonic-ladder architecture from $10^{-6}$ m to $10^{-35}$ m [11]. All of this evidence is Archimedean; our paper tests what survives a change of place. In the same universality spirit, [8] finds a universal mass scale for all $q$-form fields in multi-brane worlds: the $(+-+)$ model's Kaluza–Klein reduction puts the fluctuation $\psi^{n}(y)$ into a Schrödinger equation $\left(-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{z}+V(z)\right)\hat{\psi}^{n}(z)=\frac{m_{n}^{2}}{2}e^{2\sigma(y)}\hat{\psi}^{n}(z)$, the earlier literature found ultralight modes $m_{1}^{2}\approx\sqrt{4\nu^{2}-1}\,ke^{-(\nu+1/2)kl}$ with $\nu=3/2$ for gravity but with mass terms $m^{2}=\alpha(\sigma'(y)^{2})+\beta\sigma''(y)$ containing free parameters and breaking general covariance, and the paper proposes a covariant geometrical or dilaton coupling to multi-localize the $q$-form fields so that the universal scale is fixed [8]. This is a parallel universality claim in the bosonic domain whose scale is geometric — exponential in the brane separation $kl$ — and whose mechanism (exponential hierarchy from geometry) is structurally akin to our geometric $p$-adic ladder.

**Applications as stress tests.** The QHO battery model of [1] considers a charger QHO interacting with a battery QHO for a time period $(0,\tau)$ under the total Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}=\omega_{o}a^{\dagger}a+\omega_{o}b^{\dagger}b+g(ab^{\dagger}+a^{\dagger}b)+F(ae^{i\omega_{f}t}+a^{\dagger}e^{-i\omega_{f}t})$, with the charger laser-driven and dissipative; its conclusion reports that tuning the laser frequency $\omega_f$ to the global frequency $\omega_{\pm}$ rather than the local $\omega_o$ lets the battery store more energy, that no signature of boundless charging or period-$1/\Delta$ oscillation appears when the charger is dissipative, that catalysts are not essential for efficient charging, and that self-discharging of catalytic and non-catalytic batteries is faster than charging, making them unstable against the environment [1]. This is an Archimedean-place technology built directly on the oscillator ladder; it is a reminder of what is at stake in universality claims, since a place-resolved restatement of oscillator physics would have to say which of these charging properties are spectral (gap-dependent) and which are dynamical. The cautionary episode of [7] documents a claim at the QSCP XIX conference that a scaled Fourier transform, with scaling determined by the interaction and particle mass for a harmonic oscillator, could achieve simultaneous position–momentum resolution greater than the standard Heisenberg value of $1/2$; the paper states plainly that the procedure is invalid for quantum mechanics, and its submission history records version v3 withdrawn on 6 February 2015 [7]. This is a warning against overclaiming oscillator universality that we heed in Section 6.

**The prior place-resolved analysis and its audit trail.** The prior version of this analysis [10] set up the same place-resolved question and reported six results: exact verification of the product formula for $x=2/3$; the geometric $p$-adic gap structure with ratio $p$ under the stated model; Bruhat–Tits vertex counts $(p+1)p^{n-1}$; the exponential RG approach $\alpha_v(\ell)=e^{-0.1\ell}$ with $\alpha_v(20)\approx 0.135335$, $\alpha_v(40)\approx 0.018316$, $\alpha_v(60)\approx 0.002479$, explicitly labeled projections rather than measurements; the failure of adelic linkage for generic couplings; and the structural-transformation verdict [10]. Its own limitations section identifies the $p$-adic level formula $E^{(p)}_n=E_0\,p^{-n}$ as the weakest link, notes the RG flow is linearized with an illustrative coupling, and flags richer adelic constraints involving local $L$-factors as unexplored [10]. The present paper is the remediated revision of that analysis: it restates the framework with every input number sourced or labeled as an assumption, sharpens the linkage test, and adds the RG-threshold calculation of A7 below. The audit of [12] supplies the motivation from the paradigm's own side: the 8-rung ladder explicitly invokes $p$-adic oscillators and Bruhat–Tits trees at rungs 7–8, the core mechanisms were tested exclusively over $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is not ordered — which the audit states eliminates the S-matrix, Noether's theorem, and measurement as usually formulated — and quantities such as $\pi$, $\alpha$, $m$, $\lambda_C$, and $r_e$ are adelic in the sense of existing at every Ostrowski completion with the product formula as the connecting identity [12]. Our paper addresses the open question thereby identified, while respecting the audit's warning that non-Archimedean places lack the order structure on which much of Archimedean quantum mechanics rests.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Places and absolute values

A place of $\mathbb{Q}$ is an equivalence class of absolute values. We write $v=\infty$ for the Archimedean place with $|x|_\infty$ the usual real absolute value, and $v=p$ for the $p$-adic places, where $|p^k m|_p=p^{-k}$ for $m$ coprime to $p$. The adele ring is $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod'_p \mathbb{Q}_p$, the restricted product, with every nonzero rational $x$ embedded diagonally [12]. The adelic product formula states that for every nonzero rational $x$,

$$\prod_v |x|_v = |x|_\infty \prod_p |x|_p = 1.$$

This identity is the only adelic input used in this paper; we do not assume any additional adelic dynamical principle.

### 3.2 The two oscillator fixed points

**Archimedean fixed point.** The standard quantum harmonic oscillator has Hamiltonian $H_\infty=\frac{\hat{P}^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2\hat{X}^2}{2}$ and the equally spaced spectrum written in [13] and [6],

$$E^{(\infty)}_n=\hbar\omega\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots$$

with constant spacing $\Delta E_\infty=\hbar\omega$ and nondegenerate levels. In the RG–harmonic isomorphism of [13], this spectrum is the object whose level structure mirrors the RG's momentum-shell hierarchy.

**$p$-adic fixed point: a stated modeling assumption.** The Harmonic Paradigm's rung 7 invokes a $p$-adic oscillator organized on the Bruhat–Tits tree [12], but no work in this paper's source base states an explicit $p$-adic level formula, and we do not import one from memory. We therefore adopt, as a stated modeling assumption of this paper, a putative $p$-adic fixed point whose spectrum is organized on the levels of the Bruhat–Tits tree of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, which we take to be the $(p+1)$-regular tree on which $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ acts; this tree structure and the level-energy law below are definitions adopted by this paper, not results sourced from any cited work. We model the level-$n$ energy as

$$E^{(p)}_n=E_0\,p^{-n},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots$$

with $E_0>0$ a fixed ground-scale. The spacing is

$$\Delta E^{(p)}_n=E^{(p)}_n-E^{(p)}_{n+1}=E_0\,p^{-n}\left(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\right),$$

so adjacent gaps shrink geometrically: the ratio of the gap at level $n$ to the gap at level $n+1$ is $p$. The degeneracy of level $n$ is the number of tree vertices at distance $n$ from a base vertex: $g_p(0)=1$ and $g_p(n)=(p+1)p^{n-1}$ for $n\geq 1$. Every result that depends on this ladder (R2, and the spectral interpretation of R3) inherits its model-based status, and Section 6 states what would replace it.
