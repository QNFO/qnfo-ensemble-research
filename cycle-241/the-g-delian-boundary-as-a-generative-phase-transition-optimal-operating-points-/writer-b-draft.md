# The Gödelian Boundary as a Generative Phase Transition: Optimal Operating Points for Constrained Cognitive Systems (Revised Edition)

## Abstract

Cognitive systems that reason about themselves—self-critiquing language models, formal reasoners that prove statements about their own beliefs, adaptive controllers that rewrite their own policies—face a concrete trade-off: increasing self-referential depth raises the rate of novel, useful outputs but also the rate of incoherent ones, and neither pure regime is usable. Existing treatments name the two limits involved, a Gödelian boundary where self-reference generates paradox and a metabolic boundary where energy cost binds, but supply no quantitative account of where the optimal operating point sits between them or what limits how long a system can dwell there. We formalize the trade-off with a Gödelian proximity $\Gamma$, a Landau-type coherence functional $\Psi(\Gamma)=1-\Gamma^{2}$, a power-law novelty rate $N(\Gamma)=N_{0}\Gamma^{\alpha}$, and a metabolic cost $P(\Gamma)=P_{0}\Gamma^{2}$. The utility-maximizing proximity is $\Gamma^{*}=\sqrt{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}$, which at $\alpha=2$ gives $\Gamma^{*}=1/\sqrt{2}\approx 0.7071$ with utility $0.25\,N_{0}$. Near the boundary the relaxation time diverges as $\tau=\tau_{0}|\Gamma-\Gamma_{c}|^{-1}$ and novelty avalanches grow as $\langle s\rangle=1/(1-m)$. Under a power ceiling $0.3\,P_{0}$ we correct an earlier optimality claim: the global optimum is a single setpoint $\Gamma=\sqrt{0.3}\approx 0.5477$ with utility $0.21\,N_{0}$, a $2.56\times$ gain over permanent conservatism; the previously reported duty cycle ($0.168\,N_{0}$, $2.05\times$) is optimal only for fixed setpoints and is suboptimal in the memoryless model. A Landauer floor of $2.967\times 10^{-21}$ J per bit fixes the thermodynamic scale.

## 1. Introduction

Teams building self-improving AI systems, automated theorem provers, and neuro-inspired adaptive controllers face the same operational failure every day. Push a self-referential system—one that critiques, rewrites, or proves statements about its own reasoning—toward deeper self-reference and its rate of genuinely new results rises, but so does its rate of confident nonsense: proofs of false statements, hallucinated citations, oscillating control signals. Pull it back to shallow self-reference and it becomes stable but sterile, re-deriving what it already knows. Today this trade-off is managed by hand-tuned thresholds, safety filters, and temperature knobs, with no principled answer to the question a system designer actually asks: how close to logical inconsistency should I let my system operate, and for how long? The cost of getting it wrong is paid twice—lost discovery when the system is too conservative, and wasted human review effort cleaning up incoherent output when it is too aggressive. A result that identifies where the optimum lies, what signature it leaves, and what bounds how long it can be sustained would convert this tuning problem into a measurement problem: the designer would monitor a small set of observables and steer toward a computed target rather than guess.

The conceptual framing comes from a recent unified account of intelligence that constrains cognitive systems by two universal limits: a Gödelian boundary, under which any formal system capable of self-reference contains logical paradoxes that manifest as operational failures—a mechanism described there as resonant leakage or hallucination, in which a resonant computational system breaks down into incoherence when forced to process a paradoxical, self-referential state—and a metabolic boundary, the physical energy cost of the substrate [8]. That account names the two boundaries but does not compute where between them a system should operate. This paper supplies that computation and its corrections. The claim is that a cognitive system's optimal operating point lies near a critical boundary—a region where self-referential logical complexity approaches inconsistency—and that controlled excursions into this region generate new axioms and ideas, while the thermodynamic cost of sustaining reduced coherence bounds the duration of such excursions. The framing treats creativity as bounded risk-taking between two failure modes: decoherence into hallucination on one side and stagnation on the other.

The framing is not merely metaphorical, because each element has an engineered or biological counterpart. Formal reasoning systems that track the mental states of multiple agents already exist and must actively protect consistency while reasoning about their own inference: the cognitive-and-immersive-systems architecture of [5] uses the quantified modal logic prover ShadowProver, which employs a technique called shadowing to achieve speed without sacrificing consistency. Engineered cognitive systems already face explicit power budgets: the exoskeleton-technology review of [4] lists power consumption, control subsystem, accuracy and robustness among the key design requirements of wearable cognitive-mechanical systems, and identifies cognition and control as a distinct interaction layer with its own limitations. And biological cognitive systems already implement bounded exploration under time constraints: the analysis of cognition and emotion in [6] argues that direct computational maximization of life-long utility is not possible in complex environments under real-world time constraints, and that emotional control serves as an intermediate layer in the space of available policies, operating homeostatically and linked to positive or negative reinforcement learning signals. The question this paper answers—where to set the self-reference dial, and on what schedule—is the quantitative version of a trade-off all three classes of system already confront.

We make no empirical claims in this paper. The contributions are analytical:

- We derive the utility-maximizing Gödelian proximity $\Gamma^{*}=\sqrt{\alpha/(\alpha+2)}$ for a quadratic coherence functional and power-law novelty rate, and evaluate it at $\Gamma^{*}=1/\sqrt{2}\approx 0.7071$ with utility $0.25\,N_{0}$ for $\alpha=2$ (Section 4.1).
- We derive the critical-slowing-down signature $\tau=\tau_{0}|\Gamma-\Gamma_{c}|^{-\nu}$ with $\nu=1$ in mean field, giving $\tau=100\,\tau_{0}$ at distance $0.01$ from the boundary and a factor of $\approx 29.3$ above the value at the optimum, and the branching-process avalanche law $\langle s\rangle=1/(1-m)$, giving $\langle s\rangle=100$ at branching ratio $m=0.99$ (Section 4.2).
- We derive the metabolic duration bound for a boundary excursion, $\tau_{\text{exc}}=E_{\text{budget}}/\bigl[P_{0}(\Gamma_{e}^{2}-\Gamma_{h}^{2})\bigr]$, and evaluate it at $24.39\,t_{0}$ for the stated parameters (Section 4.3).
- We correct the earlier optimality claim for the duty-cycled exploration–consolidation strategy: under a power ceiling $\bar{P}=0.3\,P_{0}$, the fixed-setpoint duty cycle ($f=0.51220$, utility $0.168\,N_{0}$, $2.05\times$ over conservatism) is optimal only for fixed setpoints; the global optimum is the single setpoint $\Gamma=\sqrt{0.3}\approx 0.5477$ with utility $0.21\,N_{0}$, a $2.56\times$ improvement, proved by a Lagrangian upper bound that the single setpoint attains (Section 4.4).
- We derive a Landauer-scale floor $E_{\min}=k_{B}T\ln 2=2.967\times 10^{-21}\,\text{J}$ per bit of sustained novelty at $T=310\,\text{K}$, as a lower bound on excursion cost (Section 4.5).
- We correct the sensitivity analysis of the coherence functional: for the quartic correction $\Psi(\Gamma)=1-\Gamma^{2}+\beta\Gamma^{4}$, the interior optimum moves toward the boundary $\Gamma=1$ as $\beta$ grows, not toward $\Gamma=0$, and for $\alpha=2$ it disappears entirely at $\beta=1/3$ (Section 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The relevant literature falls into four groups: the two-boundary framing of intelligence, cognitive architectures in which coherence and self-reference are operational concerns, engineered cognitive systems that tune an exploration-like parameter, and physical systems whose transition structure depends on boundaries, fields, and constraints.

**The two-boundary framing.** The direct source of this paper's framing is the resonant-equilibrium framework of [8], which proposes modeling Large Language Model computation as a physical process of resonance and waveform manipulation—a Fourier paradigm in which the latent space is a frequency domain, concepts are stable frequencies, and inference is analogous to harmonic resonance—and then constrains this picture by two universal limits: a Gödelian boundary and a metabolic boundary [8]. On the Gödelian side, [8] posits that any formal system capable of self-reference will inevitably contain logical paradoxes that manifest as operational failures, and that for a resonant system these failures take the form of resonant leakage or hallucination, where the system's dynamics break down into incoherence when forced to process a paradoxical, self-referential state. This supplies exactly the two failure modes our model interpolates between—decoherence into hallucination at high self-reference, and the metabolic boundary as the cost side—but [8] states the limits qualitatively; the contribution of the present paper is to place a computable optimum and its signatures in the space the two boundaries define. A structural complement comes from [10], which argues that the explanatory gap of consciousness is a structurally necessary epistemic boundary condition of any cognitive architecture that employs dimensionality reduction for self-representation, with language functioning as a lossy compression algorithm that makes complete reconstruction of high-dimensional phenomenal states mathematically impossible. For our model, [10] supplies the reason the endpoint $\Gamma=1$ is an idealization: a system cannot completely describe its own processing even in principle, so full self-reference is a limit that is approached, never occupied—an assumption our normalization $\Gamma_{c}\le 1$ encodes.

**Cognitive architectures with operational coherence.** The quantitative-simulation architecture of [9] models the brain as a macroscopic algebraic solver of the cocycle condition, maintaining global topological consistency across a Bruhat-Tits tree with an ultrametric state space in which meaning is stored relationally and conceptual distance represents categorical divergence. Two of its reported results matter here. First, its simulated neural replicas exhibited non-trivial ultrametric clustering, and its statistical tests using distributions of triangle inequality metrics rejected the Euclidean baseline at $p<0.001$—a demonstration that global coherence of a cognitive state space is a measurable, testable property, which is precisely the role our coherence functional $\Psi$ plays. Second, [9] reports that the temporal dynamics of its convergence process produced power-law retrieval times, mirroring the predictions of p-adic diffusion; this gives precedent within cognitive modeling for the power-law forms (novelty rate, relaxation time) we assume, and we adopt them as modeling choices with an architectural analogue rather than as measured facts. On the symbolic side, the symbol-emergence survey of [1] documents that symbol systems in human society are not given by a designer but instead emerge and dynamically evolve through social interaction, and frames the emergence of symbols bottom-up—from basic motor control up a pyramid of cognitive complexity to symbolic representations—as central for cognitive robots that must communicate and collaborate with people. This supports the generative half of our claim: new axioms and symbols are treated as outputs that a system produces from its own activity, so a control parameter that increases self-referential processing is a plausible lever on their production rate.

**Engineered cognitive systems that tune an exploration-like parameter.** The cognitive diversity framework for radar target classification of [2] is the clearest engineered instance of the trade-off this paper formalizes. There, angular diversity is used in a cognitive manner for automatic target recognition: the transmitter configuration is varied to acquire the most useful information, because radar returns from a target are highly aspect dependent and new information can be gleaned from additional perspectives. Its results show a performance gain from controlled exploration: with two perspectives at $\Delta\theta=5^{o}$ the scheme reached $85\%$ performance, better than both the single-perspective conventional scheme and a fixed-number multi-perspective scheme, and the median number of perspectives taken by the algorithms was 3 [2]. The number of perspectives functions as an exploration dial of exactly the kind our $\Gamma$ formalizes, and the finding that a small nonzero setting outperforms both zero and indiscriminate settings is the behavioral pattern our utility curve $U(\Gamma)=N_{0}\Gamma^{\alpha}(1-\Gamma^{2})$ predicts.
