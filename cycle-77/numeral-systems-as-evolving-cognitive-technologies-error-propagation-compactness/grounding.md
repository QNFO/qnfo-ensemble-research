## Research idea

Numeral Systems as Evolving Cognitive Technologies, Not Final Optima. Claim: positional (Arabic) notation is a historically contingent 'survivor,' not an optimal endpoint; additive systems like Roman numerals offer cognitive advantages (error resistance, magnitude salience, no zero placeholder) that positional notation sacrifices. Question: can these tradeoffs be formalized and measured? A paper could (1) define error models (transposition, digit-drop, misreading) and prove additive systems have lower error propagation under specific noise channels, while positional wins on compactness and arithmetic complexity; (2) run human-subject or agent simulations comparing transcription/verification accuracy across Roman, positional, and hybrid notations; (3) frame numeral-system evolution as a fitness landscape over task environments (ledger-keeping vs. calculation), showing no single dominant system—paralleling biological evolution's lack of global optimum. This would give a rigorous basis for the conjecture that notation design remains an open optimization problem.
