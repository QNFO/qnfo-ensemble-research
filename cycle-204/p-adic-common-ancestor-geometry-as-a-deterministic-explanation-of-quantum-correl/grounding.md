## Research idea

P-adic Common-Ancestor Geometry Explains Quantum Correlation Without Many-Worlds. Conjecture: quantum entanglement correlations can be modeled as shared ancestry in a fixed p-adic (ultrametric) tree, where branches encode past relational structure rather than co-existing future worlds. Entangled particles 'share a common ancestor node' in hierarchical state-space, yielding Bell-type correlations with local determinism and no superluminal signaling, while only the realized branch exists in the present. This reframes Many-Worlds branching as a misprojection of historical geometry into the future. Testability: (1) derive Bell-inequality violations from a p-adic-valued hidden-variable model with ultrametric distance metrics; (2) simulate entangled-pair measurements on p-adic trees and compare correlation functions with quantum predictions; (3) formally analyze whether ultrametric common-ancestor structure reproduces Tsirelson bounds or fails, distinguishing it from classical local hidden variables.
