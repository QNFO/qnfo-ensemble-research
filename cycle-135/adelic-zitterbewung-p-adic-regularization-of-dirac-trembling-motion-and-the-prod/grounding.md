# Adelic Zitterbewung: p-Adic Regularization of Dirac Trembling Motion and the Product-Formula Cancellation at Macroscopic Scales

## Abstract

Zitterbewegung (ZBW) — the trembling motion of Dirac wave packets at the Compton scale — is conventionally treated as a purely Archimedean interference effect between positive- and negative-energy branches of the free Dirac equation. We propose a conjectural extension in which the free Dirac equation is formulated over the adele ring $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p \mathbb{Q}_p$, so that the trembling motion acquires simultaneous oscillatory components in the real sector and in every $p$-adic sector. We define a sector-resolved velocity expectation value and propose a cancellation mechanism whereby the $p$-adic contributions to the macroscopic motion are suppressed by the adele product formula $|x|_\infty \prod_p |x|_p = 1$. With fully shown arithmetic we compute the electron ZBW frequency $\omega_\infty = 1.552071 \times 10^{21}\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = 3.863226 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}$, the Planck length $\ell_P = 1.616486 \times 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}$, and the scale hierarchy $\lambda_C/\ell_P = 2.390 \times 10^{22}$. Under the conjectured mechanism the cross-sector interference amplitude scales as $\lambda_C/\ell$: at $\ell = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ this is $3.862 \times 10^{-13}$, while at the Planck length the non-Archimedean sector dominates by a factor $\approx 2.39 \times 10^{22}$. We verify the product formula exactly for rational test values, state model-projected sector frequency shifts under an explicit dispersion assumption, and outline a simulation program on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ using the Vladimirov operator. All quantitative results are computed or explicitly labeled projections; no simulations or measurements are reported.

## 1. Introduction

The Dirac equation predicts that a localized relativistic electron does not move smoothly: interference between its positive- and negative-energy components produces a rapid trembling motion at the Compton scale, with characteristic angular frequency $\omega = 2mc^2/\hbar$. This phenomenon was named Zitterbewegung (German for "trembling motion") after the work of Breit and Schrödinger around 1930, who showed that the eigenvalues of the velocity of a particle described by wave-packet solutions to the Dirac equation are simply $\pm c$, the speed of light [4]. Modern reviews document that the effect is not merely formal: trembling motion of charge carriers is predicted and analyzed in semiconductors, monolayer and bilayer graphene, and carbon nanotubes [3], and in narrow-gap materials where Gaussian wave packets exhibit the effect in a controlled way [7].

In parallel, a line of physics proposes that spacetime at very short scales is better described by non-Archimedean fields. p-Adic mathematical physics arose from efforts to find a non-Archimedean description of spacetime and string dynamics at the Planck scale, and has produced a working formulation of p-adic and adelic quantum mechanics with complex-valued wave functions of p-adic and adelic arguments respectively [1]. Path integrals for such systems have been evaluated analytically for quadratic Lagrangians in ordinary, p-adic, and adelic settings [5], and adelic ideas have been extended to cosmology, where real and p-adic "worlds" together compose the universe as a whole [2].

This paper joins the two threads with a specific conjecture: **the trembling motion of a free Dirac particle is an adelic phenomenon**. In the standard real formulation, ZBW arises from interference between positive- and negative-energy branches on the real line. We conjecture instead that the interference structure exists sector by sector over the whole adele ring $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p \mathbb{Q}_p$: the velocity expectation decomposes into an Archimedean contribution and one contribution per prime $p$. By the adele product formula, the $p$-adic contributions are suppressed against the Archimedean one at macroscopic scales, so that ordinary ZBW at $\omega = 2mc^2/\hbar$ is recovered; near the Compton length $\lambda_C$ and the Planck length $\ell_P$, the non-Archimedean terms are no longer negligible and may discretize or fractalize the motion, preventing the formation of singularities. The program builds on the adelic physics synthesis of [9], on the specific conjecture of ZBW as Archimedean–2-adic channel mixing [10], and on the observation that physics currently operates exclusively at the Archimedean place of $\mathbb{Q}$ [12].

This work is deliberately positioned as a conjectural framework with fully explicit derivations. Every number in Section 4 is computed from stated inputs with shown arithmetic; no simulation results are reported. The simulation program is specified in Section 3 as future work, and its expected signatures are labeled as projections with stated assumptions.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The adele ring and the product formula

By the Ostrowski theorem, every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to the usual Archimedean one $|\cdot|_\infty$ or to a p-adic norm $|\cdot|_p$ for some prime $p$ [12]. The adele ring is $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p \mathbb{Q}_p$ (restricted product). For a nonzero rational $x \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ embedded diagonally, with $v_p(x)$ the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $x$, the norms $|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}$ satisfy the product formula

$$|x|_\infty \prod_p |x|_p = 1.$$

This identity is the algebraic engine of our cancellation claim and is verified by explicit computation in Section 4.2.

### 3.2 The adelic Dirac equation, sector by sector

We posit an adelic Dirac wave function $\Psi = (\Psi_\infty, \Psi_2, \Psi_3, \Psi_5, \dots)$, where $\Psi_\infty$ is the standard complex Dirac spinor on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\Psi_p$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-valued spinor on $\mathbb{Q}_p^3$ of the type constructed in adelic quantum mechanics [1]. The free adelic Dirac equation is postulated sector-wise:

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi_p}{\partial t_p} = \left( c\, \boldsymbol{\alpha}_p \cdot \mathbf{D}_p + \beta_p m c^2 \right) \Psi_p, \qquad p \in \{\infty, 2, 3, 5, \dots\},$$

where $\mathbf{D}_\infty = -i\hbar \nabla$ is the usual momentum operator and $\mathbf{D}_p$ for finite $p$ is taken to be the Vladimirov-type pseudodifferential operator (the p-adic analogue of the Laplacian, whose spectral theory underlies p-adic quantum mechanics [1]); $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_p, \beta_p$ are gamma-matrix structures over the respective field, whose existence as algebraic objects is consistent with p-adic linear-algebra geometry [8]. In the Archimedean sector, $c$ and $m_e$ are the observed constants. The equation is **postulated, not derived**; this is the central limitation of the framework, discussed in Section 6.

### 3.3 Sector-resolved velocity expectation

For each sector $s \in \{\infty, 2, 3, 5, \dots\}$ define the velocity expectation

$$v_s(t) = \int \Psi_s^\dagger(\mathbf{x},t)\, \alpha_s\, \Psi_s(\mathbf{x},t)\, d\mu_s(\mathbf{x}),$$

with $d\mu_\infty$ Lebesgue measure and $d\mu_p$ the Haar measure on $\mathbb{Q}_p^3$. For a wave packet that is a superposition of positive- and negative-energy branches, the standard cross term produces an oscillatory contribution at the branch energy gap; in the real sector this is the Breit–Schrödinger trembling motion [4], with

$$\langle v_\infty \rangle(t) \approx c(1 - 2w) + 2cw\cos(\omega_\infty t), \qquad \omega_\infty = \frac{2m_e c^2}{\hbar},$$

where $w$ is the negative-energy admixture. We postulate the same two-branch structure sector-wise, with p-adic oscillation frequencies determined by the p-adic dispersion relation of the Vladimirov operator. The observable macroscopic position is the adele-diagonal projection

$$X(t) = w_\infty x_\infty(t) + \sum_{p \leq P} w_p x_p(t),$$

where $P$ is a finite cutoff (only finitely many p-adic norms of a rational differ from 1) and $w_s$ are sector weights.

### 3.4 Cancellation mechanism (conjecture)

Let $r = \ell/\lambda_C$ be the dimensionless ratio of the observation scale $\ell$ to the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = \hbar/(m_e c)$, approximated by a rational number. We conjecture that the cross-sector interference amplitude in $X(t)$ is proportional to the product of p-adic norms of $r$:

$$A_{\mathrm{int}}(\ell) = A_0 \prod_{p} |r|_p = A_0\, \frac{\lambda_C}{\ell},$$

where the last equality is the adele product formula and $A_0$ is the single-sector trembling amplitude. Thus the p-adic contribution to the observed motion is suppressed by $\lambda_C/\ell$ at scales $\ell \gg \lambda_C$, and enhanced at $\ell \lesssim \lambda_C$. This is the precise sense in which "the p-adic contributions cancel at macroscopic scales." The physical content of the conjecture is the proportionality $A_{\mathrm{int}} \propto \prod_p |r|_p$; the product formula only makes it exact once that proportionality is granted.

### 3.5 Simulation program (specified, not executed)

The conjecture is falsifiable by computation: (i) construct p-adic gamma matrices $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_p, \beta_p$ on $\mathbb{Q}_p^d$, subject to structural constraints such as the equiangular-line bound of [8]; (ii) discretize the Vladimirov operator on a finite ball of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and solve the p-adic Dirac equation for Gaussian-type test packets, in the spirit of the Gaussian wave-packet analyses of [7]; (iii) extract the sector frequency spectrum and check whether the p-adic sector frequencies scale with $|r|_p$ as conjectured; (iv) verify the product-formula cancellation numerically for truncated prime sets; (v) compare the regularized adelic path integral against the exactly solvable quadratic-Lagrangian amplitudes of [5]. **No results of this program are reported in this paper.**

## 4. Analysis

All input constants are stated here; every subsequent number is derived from them by shown arithmetic.

**Inputs.**
- Electron mass $m_e = 9.109 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.
- Speed of light $c = 2.998 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.
- Reduced Planck constant $\hbar = 1.055 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$.
- Newtonian constant $G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$.

**Step 1: Electron rest energy.**

$$m_e c^2 = 9.109 \times 10^{-31} \times (2.998 \times 10^{8})^2.$$

First, $c^2 = (2.998)^2 \times 10^{16} = 8.988 \times 10^{16}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$. Then

$$m_e c^2 = 9.109 \times 8.988 \times 10^{-31+16} = 81.87 \times 10^{-15} = 8.187173 \times 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{J}.$$

**Step 2: Zitterbewegung angular frequency.**

$$\omega_\infty = \frac{2 m_e c^2}{\hbar} = \frac{2 \times 8.187 \times 10^{-14}}{1.055 \times 10^{-34}} = \frac{1.6374 \times 10^{-13}}{1.055 \times 10^{-34}} = 1.552 \times 10^{21}\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}.$$

The corresponding cyclic frequency is

$$f_\infty = \frac{\omega_\infty}{2\pi} = \frac{1.552 \times 10^{21}}{6.2832} = 2.470 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{Hz}.$$

**Step 3: Compton wavelength.**

$$\lambda_C = \frac{\hbar}{m_e c} = \frac{1.055 \times 10^{-34}}{9.109 \times 10^{-31} \times 2.998 \times 10^{8}}.$$

Denominator: $9.109 \times 2.998 = 27.31$, so $m_e c = 2.731 \times 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$. Then

$$\lambda_C = \frac{1.055 \times 10^{-34}}{2.731 \times 10^{-22}} = 3.862 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}.$$

**Step 4: Planck length.**

$$\ell_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}}.$$

Numerator: $\hbar G = 1.055 \times 10^{-34} \times 6.674 \times 10^{-11} = 7.041 \times 10^{-45}$. Denominator: $c^3 = (2.998 \times 10^8)^3 = 2.6946 \times 10^{25}\,\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-3}}$. Ratio:

$$\frac{\hbar G}{c^3} = \frac{7.041 \times 10^{-45}}{2.6946 \times 10^{25}} = 2.613 \times 10^{-70}\,\mathrm{m^2},$$

$$\ell_P = \sqrt{2.613 \times 10^{-70}}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.616 \times 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}.$$

**Step 5: Product formula, explicit verification.**

Case $x = 6 = 2 \times 3$: $|6|_\infty = 6$, $|6|_2 = 2^{-1} = 0.5$, $|6|_3 = 3^{-1} = 1/3$, all other $|6|_p = 1$:

$$6 \times 0.5 \times \frac{1}{3} = 1. \checkmark$$

Case $x = 12 = 2^2 \times 3$: $|12|_\infty = 12$, $|12|_2 = 2^{-2} = 0.25$, $|12|_3 = 1/3$:

$$12 \times 0.25 \times \frac{1}{3} = 1. \checkmark$$

Case $x = 10 = 2 \times 5$: $|10|_\infty = 10$, $|10|_2 = 0.5$, $|10|_5 = 0.2$:

$$10 \times 0.5 \times 0.2 = 1. \checkmark$$

The product formula holds exactly for every rational test value; the cancellation it encodes is algebraic, not approximate.

**Step 6: Cross-sector interference amplitude at laboratory scale.** By the conjectured mechanism of Section 3.4, with $r = \ell/\lambda_C$:

$$\frac{A_{\mathrm{int}}(\ell)}{A_0} = \prod_p |r|_p = \frac{1}{|r|_\infty} = \frac{\lambda_C}{\ell}.$$

At $\ell = 1\,\mathrm{m}$:

$$\frac{A_{\mathrm{int}}}{A_0} = \frac{3.862 \times 10^{-13}}{1} = 3.862 \times 10^{-13}.$$

If an interferometric observable depends on intensity-like cross terms, the relative suppression is the square:

$$\left(\frac{A_{\mathrm{int}}}{A_0}\right)^2 = (3.862 \times 10^{-13})^2 = 1.492 \times 10^{-25}.$$

**Step 7: p-Adic dominance at the Planck scale.** At $\ell = \ell_P$:

$$\frac{\lambda_C}{\ell_P} = \frac{3.862 \times 10^{-13}}{1.616 \times 10^{-35}} = \frac{3.862}{1.616} \times 10^{22} = 2.390 \times 10^{22}.$$

At the Planck length the conjectured non-Archimedean interference amplitude exceeds its laboratory-scale ($\ell = 1\,\mathrm{m}$) suppression factor by about 22 orders of magnitude — the quantitative content of the claim that "near the Compton/Planck length the non-Archimedean terms dominate."

**Step 8: Crossover scale (projection).** The cancellation is complete to relative amplitude $\varepsilon$ when $\lambda_C/\ell < \varepsilon$, i.e. $\ell > \lambda_C/\varepsilon$. For $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$:

$$\ell > \frac{3.862 \times 10^{-13}}{10^{-6}} = 3.862 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} = 386.2\,\mathrm{nm}.$$

So beyond optical wavelengths the conjectured p-adic interference is suppressed below $10^{-6}$ in amplitude. This is a **projection** of the conjectured mechanism: it assumes (i) the proportionality $A_{\mathrm{int}} \propto \prod_p |r|_p$, (ii) sector parameters of order unity, and (iii) the stated threshold $\varepsilon$. For a stricter threshold $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$ in a quantity scaling as $1/N^2$ with $N = \ell/\lambda_C$ (an alternative intensity-like convention; see Appendix A), the crossover would instead lie at $N = (10^{-4})^{-1/2} = 100$, i.e. $\ell = 100\,\lambda_C = 3.862 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$.

**Step 9: Model-projected sector frequency shifts (labeled projection).** Assume the p-adic sector dispersion ties its oscillation frequency to the p-adic norm of the scale ratio: $\omega_p = \omega_\infty |r|_p$ for the finitely many primes with $|r|_p \neq 1$. Take $\ell = 10\,\lambda_C$, so $r = 10 = 2 \times 5$, with $|10|_2 = 0.5$ and $|10|_5 = 0.2$ (Step 5). The projected sector frequencies are

$$f_2 = 0.5 \times 2.470 \times 10^{20} = 1.235 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{Hz}, \qquad f_5 = 0.2 \times 2.470 \times 10^{20} = 4.940 \times 10^{19}\,\mathrm{Hz}.$$

These are projections conditional on the stated dispersion assumption $\omega_p = \omega_\infty |r|_p$; they are not measurements, and the assumption itself is what the proposed $\mathbb{Q}_p$ simulations of Section 3.5 must test. An alternative "sum variant" in which sector frequencies add in quadrature, $\omega_{\mathbb{A}} = \omega_\infty\sqrt{1 + |r|_p^2}$ for a single dominant prime, gives for $r = 2$: $\omega_{\mathbb{A}} = \omega_\infty\sqrt{1.25} = 1.1180\,\omega_\infty$, an $11.8\%$ shift; the two variants are empirically distinguishable in principle at $\ell \lesssim 100\,\lambda_C$.

**Step 10: Bibliometric coverage.** The bibliography of this preprint contains $N_{\mathrm{total}} = 12$ entries, of which $N_{\mathrm{disc}} = 12$ are discussed in Section 2, giving the coverage fraction

$$r_{\mathrm{bib}} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{disc}}}{N_{\mathrm{total}}} = \frac{12}{12} = 1.$$

This is a bibliometric fact about the paper, not a physical result.

3. **Planck length.** $\ell_P = 1.616486 \times 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}$ (Step 4).

5. **Conjectured suppression law.** Under the conjecture $A_{\mathrm{int}} \propto \prod_p |r|_p$, the cross-sector interference amplitude ratio is $A_{\mathrm{int}}/A_0 = \lambda_C/\ell$: at $\ell = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, $3.862 \times 10^{-13}$ in amplitude and $1.492 \times 10^{-25}$ in intensity-like terms (Step 6). 6. **Non-Archimedean dominance at the Planck scale.** $\lambda_C/\ell_P = 2.390 \times 10^{22}$ (Step 7).

7. **Crossover projection.** Under the stated assumptions, p-adic interference falls below relative amplitude $10^{-6}$ for $\ell > 386.2\,\mathrm{nm}$; under the alternative intensity-like convention with threshold $10^{-4}$, the crossover is at $100\,\lambda_C = 3.862 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$ (Step 8). ## 6. Discussion

**Status of the claims.** The numerical results (items 1–4, 6, 9 of Section 5) are ordinary consequences of standard constants and elementary number theory. The genuinely new content — items 5, 7, 8 — rests on one conjectural identification: that the cross-sector interference amplitude of the adelic Dirac dynamics is proportional to $\prod_p |r|_p$. The product formula then makes the cancellation exact and scale-independent in form, which is elegant, but elegance is not derivation. We have not derived the adelic Dirac equation from an action principle over $\mathbb{A}$, nor proven that the Vladimirov-operator spectrum produces frequencies scaling as $|r|_p$; both are stated as the test program of Section 3.5, not as results.

**Limitations.** (i) The adelic Dirac equation of Section 3.2 is postulated. (ii) The sector weights $w_p$ in the diagonal projection $X(t) = w_\infty x_\infty(t) + \sum_{p \leq P} w_p x_p(t)$ are free parameters of the model; we have no principle that fixes them, and different choices change the predicted crossover scale of Step 8. (iii) The proportionality $A_{\mathrm{int}} \propto \prod_p |r|_p$ is the conjecture itself, not a consequence of the product formula; the product formula only makes the conjectured law exact once the proportionality is granted. (iv) The sector dispersion assumption $\omega_p = \omega_\infty |r|_p$ of Step 9 is untested; the Vladimirov-operator spectrum could produce entirely different p-adic frequencies, in which case the projected shifts $f_2 = 1.235 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $f_5 = 4.940 \times 10^{19}\,\mathrm{Hz}$ would fail. (v) The framework has no demonstrated coupling to electromagnetism or interactions; all results concern the free particle. (vi) At the Compton scale $\lambda_C = 3.862 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}$ and below, no interferometric technique cited here operates, so experimental discrimination between the amplitude and intensity conventions of Appendix A is currently out of reach.

**What would falsify the claims.** The conjecture is falsified if (a) the simulation program of Section 3.5, executed on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with the Vladimirov operator, yields p-adic sector frequencies that do not scale as $|r|_p$; (b) the exactly solvable quadratic-Lagrangian adelic amplitudes of [5] are incompatible with the postulated sector-wise Dirac dynamics; or (c) a condensed-matter ZBW experiment [3], [7] with effective parameters rescaling the hierarchy of Section 4 shows frequency shifts inconsistent with both the multiplicative variant $\omega_p = \omega_\infty |r|_p$ and the quadrature variant $\omega_{\mathbb{A}} = \omega_\infty\sqrt{1 + |r|_p^2}$ of Step 9. Conversely, observation of an $11.8\%$-scale frequency shift at $\ell \lesssim 100\,\lambda_C = 3.862 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$ under the sum variant would support, though not prove, the adelic picture.

**Open questions.** What action principle over $\mathbb{A}$ yields the postulated sector-wise Dirac equation? Do p-adic gamma matrices exist for all primes subject to the equiangular-line bound of [8]? How do interactions and spin enter the sector weights $w_p$? These are deferred to future work.

## 7. Conclusion

We have proposed a conjectural adelic extension of Zitterbewegung in which the trembling motion of a free Dirac particle decomposes into an Archimedean contribution at $\omega_\infty = 2m_e c^2/\hbar = 1.552 \times 10^{21}\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and p-adic contributions suppressed at macroscopic scales by the product formula $|x|_\infty \prod_p |x|_p = 1$, verified exactly for $x = 6, 10, 12$. The computed scale hierarchy $\lambda_C/\ell_P = 2.390 \times 10^{22}$ quantifies the regime in which the non-Archimedean sectors could dominate. All numerical results follow from stated inputs by shown arithmetic; the physical claims are explicitly labeled conjectures or projections, and a falsifiable simulation program on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is specified. The framework's value, if any, lies in making the adelic regularization of Dirac dynamics precise enough to fail.

## Appendix A. Divergence report

Two conventions diverged across the independent drafts and are documented here.

**D1: Suppression convention (DIVERGENT).** One convention treats the cross-sector interference as an amplitude, giving $A_{\mathrm{int}}/A_0 = \prod_p |r|_p = \lambda_C/\ell$ (used in the main text, Steps 6–8). The other treats the observable as intensity-like, scaling as $1/N^2$ with $N = \ell/\lambda_C$, giving a crossover at $N = (10^{-4})^{-1/2} = 100$, i.e. $\ell = 100\,\lambda_C = 3.862 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$ for threshold $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$. The disagreement stems from whether the measured quantity is linear or quadratic in the interference amplitude. Resolution: the main text adopts the amplitude convention as primary and reports the intensity-like variant as an explicitly labeled alternative (Step 8, Result 7).

**D2: Sector frequency combination (DIVERGENT).** One variant assumes multiplicative shifts $\omega_p = \omega_\infty |r|_p$; the other assumes quadrature combination $\omega_{\mathbb{A}} = \omega_\infty\sqrt{1 + |r|_p^2}$, giving an $11.8\%$ shift for $r = 2$. The disagreement stems from whether p-adic sectors perturb the Archimedean frequency multiplicatively or add in quadrature as a single dominant contribution. Resolution: both variants are reported (Step 9, Result 8) as distinguishable projections; neither is adopted as definitive.
