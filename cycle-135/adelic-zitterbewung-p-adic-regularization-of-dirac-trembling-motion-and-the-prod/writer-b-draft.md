# Adelic Zitterbewegung: p-Adic Regularization of Dirac Trembling Motion and the Product-Formula Cancellation at Macroscopic Scales

## Abstract

Zitterbewegung (ZBW) — the trembling motion of Dirac wave packets at the Compton scale — is conventionally treated as a purely Archimedean interference effect between the positive- and negative-energy branches of the free Dirac equation. Missing from the standard account is any mechanism by which non-Archimedean completions of the rationals could enter the dynamics, and any quantitative statement of when such contributions would or would not matter. We propose a conjectural framework in which the free Dirac equation is formulated over the adele ring $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p \mathbb{Q}_p$, so that the trembling motion acquires simultaneous oscillatory components in the real sector and in every $p$-adic sector, and we define a sector-resolved velocity expectation value together with a cancellation mechanism in which the $p$-adic contributions to macroscopic motion are suppressed by the adele product formula $|x|_\infty \prod_p |x|_p = 1$. With fully shown arithmetic we compute the electron ZBW angular frequency $\omega_\infty = 1.55207 \times 10^{21}\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ (period $4.0483 \times 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{s}$), the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = 3.86322 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m}$, the Planck length $\ell_P = 1.6166 \times 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}$, and the scale hierarchy $\lambda_C/\ell_P = 2.390 \times 10^{22}$. Under the conjectured mechanism the cross-sector interference amplitude scales as $\lambda_C/\ell$: at $\ell = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ this is $3.863 \times 10^{-13}$, while at the Planck length the non-Archimedean sector dominates by a factor $\approx 2.39 \times 10^{22}$. We verify the product formula exactly for rational test values, state model-projected sector frequency shifts under an explicit dispersion assumption, and specify a simulation program on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ using the Vladimirov operator. All quantitative results are computed or explicitly labeled projections; no simulations or measurements are reported. The framework makes the adelic regularization of Dirac dynamics precise enough to fail.

## 1. Introduction

A localized relativistic electron does not move smoothly. In the Dirac equation, the velocity operators do not commute with the free Hamiltonian, so interference between the positive- and negative-energy components of any wave packet produces a rapid periodic oscillation of the velocity and position at the Compton scale, with angular frequency $\omega = 2mc^2/\hbar$ and amplitude of order the Compton wavelength [3], [7]. This is Zitterbewegung (ZBW, German for "trembling motion"). Who faces this phenomenon today? Not vacuum electron physicists: the ZB of a free relativistic electron in a vacuum "is probably not directly observable for year to come and one has to recourse to its simulations" [3]. The working community is instead the condensed-matter and quantum-emulation community: ZB of charge carriers has been established as "a real and universal phenomenon" in semiconductors, monolayer and bilayer graphene, and carbon nanotubes [3], with frequencies set by band gaps and amplitudes reaching tens of nanometers in narrow-gap semiconductors [7]. What blocks progress is interpretive: ZBW is treated as an interference artifact of the real-number formulation, with no principled statement of what, if anything, the arithmetic of the underlying number field contributes to the dynamics. If a result held that tied the observable trembling motion to the arithmetic of $\mathbb{Q}$ across all of its completions, it would change what ZBW experiments are testing — from probing band structure alone to probing a putative adelic structure of quantum dynamics, and it would supply a concrete regularization mechanism at short distances.

Two existing threads make such a result conceivable. First, p-adic mathematical physics arose precisely from efforts to find a non-Archimedean description of spacetime and string dynamics at the Planck scale, and its main achievement is a working formulation of p-adic and adelic quantum mechanics with complex-valued wave functions of p-adic and adelic arguments [1]; path integrals for such systems have been evaluated analytically for quadratic Lagrangians in ordinary, p-adic, and adelic settings, with a formalism that is invariant under interchange of the number field [5]. Second, a recent program argues that "physics is adelic" — that the Archimedean description is the $\infty$-place readout of a richer ultrametric structure — and consolidates a six-paper chain in which ZBW is treated as a p-adic observable with falsifiable readout protocols [10]. A specific conjecture within that program reinterprets ZBW as mixing between the Archimedean and 2-adic completions, built on a two-channel effective Hamiltonian whose beat frequency reduces to the standard $\omega_{\mathrm{ZBW}} = 2E/\hbar$ under an explicit calibration condition [11].

This paper joins the threads with a definite quantitative claim: **the trembling motion of a free Dirac particle is conjecturally an adelic phenomenon, and the adele product formula controls its scale dependence**. We posit that the velocity expectation decomposes sector by sector over $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p \mathbb{Q}_p$: one Archimedean contribution and one per prime $p$. We then conjecture that the cross-sector interference amplitude in the observable position is proportional to the product of $p$-adic norms of the dimensionless scale ratio $r = \ell/\lambda_C$, which by the product formula equals $\lambda_C/\ell$. The consequences are computed exactly: suppression by $3.863 \times 10^{-13}$ at laboratory scales, dominance by $2.390 \times 10^{22}$ at the Planck scale, and a conjectured crossover near optical wavelengths. The work is deliberately positioned as a conjectural framework with fully explicit derivations; every number is computed from stated inputs with shown arithmetic, and the simulation program that could falsify the conjecture is specified but not executed.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**p-Adic and adelic quantum mechanics.** The foundational review [1] explains that p-adic mathematical physics emerged in 1987 from efforts to find a non-Archimedean approach to spacetime and string dynamics at the Planck scale, and that among its main achievements is p-adic and adelic quantum mechanics. Its survey of formulations is directly relevant to our construction: quantum mechanics on $p$-adic spaces admits two main approaches, with $p$-adic-valued wave functions (introduced by Vladimirov and Volovich and developed by Khrennikov) and with complex-valued wave functions, and the review states that the complex-valued approach "is more suitable for connection with ordinary quantum mechanics" [1]. We adopt exactly that choice: our sector wave functions $\Psi_p$ are $\mathbb{C}$-valued, so that the standard Hilbert-space machinery and the interference mechanism of ZBW carry over sector by sector. The review also records that with complex-valued wave functions one cannot construct a direct analogue of the Schrödinger equation with a $p$-adic Heisenberg algebra [1], which is one reason our sector dynamics is postulated rather than derived.

**Adelic path integrals.** Reference [5] considers Feynman path integrals in ordinary, p-adic and adelic quantum mechanics, evaluates the probability amplitudes $\mathcal{K}(x'',t'';x',t')$ for two-dimensional systems with quadratic Lagrangians analytically, generalizes the expressions to any finite-dimensional spaces, and presents the formulas in a form invariant under interchange of the relevant data. Its concluding section states that "the formalism of ordinary and $p$-adic path integrals can be regarded as the same at different levels of evaluation, and the obtained results have the same form" [5]. For our program this matters twice over: any regularized adelic path integral we propose can be checked against the exactly solvable quadratic cases of [5], and the number-field invariance that [5] demonstrates at the level of path integrals is the property our conjecture extends to the dynamical level of ZBW.

**Adelic cosmology and p-adic worlds.** Reference [2] reviews p-adic and adelic cosmology, covering p-adic and adelic aspects of gravity, classical cosmology, quantum mechanics, quantum cosmology and the wave function of the universe, and introduces "p-adic worlds" made of p-adic matter distinct from the real world of ordinary matter, with real and p-adic worlds making up the universe as a whole. Its definition of an adele as an infinite sequence $x = (x_\infty, x_2, \ldots, x_p, \ldots)$ with $x_\infty \in \mathbb{R}$, $x_p \in \mathbb{Q}_p$, and $x_p \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ for all but finitely many primes [2] is the precise object over which we formulate the sector-resolved Dirac equation. The review also reports a striking structural fact: a nonsingular form of odd degree $r$ represents all $p$-adic numbers nontrivially when the number of variables satisfies $n \geq D = r^2 + 1$, giving $D = 10$ for $r = 3$ and $D = 26$ for $r = 5$, dimensions that coincide with the critical dimensions of superstring and bosonic string theory [2] — evidence that adelic arithmetic constraints already reach into established physics.

**Zitterbewegung: origins.** Reference [4] records that around 1930 both Gregory Breit and Erwin Schrödinger showed that the eigenvalues of the velocity of a particle described by wave-packet solutions to the Dirac equation are simply $\pm c$, and that this led Schrödinger to coin the term Zitterbewegung, in which particles of matter zig-zag back and forth at the speed of light. Beyond the founding observation, [4] develops a statistical treatment in $1+1$ dimensions in which the particle moves left or right at speed $c$ with probabilities $\Pr(R) = \frac{1}{2}(1+v)$ and $\Pr(L) = \frac{1}{2}(1-v)$, and shows that macroscopic motion is consistent with special relativity through the velocity addition rule, suggesting that motion itself can be regarded as a statistical-mechanical phenomenon [4]. This statistical framing is congenial to our multi-sector extension: if ZBW admits a probabilistic decomposition, a decomposition into number-field sectors is a natural further refinement.

**Zitterbewegung in condensed matter.** Reference [3] reviews ZBW of electrons in semiconductors, presents a brief history beginning with Schrödinger's 1930 observation that the velocity operators do not commute with the free-electron Hamiltonian, considers trembling motion in semirelativistic and spin systems, and elaborates ZB of charge carriers in monolayer and bilayer graphene and carbon nanotubes. Its conclusion states that ZB is "a real and universal phenomenon" whose universal background is "an interference of states belonging to positive and negative energies," while the vacuum ZB of Schrödinger is probably not directly observable for years to come, so that one must resort to simulations, for which a proof of principle has been carried out [3]. Reference [7] reviews the theory of ZB of charge carriers in narrow-gap materials — nearly free electrons in a periodic potential, InSb-type semiconductors, bilayer and monolayer graphene, carbon nanotubes — and gives the quantitative anchors we use: the vacuum ZB frequency is about $\omega = 2m_0 c^2/\hbar$ with amplitude about the Compton wavelength $\lambda_c = \hbar/m_0 c \approx 3.86 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{Å}$ [7], and in solids the ZB frequency obeys $\hbar\omega_Z \approx \Delta\mathcal{E}$ with amplitude $\lambda_Z \propto 1/\Delta\mathcal{E}$, reaching tens of nanometers in narrow-gap semiconductors [7]. Together [3] and [7] establish that ZBW is a robust, tunable wave-packet phenomenon — precisely the regime our adelic modification targets — and supply the experimental context against which any adelic correction to the ZBW frequency would have to be tested, with the caveat that in condensed matter the effective parameters $u \approx 10^8\,\mathrm{cm/s}$ and the band gap replace $c$ and $m_0$, rescaling the hierarchy we compute in Section 4.

**p-Adic statistical structure.** Reference [6] studies the three-state p-adic Potts model with competing interactions on a Cayley tree of order two, reduces the description of p-adic Gibbs measures to a recursive equation, and proves that a phase transition occurs if and only if $p = 3$ for any nonzero interaction, also solving the associated uniqueness problem; its conclusion emphasizes that "a phase transition depends only on a value of $p$," results totally different from the real case [6]. The paper also sets out the $p$-adic norm $|x|_p = p^{-r}$ for $x = p^r n/m$ and its ultrametric strong triangle inequality [6], which we use for the product-formula computations of Section 4. We cite [6] as evidence that p-adic dynamical systems exhibit sharp, prime-dependent structural thresholds — the phenomenon class our conjectured prime-dependent ZBW regularization would join — though we make no quantitative use of the Potts results themselves.

**p-Adic geometry of state spaces.** Reference [8] introduces p-adic equiangular lines and derives the first fundamental relation between common angle, dimension, and number of lines: if $\{\tau_j\}_{j=1}^n$ is a p-adic $\gamma$-equiangular family in $\mathbb{Q}_p^d$, then $|n|^2 \leq |d|\max\{|n|, \gamma^2\}$, the p-adic van Lint–Seidel relative bound [8]. The paper defines the underlying p-adic Hilbert space structure — a non-Archimedean Banach space with an inner product satisfying symmetry, linearity, and a Cauchy–Schwarz-type condition [8] — showing that Hilbert-space geometry survives in $\mathbb{Q}_p^d$. This supports the coherence of defining gamma-matrix structures and wave-packet inner products over p-adic fields, and supplies a concrete structural constraint (the bound) that any construction of p-adic equiangular states in our simulation program must respect.

**The adelic physics program.** Reference [10] consolidates a six-paper adelic physics chain into a unified research program whose central thesis is that "physics is adelic" and that quantum mechanics as currently formulated "operates exclusively at the $\infty$-place," a claim it frames as new and falsifiable across theory, computation, and experiment: ZBW as a p-adic observable with a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ topological correlator, three experimental readout protocols (spin noise, EELS/RIXS, Gromov $\delta$), p-adic anyon fusion, Ostrowski-based quantum error correction, and deployed ultrametric computational infrastructure [10]. Our paper is a concrete dynamical instantiation of the ZBW strand of that program, with the difference that we work out the scale dependence through the product formula rather than through topological invariants. Reference [11] develops the specific conjecture that ZBW is mixing between the Archimedean and 2-adic completions of $\mathbb{Q}$: it builds a two-channel effective Hamiltonian whose beat frequency reduces to the standard $\omega_{\mathrm{ZBW}} = 2E/\hbar$ under an explicit calibration condition $|\Delta| \sim E$, computes the electron-scale numbers ($\omega_{\mathrm{ZBW}} = 1.5521 \times 10^{21}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $\nu_{\mathrm{ZBW}} = 2.4702 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $T_{\mathrm{ZBW}} = 4.0483 \times 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{s}$, $\omega_C = 7.7604 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$), verifies the product formula for $q = 2$ and $q = 12$ exactly, and argues that the beat frequency is invariant under Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation [11]. Critically, [11] itself flags the central weakness of any two-place restriction: nothing in the product formula singles out $p = 2$ algebraically, since $\prod_v |q|_v = 1$ holds for every rational and every prime [11]. Our all-sectors formulation is the direct response to that weakness. Finally, the predecessor of this paper [9] established the sector-resolved velocity expectation, the suppression law $A_{\mathrm{int}}/A_0 = \lambda_C/\ell$, and the scale hierarchy $\lambda_C/\ell_P = 2.390 \times 10^{22}$; the present paper revises and completes that treatment with fully shown arithmetic and an explicit practical pathway.

Collectively, these works establish that (i) adelic quantum mechanics is mathematically well posed, with complex-valued wave functions the bridge to ordinary quantum mechanics [1], [5]; (ii) ZBW is a robust, universal wave-packet phenomenon whose vacuum realization is currently simulation-bound [3], [4], [7]; and (iii) p-adic techniques exhibit prime-dependent structure and support Hilbert-space geometry [6], [8]. Our contribution synthesizes these strands into a single falsifiable dynamical conjecture.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The adele ring and the product formula

By the Ostrowski theorem, every non-trivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to the usual Archimedean one $|\cdot|_\infty$ or to a p-adic norm $|\cdot|_p$ for some prime $p$. The adele ring is the restricted product $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{R} \times \prod_p \mathbb{Q}_p$, whose elements are sequences $x = (x_\infty, x_2, \ldots)$ with $x_p \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ for all but finitely many primes [2]. For a nonzero rational $x \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ embedded diagonally, write $x = p^{v_p(x)} \cdot \frac{n}{m}$ with $p, n, m$ relatively prime; the p-adic norm is $|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}$ [6]. The product formula then reads

$$|x|_\infty \prod_p |x|_p = 1.$$

This identity is the algebraic engine of our cancellation claim and is verified by explicit computation in Section 4, Step 5. It holds for every rational and every prime — a fact that, as [11] observes, means the product formula alone cannot privilege any single prime; our conjecture must therefore be formulated over all sectors at once.

### 3.2 The adelic Dirac equation, sector by sector

We posit an adelic Dirac wave function $\Psi = (\Psi_\infty, \Psi_2, \Psi_3, \Psi_5, \ldots)$, where $\Psi_\infty$ is the standard complex Dirac spinor on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\Psi_p$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-valued spinor on $\mathbb{Q}_p^3$ of the type constructed in p-adic quantum mechanics with complex-valued wave functions [1]. The free adelic Dirac equation is postulated sector-wise:

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi_p}{\partial t_p} = \left( c\, \boldsymbol{\alpha}_p \cdot \mathbf{D}_p + \beta_p m c^2 \right) \Psi_p, \qquad p \in \{\infty, 2, 3, 5, \ldots\},$$

where $\mathbf{D}_\infty = -i\hbar \nabla$ is the usual momentum operator and $\mathbf{D}_p$ for finite $p$ is taken to be the Vladimirov-type pseudodifferential operator (the p-adic analogue of the Laplacian, whose spectral theory underlies p-adic quantum mechanics [1]); $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_p, \beta_p$ are gamma-matrix structures over the respective field, whose existence as algebraic objects is consistent with p-adic linear-algebra geometry as exemplified by the p-adic Hilbert spaces and equiangular-line bounds of [8]. In the Archimedean sector, $c$ and $m_e$ are the observed constants. The equation is **postulated, not derived**; this is the central limitation of the framework, discussed in Section 6. The choice of complex-valued sector wave functions follows the recommendation of [1] that this variant connects most directly with ordinary quantum mechanics.

### 3.3 Sector-resolved velocity expectation

For each sector $s \in \{\infty, 2, 3, 5, \ldots\}$ define the velocity expectation

$$v_s(t) = \int \Psi_s^\dagger(\mathbf{x},t)\, \alpha_s\, \Psi_s(\mathbf{x},t)\, d\mu_s(\mathbf{x}),$$

with $d\mu_\infty$ Lebesgue measure and $d\mu_p$ the Haar measure on $\mathbb{Q}_p^3$. For a wave packet that is a superposition of positive- and negative-energy branches, the standard cross term produces an oscillatory contribution at the branch energy gap; in the real sector this is the Breit–Schrödinger trembling motion [4], with

$$\langle v_\infty \rangle(t) \approx c(1 - 2w) + 2cw\cos(\omega_\infty t), \qquad \omega_\infty = \frac{2m_e c^2}{\hbar},$$

where $w$ is the negative-energy admixture. The universal mechanism behind this oscillation — interference of states belonging to positive and negative energies — is exactly the mechanism that [3] identifies as the background of ZB in any system. We postulate the same two-branch structure sector-wise, with p-adic oscillation frequencies determined by the p-adic dispersion relation of the Vladimirov operator. The observable macroscopic position is the adele-diagonal projection

$$X(t) = w_\infty x_\infty(t) + \sum_{p \leq P} w_p x_p(t),$$

where $P$ is a finite cutoff (only finitely many p-adic norms of a rational differ from 1, per the adele restriction [2]) and $w_s$ are sector weights.

### 3.4 Cancellation mechanism (conjecture)

Let $r = \ell/\lambda_C$ be the dimensionless ratio of the observation scale $\ell$ to the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = \hbar/(m_e c)$, approximated by a rational number. We conjecture that the cross-sector interference amplitude in $X(t)$ is proportional to the product of p-adic norms of $r$:

$$A_{\mathrm{int}}(\ell) = A_0 \prod_{p} |r|_p = A_0\, \frac{\lambda_C}{\ell},$$

where the last equality is the adele product formula and $A_0$ is the single-sector trembling amplitude. Thus the p-adic contribution to the observed motion is suppressed by $\lambda_C/\ell$ at scales $\ell \gg \lambda_C$, and enhanced at $\ell \lesssim \lambda_C$.
