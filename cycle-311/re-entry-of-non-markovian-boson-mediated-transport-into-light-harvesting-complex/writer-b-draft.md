# Re‑Entry of Non‑Markovian Boson‑Mediated Transport into Light‑Harvesting Complexes: A Timescale‑Based Applicability Analysis

## Abstract

Photosynthetic antennae transfer excitation energy with near‑unity efficiency on sub‑picosecond timescales, and computational modelers must decide whether the expensive non‑Markovian boson‑mediated framework is required or a Markovian dephasing master equation suffices. Published timescale‑based applicability tests have used parameter boxes wider than the experimentally reported couplings and site‑energy gaps, and a dimensionally incorrect transfer‑time formula, so their numerical classifications do not stand. We rebuild the test on the experimentally reported FMO BChl 3–4 parameters, an excitonic coupling $V\in[50,75]\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and site‑energy difference $\Delta\in[110,180]\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, and correct the transfer time to $\tau_{T}=\Delta/(2\pi cV^{2})$, free of the spurious factor of $\hbar$. For a two‑site Haken–Strobl model we derive the optimal dephasing rate $\Gamma^{*}=\Delta$, the hopping rate $k^{*}=V^{2}/\Delta$, and at the representative point $(V,\Delta)=(50,110)\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ a transfer time $\tau_{T}=0.2336\ \mathrm{ps}$ and a single‑hop trapping efficiency $\eta=0.999001$. Over the full reported box the memory ratio $R=\tau_{B}/\tau_{T}$ lies in $[0.26,0.96]$ for fast baths ($\tau_{B}=0.1\ \mathrm{ps}$) and in $[2.6,9.6]$ for slow baths ($\tau_{B}=1\ \mathrm{ps}$): slow vibrational modes demand a non‑Markovian treatment everywhere in the reported box, and even fast baths never enter the Markovian sector. The rule $\tau_{B}\gtrsim0.5\,\tau_{T}$ gives modelers an analytically derived, falsifiable resource‑allocation criterion.

## 1. Introduction

Photosynthetic organisms convert sunlight into chemical free energy after funnelling excitation through pigment networks with efficiencies approaching $100\%$ on sub‑picosecond excitation‑energy transfer (EET) timescales. Every computational study of this process faces a concrete methodological decision with real costs on both sides: implement the full non‑Markovian boson‑mediated framework, which retains bath memory at the price of time‑nonlocal kernels and large numerical effort, or use a Markovian dephasing master equation, which is cheap but discards physics exactly when bath correlation times approach the transfer time. Choosing wrongly either wastes large allocations of high‑performance computing or produces transfer rates and optimal dephasing conditions that are quantitatively wrong, which in turn misguides design parameters for bio‑inspired light‑harvesting devices and the interpretation of ultrafast spectra. A validated, analytically derived decision rule would let a modeler allocate compute where memory effects matter and skip them where they do not.

Two decades of theory establish that environmental noise can assist rather than hinder transport: dephasing‑assisted transport raises EET efficiencies above $90\%$ across the Fenna–Matthews–Olson (FMO) complex within a fully Markovian treatment [2], and the underlying mechanisms—suppression of destructive interference and dephasing‑broadened site overlap—are worked out analytically for fully connected networks [8]. On the experimental side, single‑molecule pump–probe spectroscopy resolves sub‑100 fs energy relaxation in individual light‑harvesting complexes [7], so fast solvent‑like bath components with correlation times of order $0.1\ \mathrm{ps}$ are measured, not inferred. Mixed quantum–classical simulations show that treating high‑frequency chromophore vibrations quantum‑mechanically instead of classically slows transfer rates, although by less than a factor of two for biologically relevant parameters [6]. The target framework whose applicability we assess is the non‑Markovian boson‑mediated description, in which structured phonon fields direct exciton transport and are claimed to sustain efficiencies above $95\%$ [10]; the published applicability analysis of that framework [9] proposed a memory‑ratio test but, as we show below, rested on a parameter box wider than the experimentally reported values and on a transfer‑time formula carrying a spurious factor of $\hbar$.

This paper is a post‑publication revision of that analysis [9]. We keep its central construction—the memory ratio $R=\tau_{B}/\tau_{T}$ with explicit regime thresholds—because it is sound and falsifiable, and we correct four specific defects: the claimed coupling range $V\in[20,75]\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ is not "the full experimentally reported range", because the reported FMO BChl 3–4 coupling is $50$–$75\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and the reported site‑energy difference is $110$–$180\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ [4]; the transfer‑time formula $\tau_{T}=\hbar\Delta/(2\pi cV^{2})$ is dimensionally inconsistent and is corrected to $\tau_{T}=\Delta/(2\pi cV^{2})$; the abstract ranges $R=0.17$–$1.70$ and the classification of fast baths as merely "transitional" follow from the erroneous box and are recomputed; and an analogy that mapped the memory‑ratio result onto urban transportation multiplex networks [3] is removed, because that work develops an overlap‑census method for city transport layers and establishes nothing about exciton–phonon dynamics.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Dephasing‑assisted transport in FMO.** Reference [2] provides physically intuitive mechanisms for the effect of noise on EET and, using a hybrid basis of excitons and sites, shows how pure dephasing enables energy transfer with efficiencies well above $90\%$ across the FMO complex. Its network model treats site energies $\hbar\omega_{j}$ and tunnelling amplitudes $v_{j,l}$ as free parameters, and its conclusion is that any noise model that enhances transport must suppress the inefficient pathways that a fully Markovian pure‑dephasing treatment already suppresses. This both motivates our optimal‑dephasing condition $\Gamma^{*}=\Delta$ and sharpens the question we address: if Markovian dephasing already recovers high efficiency, the non‑Markovian framework earns its cost only through memory‑specific corrections, which is exactly what the memory ratio is designed to predict.

**Exact noise‑assisted mechanisms in fully connected networks.** Reference [8] analyses transport assisted by dephasing and more general noise in dissipative networks, and for the uniform fully connected network obtains an exact analytical solution of the density matrix for arbitrarily large sizes. In the noiseless case its exact solution gives a total transferred population $p_{\mathrm{sink}}(\infty)=1/(N-1)$, a striking demonstration that destructive interference can nearly block transport, and its concluding analysis identifies suppression of destructive interference and dephasing‑broadened line overlap as the key mechanisms. We use these results as the theoretical foundation for the dephasing‑optimal regime that our $\Gamma^{*}=\Delta$ condition recovers in the two‑site limit.

**Experimentally reported FMO dimer parameters.** Reference [4] studies a vibronic exciton dimer with parameters chosen to mimic FMO and reports, from prior spectroscopic modelling, a transition energy difference between BChl 3 and 4 of about $110$ to $180\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and an excitonic coupling between these two molecules of about $-50$ to $-75\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, with vibrational modes near $180\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ carrying a total Huang–Rhys factor of $0.03$.
