## Research idea

Network Tracing Capacity as an Informational Event Horizon. The note conjectures a precise isomorphism: in networked control systems, the Network Tracing Capacity (NTC)—the maximum substrate instability rate a communication topology can propagate—plays the role of a causal horizon, exactly as the invariant speed of light c does in spacetime. Claim: if a plant's instability growth rate exceeds the topology-limited information propagation speed, the state becomes provably unobservable/uncontrollable, and this boundary is a structural analog of a relativistic event horizon, not merely an engineering limitation. This matters because it would unify control-theoretic observability with relativistic causality under a single invariance principle, yielding topology-independent bounds on controllability. Testable: (1) derive a formal correspondence mapping (instability rate, propagation delay, topology) onto (expansion rate, c, spacetime metric) and prove horizon-formation conditions; (2) simulate linear unstable plants on delayed networks (Khan–Jadbabaie framework) to verify that unobservability onset occurs at the predicted NTC threshold, mirroring horizon crossing; (3) check whether regularization/early-stopping rules in RKHS learning satisfy the same horizon inequality, confirming the claimed cross-domain invariant.
