# Real Numbers as Coarse-Grained Projections of Re-Entry-Generated p-Adic Trees: A Constructive Program with Partial Results

## Abstract

Physical measurement yields finite rational numbers with Archimedean ordering, yet theoretical physics is formulated over the real continuum, and the relationship between these two number systems is usually taken as given rather than derived. This paper addresses a specific gap: no existing construction derives the real line from a minimal, distinction-based calculus in a way that makes the loss of information explicit and computable. We formalize a conjecture of the re-entry program — that extending Spencer-Brown's calculus of indications with self-referential boundaries ($f = \neg f$) generates structures isomorphic to the $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and that the real line is a lossy metric projection of the resulting ultrametric boundary trees. For the acyclic fragment we prove that normal forms of the rewriting system biject with $\mathbb{Z}_2$, that the integers embed as the dense set of finite unfoldings with $|x - m_n(x)|_2 \leq 2^{-n}$, and that the re-entry fixed point of $x = 1 - x$ solves to $x = 1/2$ with $p$-adic order $-1$, escaping the integers. We define an explicit coarse-graining $\varphi_p : \mathbb{Z}_p \to [0,1]$, prove it is $1$-Lipschitz ($|\varphi_p(x) - \varphi_p(y)| \leq |x - y|_p$), show its distance-distortion ratio attains every value in $[0,1]$, and characterize its collision set as countable and dense. The program grounds real analysis in boundary manipulations and yields a computationally checkable normalization test.

## 1. Introduction

Every instrument reading in physics, engineering, and data science is recorded as a rational number with finitely many digits: a voltage, a counts-per-second rate, a qubit fidelity. The theoretical models that make sense of these readings, by contrast, are formulated over the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$, a continuum that no measurement ever accesses and whose points carry infinite information. This mismatch is not merely philosophical. It has a computational cost: numerical analysis spends enormous effort controlling the artifacts of forcing continuum mathematics onto finite machinery, and in quantum technology it has an engineering cost, because a qubit described as a point of the continuous Bloch sphere has no discrete boundary at which drift can be arrested, so active error correction must fight accumulation at scale — the surface-code approach cited in the Alpha Pi program requires physical-to-logical qubit ratios of roughly $1000:1$ [9]. If the real line could instead be derived as a projection of a discrete, distinction-based structure — with the projection's losses characterized exactly — then the choice of number system becomes a design decision with a computable error budget, rather than an inherited axiom.

The specific question this paper bears on is whether such a derivation is possible from a minimal starting point. Spencer-Brown's calculus of indications begins with a single act, drawing a distinction, and a single operation, crossing the boundary. When the calculus is extended to allow re-entry — a boundary whose interior contains a copy of the whole expression, exemplified by the self-referential equation $f = \neg f$ — the resulting structures are self-similar trees of distinctions. The conjecture under investigation is that these trees, properly formalized, carry the algebraic and metric structure of the $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and that the familiar real line arises only when an Archimedean metric is imposed on them as a coarse-graining, destroying most of the underlying information. A positive answer would ground real analysis in boundary manipulations that are constructive and machine-checkable; a negative answer would locate precisely where the derivation fails.

The existing literature supplies the $p$-adic side of this bridge but not the distinction-based side. The $p$-adic numbers are a completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ under a norm satisfying the strong triangle inequality $|x+y|_p \leq \max\{|x|_p, |y|_p\}$, i.e., an ultrametric [1]; they admit a canonical continuous map to the non-negative reals whose inverse is obstructed by the non-uniqueness of digit expansions [4]; and measurements themselves return only rationals with Archimedean norm and natural ordering, so that "$p$-adic numbers are not results of measurements" [2]. What is missing is a formal object on the distinction-calculus side whose normal forms can be proved isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and an explicit accounting of which properties of $\mathbb{R}$ survive the projection and which do not.

This paper contributes the following results, each derived in the section indicated:

- A formalization of the re-entry calculus as a term rewriting system, and a proof (Proposition 3.1, Section 4) that the normal forms of its acyclic fragment biject with the $2$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_2$, with the full re-entry case stated as a conjecture with a precise refutation test (Sections 3 and 7).
- A density derivation showing the finite unfoldings of re-entry embed the integers $\mathbb{Z}$ densely in $\mathbb{Z}_2$, with the explicit bound $|x - m_n(x)|_2 \leq 2^{-n}$ (Section 4).
- A solution of the canonical re-entry equation $x = 1 - x$ in $\mathbb{Q}_2$, giving $x = 1/2$ with $p$-adic order $-1$, so that the self-referential fixed point escapes the ring of integers (Section 4).
- An explicit coarse-graining map $\varphi_p : \mathbb{Z}_p \to [0,1]$, proved $1$-Lipschitz with distortion ratio attaining every value in $[0,1]$, and with a collision set that is countable and dense (Section 4).
- A covering-number equivalence showing that at every finite depth $n$ the re-entry tree and the dyadic interval tree have exactly $2^n$ cells, hence Hausdorff dimension $1$ under the projection, with the loss localized in the metric and not the combinatorics (Section 4).
- A computational refutation protocol for the full re-entry isomorphism conjecture, stated as an executable test on normal forms modulo $2^n$ (Section 7).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The $p$-adic framework used here is standard and we summarize it through the sources that state it precisely. For a fixed prime $p$, every nonzero rational $x$ can be written $x = p^r \frac{n}{m}$ with $p \nmid nm$, and the $p$-adic norm is $|x|_p = p^{-r}$; this norm satisfies the strong triangle inequality $|x+y|_p \leq \max\{|x|_p, |y|_p\}$, called ultrametricity, and the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ in this norm is $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [1]. The same norm is presented via the digit expansion $x = \sum_{k \geq k_0} x(k) p^k$ with $x(k) \in \{0, \dots, p-1\}$ and $|x| = p^{-k_0(x)}$, so that the norm depends only on the lowest $p$-ary digit — a drastic difference from the real norm, since arbitrarily high powers may occur and the norm remains finite for numbers infinite in the ordinary sense [4]. In several variables the norm extends as $\|x\|_p = \max_i |x_i|_p = p^{-\mathrm{ord}(x)}$ with $\mathrm{ord}(x) = \min_i \mathrm{ord}(x_i)$ [6]. These are the exact structures our re-entry trees are conjectured to carry.

Several works demonstrate that $p$-adic structures support full analytical theories, which is why an isomorphism with a boundary calculus would inherit genuine mathematical power. On $\mathbb{Q}_p^n$ one defines Bruhat-Schwartz test functions as locally constant functions with compact support, where local constancy means $\varphi(x + x') = \varphi(x)$ for $x'$ in a ball $B^n_{l(x)}$, and builds the Fourier transform and distribution theory on this base [6]. Kochubei's one-dimensional theory of parabolic-type pseudodifferential equations with variable coefficients, whose fundamental solutions are transition densities of Markov processes on the $p$-adic line, motivates the $n$-dimensional theory developed there, and the introduction records that ultrametric stochastic processes have been connected for thirty years to models of complex systems such as glasses and proteins [6]. This matters for our program because it shows that if re-entry normal forms realize $\mathbb{Z}_p$, then diffusion and dynamics on the boundary trees are already mathematically available, not speculative.

The global behavior of $p$-adic systems also differs qualitatively from the real case, which constrains what a projection to $\mathbb{R}$ can preserve. In a three-state $p$-adic Potts model with competing interactions on a Cayley tree of order two, the problem of describing $p$-adic Gibbs measures reduces to a recursive equation, and a phase transition occurs if and only if $p = 3$ for any nonzero interaction value, while for $p \neq 3$ the Gibbs measure is unique; the authors conclude that the phase transition depends only on the value of $p$ and that the results are totally different from the real case [1]. The same source develops the non-Kolmogorovian probability theory on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ in which probabilities are $p$-adic valued, with limit theorems and a non-Archimedean analogue of the Kolmogorov theorem [1]. For our purposes this is a warning and an opportunity: an ultrametric foundation supports probability and statistical mechanics, but its phase structure is not the real one, so any claim that the reals are a mere projection must explain which global phenomena (like real-case phase transitions) are artifacts of the metric imposition.

The bridge from $p$-adic to real numbers has a canonical precedent that our coarse-graining generalizes. Pitkänen's p-adic TGD program defines a natural continuous map $\mathrm{Id} : R_p \to R_+$ from $p$-adic numbers to non-negative reals given by re-reading the "pinary" expansion as a real expansion, notes that this map is continuous, and records the key difficulty that the inverse map is problematic because the pinary expansion, like the decimal expansion, is not unique ($1 = 0.999\dots$) [4]. Our map $\varphi_p$ in Section 3 is exactly this re-reading restricted to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and our collision analysis in Section 4 makes the non-uniqueness obstacle quantitative: the fibers are countable and dense. The same program computes that the algebraic extensions of $p$-adic numbers allowing a square root near the $p$-adic real axis have dimension $D = 4$ for $p > 2$ and $D = 8$ for the $2$-adic case, that the convergence radius of the square-root Taylor series is nonvanishing, and that the resulting region forms a $p$-adic counterpart of the future light cone consisting of cylinders parallel to the time axis with radius $N_p(t) = p^k$ and length $p^{k-1}(p-1)$ [3]. This shows that even basic real operations, such as taking a square root, become region-restricted and dimension-changing on the $p$-adic side — a concrete instance of the asymmetry our projection must account for.

On the side of what measurement actually accesses, Dragovich states the operational constraint directly: any measurement is a comparison producing a ratio that is a rational number with finitely many digits in the decimal expansion, so results of measurements are rational numbers with Archimedean norm and natural ordering, characteristics of real but not of $p$-adic numbers; $p$-adic numbers are therefore not results of measurements, yet play a significant role in $p$-adic mathematical physics, from Volovich's $1987$ proposal of $p$-adic numbers at the Planck scale and in string theory to $p$-adic scattering amplitudes [2]. This is the strongest published statement of the position our paper formalizes: the Archimedean ordering is a property of the measurement record, not of the underlying structure, and $\mathbb{Q}$ serves as a bridge dense in both $\mathbb{R}$ and all $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [2]. Our coarse-graining theorem gives this position a mechanism: the projection $\varphi_p$ is exactly a re-reading of digits that manufactures Archimedean ordering out of a tree.
