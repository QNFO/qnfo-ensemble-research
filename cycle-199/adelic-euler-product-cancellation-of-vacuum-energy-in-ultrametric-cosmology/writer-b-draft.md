# Adelic Euler-Product Cancellation of Vacuum Energy: A Tree-Lattice Model and Quantitative Consistency Analysis

## Abstract

The cosmological constant problem—the mismatch between naive Planck-scale vacuum energy and the observed dark-energy density, a discrepancy of roughly $10^{100}$ to $10^{120}$—remains unresolved, and dark-energy survey programs are built on the assumption that some unknown physics suppresses the vacuum contribution. Existing cancellation mechanisms insert new dynamical fields or negative-energy fluids, each with known pathologies. We investigate a different route suggested by the adelic program: computing vacuum energy not at the real place alone but as an Euler-type product over the Archimedean place and the $p$-adic places, so that the product formula $\prod_v |q|_v = 1$ over rationals enforces cancellation by topology rather than by fine-tuning. We model the $p$-adic sector as a scalar oscillator system on a Bruhat–Tits tree (a Bethe lattice) whose level-$n$ modes carry the ultrametric scale $p^{-n}$. We derive the $p$-adic zero-point energy in closed form, $E_p = \frac{\omega_0}{2}\,\frac{b}{p-b+1}$ for tree branching $b$, convergent if and only if $b \leq p$; for $b = 2$ this reduces to $E_p = \omega_0/(p-1)$. Matching the real-sector density $\rho_\infty = \Lambda^4/(16\pi^2) \approx 6.33\times 10^{-3}\,\Lambda^4$ fixes the tree oscillator frequency to $\omega_0 = (p-1)\Lambda^4/(16\pi^2)$, e.g. $\omega_0 \approx 1.27\times 10^{-2}\,\Lambda^4$ at $p=3$. Under the adelic product ansatz the logarithmic vacuum energy vanishes identically for rational mode weights, and reproducing the observed residual requires a cancellation accurate to $\varepsilon = 10^{-120}$, reachable at tree depth $n \approx 399$ for $p=2$. The result supplies a falsifiable template: any consistent $p$-adic scalar field theory whose tree-lattice ground-state energy violates the $b \leq p$ convergence law or leaves an order-one adelic residual refutes the mechanism.

## 1. Introduction

Observational cosmology has established that the expansion of the Universe is accelerating, and the most prosaic explanation—vacuum energy—accounts for about three quarters of the energy density in the Universe [2]. The same observations expose a theoretical crisis: naive predictions based on the Planck scale would predict vacuum energy densities roughly 100 orders of magnitude larger than observed, implying that some unknown physics suppresses the predicted vacuum energy while leaving a very small residual that drives the current expansion rate [2]. This is the quantitative core of the cosmological constant problem, and in its sharpest form the discrepancy is quoted as a factor of $10^{120}$ between the Planck-scale zero-point contribution and the observed dark-energy density.

Who faces this problem in practice? The communities operating the coming generation of dark-energy surveys. The Large Synoptic Survey Telescope Dark Energy Science Collaboration was constituted precisely to study dark energy and related topics in fundamental physics with a decade of wide-field optical imaging, treating the vacuum-energy residual as the observable to be characterized [2]. The Dark Energy Survey was designed as a 5000 square degree optical–near-infrared survey to measure the dark energy and dark matter densities and the dark energy equation of state [4]. Every constraint these programs place on the equation of state $w$ of the dark component presupposes that theory can say something about why the vacuum energy is what it is; at present it cannot, because the Archimedean zero-point calculation overshoots by $\sim 10^{120}$.

Existing responses each pay a known price. Dynamical cancellation can be engineered with a conformal compensator—a second scalar field $\lambda$ that, when the conformal factor $A(\varphi)$ between Jordan and Einstein frames becomes time-dependent, couples to the trace of the energy-momentum tensor and cancels the vacuum energy density, leading to a radiation-like expansion [1]. Cyclic or bouncing cosmologies require a non-conventional fluid with negative effective energy density to cancel the matter component before a bounce, and such fluids typically produce quantum instabilities that make the theory ill-defined [5]. Running-vacuum models with curvature introduce free parameters $\nu$ and $b$ that must be constrained observationally [7]. All of these add structure to cancel a contribution that, in a place-democratic treatment of numbers, might never arise in the first place: physics currently operates exclusively at the Archimedean completion of $\mathbb{Q}$, although Ostrowski's theorem classifies all completions and the product formula $\prod_v |q|_v = 1$ ties them together exactly [9], [10].

If the conjecture examined here holds—that an adelic Euler-product factorization of the vacuum energy neutralizes the Archimedean zero-point contribution against the $p$-adic sectors—the decision that changes is foundational but concrete: the cosmological constant problem would be reclassified from a fine-tuning puzzle requiring new fields or fluids into a consequence of number-theoretic topology, and the target of survey physics would shift from mitigating a $10^{120}$ mistake to measuring a small topological residual. If it fails, the failure mode itself (which sector diverges, at which branching) constrains any future adelic cosmology.

This paper does not claim to solve the problem. It builds the smallest computable model in which the conjecture can be tested, derives its closed-form zero-point energies, and states the exact conditions under which the mechanism works or breaks. The contributions are:

- A closed-form derivation of the zero-point energy of oscillator modes on a Bruhat–Tits tree of branching $b$ at prime $p$, $E_p = \frac{\omega_0}{2}\,\frac{b}{p-b+1}$, with the convergence law $b \leq p$ (Sections 3 and 4).
- A matching condition that fixes the tree oscillator frequency $\omega_0 = (p-1)\Lambda^4/(16\pi^2)$ against the Archimedean density $\rho_\infty = \Lambda^4/(16\pi^2) \approx 6.33\times 10^{-3}\,\Lambda^4$, with worked numbers at $p = 2$ and $p = 3$ (Section 4).
- A demonstration that under the adelic product ansatz the total logarithmic vacuum energy vanishes for rational mode weights, that the observed residual requires cancellation precision $\varepsilon = 10^{-120}$, and that a tree of depth $n \approx 399$ at $p = 2$ spans the full Planck-to-observed hierarchy (Sections 4 and 5).
- An explicit falsification criterion and a smallest-next-test proposal based on quantum simulation of an adelic model on a Bruhat–Tits tree with qudits, a simulation target already identified in the adelic literature [8] (Sections 6 and 7).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The cosmological constant problem and its observational stakes. The accelerating expansion discovered at low redshift renewed interest in the cosmological constant problem, and the "new" problem—producing a late-time self-acceleration matching data—is typically addressed by adding a quintessence fluid or modifying gravity on large scales, with nonlinear screening required to recover General Relativity on small scales where pulsar-binary and solar-system data constrain deviations strongly [1]. The LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration white paper states the quantitative dilemma directly: vacuum energy constitutes about three quarters of the energy density of the Universe, yet Planck-scale predictions exceed the observed density by roughly 100 orders of magnitude, so unknown physics must suppress the prediction while preserving a small residual [2]. The Dark Energy Survey was proposed as a 5000 square degree survey to ~24th magnitude in $griz$ using a 3 square degree CCD camera on the Blanco 4-m telescope, to measure the dark energy and dark matter densities and the dark energy equation of state [4]. These programs define the observational target that any vacuum-energy mechanism must ultimately confront.

Dynamical cancellation mechanisms. The conformal compensator model constructs a total action $S = S_{\rm EH} + S_{\rm m} + S_{\varphi,\lambda}$ with an Einstein–Hilbert term in the Einstein frame and a matter Lagrangian in the Jordan frame, the two related by a conformal factor $A(\varphi)$; the matter energy-momentum tensors then obey $T^{\mu}_{({\rm m})\nu} = A^4\,\tilde{T}^{\mu}_{({\rm m})\nu}$ [1]. When $A$ develops a nonzero first derivative and becomes time-dependent, it induces a coupling between $\lambda$ and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor such that $\lambda$ cancels the vacuum energy density $\tilde{V}_{\rm vac}$, driving radiation-like expansion in the Einstein frame [1]. This is cancellation by dynamics: a field equation does the work. The mechanism examined here is complementary—cancellation by arithmetic, in which the product formula over all completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ does the work before any field equation is invoked.

Alternative cosmological frameworks. In quantum cosmology, the Wheeler–DeWitt equation $\hat{H}\Psi = 0$ contains no time parameter and resembles a zero-energy Schrödinger equation; exact quantum–classical correspondence in a Friedmann model—achieved when the quantum potential vanishes—occurs for a total energy density varying as $1/a^n$ with $n = 2$, yielding coasting evolution in which the well-known problems of quantum cosmology disappear [3]. Coasting evolution is precisely the phenomenology a perfectly cancelled vacuum would approximate, and the $n=2$ result gives a benchmark expansion law against which a decoupled vacuum sector could be compared. Non-singular cyclic cosmology within General Relativity requires a non-conventional fluid with negative effective energy density to cancel the matter component before the bounce; the quintom realization uses a phantom field with negative kinetic term, and such constructions typically suffer quantum instabilities that make the theory ill-defined, motivating extended-gravity alternatives whose authors note that the whole discussion rests on the cosmological principle, an assumption questioned by galaxy-distribution analyses [5]. Running-vacuum models with spatial curvature and warm dark matter derive background and perturbation equations for a running $\rho_\Lambda$ fixed by covariance of the effective vacuum action, and constrain the parameters $\nu$, $b$, and $\Omega_k^0$ against observational data including large-scale-structure formation [7]. Both frameworks illustrate the cost of carrying vacuum-energy cancellation inside standard Archimedean field theory: extra fluids, extra parameters, extra instability risks. An adelic mechanism, if viable, removes the need for any of them by removing the overshoot at its source.

Ultrametric and $p$-adic structure. The mathematical substrate of the $p$-adic sector is the ultrametric: a metric $d$ satisfying the strong triangle inequality $d(x,y) \leq d(x,z) \vee d(z,y)$, where $\vee$ is the maximum operator [6]. Ultrametric spaces admit embedding, extension, and interpolation theorems paralleling those for ordinary metric spaces—the ultrametric versions of the Arens–Eells isometric embedding theorem, the Hausdorff extension theorem, and the Niemytzki–Tychonoff characterization theorem are proved via ultra-normed modules over commutative rings and a zero-dimensional Michael continuous selection theorem [6]. These results matter here because they guarantee that the tree-lattice spaces on which we place oscillator modes are not ad hoc graphs but members of a well-behaved class of ultrametrizable spaces with controlled extension properties.
