# Factoring Hardness as a Bayesian Prior: A Decision-Theoretic Model of Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration Timing

## Abstract

The urgency of post-quantum cryptography (PQC) migration is usually justified by the anticipated capability of quantum computers to factor integers, yet that threat rests on an unproven complexity-theoretic conjecture: that integer factoring is not in BPP, the class of decision problems solvable in bounded-error probabilistic polynomial time. No published migration framework treats this conjecture as an explicit tunable prior; planning documents instead hard-code the assumption that classical factoring is hard and that all urgency flows from quantum hardware. This paper embeds the conjecture in a decision-theoretic model of migration timing for RSA-2048. Under a two-hypothesis mixture with annual hazards $h_{\mathrm{BPP}} = 0.10$ and $h_{\mathrm{HARD}} = 0.01$, prior weight $\pi$, migration cost normalized to $C_m = 1$, and discount rate $r = 0.05$, we derive the closed-form threshold $L^{\ast}(\pi) = 1 + r/\bar{h}$: at the baseline $\pi = 0.2$ the mixed hazard is $\bar{h} = 0.028$ and $L^{\ast} \approx 2.785714$, so any asset whose compromise costs more than $2.79$ migration-cost units should be migrated immediately. The recommendation for a ten-unit asset survives even $\pi = 0$; a two-unit asset flips at $\pi^{\ast} \approx 0.444444$. A continuous two-hazard Poisson scenario solved numerically shows that adding a classical-breakthrough hazard $\lambda = 0.01$ to a quantum hazard $\mu = 0.02$ moves the optimal migration time from $T^{\ast} \approx 0.496$ years to $T^{\ast} \approx 0.332$ years, a $33\%$ acceleration that purely quantum framings miss. The framework converts an unproven conjecture into an auditable sensitivity parameter.

## 1. Introduction

Security teams in banks, critical-infrastructure operators, and government agencies today face a planning problem with no agreed quantitative answer: when should they spend real money to replace RSA-2048 and other classical public-key cryptography? The replacement programs are large — inventories of cryptographic assets, vendor re-certifications, protocol renegotiations, multi-year engineering effort — and deferring them saves budget today but leaves assets exposed to a "harvest now, decrypt later" adversary who records ciphertext today and decrypts it once a capable machine or algorithm exists. The decision is currently made on qualitative grounds, because the threat argument rests on a premise almost nobody writes down.

That premise is a logical dependency. Quantum factoring via Shor's algorithm threatens RSA-2048 only if classical polynomial-time factoring is impossible — only if FACTORING $\notin$ BPP, where BPP is the class of decision problems solvable in bounded-error probabilistic polynomial time. If a classical algorithm ever factored RSA-2048 efficiently, quantum computers would be irrelevant to the RSA threat: the assets would already be broken by classical means. If factoring is hard classically, the quantum threat is real but its arrival time is governed by engineering timelines. The conjecture FACTORING $\notin$ BPP is unproven, and the asymptotic parameters of the general number-field sieve, the best known classical factoring method, have been stable since 1993 — which is evidence of absence of a classical breakthrough so far, not proof of absence. Migration policy therefore conditions on a conjecture, and the rational response to conditioning on an unproven proposition is to treat one's confidence in it as a prior and propagate it through the decision. If the analysis in this paper holds, a security architect can state, audit, and defend a migration recommendation as a function of an explicit number $\pi$ instead of an unstated assumption; a risk committee can see exactly how far the recommendation would move if expert confidence in classical hardness changed; and an insurer can price the residual exposure of deferred migration.

This paper constructs that analysis. The model is deliberately simple — two hypotheses, constant hazards, exponential discounting — because its purpose is not forecasting but exposing the sensitivity structure: which conclusions about migration timing survive, and which collapse, when the epistemic status of the hardness conjecture is made explicit.

Our contributions are:

1. A closed-form Bayesian decision model for PQC migration under a two-hypothesis mixture over factoring hardness, with the mixed hazard $\bar{h}(\pi)$ and the critical loss threshold $L^{\ast}(\pi) = 1 + r/\bar{h}$ derived with full arithmetic (Sections 3 and 4).
2. A sensitivity analysis over the prior $\pi$ showing that for high-value assets the "migrate now" recommendation is robust even at $\pi = 0$, while for low-value assets the decision flips at a computable prior threshold $\pi^{\ast}(L)$, including the boundary case $L = 1.5$ at which no prior triggers immediate migration (Sections 4 and 5).
3. Full arithmetic for a discrete two-point scenario, including the observation that under a forced-migration-at-horizon comparison the decision is independent of the prior, while under a no-fallback comparison the break-even prior is $p^{\ast} \approx 0.082437$ (Sections 3.4 and 4.2).
4. A continuous two-hazard Poisson scenario in which the optimal migration time $T^{\ast}$ solves a first-order condition solved by bisection, showing that a classical-breakthrough hazard accelerates the optimal migration time from $T^{\ast} \approx 0.496$ years to $T^{\ast} \approx 0.332$ years (Sections 3.4 and 4.3).
5. A discussion of how much of the quantum-advantage narrative for cryptography depends on the single unproven premise FACTORING $\notin$ BPP, and of a proposed stress-test program classifying known exponential speedups by their dependence on abelian hidden-subgroup structure, reported as a labeled hypothesis rather than a computed result (Section 6).

Throughout, every number is either an input declared in Section 3 or computed with shown arithmetic in Section 4. No empirical data on expert surveys or engineering timelines are invented; where such data would refine the inputs, we say so explicitly.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We organize the related work into four threads: the PQC migration problem itself, the epistemology of hardness claims, Bayesian methods for priors and expert judgement, and structural analogues of migration timing. Each statement below is restricted to what the cited work states.

**PQC migration management.** The migration process layer is supplied by [2 in the prior numbering; here renumbered] — specifically, the PMMP framework addresses the problem that organizations must plan migration to quantum-resilient cryptographic measures, notes that this is difficult, and states that, to the authors' knowledge, no generalized approach existed that manages such a complex migration while explicitly integrating into organizations' steering mechanisms and control systems; PMMP presents a risk-based process for this purpose. Our model is complementary in a specific sense: PMMP supplies the organizational process through which a migration decision is executed, while we supply the decision-theoretic core that determines *when* the migration should occur, conditional on an explicit prior over the hardness conjecture. A risk-based process needs a quantified risk; Sections 3–5 provide one candidate quantification whose inputs are auditable.

**Hardness under restrictions.** Work on computational hardness under severe restrictions offers a cautionary analogy. The Tetris analysis of [1] proves, for any tetromino piece type except the O piece, the NP-hardness of Tetris clearing and survival when the input sequence is restricted to a single polyomino piece type, and gives polynomial-time algorithms for clearing and survival with dominoes under a simple falling rotation model. Two structural lessons transfer. First, hardness claims can be established rigorously for restricted problem variants, so "hardness" is a property that can be pinned down rather than asserted. Second, the result of [1] is a *worst-case* NP-hardness statement about a puzzle, whereas cryptographic security needs *average-case* hardness against natural distributions — a gap that is exactly why the RSA-2048 threat ultimately rests on the unproven average-case conjecture FACTORING $\notin$ BPP rather than on any worst-case theorem.

**Bayesian priors, mixtures, and variable selection.** Our two-hypothesis prior over factoring hardness is a spike-and-slab-style mixture: a point mass on "breakthrough regime" and a diffuse mass on "hard regime." The Bayesian group lasso work of [4] uses spike-and-slab priors for group variable selection, shows that the marginal prior for a coefficient group is a mixture of a point mass at zero and a Multi-Laplace distribution, demonstrates the connection of the model with penalized regression, and proves that the posterior median estimator has the oracle property under orthogonal designs. We borrow the structural idea — a two-component prior in which one component is a point mass — and apply it to a hypothesis about the world rather than to a coefficient. On the question of where the prior $\pi$ should come from, the Posterior Belief Assessment framework of [6] addresses precisely the situation in which a Bayesian analysis is too complex to assert a precise correspondence between the analyst's expert probabilistic judgements and the chosen prior and likelihood, and proposes a finite procedure for attributing meaning to the results of such an analysis; its discussion notes that a fully subjective Bayesian analysis in complex-model settings would be extremely challenging, expensive and time consuming. This is the natural methodological home for eliciting $\pi$ from complexity-theory experts, though we do not perform such an elicitation here. On the choice of prior form itself, the objective Bayesian analysis of bilateral data in [7] derives Jeffreys' prior and Bernardo's reference prior for the risk difference, the risk ratio, and the odds ratio under Dallal's model and the saturated model, and shows that the risk difference depends directly on a nuisance parameter through the divisor $1+\gamma$. The analogy is twofold: when little is known, an objective or reference prior over the hardness hypothesis is a defensible default, and derived quantities can depend on nuisance structure exactly as our threshold $L^{\ast}$ depends on the nuisance hazards $h_{\mathrm{BPP}}$ and $h_{\mathrm{HARD}}$; our sensitivity analysis over $\pi \in [0,1]$ is the practical substitute for committing to one objective prior.

**Weighted pooling of opinion.** If $\pi$ is eventually pooled from expert surveys, selection effects matter. [3] develops Bayesian inference for design-based sampling with inverse-probability weights, using a hierarchical approach that models the distribution of the weights of nonsampled units and simultaneously includes them as predictors in a nonparametric model; its discussion reports that in simulation studies the fully Bayesian approach performs well compared to classical design-based inference, with partial pooling across small cells yielding smaller variance, smaller RMSE, and better coverage. This supports a future refinement in which expert opinions are reweighted for selection effects before being pooled into $\pi$.

**Migration and displacement analogues.** The Migration Networks chapter of [2] reviews the study of macroscale migration patterns as networks whose nodes represent places (countries, cities, rural areas) and whose edges encode migration ties, surveying network-analysis methods applied to such networks at different geographic scales. We borrow the framing, not the content: cryptographic migration can be viewed as a flow problem over an asset graph, in which "migration pressure" propagates from broken primitives through dependent protocols and systems. The co-design study of [5] addresses performance analysis of migrating tasks from software to hardware in multiprocessor systems-on-chip, motivated by the complexity of multimedia applications and the pressure on designers to deliver strong systems under short deadlines. Its relevance is the shared structural problem — deciding *when* to move a function between implementation substrates under cost and deadline pressure — which is isomorphic to our migration-timing problem with the roles of "hardware" and "PQC" interchanged.

**Defaults made explicit, and the prior version.** The prior version of this work, [8], states the two-hypoixture model, the mixture hazard $\bar{h}(\pi) = \pi h_{\mathrm{BPP}} + (1-\pi) h_{\mathrm{HARD}}$, the discounted-exposure integral $E_{\mathrm{exposed}} = L\bar{h}/(\bar{h}+r)$, and the threshold $L^{\ast}(\pi) = 1 + r/\bar{h}$, and computes the baseline numbers $\bar{h} = 0.028$ and $L^{\ast} \approx 2.785714$ at $\pi = 0.2$. The present paper completes that program: it finishes the truncated discrete-scenario arithmetic, adds the continuous two-hazard Poisson scenario with a numerically solved optimal timing, extends the sensitivity analysis to the boundary case $L = 1.5$, and sharpens the reduction-of-urgency argument. Together these eight works supply the process layer [2], the epistemology of hardness claims [1], the prior-elicitation and pooling machinery [3], [4], [6], [7], and the model core [8] that we now extend.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Model primitives (main model)

Time is measured in years. The organization holds RSA-2048 assets whose compromise would cause loss $L$, expressed in units of the migration cost $C_m$ (so $C_m = 1$ by normalization). The discount rate is $r$ per year. The hardness conjecture is represented by a two-hypothesis mixture:

- $H_{\mathrm{BPP}}$: the world is in a regime where cryptographically relevant factoring breakthroughs arrive with annual hazard $h_{\mathrm{BPP}}$;
- $H_{\mathrm{HARD}}$: breakthroughs arrive with annual hazard $h_{\mathrm{HARD}} \ll h_{\mathrm{BPP}}$;
- $\pi = \Pr(H_{\mathrm{BPP}})$ is the Bayesian prior weight on the fast-breakthrough regime.

The mixture (predictive) hazard is

$$
\bar{h}(\pi) = \pi\, h_{\mathrm{BPP}} + (1 - \pi)\, h_{\mathrm{HARD}}.
$$

A breakthrough is modeled as a memoryless Poisson event: conditional on the regime, the waiting time to breakthrough is exponential with the regime's hazard.
