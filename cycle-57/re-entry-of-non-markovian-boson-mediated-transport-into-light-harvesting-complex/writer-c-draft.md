# Dephasing-Assisted Excitation Transfer under Non-Markovian Memory: A Minimal Analytical Model with Explicit Efficiency Bounds

## Abstract

Environment-assisted transport in photosynthetic light-harvesting complexes poses a puzzle: environmental noise, usually destructive to quantum coherence, can enhance excitation energy transfer (EET) efficiency. Building on the dephasing-assisted transport (DAT) mechanisms formalized for the Fenna–Matthews–Olson (FMO) complex and on the non-Markovian Hamiltonian framework for boson-mediated energy transfer (Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.18382696), we construct a minimal analytically solvable source–bridge–trap model that isolates the role of pure dephasing in the presence of bath memory. We derive a closed-form trapping efficiency $\eta(\gamma)$ as a function of the dephasing rate $\gamma$, the electronic coupling $J$, the trapping rate $\kappa$, and the exciton recombination rate $k_r$, and we evaluate it for a FMO-motivated parameter set ($J = 30\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, $\kappa = 1\ \text{ps}^{-1}$, $k_r = 0.1\ \text{ps}^{-1}$). Dephasing raises the efficiency from the coherent value $\eta_{\text{coh}} \approx 0.833$ to $\eta \approx 0.896$ at $\gamma = 50\ \text{cm}^{-1}$, a relative gain of $\approx 7.5\%$, while excessive dephasing ($\gamma = 2500\ \text{cm}^{-1}$) suppresses efficiency to $\approx 0.523$ via the quantum Zeno effect. The model reproduces, in closed form, the qualitative structure of noise-assisted transport observed in full network simulations and provides transparent bounds that can guide parameter scans in non-Markovian treatments of Photosystem II and related complexes.

## 1. Introduction

Excitation energy transfer in photosynthetic antenna complexes proceeds with near-unity efficiency over femtosecond-to-picosecond timescales, despite the fact that the chromophore network is strongly coupled to a structured protein–phonon environment. Two facts make this system a benchmark for open quantum systems theory. First, the environment is not merely a source of noise: intermediate dephasing rates can *improve* transport, a phenomenon known as environment-assisted quantum transport (ENAQT) or dephasing-assisted transport (DAT) [1]. Second, the environment possesses memory: the bath correlation time is comparable to the system timescale, so the dynamical map is not Markovian, and the tools of non-Markovian quantum dynamics — exact master equations with time-dependent coefficients, time-convolutionless generators, and divisibility analysis — become relevant [2], [3], [4].

The present paper is a re-entry of Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.18382696 ("Non-Markian Hamiltonian Dynamics of Boson-Mediated Energy Transfer") into the light-harvesting domain. The question we pose is deliberately narrow: *how much of the noise-assisted transport effect survives in the smallest possible analytically tractable model, and what are the explicit numerical bounds on the gain?* Rather than simulating the full seven-site FMO Hamiltonian or the Photosystem II (PSII) antenna, we reduce the network to a source–bridge–trap chain and derive a closed-form expression for the trapping efficiency. The value of such a reduction is threefold: (i) every number in the paper is reproducible by hand; (ii) the analytic dependence on the dephasing rate $\gamma$ exposes the trade-off between noise-assisted delocalization and Zeno suppression without numerical scanning; and (iii) the model provides a null hypothesis against which full non-Markovian simulations of FMO [1], [6] and PSII [10] should be judged — a gain larger than the analytic bound derived here must be attributable to network topology, not to dephasing per se.

Our headline quantitative results, derived in full in Section 4, are: a coherent-baseline efficiency $\eta_{\text{coh}} = 0.8333$; a dephasing-assisted maximum $\eta = 0.8958$ at $\gamma = 50\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ (relative gain $7.5\%$); and a Zeno-suppressed value $\eta = 0.5233$ at $\gamma = 2500\ \text{cm}^{-1}$. All parameter values are stated with their provenance, and all arithmetic is shown step by step.

## 2. Background and Related Work

We discuss the bibliography in its given order.

**[1] Noise-assisted energy transfer in quantum networks and light-harvesting complexes (arXiv:0910.4153v2).** This work provides the physically intuitive mechanisms of DAT in a hybrid exciton–site basis and shows that noise enables transfer with efficiencies well above $90\%$ across the FMO complex. It is the direct conceptual ancestor of our model: our source–bridge–trap chain is the minimal two-coupling skeleton of the FMO network analyzed there, and our closed-form $\eta(\gamma)$ makes explicit the trade-off that [1] demonstrates numerically.

**[2] Non-Markovian quantum dynamics: What is it good for? (arXiv:2001.02247v1).** This perspective surveys experimental characterization and quantification of non-Markovianity enabled by modern quantum engineering and control. It motivates our insistence that any claim of "memory-assisted" transport be phrased in terms of operationally defined quantities — here, the trapping efficiency — rather than abstract non-Markovianity measures.

**[3] Non-Markovian decoherence dynamics of entangled coherent states (arXiv:0705.2472v3).** Using the Feynman–Vernon influence functional in the coherent-state representation, this work derives an exact master equation with time-dependent coefficients reflecting environmental memory, recovering the Markovian limit under approximation. It supplies the microscopic template by which our phenomenological dephasing rate $\gamma$ could be replaced by a structured bath spectral density; our model is the Markovian-dephasing corner of that more general framework.

**[4] Floquet stroboscopic divisibility in non-Markovian dynamics (arXiv:1707.04423v2).** For time-convolutionless master equations with time-periodic rates, this work shows that stroboscopically divisible dynamical maps can exist at discrete times. This is relevant as a caution: our efficiency functional $\eta(\gamma)$ assumes a time-independent dephasing rate, and any extension to periodically modulated environments must reckon with the divisibility structure analyzed in [4].

**[5] Availing non-Markovian dynamics in effective negative temperature-based transient quantum Otto engines (arXiv:2310.04347v2).** This work demonstrates that terminating isochoric strokes before equilibration improves engine efficiency, for both Markovian and non-Markovian reservoirs. It is a thermodynamic analogue of our central observation: incomplete relaxation (finite memory, finite interaction time) can be a resource rather than a defect, and both papers exploit the same structural fact that the naive Markovian fixed point is not optimal.

**[6] Efficient estimation of energy transfer efficiency in light-harvesting complexes (arXiv:1103.3823v4).** Addressing the computational cost of simulating EET under non-perturbative, non-Markovian interactions with protein backbone and photonic/phononic environments, this work develops efficient estimation strategies for the transfer efficiency. Our closed-form model serves the same goal at the opposite end of the complexity spectrum: where [6] accelerates full simulations, we bound what full simulations can achieve with a two-parameter analytic expression.

**[7] Nuclear quantum effects slow down the energy transfer in biological light-harvesting complexes (arXiv:2501.02212v1).** Using a mixed quantum–classical theory consistent with quantum–classical equilibrium, this work finds that high-frequency chromophore vibrations *slow* EET. This is an important counterpoint to the DAT narrative: not all environmental coupling assists.
