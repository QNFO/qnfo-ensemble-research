## Research idea

Ultrametric Tree Geometry Enables Passive Quantum Error Confinement. Claim: encoding logical qubits on ultrametric (p-adic/Bruhat–Tits tree) state spaces confines errors geometrically, yielding passive fault tolerance with near-zero logical error rates even at physical error rates up to 40%, without active correction. Why it matters: it challenges the inherited continuity assumption of quantum state space and could drastically reduce qubit overhead and cooling requirements. How to test: (1) rigorously derive whether a well-defined ultrametric qubit Hilbert space exists with unitary gate structure and noise channels respecting the tree metric; (2) audit the existing simulation for hidden assumptions (e.g., whether 'error confinement' merely reflects projection/truncation rather than physical suppression); (3) compare logical error scaling versus depth against surface codes under identical noise models; (4) verify the claimed 5% threshold and depth-7 predictions analytically or via independent Monte Carlo replication.
