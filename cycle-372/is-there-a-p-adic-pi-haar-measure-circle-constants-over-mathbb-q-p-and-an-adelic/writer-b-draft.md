# Is There a $p$-Adic $\pi$? Haar-Measure Circle Constants over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, an Adelic Product Formula, and a Test of the Corpus Conjecture $\pi_p=(p-1)/p$

## Abstract

The real constant $\pi \approx 3.14159$ is defined as the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, and an active adelic-physics program postulates a place-wise analogue $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ at every prime $p$. No geometric computation has previously tested whether $p$-adic circle geometry supports such a value under any canonical definition of circumference. We compute, in the Haar-measure framework on $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ with the sup-norm, the measure of the $p$-adic sphere $S_r = \{x : \|x\| = r\}$ and the associated scale-invariant ratios. The analysis yields the exact formulas $\mu(B_r) = r^2$, $C_p(r) = r^2(1-p^{-2})$, $\kappa_p = 1 - p^{-2}$, and $\kappa'_p = \tfrac{1}{2}(1-p^{-2})$, all independent of the radius $r$. The finite product over $p \leq 13$ is $P_{13} = 442368/715715 \approx 0.618078$, the Euler product over all primes is $\prod_p \kappa_p = 6/\pi^2 \approx 0.607927$, and the all-place combination is $\pi \prod_p \kappa_p = 6/\pi \approx 1.909859 \neq 1$. Comparing with the conjecture gives the exact difference $\kappa_p - (p-1)/p = (p-1)/p^2 > 0$ for every prime. Under the Haar-measure definition, therefore, the conjectured value $(p-1)/p$ is not supported; the computation constrains the adelic-$\pi$ program without refuting other possible definitions, and provides a concrete consistency check for any adelic model that assumes a place-wise $\pi_p$.

## 1. Introduction

A community of physicists and mathematicians is currently building an adelic research program in which every physical quantity defined over the rationals should have a well-defined value at each completion of $\mathbb{Q}$: the real place and one place $p$ per prime. This program faces a concrete, blocking problem today. Its central documents assert that the circle constant is adelic with the specific value $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ at each prime [9], and they propose to rebuild quantum mechanics, cosmology, and physical law on this basis [10]. If that assertion is correct, adelic models can replace the archimedean $\pi$ by the family $\{\pi_p\}$ place by place, and every consistency check across places changes. If it is incorrect, resources invested in such models are built on an unverified value, and the program must either produce a different definition of "$p$-adic circumference" or abandon the place-wise constant. No geometric computation existed that tests the asserted value against what $p$-adic circle geometry actually yields under a canonical measure.

This paper performs that test under the most canonical available definition. On $\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ with the sup-norm, Haar measure is the unique translation-invariant Borel measure, normalized here by $\mu(B_1) = 1$. We define the $p$-adic circle of radius $r$ as the sphere $S_r = \{x : \|x\| = r\}$, its circumference as $C_p(r) = \mu(S_r)$, and its diameter as the ultrametric diameter $D_p(r) = \operatorname{diam}(S_r)$, with the archimedean-style alternative $D'_p(r) = 2r$ reported alongside. The resulting scale-invariant ratios $\kappa_p = C_p(r)/D_p(r)$ and $\kappa'_p = C_p(r)/D'_p(r)$ are then compared, term by term, with the conjectured $\pi_p = (p-1)/p$ of [9].

The outcome is unambiguous for this definition: the natural circle constant is $\kappa_p = 1 - p^{-2}$, a rational number in $(0,1)$ that tends to $1$ as $p \to \infty$, not to $\pi$ and not to $(p-1)/p$. The conjectured value fails under this definition by the exact positive gap $(p-1)/p^2$ at every prime. What the computation does not do is disprove the adelic-$\pi$ program as a whole: the program's claim concerns a quantity whose definition it has not fixed, and a different principled definition of $p$-adic arc length could yield a different constant. The paper therefore reports a refutation of the conjectured value under one canonical definition, together with the exact conditions under which that refutation would be overturned.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 surveys the related work in p-adic number theory, p-adic mathematical physics, and the adelic program itself. Section 3 fixes the norm, the measure, the circle, and the comparison protocol. Section 4 carries out the full arithmetic derivation. Section 5 collects the numerical outcomes. Section 6 discusses implications, limitations, and falsifiability. Section 7 translates the findings into practical guidance. Section 8 concludes.

## 2. Background and Related Work

The question of a $p$-adic circle constant sits at the intersection of three bodies of work: the arithmetic of special values, p-adic mathematical physics, and the adelic program that motivates the conjecture tested here.

**Special values and $p$-adic analogues of classical constants.** The p-adic Beilinson conjecture of [1] formulates a p-adic analogue of Borel's theorem, relating regulators for higher $K$-groups of number fields to special values of zeta-functions via syntomic regulators and p-adic $L$-functions. Its Section 2 recalls the classical formula $w\sqrt{|D_k|}\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_k(s) = 2^{r_1}(2\pi)^{r_2}|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_k)|R$ for a totally real field $k$, in which the archimedean $2\pi$ enters the special-value formula through the complex embeddings of $k$; its Section 3 then refines the conjecture to Artin motives by decomposing with idempotents $\pi$ in a group ring $E[G]$. This is the precise sense in which translating archimedean constants into $p$-adic language is an active and technically developed program: the archimedean $\pi$ appears inside formulas whose $p$-adic analogues are conjectured to exist, but the conjectures concern $L$-values and regulators, not a geometric circle constant. The work of [3] extends this machinery: for an odd prime $p$ and a $p$-stabilized Artin representation $\rho$, it introduces a family of $p$-adic Stark regulators, formulates an Iwasawa-Greenberg main conjecture, and proves an interpolation formula (its Theorem A) for a generator of the characteristic ideal of the associated Selmer module. Both [1] and [3] support the premise that place-wise analogues of classical quantities are well-defined objects of study; neither provides, nor claims to provide, a $p$-adic value of $\pi$.
