## Research idea

Detecting Simulation Glitches via Cross-Modal Temporal Binding Asynchronies. Claim: If reality is simulated, the first detectable inconsistency lies not in fundamental physics but at the mind-world interface—where sensory streams are rendered by separate subroutines with different clock rates. This predicts systematic, non-physiological cross-modal timing offsets (e.g., audiovisual synchrony judgments that vary with cognitive load or scene complexity) and inter-hemispheric delays inconsistent with known corpus callosum latencies. Testable via psychophysics: 40 Hz flickering visual stimuli paired with auditory clicks, EEG/MEG recording of temporal binding windows, searching for asynchrony signatures that violate neurophysiological constraints or correlate with external 'computational load' proxies. Formal analysis could model what latency jitter distributions a renderer would produce versus biological noise, yielding a falsifiable statistical signature distinguishing simulation artifacts from ordinary neural variability.
