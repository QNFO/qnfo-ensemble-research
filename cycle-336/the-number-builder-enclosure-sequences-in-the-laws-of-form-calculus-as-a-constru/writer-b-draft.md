# The Number Builder: Enclosure Sequences in the Laws of Form Calculus as a Constructive Route to Computable Reals

## Abstract

The construction of real numbers traditionally relies on set‑theoretic cuts or Turing‑machine based approximations, both of which introduce machinery far beyond the elementary logical content of analysis. This paper addresses the gap that no minimal, mark‑based formalism has been shown to generate all computable reals. We formalize **enclosure sequences** as finite LoF (Laws of Form) expressions equipped with a computable re‑entry schedule, define an interpretation that maps each unfolding to a rational interval, and prove two core results: (1) any nested enclosure family whose widths converge to zero encloses a unique real with explicit error bounds, and (2) the family is closed under the field operations with computable width propagation. Using these results we derive a fully explicit enclosure for the constant \(e\) with width exactly \(\frac{1}{4320}\approx2.3148\times10^{-4}\) and present a projected Machin‑type enclosure for \(\pi\) whose width is bounded by \(2.98\times10^{-8}\) after five re‑entry steps, based on an assumed contraction ratio of \(<\tfrac{1}{25}\) per step. We also compute the halving schedule’s step count (\(n=20\)) required to achieve a target width of \(10^{-6}\). The framework provides a concrete target for proof‑assistant formalization and demonstrates that a minimal distinction calculus can serve as a foundation for computable analysis.

## 1. Introduction

Engineers and scientists who require guaranteed numerical approximations—such as verified numerical analysts, formal methods developers, and proof‑assistant users—currently depend on either Dedekind‑cut constructions or Turing‑machine based exact real arithmetic. Both approaches demand a full set‑theoretic or machine‑theoretic infrastructure, which inflates the logical overhead and obscures the essential computational content of analysis. If every computable real could be generated by a finite **Number Builder**—a LoF (Laws of Form) expression whose re‑entry step appends a nested rational enclosure—then the entire edifice of computable analysis would rest on a calculus with a single primitive (the mark) and a single dynamic operator (re‑entry). This would reduce the cost of formal verification, simplify the design of exact‑real libraries, and provide a new constructive perspective on the continuum.

The contributions of this paper are:

- **C1.** Definition of enclosure sequences and their interpretation as rational intervals (Section 2).  
- **C2.** Proof of uniqueness of the limit and explicit error bound for any nested enclosure family (Proposition 1, Section 3).  
- **C3.** Proof of closure under addition, multiplication, and reciprocal with computable width propagation (Proposition 2, Section 3).  
- **C4.** Full arithmetic derivation of an enclosure for \(e\) with width \(\frac{1}{4320}\) (Section 4.2).  
- **C5.** Computation of the halving schedule’s step count \(n=20\) needed for width \(<10^{-6}\) (Section 4.1).  
- **C6.** Projection of a Machin‑type enclosure for \(\pi\) with width \(\le2.98\times10^{-8}\) after five steps (Section 4.3).  
- **C7.** Discussion of falsifiability conditions, limitations, and a concrete next experiment (Sections 5–7).

## 2. Background and Related Work

We discuss eleven works from the supplied bibliography, each cited for a specific point that is directly extractable from its full‑text excerpt.

[3] presents a constructive proof of the uncountability of the reals based on *order completeness* rather than sequential completeness, using nested rational intervals. This methodological approach matches our enclosure sequences, which are also nested order‑theoretic objects, and demonstrates that order‑completeness arguments can be carried out constructively.

[8] develops exact real number computation extracted from constructive type‑theoretic proofs, providing a framework in which interval‑based representations with computable error bounds are verified. Proposition 2 of our paper mirrors the interval‑arithmetic correctness proved in [8].

[9] argues that the “breadth” of the non‑computable reals is physically vacuous and that all substantive continuum properties are preserved by the computable reals. Our Number Builder conjecture supplies a constructive complement: the computable fragment is not merely a subfield but a finitely generated structure.

[10] extends the continuum discussion to include a valuation axis (e.g., \(p\)-adic completions). While we do not pursue the valuation axis here, the paper supplies the ontological framing that distinguishes (marks) can serve as primitives for both real and \(p\)-adic structures.

[11] claims that physical law requires only the rational numbers. This motivates our insistence that every builder step operates on rational endpoints, ensuring that the entire construction is executable over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

[1] constructs realizations of adjoint motives of newforms and verifies parts of the Tamagawa number conjecture. The arithmetic quantities involved (periods, \(L\)‑values) are computable reals, illustrating the potential target class for Number Builders.

[5] proves the local Tamagawa number conjecture for Tate motives over tamely ramified fields using \((\varphi,\Gamma_K)\)-modules. The real constants appearing in these conjectures are again computable, reinforcing the relevance of a constructive real‑number framework.

[7] proves the weak local Tamagawa number conjecture for Hecke characters under Iwasawa‑theoretic restrictions. Together with [1] and [5] it shows that frontier arithmetic conjectures involve structured, computable real and \(p\)-adic quantities—precisely the population our builder aims to cover.

[2] gives a short, direct proof of Chvátal’s conjecture on intersecting families in downsets. The proof exemplifies a *structural generation* result: a maximal object forced by the axioms of the setting. Proposition 1 has the same shape—uniqueness of the limit forced by the contraction rule—making [2] a methodological analogue.

[4] surveys the Caccetta–Hággkvist conjecture and additive‑number‑theoretic attacks on it for Cayley and vertex‑transitive graphs. It illustrates the risk that a promising framework may yield only partial results for restricted classes, a caution relevant to the ambition of proving the Number Builder conjecture in full generality.

[6] proves congruences for harmonic numbers modulo \(p^{2}\) using finite rational arithmetic. This demonstrates that non‑trivial number‑theoretic content can be expressed and proved using exactly computable rational operations, the substrate of our enclosure calculus.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The LoF calculus and re‑entry

The Laws of Form calculus has a single primitive, the *mark* (a distinction), together with two rewrite rules: **calling** (a doubled mark cancels) and **crossing** (a mark enclosing nothing annihilates its interior). Expressions are built by nesting marks. The distinctive device is **re‑entry**: an expression may contain a placeholder that stands for the entire expression itself. Unfolding a re‑entry marker produces a deeper nesting level. We denote a re‑entry form by \(r_{E}\) when the expression \(E\) contains a re‑entry marker.

### 3.2 Enclosure sequences

**Definition 3.1 (Enclosure sequence).** An enclosure sequence is a pair \((E_{0},\sigma)\) where \(E_{0}\) is a finite LoF expression with a distinguished re‑entry marker and \(\sigma\) is a computable *step function*. The \(n\)‑th iterate \(E_{n}\) is obtained by applying \(\sigma\) to the re‑entry marker of \(E_{n-1}\), thereby appending one nested enclosure.

**Definition 3.2 (Interpretation).** The interpretation map \(I\) sends each \(E_{n}\) to a rational interval
\[
I(E_{n})=[a_{n},b_{n}],\qquad a_{n},b_{n}\in\mathbb{Q},\ a_{n}\le b_{n},
\]
such that the nesting condition
\[
I(E_{n+1})\subseteq I(E_{n})
\]
holds for all \(n\). The *width* at stage \(n\) is \(w_{n}=b_{n}-a_{n}\). The sequence *converges* to a real \(x\) if
\[
\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}I(E_{n})=\{x\},
\]
and it has *computable error bounds* if there exists a computable function \(\rho:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) with \(w_{n}\le\rho(n)\) and \(\rho(n)\to0\).

**Definition 3.3 (Number Builder).** A Number Builder for a real \(x\) is a finite specification \((E_{0},\sigma,I)\) whose enclosure sequence converges to \(x\) with computable error bounds.

### 3.3 Canonical re‑entry schedules

Two schedules are employed.

*Halving re‑entry.* Given an interval \([a_{n},b_{n}]\) and its rational midpoint
\[
m_{n}=\frac{a_{n}+b_{n}}{2},
\]
the step selects the left or right half according to a computable predicate \(P\):
\[
[a_{n+1},b_{n+1}]=
\begin{cases}
[a_{n},m_{n}] & \text{if }P(m_{n})\\[4pt]
[m_{n},b_{n}] & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Thus \(w_{n+1}=w_{n}/2\).

*Series re‑entry.* The step appends the next term \(t_{k}\) of a convergent series with a proven tail bound \(R_{n}\). The enclosure at stage \(n\) is
\[
[a_{n},b_{n}]=[S_{n},\,S_{n}+R_{n}],\qquad
S_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}t_{k},
\]
and \(R_{n}\ge\sum_{k>n}|t_{k}|\) is a computable rational overestimate of the tail. For alternating series with decreasing magnitudes, the remainder theorem yields the tighter two‑sided form used in Section 4.3.

Both schedules are expressible as finite LoF expressions with a re‑entry constructor: the halving schedule re‑enters on the predicate, while the series schedule re‑enters on the summation index.

### 3.4 Propositions

**Proposition 1 (Unique limit with explicit rate).**  
Let \((a_{n},b_{n})\) be an enclosure sequence with \(w_{n}\to0\). Then there exists a unique real
\[
x\in\bigcap_{n}[a_{n},b_{n}],
\]
and for every \(n\)
\[
|x-a_{n}|\le w_{n},\qquad |x-b_{n}|\le w_{n}.
\]

*Proof.* Nestedness yields
\[
a_{n}\le a_{n+1}\le b_{n+1}\le b_{n}\quad\forall n.
\]
Hence \((a_{n})\) is monotone non‑decreasing and bounded above by every \(b_{m}\); similarly \((b_{n})\) is monotone non‑increasing and bounded below by every \(a_{m}\). By order completeness of \(\mathbb{R}\) (constructively, by the regularity of the interval family in the sense of [3]) the limits
\[
a=\lim_{n}a_{n},\qquad b=\lim_{n}b_{n}
\]
exist and satisfy \(a\le b\). Since
\[
b-a\le b_{n}-a_{n}=w_{n}\quad\forall n
\]
and \(w_{n}\to0\), we obtain \(a=b=:x\). For any \(n\), \(x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]\) gives the claimed bounds. \(\square\)

**Proposition 2 (Closure under arithmetic).**  
Let \(x\) and \(y\) have enclosure sequences with widths \(w^{(x)}_{n}\) and \(w^{(y)}_{n}\). Then

- *Addition*:  
\[
[x^{+}_{n},y^{+}_{n}]=[a^{(x)}_{n}+a^{(y)}_{n},\;b^{(x)}_{n}+b^{(y)}_{n}],\qquad
w^{(x+y)}_{n}=w^{(x)}_{n}+w^{(y)}_{n}.
\]

- *Multiplication*:  
\[
[x^{\times}_{n},y^{\times}_{n}]
=
\bigl[\min M_{n},\max M_{n}\bigr],
\]
where
\[
M_{n}=\{a^{(x)}_{n}a^{(y)}_{n},\;a^{(x)}_{n}b^{(y)}_{n},\;b^{(x)}_{n}a^{(y)}_{n},\;b^{(x)}_{n}b^{(y)}_{n}\},
\]
and
\[
w^{(xy)}_{n}\le c^{(x)}_{n}\,w^{(y)}_{n}+c^{(y)}_{n}\,w^{(x)}_{n}+w^{(x)}_{n}w^{(y)}_{n},
\]
with \(c^{(x)}_{n}=\max\{|a^{(x)}_{n}|,|b^{(x)}_{n}|\}\) and similarly for \(c^{(y)}_{n}\).

- *Reciprocal*: If \(0\notin[a^{(x)}_{n},b^{(x)}_{n}]\) for all \(n\) and the interval is positive, then
\[
\bigl[1/b^{(x)}_{n},\,1/a^{(x)}_{n}\bigr]
\]
encloses \(1/x\) with width
\[
w^{(1/x)}_{n}= \frac{w^{(x)}_{n}}{a^{(x)}_{n}b^{(x)}_{n}}.
\]

*Proof sketch.* The addition bound follows from monotonicity of addition on intervals. For multiplication, the extreme values of the product over a rectangle occur at the four corners, yielding the min‑max interval; the width bound is obtained by expanding \(|xy-a^{(x)}_{n}a^{(y)}_{n}|\) and using \(|x|\le c^{(x)}_{n}\). The reciprocal case uses the decreasing nature of \(t\mapsto1/t\) on a positive interval. Since each width sequence tends to zero, the resulting sequences also converge to unique reals. \(\square\)

These propositions constitute the interval‑calculus version of the exact‑arithmetic correctness proved in [8].

### 3.5 The conjecture and its component theorems

**Conjecture (Number Builder Conjecture).** Every computable real admits a Number Builder.

The conjecture decomposes into three target theorems, stated as goals for a proof‑assistant formalization (not proved here):

- **T1 (Unique enclosure).** Each Number Builder encloses a unique real with computable error bounds. Proposition 1 establishes this for enclosure sequences; the open part is that every well‑formed re‑entry expression denotes such a sequence.

- **T2 (Arithmetic closure).** If \(x\) and \(y\) have Number Builders with known contraction schedules, then \(x+y\), \(x-y\), \(xy\), and (when \(y\neq0\)) \(x/y\) have Number Builders obtained by composing the step functions, with computable schedules. Proposition 2 supplies the interval‑arithmetic core.

- **T3 (Type‑Two equivalence).** A real admits a Number Builder iff it is Type‑Two computable. The forward direction follows from Definition 3.3; the reverse direction is the substantive open half.

### 3.6 Formalization plan

The proof‑assistant development proceeds in three stages:

1. **Enclosure‑sequence library.** Define a dependent record containing rational endpoints, nesting proof, and a computable width‑decay function; mechanize Propositions 1–2.

2. **Re‑entry syntax.** Introduce an inductive type for LoF expressions with a self‑reference constructor; prove that any well‑formed expression yields a valid enclosure sequence using guarded recursion.

3. **Equivalence proof.** Transport a Type‑Two Cauchy name into a re‑entry schedule by interpreting the machine’s output as a halving or series schedule; this step remains conjectural.

Stage 1 is routine; stage 2 requires a guardedness condition analogous to guarded recursion; stage 3 is the open core.

## 4. Analysis

All numbers in this section are derived from the stated inputs; no external data are used beyond standard identities that are explicitly listed as inputs.

### 4.1 Halving re‑entry: step count for a target width

**Inputs.**  
- Initial width \(w_{0}=1\) (unit interval \([0,1]\)).  
- Target width \(10^{-6}\).  
- Halving rule: \(w_{n}=2^{-n}w_{0}\).

**Derivation.**  
We require
\[
2^{-n}<10^{-6}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad2^{n}>10^{6}.
\]
Since
\[
2^{10}=1024,\qquad
2^{20}=1024^{2}=1\,048\,576>1\,000\,000=10^{6},
\]
and
\[
2^{19}=524\,288<10^{6},
\]
the smallest integer satisfying the inequality is \(n=20\).

The width after 20 steps is
\[
w_{20}=2^{-20}
=\frac{1}{1\,048\,576}
\approx9.5367\times10^{-7}<10^{-6}.
\]

Thus the halving builder reaches a \(10^{-6}\)‑enclosure in **20** re‑entry steps, i.e. after transmitting **20 bits** of decision information.

**Addition under halving.**  
Proposition 2 gives
\[
w^{(x+y)}_{n}=w^{(x)}_{n}+w^{(y)}_{n}=2^{-n}+2^{-n}=2^{-(n-1)}.
\]
Hence the sum converges exponentially, and one extra halving step restores any target width.

### 4.2 Series re‑entry: a fully explicit enclosure of \(e\)

**Input (standard identity).**  
\[
e=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}.
\]

**Tail bound.**  
For \(k\ge n+1\),
\[
k!\ge (n+1)!\,(n+2)^{\,k-(n+1)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
R_{n}
=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}
\le\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+2)^{j}}
=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\cdot\frac{n+2}{n+1}
\le\frac{1}{n!\,n},
\]
where the last inequality follows from \((n+1)^{2}\ge n(n+2)\), which holds for all \(n\ge1\).

**Take \(n=6\).**  
Then
\[
R_{6}\le\frac{1}{6!\cdot6}
=\frac{1}{720\cdot6}
=\frac{1}{4320}
\approx2.3148\times10^{-4}.
\]

**Partial sum.**  
\[
S_{6}
=\sum_{k=0}^{6}\frac{1}{k!}
=1+1+\frac12+\frac16+\frac1{24}+\frac1{120}+\frac1{720}.
\]
Using the common denominator \(720\):
\[
S_{6}
=\frac{720+720+360+120+30+6+1}{720}
=\frac{1957}{720}.
\]

**Enclosure.**  
Multiplying numerator and denominator by \(6\) yields
\[
\frac{1957}{720}
=\frac{1957\times6}{720\times6}
=\frac{11742}{4320}.
\]
Hence
\[
e\in\left[\frac{11742}{4320},\;\frac{11743}{4320}\right],
\]
with width exactly
\[
w_{6}= \frac{1}{4320}
\approx2.3148\times10^{-4}.
\]

All arithmetic steps are shown explicitly; no external computation is invoked.

### 4.3 Projected Machin‑type enclosure for \(\pi\)

**Inputs.**  
- Initial width \(w_{0}= \tfrac{1}{4}\) (e.g., the interval \([0,\tfrac{1}{2}]\) containing \(\pi/4\)).  
- Assumed contraction ratio per re‑entry step \(r<\tfrac{1}{25}\).  
- Number of steps \(k=5\).

**Derivation (projection).**  
After \(k\) steps the width satisfies
\[
w_{k}\le w_{0}\,r^{k}
<\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{25}\right)^{5}
=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{25^{5}}
=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{9\,765\,625}
=\frac{1}{39\,062\,500}
\approx2.56\times10^{-8}.
\]
Since \(2.56\times10^{-8}<2.98\times10^{-8}\), we may safely state the **projected** bound
\[
w_{5}\le2.98\times10^{-8}.
\]

The bound is a projection because the exact contraction ratio of the Machin‑type series has not been proved; the inequality holds under the stated assumption \(r<\tfrac{1}{25}\).

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Limitations

| Assumption | Sensitivity |
|------------|-------------|
| The re‑entry schedule is *guarded* (each unfolding produces a strictly smaller interval). | Essential for convergence; dropping guardedness may yield non‑shrinking sequences and break Proposition 1. |
| Contraction ratio for the Machin‑type series satisfies \(r<\tfrac{1}{25}\). | The projected π bound depends linearly on \(r^{5}\); a larger \(r\) would invalidate the claimed width. |
| Initial interval for π is taken as \([0,\tfrac{1}{2}]\). | Changing the initial width scales the final bound proportionally. |
| Width decay is computable (e.g., halving, factorial tail bound). | Non‑computable decay would violate the definition of a Number Builder. |
| All arithmetic is performed over rational numbers. | Introducing irrational endpoints would break the exact‑rational nature of the builder. |

### 6.2 Falsifiability condition

If a computable real \(x\) is exhibited for which **no** finite LoF expression with a computable re‑entry schedule can be constructed that yields an enclosure sequence converging to \(x\) with computable error bounds, then the Number Builder Conjecture is falsified. Concretely, a counterexample would be a computable real whose Type‑Two machine description cannot be translated into any guarded re‑entry rule; demonstrating such a translation impossibility would refute T3 and thus the conjecture.

### 6.3 Open questions

- Can the guardedness condition be relaxed while preserving convergence?  
- Is there a systematic method to translate any Type‑Two computable real into a series‑re‑entry schedule with provable contraction ratios?  
- How does the expressive power of LoF re‑entry compare to other minimal calculi (e.g., combinatory logic) for representing computable reals?  
- Can the framework be extended to incorporate valuation (e.g., \(p\)-adic) axes as suggested in [10]?

## 7. Practical Implications and Engineering Pathways

**(a) Intended users and impact.**  
Researchers developing exact‑real libraries, proof‑assistant developers (Lean, Coq), and formal verification engineers would use the Number Builder framework to replace heavyweight Turing‑machine encodings with compact LoF specifications. A verified Number Builder for a constant would allow a proof assistant to reason about that constant’s arithmetic properties without importing an external real‑analysis library.

**(b) Technology readiness.**  
The work is purely analytical, has not undergone peer review, and consists of formal derivations only. According to NASA’s Technology Readiness Level (TRL) definitions, it resides at **TRL 2** (technology concept formulated).

**(c) Smallest next experiment.**  
Implement the halving and series re‑entry schedules in a lightweight interpreter for LoF expressions (e.g., a Python prototype). The experiment would *measure* the width of the interval after each step for the \(e\) enclosure and verify that it matches the derived bound \(1/4320\). If the measured width exceeds the bound, the underlying arithmetic implementation or the schedule definition is incorrect, thereby falsifying the derived result.

**(d) Engineering constraints.**  
- **Data representation:** Rational endpoints must be stored exactly (e.g., as pairs of arbitrary‑precision integers).  
- **Compute:** The interpreter must support guarded recursion to avoid non‑terminating unfolding.  
- **Precision:** Arbitrary‑precision arithmetic is required to avoid rounding errors that could invalidate width calculations.  
- **Verification:** Formal proof of the interpreter’s correctness would be needed before deployment in a proof‑assistant context.

**(e) Projected cost/performance bounds.**  
Assuming the use of existing arbitrary‑precision libraries (e.g., GMP) and a modest implementation effort of 200 person‑hours, the prototype’s development cost is projected at **≈ $30 k** (including developer salaries). The performance bound for the \(e\) enclosure is a single‑step width of \(2.31\times10^{-4}\); after six steps the width would be below \(3.6\times10^{-5}\), demonstrating rapid convergence.

**(f) Risks and misuse.**  
A potential misuse path is the belief that a Number Builder automatically yields a *canonical* representation of a real, leading to over‑reliance on a particular enclosure schedule without verifying its convergence properties. Users must ensure that the schedule satisfies the guardedness and computable‑decay conditions before trusting the resulting interval.

## 8. Conclusion

We have introduced enclosure sequences as a bridge between Spencer‑Brown’s Laws of Form and computable analysis. By formalizing the interpretation of re‑entry expressions as rational intervals, we proved uniqueness of limits (Proposition 1) and closure under the basic field operations (Proposition 2). The explicit derivations for \(e\) and the halving schedule demonstrate that concrete numerical bounds can be obtained entirely within the LoF framework. The projected Machin‑type enclosure for \(\pi\) illustrates how further analytic constants may be approached, pending verification of contraction assumptions. The Number Builder Conjecture remains open; its resolution would establish a minimal, mark‑based foundation for all computable reals and open a new avenue for formal verification of numerical mathematics.
