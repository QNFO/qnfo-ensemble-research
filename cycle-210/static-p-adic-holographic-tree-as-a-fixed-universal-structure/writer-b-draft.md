# A Static p-Adic Holographic Tree as a Fixed Universe Structure: Observer Embeddings, Effective Time, and Entropy Scaling

## Abstract

Cosmological observation rests on the assumption that the universe evolves, yet quantum gravity suggests that the fundamental description may be static, with time emerging from correlations. What is missing is a single fixed structure from which entropy scaling, an effective Friedmann-like expansion, and a nearly scale-invariant fluctuation spectrum can all be derived and confronted with data. We formalize a static holographic tree whose geometry is $p$-adic: nodes are elementary events, edges are relations, and observer worldlines are infinite paths. Conditioning on a clock subsystem in the Page-Wootters manner yields an effective time with scale factor $a(\tau)=p^{\tau/\tau_0}$ and expansion rate $H=\ln p/\tau_0$; for $p=3$ this gives $H=1.0986$ per tick. Entropy computed as edge boundary count gives $S(l)=\log_p l$ for a comb-shaped tree, exactly saturating the MERA upper bound $S_l\leq\log_2 l$ in one dimension, while a balanced $p$-ary tree gives $S(l)=2\log_p l$. Random-walk accumulation of fluctuations along worldlines of length $n$ gives amplitude $\sigma=\sqrt{n}$ and a per-level drift of $\sqrt{21/20}-1=2.47\%$, below the $5\%$ threshold reported for Planck detectability of $p$-adic modulations. The framework converts a static ontology into three quantitative, falsifiable predictions.

## 1. Introduction

Cosmologists and quantum-gravity theorists face a structural problem that blocks progress on the interpretation of cosmic microwave background (CMB) statistics: the standard analysis pipeline assumes a dynamical spacetime, so any underlying static description of the universe has no quantitative channel through which to touch data. A researcher who holds that the fundamental ontology is timeless — as in the Wheeler-DeWitt sector, where the constraint $H|\Psi\rangle=0$ removes external time — currently cannot answer what entropy scaling, expansion, or the CMB spectrum should look like in such a world. The cost is conceptual and practical at once: static ontologies remain philosophy-adjacent because they generate no numbers, while the data analysis community has no reason to search for the signatures such ontologies predict. If a single fixed mathematical structure could be shown to reproduce holographic entropy bounds, an effective expanding universe, and a nearly scale-invariant spectrum with a computable detectability threshold, then two things change: static ontology programs acquire falsifiable content, and CMB data analysis acquires a concrete new target, namely log-periodic modulations tied to a radix $p$.

This paper builds that channel for one specific static structure. The universe is modeled as a single fixed hierarchical tree $\mathcal{T}_{p,L}$ with $p$-adic (ultrametric) geometry and holographic information content. Nothing in the tree evolves. Nodes are elementary events, edges are relations, and all apparent dynamics — expansion, fluctuation growth, thermal spectra — are derived from the way observer worldlines, defined as infinite paths through the tree, embed in and sample the fixed structure. The program follows the static relational proposal of [12], which argues that the fundamental ontology is a timeless, discrete, non-Archimedean, holographic network and that experienced space and time are an epistemic interface generated when a subgraph (the observer) interacts with the network. The present work supplies the derivations that proposal sketches: effective time, entropy scaling, and spectral statistics.

The $p$-adic choice is not decorative. Ultrametric distances satisfy the strong triangle inequality and are exactly tree metrics, so a hierarchical tree and a $p$-adic valuation describe the same geometry; the synthesis in [14] makes precisely this chain explicit, from a radix $p$ through $p$-adic numbers $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and the ultrametric $d_p(x,y)=|x-y|_p$ to tree hierarchies and Bruhat-Tits buildings. The holographic choice is likewise operational: on a tree, the information accessible to a region is counted by its boundary, and the entanglement entropy of tensor networks built on branching trees has known scaling bounds [11], giving an immediate quantitative handle.

The contributions are:

- A formal definition of the static $p$-adic holographic tree $\mathcal{T}_{p,L}$, its ultrametric, and observer worldlines as paths, with node and information counts computed explicitly for $p=3$, $L=20$ (Section 3, quantified in Section 4).
- A derivation of effective time by Page-Wootters conditioning on a clock path, yielding scale factor $a(\tau)=p^{\tau/\tau_0}$ and $H=\ln p/\tau_0$, with the value $H=1.0986$ per tick at $p=3$ (Sections 3 and 4).
- A derivation of holographic entropy scaling from edge-boundary counting: $S(l)=\log_p l$ for the comb tree, which saturates the MERA bound $S_l\leq\log_2 l$ of [11] at $p=2$, versus $S(l)=2\log_p l$ for the balanced tree (Section 4).
- A derivation of the fluctuation amplitude from random-walk accumulation along worldlines, $\sigma=\sqrt{n}$ following the $\sqrt{n}$ law demonstrated for coupled oscillators in [8], giving a per-level amplitude drift of $2.47\%$ and a detectability assessment against the $5\%$ Planck threshold reported in [14] (Section 4).
- A statement of the falsification conditions, including the no-go result of [14] that Page-Wootters conditional clock distances cannot be exactly ultrametric for nontrivial finite spectra, which bounds the claim of exact $p$-adic geometry for the effective time sector (Sections 4 and 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The closest formal antecedent for the entropy side of this program is the analysis of entanglement scaling in the multi-scale entanglement renormalization ansatz (MERA) and its branching generalization. [11] derives upper bounds on entanglement entropy for tensor networks built on holographic trees, of the form $S_l\leq\log_2(l)$ in $D=1$ dimensions and $S\approx l^{D-1}$ in $D>1$, and explains the mechanism: in $D=1$ the number of indices $n^{\text{tr}}_z$ traced out at scale $z$ is constant, so all $O(\log_2 l)$ scales contribute equally and the scaling is logarithmic, whereas in $D>1$ that number decays exponentially and a strict boundary law results. [11] further shows the bounds take the form $S_l\approx l^{D-1}f(l)$ with corrections $f(l)=(\log l)^{\kappa}$ or $f(l)=l^{\alpha}$, $0\leq\alpha\leq1$, depending on the branching pattern of the holographic tree. This is exactly the quantity this paper computes from edge boundaries, and the comb-versus-balanced tree contrast in Section 4 realizes the two regimes the MERA analysis distinguishes.

On the graph-model side, [3] develops the construction of holographic simple tree graphs that realize a given entropy vector subject to a chordality condition, working in entropy space $\mathbb{R}^{\sf D}$ with $\sf D=2^{\sf N}-1$ for $\sf N$ parties, and builds a correlation-hypergraph toolkit including coarse-graining and fine-graining of subsystems. [3] reports that the algorithm succeeds for entropy vectors satisfying chordality, that sufficiency of chordality is conjectured but not yet proven, and that chordality is not necessary for realizability by non-simple trees. For this paper, [3] establishes that tree geometries can carry holographic entropy data nontrivially, which is the load-bearing assumption behind computing entropy as boundary count on $\mathcal{T}_{p,L}$.

The static-ontology framing comes from [12], which defines the central thesis that the fundamental ontology is a static, discrete, relational network that is timeless, non-Archimedean, and holographic, with experience an epistemic interface generated by a subgraph-observer interacting with the network, and which grounds the program in the Wheeler-DeWitt timeless wave function. [12] also draws the epistemic-ontological distinction that this paper respects by keeping the fixed tree ontological and the derived time epistemic. A methodological caution is supplied by [13], which analyzes reification — the error of treating mathematical constructs as mind-independent physical entities — and argues that experiments should discriminate between competing process models via relational signatures such as network connectivity measures and information flow patterns rather than entity detections. Section 6 of the present paper adopts that stance: the tree is a model whose process signatures, not its nodes, are the testable content.

The $p$-adic and emergent-time machinery is assessed in [14], which lays out the chain from radix $p$ to $p$-adic numbers, ultrametrics, tree hierarchies, and Bruhat-Tits buildings, and connects it to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation $H|\Psi\rangle=0$ and Page-Wootters clock conditioning $|\psi(\tau)\rangle_R=\langle\tau|_C|\Psi\rangle_{CR}$. Two results of [14] constrain this paper directly. First, its formal no-go theorem proves that for any finite-dimensional real energy spectrum with non-negative weights, the Page-Wootters conditional state distance $d(\tau_1,\tau_2)=1-|\sum_n w_n\exp(-iE_n(\tau_1-\tau_2))|$ is not strictly ultrametric for all $\tau_1,\tau_2$ unless the clock is trivial (one state, or all energies equal), because a non-constant ultrametric on connected $\mathbb{R}$ would require clopen balls that do not exist there. Second, its detectability analysis reports that a $3\%$ $p$-adic amplitude in simulated Planck data corresponds to $0.03\sigma$ and is undetectable, and that the signal must exceed $5\%$ for Planck detection, with public Planck data enabling an analysis in under an hour. Both results are used in Sections 4 and 6.

Well-foundedness and self-similarity of the tree invoke ordinal-type arguments. [1] studies normal functions — strictly increasing, limit-continuous ordinal functions, equivalently increasing enumerations of closed unbounded classes — and shows that fixed points are reached by iterates, with $f'(0)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f^n(0)$ the smallest fixed point obtained using continuity at the limit; the paper also proves equivalences between fixed-point existence for normal dilators and $\Pi^1_1$-bar induction, using families of trees $\mathcal{T}_n$ and their branches, where well-foundedness, absence of branches, and well-foundedness of the Kleene-Brouwer order are equivalent. In Section 3, the level-renormalization map of the tree is a normal function in this sense, and its fixed point defines the continuum limit of the hierarchy; the branch/well-foundedness equivalence of [1] is what makes "observer worldline" and "infinite path" interchangeable without paradox.

That a fixed substrate can generate apparent dynamics is illustrated from several directions. [9] constructs a homeomorphism on a compact metric space whose non-wandering set is a single fixed point while the induced map on the two-fold symmetric product $F_2(X)$ has infinitely many non-fixed non-wandering points, proving via Proposition 3 that pairs $\{x,y\}$ approached by orbits of the base map become non-wandering in the hyperspace even though the base dynamics is trivial. This is a precise precedent for the central claim of this paper: collective observables of an embedding (here, observer pairs and their correlations) can be dynamical even when the base structure is completely static. [6] provides the numerical analogue: its collapse method keeps the computational grid fixed while a linear term in the shift vector makes coordinates zoom in toward the centre, so apparent refinement is coordinate motion on a fixed domain — the same fixed-structure/moving-observer split used here, with the observer's path playing the role of the adapting coordinates. [8] supplies the fluctuation mechanism: for coupled harmonic oscillators with a single fixed endpoint, the averaged variance of the $n$-th particle grows linearly in $n$, so the averaged standard deviation is proportional to $\sqrt{n}$, a pseudo-random behavior arising from superposition of harmonic oscillations with irrational frequencies despite deterministic evolution. Section 4 imports this $\sqrt{n}$ law as the amplitude accumulator along worldlines.

Two further works support the design principle that a single fixed element can carry complex function. [4] proves by construction that repeated compositions $\mathcal{L}_2\circ\boldsymbol{g}^{\circ r}\circ\mathcal{L}_1$ of a single fixed-size ReLU network block exhibit expressive power far beyond the individual block, approximating functions by partitioning $[0,1]^d$ into cubes indexed by $\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}^d$; the analogue here is that iterating one fixed local branching rule over $L$ levels generates the tree's global complexity. [5] constructs, for every $n$, a single fixed shallow quantum analyzer whose fixed Weyl-Heisenberg measurement with fiducial $|\phi_n\rangle$ replaces shot-dependent measurement settings, so that the Born outcome itself labels the snapshot and the system-facing entangling depth is one; this is the measurement-theoretic version of a fixed interface generating varied readout, matching the observer-interface role of the worldline. [7] shows in hardware that a single fixed-frequency resonator coupler can mediate interactions among three transmon qubits with average CNOT fidelity exceeding $0.98$, demonstrating that a single fixed mediator is an implementable design pattern rather than a limitation. Finally, [2] models universe structure itself through a second-order homogeneous differential equation with constant real coefficients whose solutions must satisfy stability conditions drawn from the observed stability of celestial motion, an early example of deriving cosmological structure from stability constraints, which is the spirit in which Section 4 fixes $p$ by self-sistency rather than by observation.
