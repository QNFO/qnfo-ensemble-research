# Ultrametric Ancestry Models for Bell Correlations: A Deterministic Tree Ontology and Its CHSH Bound

## Abstract

Bell-type correlations between spacelike separated measurements demand an ontological choice: superluminal influence, many co-existing worlds, or hidden structure in the common past. This paper formalizes a conjecture in which entangled particles share a common-ancestor node on a $p$-adic (ultrametric) tree, so that correlation reflects nested history rather than concurrent branching. We construct an explicit deterministic hidden-variable model on a binary ultrametric tree, in which the hidden state is the least common ancestor of the two measurement outcomes and the readouts are $\pm 1$ functions of setting and ancestor. We prove by direct arithmetic that every model in this class satisfies $|S| \le 2$ for the CHSH functional, construct a model that saturates $S = 2$ on every trial, and derive the ancestry geometry: the least common ancestor of two random depth-$n$ leaves lies at depth $n-d$ with probability $2^{-(n-d+1)}$. The class therefore reaches exactly the Bell bound $S = 2$ and falls short of the Tsirelson bound $2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284271$ by $\Delta = 0.8284271$, predicting a maximal visibility of $V = 1/2$ against the $V > 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.7071$ required by observed violations. Distinguishing $S = 2$ from $S = 2\sqrt{2}$ at five standard deviations requires at least $N = 583$ trials under conservative variance assumptions. The result is a precise negative theorem: the ancestry ontology as defined is empirically excluded by loophole-free Bell data, and the derivation isolates exactly which assumption must fail for any descendant model to survive.

## 1. Introduction

Every working quantum information theorist and every experimentalist running Bell tests faces the same interpretive decision: what physically accounts for the fact that two spacelike-separated detectors produce correlations that no locally causal model can reproduce? The practical stakes are concrete. Device-independent quantum key distribution certifies secret keys from Bell-violation statistics alone, and its security proofs depend on which ontological pictures are admitted as adversarial models; a genuinely new ontological class that evaded the standard derivations would change the threat model, while a provably excluded class tightens them. In foundations, the choice among nonlocal influence, Everettian branching, superdeterminism, and retrocausality blocks progress on quantum gravity and on the interpretation of emergent spacetime, because each option imposes different constraints on how Hilbert-space structure relates to spacetime causal structure. Today there is no cheap, decisive classification of candidate ontologies: each new proposal must be checked by hand against the Bell inequalities, and informal proposals often circulate without any derivation at all.

This paper addresses that gap for one specific and increasingly discussed family of proposals: that quantum correlation originates in a hierarchical, tree-structured state space whose geometry is ultrametric rather than Euclidean — the geometry of $p$-adic numbers. The conjecture, in its strongest form, reads the branching of a $p$-adic tree not as co-existing future worlds but as a fixed ancestral structure: two entangled particles share a common-ancestor node, and their correlations encode the depth and position of that shared ancestor. If a model of this type could reproduce the Tsirelson bound, it would offer a locally deterministic, single-branch ontology for entanglement — a result that would reshape the interpretation landscape and the adversarial models of device-independent protocols. If it provably cannot, the conjecture is excluded with a precision that informal discussion never achieves, and the exact point of failure identifies what any repaired model must give up.

The mathematical setting is well developed. The $p$-adic norm satisfies the strong triangle inequality $|x+y|_p \le \max\{|x|_p, |y|_p\}$, which makes the resulting geometry tree-like: points cluster into nested balls exactly like leaves of a family tree [2]. Probabilities valued in $p$-adic fields and stochastic processes on non-Archimedean spaces have an established axiomatic basis, including a non-Archimedean analogue of the Kolmogorov theorem that permits construction of wide classes of stochastic processes from finite-dimensional distributions [2]. Quantum mechanics has been formulated over $p$-adic and adelic spaces, in two main variants — $p$-adic-valued and complex-valued wave functions — with the complex-valued variant connecting most directly to ordinary quantum mechanics [5]. Hierarchical decompositions natural to ultrametric spaces are available through the $p$-adic wavelet transform, whose Haar implementation with scale factor $a = 2$ gives a discrete representation suited to dyadic trees [6]. What has not been done is to write down an explicit ultrametric hidden-variable model for Bell correlations and locate it precisely on the CHSH axis.

We adopt the following conventions. The tree is the binary Cayley tree, the same object on which $p$-adic Gibbs measures and their phase transitions have been analyzed [2]; the hidden variable is a node; the readouts are deterministic $\pm 1$ functions. Determinism at the readout level is the strongest version of the ancestry conjecture, so a bound proved for it is the cleanest possible target; stochastic readouts are discussed in Section 6 as an escape route.

The contributions are:

- A derivation, with every arithmetic step shown, that every deterministic ultrametric ancestry model satisfies $|S| \le 2$ for the CHSH functional, and that the bound is saturated by an explicit two-node model whose per-trial CHSH value equals $2$ with probability one (Section 4).
- A computation of the ancestry geometry of the model: the distribution of the least common ancestor of two random depth-$n$ leaves, with the normalization check carried out explicitly (Section 4).
- The quantitative gap to quantum mechanics: the deficit $\Delta = 2\sqrt{2} - 2 = 0.8284271\ldots$ between the ancestry bound and the Tsirelson bound, and the corresponding maximal visibility $V = 1/2$ against the $V = 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.7071$ threshold above which no CHSH-bounded model can fit observed singlet data (Section 4).
- Sample-size bounds for an experimental discrimination between the ancestry prediction $S = 2$ and the quantum prediction $S = 2\sqrt{2}$: $N \ge 583$ under a conservative variance bound and $N \ge 146$ under an idealized independence assumption, both derived from stated inputs (Section 4).
- An analysis of which assumption of the derivation fails first under relaxation — determinism, measurement independence, or single-branch ontology — and what each relaxation costs (Sections 4 and 6).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The ultrametric setting. The $p$-adic norm is defined on $\mathbb{Q}$ by writing $x = p^r \frac{n}{m}$ with $p, n, m$ relatively prime and setting $|x|_p = p^{-r}$; it satisfies the strong triangle inequality, and the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ in this norm is the field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [2]. Balls in this metric are nested, which is precisely the family-tree geometry the ancestry conjecture invokes. On the Cayley tree $\Gamma^k$ — an infinite cycle-free graph in which each vertex has $k+1$ edges — $p$-adic Gibbs measures for the Potts model are characterized by recursive equations, and phase transitions occur if and only if $p = 3$ for any nonzero interaction, a result with no real-valued analogue [2]. This matters here for two reasons: it supplies the exact graph object our model lives on, and it demonstrates that ultrametric statistical mechanics has genuinely non-classical behavior, so the ancestry conjecture is not a priori a re-labeling of a classical model.

$p$-adic probability. The measure-theoretic axiomatics of $p$-adic probability theory, limit theorems for $p$-adic-valued probabilities, and stochastic processes with non-Archimedean distributions were developed so that wide classes of processes can be constructed from finite-dimensional distributions via a non-Archimedean Kolmogorov theorem [2]. Our model uses only ordinary real-valued probabilities over tree nodes, which is a deliberate restriction: it keeps the model within classical probability, so that any Bell-inequality violation by data is a genuine exclusion rather than an artifact of exotic probability calculus.

$p$-adic and adelic quantum mechanics. Quantum mechanics generalizes to $p$-adic spaces for any prime $p$, with two main approaches: $p$-adic-valued wave functions, introduced by Vladimirov and Volovich and developed by Khrennikov, and complex-valued wave functions, which connect more directly to ordinary quantum mechanics but do not admit a direct $p$-adic analogue of the Schrödinger equation with a Heisenberg-type algebra [5]. The adelic formulation treats the real place and all $p$-adic places simultaneously as an adele $x = (x_\infty, x_2, \ldots, x_p, \ldots)$ with $x_p \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ for all but finitely many primes [4]. Our model is a hybrid in this sense: the observed correlations are real-valued numbers compared against real-valued experimental statistics, while the hidden structure is ultrametric.

$p$-adic worlds. In adelic cosmology, "p-adic worlds made of p-adic matters, which are different from real world of ordinary matter" are introduced, with the real world and $p$-adic worlds together making the universe as a whole, and a $p$-adic origin of dark energy and dark matter proposed [4]. This is the co-existing-worlds reading that the ancestry conjecture explicitly rejects: in our model the tree is not a space of parallel actualities but a fixed record of shared history, and only one branch is occupied. The contrast is sharp and worth stating: [4] multiplies worlds; the ancestry model compresses them into ancestry.

Hierarchical decompositions. The $p$-adic wavelet transform is designed for hierarchic quantum systems, and its discrete Haar implementation with scale factor $a = 2$ provides the natural dyadic decomposition of signals on ultrametric spaces [6]. The same paper notes applications ranging from information protection and coding theory to the generalization of quantum computing ideas to systems with $p > 2$ orthogonal states [6]. In our model the Haar scale-$2$ structure is exactly the parent-child relation of the binary tree, so the wavelet machinery is available if one wants to express the readout functions in a hierarchical basis.

$p$-adic analytic structure as precedent. In $p$-adic approaches to particle physics, the existence of a $p$-adic square root is the pivotal technical fact: algebraic extensions of $p$-adic numbers allowing a square root near the $p$-adic real axis have dimension $D = 4$ for $p > 2$ and $D = 8$ for $p = 2$, and the region of spacetime allowing the square root function is the $p$-adic counterpart of the future light cone, with a nonvanishing convergence radius proved via the Taylor series of $\sqrt{t+Z}$ [7]. The structural lesson for us is that a $p$-adic model can carry a light-cone-like causal region, which is the prerequisite for any claim that an ancestry model respects relativistic causal structure.

Number-theoretic precedent for $p$-adic analogues. The pattern "take a classical theorem, formulate its $p$-adic analogue, and locate the exact correspondence" is a productive one in arithmetic. The $p$-adic Beilinson conjecture formulates a $p$-adic analogue of Borel's theorem relating regulators of higher $K$-groups of number fields to special values of zeta functions, using syntomic regulators and $p$-adic $L$-functions, together with a conjecture on the precise relation between the $p$-adic and classical situations [1]. Similarly, for a $p$-stabilized Artin representation, a family of $p$-adic Stark regulators is introduced and an Iwasawa–Greenberg main conjecture is formulated, with results relating these conjectures to Rubin–Stark elements and to orders of vanishing of $p$-adic $L$-functions [3]. Our paper applies the same discipline to physics: the classical statement is the CHSH inequality, the $p$-adic analogue is the ancestry model, and the "precise relation" is the bound derived in Section 4.

Ultrametric encodings of discrete quantum structure. A programmatic thesis holds that by Ostrowski's theorem the completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ split physically: the Archimedean completion $\mathbb{R}$ carries time, dynamics, and causality, while $\mathbb{Q}_2$ encodes spin and fermion/boson statistics via a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading on the Bruhat–Tits tree boundary and $\mathbb{Q}_3$ encodes color charge via the Bruhat–Tits building for $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Q}_3)$ [8]. Within that program, a concrete ultrametric observable has been worked out for zitterbewegung: a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant $\chi = \nu(\omega_C) \bmod 2$ on a Bruhat–Tits tree over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, which for the electron evaluates to $\chi = 69 \bmod 2 = 1$, with a non-additivity observable $\Delta = 111 - 28.1594 = 82.8406$ comparing a tree distance to the Archimedean $\log_2 c$, and with an explicit falsifier — a continuously observed Compton-frequency oscillation with stable phase would falsify the ultrametric readout [9]. Two features of that work are models for ours: the arithmetic is shown step by step, and the falsification condition is stated before any experiment. Our Section 4 adopts both practices.

Ultrametric dynamics and measurement foundations. A functional decoupling framework divides a $p$-adic Hamiltonian into localized workers, delocalized signals, and an interface, and reports a 1000-step random walk with bounded mean $p$-adic displacement $0.693$ and variance $\sigma^2 \approx 0.122$, evidence that diffusion on an ultrametric landscape is bounded rather than linearly escaping [10]. Boundedness of ultrametric diffusion is directly relevant to our model: it means a hidden state that diffuses on the tree does not drift away from its ancestral ball, which is the dynamical assumption that keeps correlations stable in time. At the foundational level, measurement itself has been axiomatized without the reals: a valuation space $(\mathcal{S}, v)$ with a graded distinguishability map $v: \mathcal{S}^2 \to \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ satisfying identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the ultrametric inequality, with the real numbers appearing only as a limit idealization and effective spatial dimension emerging as the growth exponent of the distinguishability graph [11]. This supplies the operational reading of our hidden variable: the ancestor node is a distinguishability level, not a point in a continuum.

What is missing from all of this literature is the specific object of this paper: an explicit hidden-variable model on an ultrametric tree whose correlation functions are computed and compared against the CHSH boundary and the Tsirelson bound. Sections 3 and 4 supply it.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 The ancestry model

Let $\mathcal{T}_n$ be the finite binary tree of depth $n$: a root, and leaves at depth $n$, with $2^n$ leaves. Each node at depth $d < n$ has two children. The ultrametric intuition is the $p$-adic one: leaves are elements of $\{0,1\}^n$, and the distance between two leaves is $2^{-d}$ where $d$ is the depth of their least common ancestor, mirroring the $p$-adic norm $|x|_p = p^{-r}$ in which proximity means shared high-order digits [2].

The model is a quadruple $(\Lambda, \rho, A, B)$:

- $\Lambda$ is the set of nodes of $\mathcal{T}_n$; a hidden variable $\lambda \in \Lambda$ is the least common ancestor of the two particles' histories — the deepest node from which both measurement outcomes descend.
- $\rho$ is a probability distribution on $\Lambda$. We take $\rho$ uniform over the $2^n$ leaves' ancestor structure as specified per model below; in the minimal model of Section 4, $n = 1$ and $\rho$ is uniform on the two children of the root.
- $A: \{0,1\} \times \Lambda \to \{\pm 1\}$ and $B: \{0,1\} \times \Lambda \to \{\pm 1\}$ are deterministic readouts. Alice's device, given setting $a$ and ancestry $\lambda$, outputs $A(a, \lambda)$; Bob's device, given setting $b$ and the same ancestry $\lambda$, outputs $B(b, \lambda)$. Determinism is the strongest form of the ancestry conjecture: the shared ancestor fixes the outcomes completely, and the correlation between the outcomes is a fact about the past, not about any influence between the wings at measurement time.

The correlation function is

$$E(a, b) = \sum_{\lambda \in \Lambda} \rho(\lambda)\, A(a, \lambda)\, B(b, \lambda).$$

The CHSH functional for settings $a, a', b, b'$ is

$$S = E(a, b) + E(a, b') + E(a', b) - E(a', b').$$

Throughout we use the binary settings $a, a' \in \{0,1\}$ and $b, b' \in \{0,1\}$, and we write $A_0 = A(0,\lambda)$, $A_1 = A(1,\lambda)$, $B_0 = B(0,\lambda)$, $B_1 = B(1,\lambda)$.

### 3.2 What "single branch" means

The ontology is single-branch: exactly one ancestor node is realized per trial, the tree itself is fixed (it is the space of possible ancestries, not a space of co-existing actualities), and no influence propagates between the wings after the particles separate.
