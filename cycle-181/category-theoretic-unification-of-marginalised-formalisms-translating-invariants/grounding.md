## Research idea

Marginalized formalisms' invariants are fully expressible in category theory. Claim: the core invariants of Laws of Form, Existential Graphs, Viable System Model, Catastrophe Theory, Pattern Language, and Polycontextural Logic are expressible in standard category-theoretic language with no loss of mathematical content; hence their marginalization is a notation problem, not a content problem. Test: for each formalism, construct the proposed structure rigorously—idempotent monad on a 2-category of distinctions (LoF), topos-theoretic reading of diagrammatic negation (EG), endofunctor with fixed points for recursive viability (VSM), sheaf of unfoldings on stratified spaces (CT), coalgebra for pattern composition (PL), presheaf of Heyting algebras over a contexture site (PC)—and prove equivalence or faithful translation theorems. Where a translation fails or loses content, the hypothesis is falsified and the marginalization has substantive grounds. A comparative analysis of what survives translation would yield a formal criterion separating notational from mathematical marginalization.
