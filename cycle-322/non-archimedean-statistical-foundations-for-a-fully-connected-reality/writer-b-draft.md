# Ultrametric Dependence and the Failure of Independence-Based Significance Testing: Exact Variance Calculations on a Causal Tree Model

## Abstract

Classical significance tests assume that observations are independent and identically distributed, yet many real datasets — phylogenetic measurements, multicenter clinical aggregates, repeated trials of human subjects in a shared apparatus — are generated by processes with a shared causal root, so that every pair of observations shares ancestry. This paper asks what that shared-root structure does to a p-value. We construct an explicit probability model in which $n = 2^L$ observations sit on the leaves of a binary tree, with Gaussian errors whose correlation decays as $r^{m}$ in the number $m$ of levels below the most recent common ancestor, and we interpret the tree as the branch space of a $p$-adic (ultrametric) probability measure in which basic events are nested or disjoint, mirroring the non-archimedean frame axioms. We derive the exact variance inflation factor of the sample mean: $F = 1 + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}(2r)^{j}$, which equals $1 + L/2$ when $r = 1/2$ and converges to $(1-r)/(1-2r)$ when $r < 1/2$. For $n = 64$ leaves and $r = 0.5$, a classical $z$-test at nominal level $\alpha = 0.05$ has actual size $0.327$ and power $0.516$ against an effect of $0.5\sigma$, against nominal power $0.979$; the effective sample size is $16$, not $64$. The consistency threshold $r < 1/2$ is the ultrametric analogue of a central-limit condition, and the factor $F$ is an immediately applicable correction.

## 1. Introduction

A working scientist who tests a hypothesis with a standard $t$-test or $z$-test is betting that the deviations of her observations from the mean behave like independent draws. In many fields that bet is false in a specific, structural way: the observations descend from common causes. Sequences related by a molecular clock carry ultrametric distance matrices in which every pair of taxa shares a most recent common ancestor [10]. A human-subjects experiment such as the Reality Distortion Room study, in which 20 participants each completed seven locomotion trials in a single instrumented room, produces trials that share the room, the apparatus, the instruction set, and the participant [7]. A population-health analytics pipeline such as the Bodylogical-based augmented reality tool, which simulates each individual's future health from dozens of biomarkers inside one shared model, produces outputs whose dependence runs through the model itself [6]. In each case the analyst applies a test whose null distribution is computed under independence.

The cost of that mismatch is not abstract. If the true variance of the sample mean is larger than the independence formula assumes, the test rejects far more often than its nominal level: we show below that a nominal $0.05$ test can have actual size $0.327$, meaning roughly one in three "significant" results at that level is a false positive caused by tree dependence alone. Conversely, if an analyst discards the tree structure and models the residual as generic noise, the signatures of correlated history are smoothed away exactly as the process-ontology critique of error terms describes: the error term becomes "a conceptual dumping ground for everything we do not understand" [12]. What would change if the present result holds is concrete: an analyst who estimates a single dependence parameter could replace the independence variance with a corrected variance, recover a valid test level, and know in advance when the correction is unnecessary.

The mathematical setting for shared-root dependence is naturally non-Archimedean. Ostrowski's theorem classifies the completions of the rationals, and the $p$-adic completion carries the strong triangle inequality $|x+y|_p \leq \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$, whose induced metric is ultrametric: every triangle is isosceles with the base no longer than either leg [10]. Under an ultrametric, any two balls are either nested or disjoint — precisely the geometry of a tree, and precisely the nesting structure of causal ancestry. The default to the real numbers in statistics, as elsewhere, "is not forced by nature. It is a historical and pedagogical choice" [10]. Whether a non-Archimedean substrate can displace the Archimedean default in statistics is assessed pessimistically in [10] — its conclusion notes that zero of the three closest historical analogues achieved mainstream adoption within a decade and that no empirical anomaly has been identified for the Archimedean default — but the assessment also identifies phylogenetics as an existence proof where ultrametric distance is already the native substrate [10]. Statistical testing under tree dependence is a domain where the anomaly can be produced by calculation rather than awaited.

The foundations of statistical inference are, independently, in active dispute. Mayo's refutation of Birnbaum's theorem shows that methods violating the likelihood principle need not violate sufficiency or conditionality, dissolving the argument that all standard frequentist methods are excluded [5]. The same discussion argues that the sampling model alone is insufficient for valid probabilistic inference and that something more — in the inferential-model framework, a predictable auxiliary quantity — must be added [5]. Our contribution supplies a specific "something more" for tree-structured data: an explicit dependence functional $F$ attached to the sampling model, in the spirit of the census-versus-poll reframing in which science is statistical inference from a noisy sample (the physical poll $\mathcal{E}$) to a complete population (the informational census $\Omega$) [11].

This paper's contributions are:

1. A construction of a $p$-adic (ultrametric) probability measure on the branch space of a finite causal tree, in which basic events satisfy the nested-or-disjoint axiom of non-archimedean frames (Section 3).
2. An exact derivation of the variance inflation factor $F = 1 + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}(2r)^{j}$ for the sample mean under tree-decaying correlation, the closed forms $F = 1 + L/2$ at $r = 1/2$ and $F \to (1-r)/(1-2r)$ for $r < 1/2$, and the consistency threshold $r < 1/2$ (Section 4).
3. Exact arithmetic for $n = 64$ showing actual test size $0.327$ (compound-symmetry case $0.336$), power collapse from $0.979$ to $0.516$, and effective sample size $16$ (Sections 4 and 5).
4. A sensitivity analysis over the decay parameter $r \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$ and the honest observation that the shared-root model generates inflation only, never deflation (Section 5).
5. A practical correction procedure — estimate $r$, replace $s^2/n$ with $F s^2/n$ — with its estimation risk, its readiness level, and the smallest study that would test it (Section 7).

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Non-Archimedean geometry as a working foundation.** The $p$-adic numbers are not an exotic generalization but one of two complete families of absolute values on the rationals; the strong triangle inequality makes their metric ultrametric, so that "every triangle is isosceles with the base no longer than either leg" [10]. The same source surveys where non-Archimedean modeling is already native — phylogenetics, where constant mutation rates produce ultrametric distance matrices, and isogeny-based cryptography — and tabulates the activity in adjacent domains, from roughly 50 papers per year in $p$-adic quantum mechanics to fewer than 5 in ultrametric code metrics and $p$-adic channel coding [10]. Its conclusion is a caution we adopt: the historical reference class for paradigm displacement is unfavorable, and the bet that non-Archimedean modeling displaces the real numbers in a new domain by 2036 rests on candidate domains, not demonstrated anomalies [10]. Our paper does not bet on displacement; it computes a specific quantity — a variance factor — that the ultrametric structure determines exactly.

**Frames and trees.** The pointwise geometry of $p$-adic spaces can be treated frame-theoretically. The frame of $p$-adic numbers $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is freely generated by balls $B_r(a)$ with $r \in |\mathbb{Q}| = \{p^{-n} : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, subject to relations that force any two generators to meet in one of three ways: disjoint, or one contained in the other [3]. The general definition makes this the hallmark of the category: a non-archimedean frame is a regular frame with a base in which every pair $b_1, b_2$ satisfies $b_1 \wedge b_2 = 0$, or $b_1 \leq b_2$, or $b_2 \leq b_1$ [3]. The same work attaches to each non-archimedean frame a base constituting a tree and shows every such frame is a quotient of the frame of opens of the tree's branch space [3]. This is exactly the property our probability construction exploits: cylinder events on a tree are nested or disjoint, so a measure on cylinders is automatically a measure on a non-archimedean base.
