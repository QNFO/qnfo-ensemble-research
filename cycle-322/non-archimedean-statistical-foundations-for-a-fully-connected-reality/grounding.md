## Research idea

Non-Archimedean Statistical Foundations for a Fully Connected Reality. Claim: standard parametric statistics assume independent, identically distributed alternatives, but if reality is ultrametric/p-adic—every event sharing a common causal root (Big Bang)—independence is an approximation that misestimates significance. Question: can a p-adic (non-Archimedean) statistical framework replace classical significance testing, yielding different inferences for drug trials, evolution, and social science? Why it matters: shared-tree dependence could inflate or deflate apparent 'chance' effects, invalidating p-values. Test: (1) formalize a p-adic probability measure on a Bruhat-Tits tree encoding causal ancestry; (2) derive analogues of the CLT and hypothesis tests under ultrametric dependence; (3) simulate hierarchical datasets with root-shared dependence and compare classical vs. p-adic test error rates, quantifying when classical methods fail.

  § 2.1 A Validity Principle: The sampling model for observable data $X$ , depending on an unknown parameter $\theta$ , is the familiar starting point.
