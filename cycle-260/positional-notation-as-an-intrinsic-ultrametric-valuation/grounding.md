## Research idea

Positional Notation Natively Encodes an Ultrametric Valuation. Claim: any base-b positional numeral intrinsically carries a non-Archimedean valuation — distance measured by the highest power of b dividing the difference of two integers — so the digit-string tree (clustering by trailing shared digits) is the native metric, and the Euclidean number line is an external, lossy 'flattening' frame. Conjecture ('Silent Frame Theorem'): in any positional notation formalized via a calculus of indications (marks within marks), the representation intrinsically carries an ultrametric valuation, and assuming an Archimedean metric is an undeclared additional frame. Why it matters: it reframes number representation as tree-native rather than line-native, with historical and pedagogical consequences. Test: formally prove the valuation axioms hold for digit-string comparison in arbitrary bases (including composite bases on integers); simulate hierarchical clustering of integers under b-adic vs Euclidean metrics to quantify structural divergence; formalize the LoF nesting-depth correspondence and verify it equals trailing-digit agreement depth.
