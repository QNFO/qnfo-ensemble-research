# L-Functions as Gauge-Invariant Spectra across Archimedean, Ultrametric, and Hierarchical Geometries

## Abstract

A recurring obstacle in mathematical physics is that the same arithmetic object — an $L$-function — is studied through incompatible geometries: the Archimedean hyperbolic plane, the Bruhat–Tits tree of a $p$-adic field, and discrete hierarchies such as the prime-factorization tree, with no computable procedure that says which geometric features are intrinsic and which are artifacts of the chosen field. This paper treats the choice between $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p$ as a gauge choice and asks what survives it. We work with the theta $L$-function of the square lattice, the Laplacian on the $(p+1)$-regular Bruhat–Tits tree, and a cophenetic distance on the prime-factorization hierarchy, and we carry out the arithmetic explicitly. We verify the Kummer congruence $B_{10}/10 \equiv B_6/6 \pmod 5$ and $B_6/6 \equiv B_2/2 \pmod 5$ by direct computation, bracket the theta $L$-value $L(2,\theta)$ in the interval $[1.50465,\,1.50921]$, derive the tree spectral edge $2\sqrt{p}$ (for $p=2$: $2.8284271$), and show by counterexample ($m=4$, $n=6$, $r=9$) that the naive cophenetic distance fails the ultrametric inequality, $d(4,9)=4 > \max(d(4,6),d(6,9))=3$. The results convert a conjectural correspondence into a finite test program: congruence checks at roots of unity, spectral comparison on trees, and explicit distance computations, each of which can confirm or falsify the gauge-invariance claim.

## 1. Introduction

Researchers who model arithmetic or physical systems on non-Archimedean ground fields face a concrete methodological problem today. A team simulating quantum error correction on a Bruhat–Tits tree, a number theorist computing Maass forms on the hyperbolic plane, and an analyst of hierarchical data all manipulate objects that are formally the same — spectra of Laplacians, $L$-functions, distance structures — but no shared computational protocol exists that tells them which numerical features of their results depend on the underlying field and which are intrinsic to the arithmetic object itself. The cost is practical: duplicated effort, numerical comparisons that cannot be replicated across fields, and cross-domain claims (for example, that tree spectra and hyperbolic spectra share governing zeros) that are stated but never subjected to a finite, checkable test. If a single $L$-function could be shown to serve as an invariant readable in all three geometries, then a researcher could validate a $p$-adic computation against an Archimedean one, and a discrete hierarchical model against both, using a shared checklist of numbers.

The stakes are not abstract. The literature already contains proved instances of arithmetic objects behaving identically across field choices: the Modularity Theorem identifies the local point counts $a_p$ of an elliptic curve with the Fourier coefficients $a_p$ of a weight-two modular form, so that $L(E,s) = L(f,s)$ identically [9], and the Breuil–Colmez theorem establishes the $p$-adic Langlands correspondence as a bijection internal to the $p$-adic world [9]. Ostrowski's theorem guarantees that the Archimedean and $p$-adic absolute values exhaust all ways of measuring a rational number, so any structure that must interact with all measurements must accommodate both views [9]. What is missing between these proved bridgeheads and the proposed unified pictures is a middle layer: explicit, small-scale computations in which the same $L$-function is evaluated in each geometry and the outputs are compared number by number.

This paper builds that middle layer for one concrete object, the theta $L$-function of the square lattice, and for two companion structures: the adjacency/Laplacian spectrum of the $(p+1)$-regular tree and a cophenetic distance on the prime-factorization hierarchy. We treat the selection of the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ (real or $p$-adic) as a gauge choice: it changes the visible geometry but, under the conjecture tested here, not the governing spectrum. The $p$-adic side of the program is well developed in the literature — $p$-adic $L$-functions are constructed by analytic interpolation [1, 3, 8], in families [4, 6], and are connected to arithmetic conjectures such as Bloch–Kato [7] — and the physical side has been articulated in terms of ultrametric computation and $q$-adic geometry [9, 10, 11, 12]. Our contribution is the explicit arithmetic that any such unification must survive.

The contributions are:

- A derivation of the Bernoulli number $B_{10} = 5/66$ from the generating-function recurrence and a numerical verification of the Kummer congruence at $p=5$ for the pairs $(m,p) = (2,5)$ and $(6,5)$, establishing that the congruence structure underlying $p$-adic interpolation of the theta $L$-function holds at the computed points (Section 4.1).
- A bracketing of the theta $L$-value $L(2,\theta) = \zeta(2)\,L(2,\chi_{-4})$ in the interval $[1.50465,\,1.50921]$, computed from a partial Euler sum with a rigorous alternating-series error bound (Section 4.2).
- An explicit computation of the root-of-unity theta sum $S = \sum_{n \geq 1} 2^{-n} i^{n^2} = \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{2}{3}i$ with $|S| = \sqrt{5}/3 \approx 0.745356$, as the finite prototype of the interference test at $q = e^{2\pi i a/b}$ (Section 4.3).
- A derivation of the spectral edge $2\sqrt{p}$ of the adjacency operator on the $(p+1)$-regular Bruhat–Tits tree, with the values $2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284271$ and $2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.4641016$ (Section 4.4).
- A counterexample showing that the cophenetic distance $d(m,n) = \Omega(m) + \Omega(n) - 2\,\Omega(\gcd(m,n))$ on the prime-factorization hierarchy violates the ultrametric inequality, with $d(4,9) = 4 > \max(d(4,6), d(6,9)) = 3$ (Section 4.5).

## 2. Background and Related Work

The $p$-adic $L$-function literature supplies the interpolation machinery that any gauge-invariance claim must rest on. Washington-style analytic constructions are the oldest layer: [1] constructs $p$-adic meromorphic families of Siegel–Eisenstein series whose Fourier coefficients are $p$-adically continued for all primes $p$, using the reciprocal of a product of $L$-functions; its main-theorem proof writes each coefficient as a quotient $a_h^{(p)}(k) = S^E_{\theta,h}(t)/P^E_{\theta,h}(t)$ of a numerator built from unit factors $U^*_{\theta,c}$ and a denominator that is a distinguished polynomial $P_{\theta,c}$, meromorphic in the unit disc with finitely many poles at the roots of $P_\theta$. This quotient structure matters here because it shows that the $p$-adic incarnation of an $L$-function is literally a ratio of polynomials in the cyclotomic variable $t = (1+p)^k - 1$: the "spectrum" of the $L$-function in the $p$-adic gauge is the zero set of an explicit distinguished polynomial, which is exactly the kind of object a tree-side computation could target.

The congruence substrate of that interpolation is classical.
