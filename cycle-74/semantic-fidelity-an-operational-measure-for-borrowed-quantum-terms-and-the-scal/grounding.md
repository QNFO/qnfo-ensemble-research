## Research idea

A Formal Semantic-Fidelity Measure for Borrowed Quantum Terms. The author conjectures that quantum computing's vocabulary imports ontological baggage from quantum optics, condensed matter, and particle physics, and that this baggage becomes actively misleading as systems scale. The concrete original claim: one can define an operational, quantitative measure of 'semantic fidelity' for borrowed terms, and identify a qubit count or code distance at which a term like 'photon' becomes provably misleading by some operational criterion. This matters because semantic drift could explain stalled collaborations, misallocated funding, and design dead ends, and because a vocabulary-neutral formulation could sharpen tests of the quantum formalism versus its particle-based interpretation. A paper could formalize semantic fidelity as a divergence between the operational predictions implied by a term's native ontology and those of the target circuit model; then simulate growing entangled systems (e.g., photonic vs. circuit-model descriptions of multi-qubit gates and error-corrected codes) to locate where the divergence becomes decision-relevant, and survey engineer mental models to validate the measure empirically.
