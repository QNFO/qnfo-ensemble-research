## Research idea

Born Rule as Haar Measure on p-Adic Tree Balls. The note conjectures that quantum probabilities arise structurally: the Born rule equals the Haar (topological volume) measure of p-adic balls on a static, ultrametric Bruhat–Tits tree, with Bell correlations explained by shared ancestry (superdeterminism) rather than nonlocality. This matters because it would derive quantum statistics from discrete geometry instead of postulating them, and make the wavefunction purely epistemic. A formal analysis could test it by (1) constructing a p-adic branching model and proving its outcome probabilities reproduce Born-rule statistics for interference and measurement sequences, or fail to; (2) checking whether the shared-ancestor correlation model can violate CHSH inequalities while satisfying the statistical-independence assumptions experimentally bounded by superdeterminism loophole tests; (3) simulating coarse-grained boundary projections to see whether continuous QFT-like behavior emerges. Any quantitative mismatch with observed Born-rule or Bell statistics would falsify the conjecture.
