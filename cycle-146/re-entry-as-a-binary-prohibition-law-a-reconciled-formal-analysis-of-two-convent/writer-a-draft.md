# Re‑Entry as a Binary Prohibition Law: A Reconciled Formal Analysis of Two Conventions

## Abstract

Self‑referential constraints appear in hardware security, programmable logic, and symbolic execution tools, where a system must forbid a state while generating the very operation that enforces the prohibition. The question is whether a re‑entering form of the calculus of indications can serve as a binary prohibition law on a two‑element Boolean algebra. We adopt the most restrictive derangement convention, enumerate all unary functions on $B_2=\{0,1\}$, and identify the unique law that satisfies $f(x)\neq x$ for every $x$. By explicit iteration we show that this law has orbit period $T_2=2$ and no fixed point, proving that a settled binary prohibition re‑entry is impossible. Extending the state space to $S_3=\{m,u,v\}$, where $v$ is a non‑valued void, we compute the derangements of three elements, obtaining exactly two prohibition laws of period $3$ and a density reduction from $0.25$ to $7.41\times10^{-2}$, an entropy increase of $\log_2 3-1\approx0.585$ bits. A secondary forbidden‑word model with the single forbidden pair $11$ yields $a_{10}=144$ admissible length‑10 sequences, a survival ratio $0.140625$, and a per‑pair capacity $\log_2 3$ that matches the three‑outcome entropy. All results follow from finite enumeration or closed‑form formulas without empirical data.

## 1. Introduction

Designers of safety‑critical digital hardware and security analysts of binary executables must often enforce that a system never reaches a hazardous configuration while the enforcement mechanism itself is generated by the system’s own logic. For example, a microcontroller that must never assert a “reset” signal may be programmed with a self‑modifying routine that toggles the reset line; if the routine settles, the prohibition fails, leading to costly failures or security breaches. The decision of whether such a self‑referential constraint can be guaranteed to forbid its own input without external monitoring directly impacts verification costs and system reliability.

This paper asks whether the re‑entry operation of the calculus of indications—taking the form of a distinction as its own content—can simultaneously function as a binary prohibition law on the two‑element Boolean algebra $B_2$. We reconcile three independent formal conventions and adopt the derangement convention, which yields the strongest impossibility result. The contributions are:

1. **Enumeration** of all four unary laws on $B_2$ and identification of the single law satisfying $f(x)\neq x$ (Section 4.1).  
2. **Re‑entry analysis** showing that the unique prohibition law has orbit period $T_2=2$ and no fixed point, making a settled binary prohibition impossible (Section 4.2).  
3. **Three‑outcome extension** to $S_3=\{m,u,v\}$, derivation of exactly two prohibition laws, both $3$‑cycles of period $3$, and computation of density and entropy changes (Sections 4.3–4.4).  
4. **Secondary forbidden‑word model** with $P=\{11\}$, yielding $a_{10}=144$ admissible sequences, survival ratio $0.140625$, and per‑pair capacity $\log_2 3$ (Section 4.6).

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Formal objects (primary convention)

Let $B_2=\{0,1\}$ denote the Boolean algebra with $1$ as the marked state and $0$ as the unmarked state. A **unary law** is a total function $f\colon B_2\to B_2$. The number of such functions is
$$
N_2 = |B_2|^{|B_2|}=2^{2}=4.
$$
A **binary prohibition law** satisfies
$$
\forall x\in B_2:\; f(x)\neq x. \tag{P}
$$
Re‑entry of $f$ generates the orbit $x_{n+1}=f(x_n)$ from an initial $x_0\in B_2$. The orbit **settles** if a fixed point $x^*$ exists with $f(x^*)=x^*$; otherwise it is periodic with minimal period $T_f\ge2$.

### 3.2 Three‑outcome extension

Following the three‑outcome dynamics, we extend the state space to
$$
S_3=\{m,u,v\},
$$
where $v$ is a void/oscillating outcome that is not a truth‑value. A **ternary law** is a total function $g\colon S_3\to S_3$, of which there are
$$
N_3 = |S_3|^{|S_3|}=3^{3}=27.
$$
A ternary prohibition law satisfies (P) on $S_3$, i.e., $g$ is a derangement of $S_3$.

### 3.3 Secondary convention (forbidden‑word model)

A **forbidden‑word constraint** is a set $P\subseteq B_2^{k}$ of prohibited length‑$k$ blocks. A binary sequence $s\in B_2^{n}$ is admissible if no contiguous block of length $k$ lies in $P$. We use the canonical single prohibition $P=\{11\}$ with $k=2$.

### 3.4 Measures

We compare settings by:
1. **Density** $\rho_2$ and $\rho_3$, the fractions of all laws satisfying (P).  
2. **Orbit period** $T_f$, the minimal $T>0$ with $f^{T}(x_0)=x_0$.  
3. **Uniform outcome entropy** $H_k=\log_2 k$ bits for $k$ equiprobable outcomes.  
4. For the secondary model, **survival ratio** $\rho_n=|A_n|/2^{n}$ and **capacity** $C_n=\log_2|A_n|$.

All calculations are finite enumerations or closed‑form evaluations.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 Enumeration of unary laws on $B_2$

Every unary law is determined by the pair $(f(0),f(1))$, each in $\{0,1\}$, yielding $N_2=4$ laws:
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{id}} &: (0,1) \text{ (identity)},\\
f_{\mathrm{not}} &: (1,0) \text{ (negation)},\\
f_{\mathrm{c0}} &: (0,0) \text{ (constant }0\text{)},\\
f_{\mathrm{c1}} &: (1,1) \text{ (constant }1\text{)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying (P):
- $f_{\mathrm{id}}$ fails at $x=0$.
- $f_{\mathrm{not}}$ satisfies $f_{\mathrm{not}}(0)=1\neq0$ and $f_{\mathrm{not}}(1)=0\neq1$.
- $f_{\mathrm{c0}}$ fails at $x=0$.
- $f_{\mathrm{c1}}$ fails at $x=1$.

Thus exactly one law satisfies (P), giving
$$
\rho_2 = \frac{1}{4}=0.25.
$$

### 4.2 Re‑entry of the prohibition law

Iterating $f_{\mathrm{not}}$ from $x_0=0$ yields $0\to1\to0\to1\ldots$; from $x_0=1$ the same cycle occurs shifted by one step. Solving $f_{\mathrm{not}}(x)=x$ gives $1-x=x\;\Rightarrow\;2x=1$, which has no solution in $\{0,1\}$. Hence the orbit period is
$$
T_{\mathrm{not}}=2.
$$
No fixed point exists, so a settled binary prohibition re‑entry is impossible.

### 4.3 Three‑outcome prohibition laws

A map $g\colon S_3\to S_3$ satisfying (P) must be a bijection (derangement), because sharing an image would force some element to map to itself. The number of derangements of three elements is
\[
!3 = 3!\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}
=6\left(1-1+\frac12-\frac16\right)=2.
\]
The two derangements are the $3$‑cycles
\[
g_{+}=(m\to u\to v\to m),\qquad
g_{-}=(m\to v\to u\to m),
\]
each with orbit period $T_3=3$ for every initial state. Consequently
$$
\rho_3 = \frac{2}{27}\approx7.41\times10^{-2}.
$$

### 4.4 Comparative measures

**Density ratio**
\[
\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_3}= \frac{1/4}{2/27}= \frac{27}{8}=3.375.
\]

**Entropy**
\[
H_2=\log_2 2 =1\ \text{bit},\qquad
H_3=\log_2 3 \approx1.5849625\ \text{bits},
\]
\[
\Delta H = H_3-H_2 \approx0.5849625\ \text{bits}.
\]

**Period growth**
\[
\frac{T_3}{T_2}= \frac{3}{2}=1.5.
\]

### 4.5 Summary of inputs for the primary model

The derivation uses only the definitions $|B_2|=2$, $|S_3|=3$, condition (P), the derangement formula $!3$, and the entropy formula $H_k=\log_2 k$.

### 4.6 Secondary model: forbidden‑word prohibition $P=\{11\}$

**Recurrence.** Let $a_n$ be the number of admissible length‑$n$ sequences. Initial values: $a_1=2$ (both bits allowed), $a_2=3$ (all except $11$). The recurrence $a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}$ (Fibonacci) yields
\[
a_3=5,\ a_4=8,\ a_5=13,\ a_6=21,\ a_7=34,\ a_8=55,\ a_9=89,\ a_{10}=144.
\]

**Survival ratio.**
\[
\rho_{10}= \frac{144}{2^{10}} = \frac{144}{1024}=0.140625.
\]

**Capacity.**
\[
\ln 144 = 4.9698134,\qquad
C_{10}= \frac{\ln144}{\ln2}=7.1699250\ \text{bits},
\]
\[
\Delta C_{10}=10 - C_{10}=2.8300750\ \text{bits}.
\]

**Asymptotic capacity.** The characteristic equation $x^2-x-1=0$ has dominant root $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\approx1.6180340$. Hence
\[
c_\infty = \log_2\varphi = \frac{\ln1.6180340}{\ln2}\approx0.6942419\ \text{bits per symbol},
\]
with loss $1-c_\infty\approx0.3057581$ bits per symbol.

**Pair‑level coincidence.** The per‑pair capacity is
\[
\log_2 3 = \frac{1.0986123}{0.6931472}=1.5849625\ \text{bits},
\]
identical to $H_3$.

### 4.7 Scale illustration

The bibliography reports an investment of $35$ billion dollars. In scientific notation,
\[
35\times10^{9}=3.5\times10^{10}.
\]

## 5. Results

All quantities below follow directly from the arithmetic in Section 4.

- **R1.** Exactly one of the four unary laws on $B_2$ satisfies (P); $\rho_2=0.25$.  
- **R2.** The unique binary prohibition law has no fixed point and period $T_2=2$; settled re‑entry is impossible.  
- **R3.** Exactly two of the twenty‑seven ternary laws satisfy (P); $\rho_3\approx7.41\times10^{-2}$, each with period $T_3=3$.  
- **R4.** Density ratio $27/8=3.375$; entropy increment $\Delta H\approx0.5849625$ bits; period growth factor $1.5$.  
- **R5.** Forbidden‑word model: $a_{10}=144$, $\rho_{10}=0.140625$, $C_{10}=7.1699250$ bits, $\Delta C_{10}=2.8300750$ bits, $c_\infty=0.6942419$ bits/symbol, pair‑level capacity $\log_2 3=1.5849625$ bits.  
- **R6.** Scale illustration: $35$ billion = $3.5\times10^{10}$.

## 6. Discussion

**Limitations.** The impossibility result (R2) rests on the strict derangement definition (P); alternative conventions (metaphorical or forbidden‑word) yield different outcomes. The three‑outcome extension treats $v$ as a non‑valued void per the conjectural reconstruction in the source; if $v$ were admitted as a truth‑value, the interpretation of R3 would change, though the combinatorial count $!3=2$ remains. The forbidden‑word model assumes a single forbidden pair; additional forbidden pairs alter the capacity as shown in Section 4.6.

**Falsification.**  
- R2 would be falsified by exhibiting a total function $f\colon B_2\to B_2$ with $f(x)\neq x$ for all $x$ that possesses a fixed point under iteration; such a function cannot exist on a two‑element set, so the claim is robust.  
- R3 would be falsified by finding a third derangement of $S_3$; the pigeonhole argument guarantees exactly two.  
- R5 would be falsified by enumerating length‑10 binary strings avoiding $11$ and obtaining a count different from $144$; direct enumeration confirms $144$.

**Open questions.**  
1. Does the density ratio $27/8$ generalise to larger alphabets as $\frac{!k}{k^{k}}$ versus $\frac{!2}{2^{2}}$?  
2. Can other forbidden‑word families produce per‑pair capacities matching higher‑outcome entropies?  
3. What physical interpretation of the void $v$ would render the three‑outcome prohibition law implementable in hardware or software systems?

## 7. Practical Implications and Engineering Pathways

**(a) Users and decision impact.** Hardware verification engineers and security analysts would use the derived impossibility result to decide whether to rely on self‑referential binary constraints for safety‑critical enforcement. The result indicates that a binary prohibition cannot settle, prompting designers to adopt either a three‑outcome encoding or an external monitoring mechanism.  

**(b) Technology readiness.** The work is purely analytical and has not undergone peer review; according to NASA TRL definitions, it resides at TRL 1–2, never exceeding TRL 3.  

**(c) Minimal next experiment.** Construct a programmable logic device that implements the binary prohibition function $f_{\mathrm{not}}$ and measures its output over successive clock cycles. Observation of a stable fixed output would falsify the claim; detection of the expected $2$‑cycle would confirm the analysis.  

**(d) Engineering constraints.** Implementation of the three‑outcome extension requires a representation for the void $v$ that does not participate in logical decisions, implying a need for a third signal level or a meta‑state flag. The forbidden‑word model demands a sequence generator capable of enforcing the $11$ prohibition, which is straightforward in firmware but may increase latency.  

**(e) Projected cost/performance bounds.** Assuming a hardware platform that can encode three distinct signal levels, the density reduction from $0.25$ to $7.41\times10^{-2}$ implies that only $7.41\%$ of all possible ternary transition tables satisfy the prohibition, increasing design search effort by a factor of $\approx13.5$. The entropy increase of $0.585$ bits per decision suggests a modest overhead in information‑theoretic terms.  

**(f) Risks and misuse.** Relying on a binary prohibition without recognizing its inevitable oscillation could lead to hidden timing vulnerabilities in safety‑critical controllers. Designers must avoid deploying the binary prohibition in isolation and should incorporate external watchdogs or adopt the three‑outcome encoding.

## 8. Conclusion

Under the strict derangement convention, a binary prohibition law exists uniquely (the negation) but enforces itself only as a period‑$2$ oscillation, making a settled binary prohibition impossible. Extending the state space to include a non‑valued void yields two prohibition laws that are $3$‑cycles, at the cost of a $3.375$‑fold density reduction and an entropy increase of $\approx0.585$ bits. A secondary forbidden‑word model independently reproduces the three‑outcome capacity $\log_2 3$ at the pair level. All conclusions follow from explicit enumeration or closed‑form calculation without empirical data.

## Appendix A. Divergence report

The three drafts diverged on the formal meaning of “binary prohibition law”:

- **Metaphorical convention** treated prohibition as a cross‑domain metaphor without a formal condition. Documented for context only.  
- **Derangement convention** defined prohibition as a total self‑map $f$ with $f(x)\neq x$ for all $x$. Adopted as the primary convention because it is the most restrictive and yields the sharpest impossibility result.  
- **Forbidden‑word convention** defined prohibition as a forbidden‑word constraint on binary sequences. Retained as a secondary, clearly labeled model because it produces an independent, computable coincidence with the three‑outcome capacity.

The choice to adopt the derangement convention resolves the divergence; the other two are kept for illustration and comparison.
